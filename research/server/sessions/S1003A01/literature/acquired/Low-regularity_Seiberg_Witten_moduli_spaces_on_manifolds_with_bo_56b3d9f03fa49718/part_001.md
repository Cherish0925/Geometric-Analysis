## Abstract

For a compact spin^{c} manifold _X_ with boundary b\_1(\\partial X)=0, we consider moduli spaces of solutions to the Seiberg–Witten equations in a generalized double Coulomb slice in W^{1,2} Sobolev regularity. We prove they are Hilbert manifolds, prove denseness and “semi-infinite-dimensionality” properties of the restriction to \\partial X, and establish a gluing theorem. To achieve these, we prove a general regularity theorem and a strong unique continuation principle for Dirac operators, and smoothness of a restriction map to configurations of higher regularity on the interior, all of which are of independent interest.

## 1 Introduction

This article is motivated by an effort to provide a new framework for constructing so-called Floer theories, which are used to construct invariants of low-dimensional manifolds, knots, as well as Lagrangians in symplectic manifolds. Originally, Floer implanted ideas from finite-dimensional Morse theory (cf. \[ [29](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR29 "Schwarz, M.: Morse Homology. Progress in Mathematics, vol. 111. Birkhäuser Verlag, Basel (1993)"), [32](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR32 "Witten, E.: Supersymmetry and Morse theory. J. Differ. Geom. 17(4), 661–692 (1982)")\]) to the study of functions on infinite-dimensional spaces (often called _functionals_ on _configuration spaces_), leading to the resolution of the Arnold conjecture in symplectic topology \[ [9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR9 "Floer, A.: Morse theory for fixed points of symplectic diffeomorphisms. Bull. Am. Math. Soc. (N.S.) 16(2), 279–281 (1987)"), [10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR10 "Floer, A.: Morse theory for Lagrangian intersections. J. Differ. Geom. 28(3), 513–547 (1988)")\]. Inspired by Donaldson’s proof of the diagonalization theorem for 4-manifolds \[ [7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR7 "Donaldson, S.K.: An application of gauge theory to four-dimensional topology. J. Differ. Geom. 18(2), 279–315 (1983)")\], Floer used these ideas to construct homological invariants of 3-manifolds, aiming to establish gluing formulas for Donaldson’s invariants. An analogous construction for knots in 3-manifolds has been carried out by Kronheimer and Mrowka and plays the key role in the proof that Khovanov homology detects the unknot \[ [16](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR16 "Kronheimer, P.B., Mrowka, T.S.: Khovanov homology is an unknot-detector. Publ. Math. Inst. Hautes Études Sci. 113, 97–208 (2011)")\]. An analogue for the Seiberg–Witten equations (in place of the anti-self-duality (ASD) equations of Donaldson’s theory) is the central ingredient of Manolescu’s disproof of the triangulation conjecture in dimensions \\ge 5 \[ [24](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR24 "Manolescu, C.: The Conley index, gauge theory, and triangulations. J. Fixed Point Theory Appl. 13(2), 431–457 (2013)")\].

While the idea of using Morse theory in infinite-dimensional settings dates back to Atiyah and Bott \[ [3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR3 "Atiyah, M.F., Bott, R.: The Yang–Mills equations over Riemann surfaces. Philos. Trans. R. Soc. Lond. Ser. A 308(1505), 523–615 (1983)")\], Floer’s novelty was dealing with functionals having critical points of infinite index. They key observation was that the unstable manifolds of critical points were of finite index with respect to a certain subbundle of the tangent space, allowing one to define finite relative indices of critical points and eventually leading to a computation of “middle-dimensional” homology groups of the space. As noted by Atiyah \[ [2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR2 "Atiyah, M.: New invariants of                                                                               $$3$$                                                               3                                                         - and                                                                               $$4$$                                                               4                                                         -dimensional manifolds. The mathematical heritage of Hermann Weyl (Durham, NC, 1987). In: Proceedings of Symposium on Pure Mathematics, vol. 48. American Mathematical Society, Providence, pp. 285–299 (1988)")\] (cf. \[ [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR5 "Cohen, R.L., Jones, J.D.S., Segal, G.B.: Floer’s infinite dimensional Morse theory and Homotopy theory. In: The Floer memorial volume. Progress in Mathematics, vol. 133. Birkhäuser, Basel, pp. 297–325 (1995)")\]), Floer theory was, from the very beginning, understood as describing the behavior of so-called _semi-infinite-dimensional cycles_.

The framework of semi-infinite-dimensional cycles developed by Lipyanskiy \[ [21](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR21 "Lipyanskiy, M.: A semi-infinite cycle construction of Floer homology (2008). Thesis (Ph.D.)—Massachusetts Institute of Technology")\] and the author \[ [31](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR31 "Suwara, P.: Semi-infinite Homology of Floer spaces (2020). Thesis (Ph.D.)—Massachusetts Institute of Technology")\] relies on a compactness property for solutions to Seiberg–Witten equations which does not hold in spaces L^2\_k for k>1. Precisely, to define a relative invariant of a 4-manifold _X_ with boundary one needs to prove that for any bounded sequence of solutions to Seiberg–Witten equations on _X_ such that the values of the Chern–Simons–Dirac functional {\\mathcal {L}}\_{CSD} on the restrictions to \\partial X are bounded there exists a (strongly) convergent subsequence. This is true in L^2\_1 since, in a precise sense, {\\mathcal {L}}\_{CSD} “controls” the L^2\_1 norm of solutions (c.f. \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR14 "Kronheimer, P.B., Mrowka, T.S.: Monopoles and Three-Manifolds. New Mathematical Monographs, vol. 10. Cambridge University Press, Cambridge (2007)"), Equation (4.21) and Theorem 5.1.1\]).

In L^2\_1, even proving that the solution space is a (Hilbert) manifold is more challenging than in higher regularity for the following reason. The Seiberg–Witten equations are nonlinear of the form D x + Q(x) = y (c.f. Definition [3.5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar18)) where _x_, _y_ are sections of some vector bundles over _X_, _D_ is a first-order linear elliptic operator and _Q_ is a quadratic operator induced by certain multiplication of sections. The differential of the left-hand side is thus of the form v \\mapsto Dv + K v, where _K_ is a certain multiplication by x \\in L^2\_k. If k \\ge 2, then by Sobolev multiplication the operator _K_ is continuous as an operator L^2\_k \\rightarrow L^2\_k and the usual methods of solving the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer (APS) boundary value problem apply (c.f. \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR14 "Kronheimer, P.B., Mrowka, T.S.: Monopoles and Three-Manifolds. New Mathematical Monographs, vol. 10. Cambridge University Press, Cambridge (2007)"), Section 17.1\]). For example, one uses the continuity of K:L^2\_k \\rightarrow L^2\_k for proving regularity results for solutions of Dv + Kv = 0 and the surjectivity of D+K:L^2\_k \\rightarrow L^2\_{k-1}. Furthermore, for k \\ge 2 on a cylinder \[a,b\] \\times Y we can express _K_ as a time-dependent multiplication K\_t: L^2\_{k-1/2} \\rightarrow L^2\_{k-1/2}, which is useful for proving unique continuation (c.f. \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR14 "Kronheimer, P.B., Mrowka, T.S.: Monopoles and Three-Manifolds. New Mathematical Monographs, vol. 10. Cambridge University Press, Cambridge (2007)"), Proposition 7.1.2\]). All in all, the main challenge when working in L^2\_1 is that _K_ does not induce a map L^2\_1 \\rightarrow L^2\_1, just a compact map L^2\_1 \\rightarrow L^2.

There are several reasons for developing this theory. As in Morse homology (cf. \[ [29](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR29 "Schwarz, M.: Morse Homology. Progress in Mathematics, vol. 111. Birkhäuser Verlag, Basel (1993)")\]), one needs to overcome analytical difficulties to even define Floer theories and prove they are well-defined and independent of choices – one needs to establish regularity and compactness of moduli spaces of trajectories between critical points. Moreover, Morse theory is generally not well-suited for equivariant constructions since one in general cannot guarantee regularity of the moduli spaces without breaking the symmetry coming from a group action. To address these problems and to allow general constructions of equivariant (cf. \[ [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR6 "Daemi, A., Scaduto, C.: Equivariant aspects of singular instanton Floer homology (2019) preprint"), [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR14 "Kronheimer, P.B., Mrowka, T.S.: Monopoles and Three-Manifolds. New Mathematical Monographs, vol. 10. Cambridge University Press, Cambridge (2007)"), [19](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR19 "Lin, F.: A Morse–Bott approach to monopole Floer homology and the triangulation conjecture. Mem. Am. Math. Soc. 255(1221), v+162 (2018)"), [26](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR26 "Miller, S.M.: Equivariant Instanton Homology, 2019. Thesis (Ph.D.)—University of California, Los Angeles")\]) and generalized Floer homologies (cf. \[ [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR1 "Abouzaid, M., Blumberg, A.J.: Arnold Conjecture and Morava K-theory (2021) preprint"), [20](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR20 "Lin, J.: Pin(2)-equivariant KO-theory and intersection forms of spin 4-manifolds. Algebr. Geom. Topol. 15(2), 863–902 (2015)"), [25](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR25 "Manolescu, C.: On the intersection forms of spin four-manifolds with boundary. Math. Ann. 359(3–4), 695–728 (2014)")\]), Lipyanskiy \[ [21](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR21 "Lipyanskiy, M.: A semi-infinite cycle construction of Floer homology (2008). Thesis (Ph.D.)—Massachusetts Institute of Technology")\] introduced a framework for using such semi-infinite-dimensional cycles as a tool for defining Floer theories and the author has further developed these methods in \[ [31](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR31 "Suwara, P.: Semi-infinite Homology of Floer spaces (2020). Thesis (Ph.D.)—Massachusetts Institute of Technology")\]. This article contains results required to rigorously define the relative invariants of 4-manifolds with boundary and maps induced by cobordisms in this construction, using the Seiberg–Witten equations. Moreover, the key result is a gluing theorem which is fundamental for establishing functoriality of the cobordism maps.

### 1.1 Results

Consider a 4-manifold _X_ with boundary a nonempty collection of rational homology spheres (i.e., b\_1(\\partial X) = 0) and a spin^{c} structure \\hat{{\\mathfrak {s}}} on _X_. We study the moduli space of solutions to the Seiberg–Witten equations for pairs (A,\\Phi ) of a spin^{c} connection _A_ on _X_ and a section \\Phi  of the spinor bundle S^+ over _X_ associated to the spin^{c} structure \\hat{{\\mathfrak {s}}}:

(where \\iota \_{\\partial X}: \\partial X \\hookrightarrow X denotes the inclusion) together with a condition restricting A-A\_0 to a subset of codimension b\_0(\\partial X) - 1 (Definition [3.16](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar35)). This additional condition depends on a choice of a gauge splitting _s_ (Definition [3.12](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar28)). The moduli space ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw73/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq41_HTML.gif) (Definition [3.32](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar59)) is the quotient of the space of L^2\_1-solutions to the Seiberg–Witten equations in this split Coulomb slice by the (discrete) action of the split gauge group (Definition [3.19](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar39)); this gauge group preserves the split Coulomb slice as well as the set of solutions to the Seiberg–Witten equations. Firstly, we choose a gauge twisting \\tau  (Definition [3.25](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar48)) and define the (S^1-equivariant) twisted restriction map ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw211/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq49_HTML.gif), taking values in the configurations on \\partial X in the Coulomb slice (Definition [3.3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar16)). Geom. Topol. 19(3), 1631–1655 (2015)"), [21](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR21 "Lipyanskiy, M.: A semi-infinite cycle construction of Floer homology (2008). ### Semi-infinite-dimensionality Theorem

The moduli spaces ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw73/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq51_HTML.gif) are Hilbert manifolds. The differential of the twisted restriction map ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw211/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq52_HTML.gif) decomposes into ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw258/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq53_HTML.gif) which is Fredholm and ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw258/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq54_HTML.gif) which is compact.

Moreover, if b\_0(\\partial X)>1, then for any connected component Y\_0 \\subset \\partial X the restriction R\_{\\tau ,Y\_0} to Y\_0 has dense differential.

The maps \\Pi ^\\pm  come from a decomposition ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw273/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq60_HTML.gif) according to the eigenvalues of the operator ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw66/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq61_HTML.gif), also called a _polarization_ (Definition [3.4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar17)).

The proof utilizes the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer boundary value problem for an extended linearized Seiberg–Witten operator (Definition [4.1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar62)). Then we prove its properties transfer to the restriction to the Coulomb slice.

Our low regularity setting requires us to prove a regularity theorem (Regularity Theorem) for an operator of the form D = D\_0 + K: L^2\_1 \\rightarrow L^2, where D\_0 is a Dirac operator and _K_ is any compact operator, making it a result of independent interest. We also prove a strong version of the unique continuation principle for _D_ (Strong UCP Theorem).

Secondly, we prove a gluing theorem for a composite cobordism. Assume _X_ splits as X = X\_1 \\cup \_Y X\_2 along a rational homology sphere _Y_. If the auxiliary data of gauge splittings and gauge twistings are compatible in a suitable sense (see Proposition [3.30](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar55) and Proposition [5.5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar86)), then the moduli space ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw73/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq65_HTML.gif) can be recovered from the fiber product of ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw84/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq66_HTML.gif) and ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw84/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq67_HTML.gif) over the configuration space of _Y_, in a way compatible with the twisted restriction maps:

### Gluing Theorem

Assume s\_{{\\mathbb {Z}}} and  (s\_{1,{\\mathbb {Z}}}, s\_{2,{\\mathbb {Z}}}) are compatible. Then there is an S^1-equivariant diffeomorphism

![](https://media.springernature.com/lw341/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_Equ95_HTML.png)

such that R\_{\\tau } is S^1-equivariantly homotopic to  \\left( R\_{\\tau \_1} \\times \_{Y} R\_{\\tau \_2} \\right) \\circ F.

The proof uses the following fact of independent interest. We show that the restriction map of solutions in ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw73/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq74_HTML.gif) to a submanifold in the interior of _X_ is smooth as a map into a configuration space of _higher_ regularity (Theorem [5.2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar80)). ### 1.2 Applications

The Semi-infinite-dimensionality Theorem together with compactness of moduli spaces proved in \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR14 "Kronheimer, P.B., Mrowka, T.S.: Monopoles and Three-Manifolds. New Mathematical Monographs, vol. 10. Cambridge University Press, Cambridge (2007)")\] (with minor modifications to account for the double Coulomb slice instead of the Coulomb-Neumann slice used in \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR14 "Kronheimer, P.B., Mrowka, T.S.: Monopoles and Three-Manifolds. New Mathematical Monographs, vol. 10. Cambridge University Press, Cambridge (2007)")\]) show that the maps ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw200/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq75_HTML.gif) are _semi-infinite-dimensional cycles_ in ![](https://media.springernature.com/lw78/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_IEq76_HTML.gif) as defined in \[ [21](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR21 "Lipyanskiy, M.: A semi-infinite cycle construction of Floer homology (2008). Thesis (Ph.D.)—Massachusetts Institute of Technology"), [31](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR31 "Suwara, P.: Semi-infinite Homology of Floer spaces (2020). Thesis (Ph.D.)—Massachusetts Institute of Technology")\], establishing the existence of relative Seiberg–Witten invariants of _X_. If the boundary \\partial X is connected, these do not depend on the choice of an integral splitting (Lemma [3.31](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar57)). This also implies that cobordisms \\partial W = - Y\_1 \\cup Y\_2 induce _correspondences_ (defined in \[ [21](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR21 "Lipyanskiy, M.: A semi-infinite cycle construction of Floer homology (2008). Thesis (Ph.D.)—Massachusetts Institute of Technology"), [31](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR31 "Suwara, P.: Semi-infinite Homology of Floer spaces (2020). Thesis (Ph.D.)—Massachusetts Institute of Technology")\]) between the configuration spaces over Y\_1 and Y\_2.

Our methods apply to perturbed equations as well, which we did not include for the sake of simplicity. Varying the metrics and perturbations gives cobordisms between the relevant moduli spaces on _X_, and changing the reference connections on _X_ gives isomorphic moduli spaces with homotopic restriction maps. This means that the relative invariant of _X_, up to a suitable cobordism relation, does not depend on the choices of perturbations and metric.

Crucially, the Gluing Theorem says that the correspondence induced by a composite cobordism is (homotopic to) the composition of the respective correspondences, proving the theory comes with a TQFT-like structure. We hope to prove that this theory recovers a non-equivariant version of monopole Floer homology \\widetilde{HM}\_\* and describe a construction supposed to recover all of the flavors of monopole Floer homology (for rational homology spheres) in an upcoming work.

Applications of Seiberg–Witten–Floer theory suggest including the case b\_1(\\partial X) > 0 or allowing _X_ to have cylindrical or conical ends (like in \[ [15](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR15 "Kronheimer, P.B., Mrowka, T.S.: Monopoles and contact structures. Invent. Math. 130(2), 209–255 (1997)")\]) with certain asymptotic conditions on the configurations. The methods presented here should suffice to prove regularity and semi-infinite-dimensionality of the corresponding moduli spaces. However, to establish the semi-infinite-dimensional theory in full one needs to carefully select a slice for the gauge action and deal with compactness issues which we hope to address in further work.

Finally, the methods presented here should be applicable (with appropriate adjustments) for defining semi-infinite-dimensional Floer theories in other contexts, e.g., in the Yang-Mills-Floer theory.

### Remark 1.1

The main results of the paper assume that b\_1(\\partial X) = 0 because the theory of semi-infinite-dimensional cycles \[ [21](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR21 "Lipyanskiy, M.: A semi-infinite cycle construction of Floer homology (2008). ### 1.3 Organization

In Sect. [2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#Sec6) we prove the regularity theorem for a Dirac operator with compact perturbation, the Regularity Theorem, and a strong unique continuation principle for a Dirac operator with a low-regularity potential, the Strong UCP Theorem.

In Sect. [3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#Sec9) we introduce the basic notions of Seiberg–Witten theory on 3- and 4-manifolds. For a chosen gauge splitting the split Coulomb slice is defined, and for a gauge twisting a twisted restriction map to the boundary is introduced. The choices of splittings and twistings are shown to be equivalent to a choice of an integral splitting, one which does not require any twisting.

Section [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#Sec14) provides proofs of some of the key properties of the moduli spaces: regularity, semi-infinite-dimensionality and denseness of the restriction map (Semi-infinite-dimensionality Theorem). Finally, Sect. [5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#Sec17) contains the proof of the Gluing Theorem, showing that moduli on a composite cobordism are a fiber product of the moduli on its components.

### 1.4 Notations

In this article we use the notation L^p\_k for Sobolev spaces of regularity _k_, in accordance with the literature in gauge theory and Floer theory in low dimensional topology. These are often denoted by W^{k,p} or H^{k,p} or, when p=2, simply by H^k.

All manifolds are assumed to be smooth, submanifolds to be smoothly embedded, and manifolds with boundary to have smooth boundary.

## 2 Analytical preparation

We prove two results which are fundamental for the surjectivity proofs in Sect. [4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#Sec14).

The first one, the Regularity Theorem, is a regularity theorem for operators of the form D = D\_0 + K: L^2\_1 \\rightarrow L^2, where D\_0 is a first-order elliptic operator and _K_ is a compact operator. In our applications _K_ is a multiplication by an L^2\_1-configuration on a 4-manifold and thus it does not factor as a map L^2\_1 \\rightarrow L^2\_1. If it did, elliptic regularity would immediately imply the regularity result. The novelty here is the general form of the perturbation _K_ which is only assumed to be compact as a map L^2\_1 \\rightarrow L^2.

The second result, the Strong UCP Theorem, is a strong unique continuation principle in a similar low regularity setting. This can be understood as a strengthening of the unique continuation results of \[ [14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR14 "Kronheimer, P.B., Mrowka, T.S.: Monopoles and Three-Manifolds. New Mathematical Monographs, vol. 10. Cambridge University Press, Cambridge (2007)"), Section 7\].

### 2.1 A regularity theorem for Dirac operators

Let _M_ be a smooth Riemannian manifold of bounded geometry (without boundary), let E, F \\rightarrow M be two complex vector bundles over _M_ of bounded geometry and let D\_0 be a uniformly elliptic operator D\_0: C^\\infty (M;E) \\rightarrow C^\\infty (M;F) of order k \\ge 1. For definitions of manifolds and bundles of bounded geometry and uniformly elliptic differential operators, see \[ [30](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR30 "Shubin, M.A.: Spectral theory of elliptic operators on noncompact manifolds. Astérisque 2075, 35–108 (1992). Méthodes semi-classiques, vol. 1 (Nantes, 1991)"), Appendix 1\].

### Remark 2.1

We state the Regularity Theorem in a general form for reference in future works, but in this article we will only apply it to the setting where the Riemannian manifold _M_ has cylindrical ends (every such manifold is of bounded geometry, and every vector bundle over it is of bounded geometry) and the differential operator D\_0 is cylindrical on these ends (every such operator is uniformly elliptic).

Let K: L^2\_k(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2(M;F) be a compact operator and denote by K^\* its formal adjoint. We require that K^\* is well-defined and continuous as a map K^\*: L^2\_k(M;F) \\rightarrow L^2(M;E) , and that _K_ extends to a continuous operator K: L^2(M; E) \\rightarrow L^2\_{-l}(M;F) for some l \\ge 0. Consider the operator

\\begin{aligned} D = D\_0 + K: L^2\_k(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2(M;F). \\end{aligned}

(Note that it extends to D: L^2(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2\_{\\min (-k,-l)}(M;F).) The aim of this subsection is to prove the following regularity theorem:

### Regularity Theorem

Assume that v \\in L^2(M;E)  satisfies D v = h  for some h \\in L^2(M;F) . Then  v \\in L^2\_k(M;E) .

Moreover, if v, h \\in L^2\_{loc}(M;E) instead, then v \\in L^2\_{k,loc}(M;E).

In the course of the proof we will make use of the following simple lemma (cf. Lipyanskiy \[ [21](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR21 "Lipyanskiy, M.: A semi-infinite cycle construction of Floer homology (2008). Thesis (Ph.D.)—Massachusetts Institute of Technology"), Lemma 44\]):

### Lemma 2.2

Suppose the sequence of Hilbert space operators  \\{A\_i: V \\rightarrow W\\}  is uniformly bounded and weakly convergent to _A_ in the sense that for any v \\in V we have  A\_i(v) \\rightarrow A(v) .

If  K: W \\rightarrow U  is compact, then  A\_i \\circ K \\rightarrow A \\circ K .

We also need the following version of the Gårding inequality, proven in \[ [30](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR30 "Shubin, M.A.: Spectral theory of elliptic operators on noncompact manifolds. Astérisque 2075, 35–108 (1992). Méthodes semi-classiques, vol. 1 (Nantes, 1991)"), Appendix 1, Lemma 1.4\]:

### Proposition 2.3

(Gårding inequality) For every s,t \\in {\\mathbb {R}} there exists C>0 such that for any u \\in C\_0^\\infty (M;E)

\\begin{aligned} \\Vert u\\Vert \_{L^2\_{s+k}} \\le C( \\Vert D\_0 u \\Vert \_{L^2\_s} + \\Vert u\\Vert \_{L^2\_t}). \\end{aligned}

With these in hand, we are ready to prove the Regularity Theorem.

### Proof of Regularity Theorem

By considering

\\begin{aligned} \\begin{pmatrix} 0 &{} D\_0^\*\\\ D\_0 &{} 0 \\end{pmatrix} + \\begin{pmatrix} 0 &{} K^\*\\\ K &{} 0 \\end{pmatrix}:L^2\_k(M;E \\oplus F) \\rightarrow L^2(M; E \\oplus F), \\end{aligned}

where D\_0^\* is the formal adjoint of D\_0, we may assume, without loss of generality, that E = F and D\_0 is formally self-adjoint. Since _M_ has bounded geometry and D\_0 is uniformly elliptic, Proposition 4.1 in \[ [30](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR30 "Shubin, M.A.: Spectral theory of elliptic operators on noncompact manifolds. Astérisque 2075, 35–108 (1992). Méthodes semi-classiques, vol. 1 (Nantes, 1991)"), Section 1\] implies that the minimal and maximal operators D\_0^{min} and D\_0^{max} of D\_0: L^2(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2(M;E) coincide, and their domains are equal to L^2\_k(M;E). Thus D\_0 is essentially self-adjoint.

Consider the equation

\\begin{aligned} (D\_0+ic) \\psi + K \\psi = h + i c v \\end{aligned}

(1)


for large c \\in {\\mathbb {R}}. Certainly, \\psi = v \\in L^2(M;E) solves the equation. We will prove that for large _c_ equation ( [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#Equ1)) has a unique solution \\psi  in L^2\_k(M;E), which is also unique in L^2(M;E), so that v = \\psi \\in L^2\_k(M;E), as wished.

Firstly, we want to prove  D\_0+ic: L^2\_k(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2(M;E)  is invertible. Since the spectrum of D\_0 is real,  (D\_0+ic)^{-1}:L^2(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2(M;E)  exists and is bounded by  \\frac{1}{\|c\|}  (cf. \[ [17](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR17 "Lang, S.: Real and Functional Analysis. Graduate Texts in Mathematics, vol. 142, 3rd edn. Springer-Verlag, New York (1993)"), Chapter XIX, Theorem 2.4\]). Therefore by Proposition [2.3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar7) we have the first inequality:

\\begin{aligned} \\Vert (D\_0+ic)^{-1} u \\Vert \_{L^2\_k}&\\le \\Vert D\_0 (D\_0 + ic)^{-1} u\\Vert \_{L^2} + \\Vert (D\_0+ic)^{-1} u \\Vert \_{L^2} \\\ {}&\\le \\Vert u - ic (D\_0+ic)^{-1} u \\Vert \_{L^2} + (1/\|c\|) \\Vert u\\Vert \_{L^2} \\\ {}&\\le (2 + 1/\|c\|) \\Vert u\\Vert \_{L^2} \\end{aligned}

and therefore  (D\_0+ic)^{-1}  is a bounded operator L^2(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2\_k(M;E).

Secondly, we want to prove weak convergence of  (D\_0+ic)^{-1}  to 0 as \|c\| \\rightarrow \\infty , i.e., that for each v \\in L^2(M;E) we have  (D\_0+ic)^{-1}(v) \\rightarrow 0 in L^2\_k(M;E) as \|c\| \\rightarrow \\infty . The spectral theorem for unbounded self-adjoint operators \[ [17](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR17 "Lang, S.: Real and Functional Analysis. Graduate Texts in Mathematics, vol. 142, 3rd edn. Springer-Verlag, New York (1993)"), Chapter XIX, Theorem 2.7\] provides us with an orthogonal decomposition  L^2(M;E) = {\\hat{\\bigoplus }}\_n H\_n such that the restriction D\_n = D\_0\|\_{H\_n}:H\_n \\rightarrow H\_n is a bounded operator (in L^2-norm on H\_n) and D\_0 = {\\hat{\\bigoplus }}\_n D\_n. In particular, H\_n \\subset \\textrm{Dom}(D\_0) = L^2\_k(M;E). For each v\_n \\in H\_n we thus have, by Proposition [2.3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar7),

\\begin{aligned} \\Vert v\_n\\Vert \_{L^2\_k} \\le C( \\Vert D\_0 v\_n\\Vert \_{L^2} + \\Vert v\_n\\Vert \_{L^2}) \\le C (C\_n + 1) \\Vert v\_n\\Vert \_{L^2} = C\_n' \\Vert v\_n\\Vert \_{L^2}, \\end{aligned}

where C\_n = \\Vert D\_n\\Vert \_{L^2}. Therefore

\\begin{aligned} \\Vert (D\_0+ic)^{-1} v\_n \\Vert \_{L^2\_k} \\le C\_n' \\Vert (D\_0 + ic)^{-1} v\_n \\Vert \_{L^2} \\le \\frac{C\_n'}{\|c\|} \\Vert v\_n\\Vert \_{L^2} \\xrightarrow {\|c\| \\rightarrow \\infty } 0 \\end{aligned}

which proves that for any v \\in L^2(M;E),  (D\_0+ic)^{-1} v \\xrightarrow {\|c\|\\rightarrow \\infty } 0  in L^2\_k(M;E), i.e.,  (D\_0 + ic)^{-1} converges weakly to 0, as wished.

We proceed to proving existence and uniqueness of solutions to ( [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#Equ1)) in L^2\_k(M;E) for large \| _c_ \|. Using Lemma [2.2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#FPar6) we conclude that  T = (D\_0+ic)^{-1} K: L^2\_k(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2\_k(M;E)  converges strongly to 0. Thus, for large \| _c_ \|, the operator  \\textrm{Id} + (D\_0+ic)^{-1} K: L^2\_k(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2\_k(M;E) is invertible. Composing with (D\_0 + ic) we conclude that  D\_0+ic + K = D+ic: L^2\_k(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2(M;E)  is invertible for large \| _c_ \|. Existence and uniqueness of  \\psi \\in L^2\_k(M;E)  solving ( [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#Equ1)) follows.

To conclude the first part of the proof we need to establish uniqueness of solutions to ( [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#Equ1)) in L^2(M;E). We have that  (D\_0 + ic + K) (\\psi - v) = 0. This implies that \\psi - v is perpendicular in L^2(M;E) to the image of  D\_0^\*- ic + K^\*= D\_0 - ic + K:L^2\_k(M;E) \\rightarrow L^2(M;E). However, we already established that for large \| _c_ \| the latter operator is invertible and it follows that \\psi - v = 0; thus  v = \\psi \\in L^2\_k(M;E) .

Finally, it remains to consider the more general case v, h \\in L^2\_{loc}. For any compact A \\subset M we can take a compactly supported bump function \\rho :M \\rightarrow \[0,1\] with \\rho \|\_A=1 and define v' = \\rho v \\in L^2(M; E). Then D v' = h'  for some h' \\in L^2(M;E) and therefore v' \\in L^2\_k(M;E) as proven above, so v\|\_A \\in L^2\_k(A;E\|\_A). Repeating the argument for every compact A \\subset M shows that v \\in L^2\_{k,loc}(M;E) .

## 3 Seiberg–Witten moduli spaces in split Coulomb slice

In this section we introduce the moduli spaces of the Seiberg–Witten equations on a Riemannian 4-manifold with boundary a collection of rational homology spheres, together with the restriction maps to the boundary. For 3-manifolds, we introduce the _Coulomb slice_ and its _polarization_, a decomposition of the tangent space into a sum of two infinite-dimensional subspaces. For 4-manifolds, we introduce the _double Coulomb slice_ and what we call the _split Coulomb slice_ together with the _split gauge group_. Since the restriction maps are generally not invariant with respect to the split gauge group, we need to introduce appropriate _twisted restriction maps_ as well.

The split gauge fixing is a key novel element that generalizes the gauge slice introduced by Khandhawit \[ [12](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR12 "Khandhawit, T.: A new gauge slice for the relative Bauer-Furuta invariants. Geom. Topol. 19(3), 1631–1655 (2015)")\] and Lipyanskiy \[ [21](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-023-00845-y#ref-CR21 "Lipyanskiy, M.: A semi-infinite cycle construction of Floer homology (2008). Thesis (Ph.D.)—Massachusetts Institute of Technology")\]. It simplifies the proof of the Gluing Theorem letting us to reduce it to the case of untwisted restriction maps.

### 3.1 Coulomb slice on 3-manifolds

We begin by introducing _polarizations_ on the Seiberg–Witten configuration space in Coulomb gauge on an oriented rational homology sphere _Y_ and collections of such.

Let _g_ be a Riemannian metric and {\\mathfrak {s}} be a spin^{c} structure on _Y_. Denote by S\_Y the associated spinor bundle and choose a smooth spin^{c} connection B\_0. The _Seiberg–Witten configuration space_ on _Y_ is the space

![](https://media.springernature.com/lw302/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_Equ98_HTML.png)

consisting of pairs (B,\\Psi ) of a spin^{c} _connection_ and a _spinor_ on _Y_.

In Seiberg–Witten theory one investigates the Chern–Simons–Dirac functional {\\mathcal {L}}

![](https://media.springernature.com/lw422/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00029-023-00845-y/MediaObjects/29_2023_845_Equ99_HTML.png)
