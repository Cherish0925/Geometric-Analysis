# Quantified monopole inequalities and a bounded Weyl model

19 September 2026. This note derives the monopole input to
`round3_rigidity.md` independently of the live Skill and research Session.
The foundational input is an actual Seiberg--Witten monopole class;
the scalar and two-form Weitzenbock identities are stated with their
normalizations below. No Einstein condition is assumed.

## 1. Conventions and rescaled solvability

Let (M,g) be a closed oriented smooth four-manifold, with a spin-c
structure of determinant line L and class \(a=c_1(L)\). Assume that the
ordinary, unperturbed Seiberg--Witten equations for this structure have
a solution for every metric conformal to g. An actual monopole class,
with solutions for every metric, supplies this hypothesis. In applications
where the class is obtained from a Seiberg--Witten invariant, all
nonvanishing and chamber hypotheses must be verified separately.

Write H for the real harmonic self-dual representative of \(a^+\),
normalized by

\[
 p=\|H\|_2^2=(a^+)^2>0,
 \qquad \operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal H^+}(iF_A^+)=2\pi H.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

Two-forms have their usual exterior-algebra norm. The Weyl operator
\(W^+:\Lambda^+\to\Lambda^+\) has its operator Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
For the quadratic spinor map use

\[
 |\sigma(\Phi)|^2=|\Phi|^4/8,\qquad
 \psi=2\sqrt2\sigma(\Phi),\qquad
 \rho=|\Phi|^2=|\psi|.
\]

For every smooth positive function f one has a solution on g of

\[
 D_A\Phi=0,\qquad -iF_A^+=f\sigma(\Phi).                    \tag{2}
\]

To verify this, solve the ordinary equations for
\(\widehat g=f^{-2}g\). Under the canonical identification of spinor
bundles, conformal covariance gives
\(\Phi=f^{-3/2}\widehat\Phi\) and
\(D_A^g\Phi=0\). As differential two-forms,
\(\sigma_g(\Phi)=f^{-1}\sigma_{\widehat g}(\widehat\Phi)\).
The self-dual splitting of two-forms is conformally invariant, proving
(2). This is the rescaling used in
[LeBrun, Lemma 3.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0611450).

The normalized spinor and two-form Weitzenbock formulas are

\[
 4\nabla_A^*\nabla_A\Phi+(s+f\rho)\Phi=0,                    \tag{3}
\]
\[
 \|d\psi\|_2^2+\|d^*\psi\|_2^2
 =\int\left(|\nabla\psi|^2+\frac{s}{3}\rho^2
                     -2\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle\right).  \tag{4}
\]

The quadratic map is parallel. In a unitary spin frame it is the Hopf
map \(z\mapsto(2\operatorname{Re}(z_1\bar z_2),
2\operatorname{Im}(z_1\bar z_2),|z_1|^2-|z_2|^2)\), up to an
orthogonal change in \(\Lambda^+\). Its differential satisfies

\[
 |d\psi_z(v)|^2
 =4|z|^2|v|^2-4\bigl(\operatorname{Im}\langle z,v\rangle\bigr)^2
 \le4|z|^2|v|^2.                                           \tag{5}
\]

Thus the quadratic-map defect used below is nonnegative pointwise.

## 2. Retaining all four differential defects

Put \(u_W=|W^+|\), \(\alpha=\sqrt{2/3}u_W\), and
\(q=-s+\sqrt6u_W=-s+3\alpha\). For this section assume q is positive.
Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_{\rm amp}&=2\int|d\rho|^2,\\
 D_{\rm quad}&=\int(4\rho|\nabla_A\Phi|^2-|\nabla\psi|^2),\\
 D_{\rm H}&=\int(|d\psi|^2+|d^*\psi|^2),\\
 D_{\rm eig}&=2\int\bigl(\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle
                                      +\alpha\rho^2\bigr),
 \qquad D_0=D_{\rm amp}+D_{\rm quad}+D_{\rm H}+D_{\rm eig}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{6}
\]

All terms are nonnegative. For the last, the smallest eigenvalue of a
trace-free symmetric three-by-three operator satisfies
\(\lambda_{\min}\ge-\sqrt{2/3}|W^+|=-\alpha\): if that eigenvalue
is w, the other two sum to -w and contribute at least \(w^2/2\) to
the squared norm, so \(|W^+|^2\ge3w^2/2\).

Pair (3) with \(\rho\Phi\) and integrate. Since
\(d\rho=2\operatorname{Re}\langle\nabla_A\Phi,\Phi\rangle\),

\[
 2\int|d\rho|^2+4\int\rho|\nabla_A\Phi|^2
                  +\int s\rho^2+\int f\rho^3=0.             \tag{7}
\]

Combine (7) with (4), then add the eigenvalue defect. This gives the
exact identity

\[
 D_0=\int\left(\frac23q\rho^2-f\rho^3\right).
\]

Write \(\varphi=3\psi/2\), \(u=|\varphi|=3\rho/2\). Then

\[
 P_f:=\int(qu^2-fu^3)=\frac{27}{8}D_0\ge0.                  \tag{8}
\]

## 3. Two Holder inequalities and the exact total deficit

For any smooth positive f define

\[
 X=\left(\int q^3f^{-2}\right)^{1/3},\quad
 Y=\left(\int f^4\right)^{1/3},\quad
 Z=\int fu^3,\quad C=\int f^2u^2,
\]
\[
 H_1=XZ^{2/3}-\int qu^2\ge0,\qquad
 H_2=YZ^{2/3}-C\ge0.
\]

The first is Holder applied to
\((q^3f^{-2})^{1/3}(fu^3)^{2/3}\); the second uses
\((f^4)^{1/3}(fu^3)^{2/3}\). Since p>0 the spinor cannot vanish
identically: otherwise \(F_A^+=0\), contradicting (1). Thus Z>0.

Let \(J_A=\|iF_A^+-2\pi H\|_2^2\). Hodge orthogonality, together
with \(f\varphi=-3\sqrt2\,iF_A^+\), gives

\[
 C=18\|F_A^+\|_2^2=72\pi^2p+18J_A.                       \tag{9}
\]

Because \(P_f+H_1=Z^{2/3}(X-Z^{1/3})\), straightforward factorization
now yields the exact decomposition

\[
 YX^2-72\pi^2p
 =18J_A+H_2+Y\frac{X+Z^{1/3}}{Z^{2/3}}(P_f+H_1).         \tag{10}
\]

The right side is a sum of nonnegative quantities.

For the optimal weight \(f=\sqrt q\), initially treating it as smooth,
put

\[
 \mathcal E=\int q^2,\qquad
 \Delta=\mathcal E-72\pi^2p,\qquad
 \theta=(Z/\mathcal E)^{1/3}.
\]

Now \(X=Y=\mathcal E^{1/3}\), \(H_1=H_2=:H\), and
\(P_f+H\ge0\) implies \(0<\theta\le1\). Equation (10) becomes

\[
 \Delta=18J_A+H+\frac{1+\theta}{\theta^2}(P_f+H).
\]

Since the last coefficient is at least two,

\[
 \boxed{\quad J_A\le\Delta/18,\quad P_f\le\Delta/2,
 \quad H\le\Delta/3,\quad D_0\le4\Delta/27.\quad}          \tag{11}
\]

In particular \(\Delta\ge0\). Self-duality implies
\(|d^*\psi|=|d\psi|\), so

\[
 \|d\rho\|_2^2\le2\Delta/27,
 \qquad\|d\psi\|_2^2\le2\Delta/27.                        \tag{12}
\]

## 4. Amplitude and harmonic projection

Use the probability measure \(d\nu=f^4d\mu/\mathcal E\) and
\(r=u/f\). Write \(c=\int r^2d\nu=C/\mathcal E\),
\(z=\int r^3d\nu=Z/\mathcal E\), and \(m=\int r\,d\nu\).
Equations (8) and Holder give \(z\le c\le z^{2/3}\), hence
\(0<c\le1\). Another Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\(c^2\le mz\le mc\), so \(m\ge c\). Therefore

\[
 \int f^2(u-f)^2
 =\mathcal E(c-2m+1)
 \le\mathcal E-C=\Delta-18J_A\le\Delta.                    \tag{13}
\]

The sign and normalization in (9) also give

\[
 \|f\varphi+6\sqrt2\pi H\|_2^2=18J_A\le\Delta.           \tag{14}
\]

Combining (13) and (14) with the reverse triangle inequality for norms,

\[
 \left\||2\pi H|-\frac{q}{3\sqrt2}\right\|_2
 \le2\sqrt{\Delta/18},\qquad
 \left\||H|-\frac{q}{6\sqrt2\pi}\right\|_2^2
 \le\frac{\Delta}{18\pi^2}.                              \tag{15}
\]

### Smooth approximation of the optimal weight

The function \(|W^+|\), and hence \(\sqrt q\), need not be smooth.
In the application \(q\ge3\), \(-s\le5\). Choose smooth positive
\(f_j\ge\sqrt3\) converging uniformly to \(\sqrt q\) and solve (2).
At a maximum of \(\rho_j\), (3) gives
\(f_j\rho_j\le-s\). Consequently \(\rho_j\le5/\sqrt3<3\),
uniformly in j. This bound is independent of derivatives of f_j.

All identities (6)--(10) hold exactly for these solutions.
Their left side in (10) tends to \(\Delta\). Moreover, if
\(\vartheta_j=Z_j^{1/3}/X_j\), then \(0<\vartheta_j\le1\) and

\[
 Y_j\frac{X_j+Z_j^{1/3}}{Z_j^{2/3}}
 =\frac{Y_j}{X_j}\frac{1+\vartheta_j}{\vartheta_j^2}
 \ge2\frac{Y_j}{X_j}\longrightarrow2.
\]

This proves (11)--(12) with errors tending to zero without requiring
spinor convergence; the two Holder remainders differ by o(1), because
\(X_j-Y_j\to0\), \(f_j^2-q\to0\) uniformly, and \(u_j\) is bounded.
For (13), evaluate the moments instead with the
fixed optimal weight \(\sqrt q\) and the variable bounded \(u_j\).
Uniform convergence and (8) give \(z_j\le c_j+o(1)\);
Holder still gives \(c_j\le z_j^{2/3}\). Thus \(c_j\le1+o(1)\).
Since \(c_j\ge72\pi^2p/\mathcal E-o(1)>0\),
\(m_j\ge c_j^2/z_j\ge c_j-o(1)\). The same calculation yields
(13) with an o(1) error. Replacing \(\sqrt q\) by f_j changes the
integrals by o(1), by the amplitude bound on this fixed compact metric.

The projected-curvature identity (14) is exact for f_j before applying
the deficit bound. Thus (13)--(15) hold with arbitrarily small errors.
In statements below one can make the sum of all such errors at most
any prescribed \(\eta>0\). This is done separately on each manifold;
it does not require a common approximation rate along varying geometries.

## 5. Eigenline alignment on any controlled spectral region

Let \(\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\lambda_3\) be the eigenvalues of W+.
On a measurable region U where \(\lambda_2-\lambda_1\ge\gamma>0\),
let \(\Pi\) project onto the lowest eigenline. Since
\(\lambda_1+\alpha\ge0\),

\[
 \langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle+\alpha\rho^2
 =\sum_j(\lambda_j+\alpha)|\psi_j|^2
 \ge\gamma|(I-\Pi)\psi|^2.
\]

Equations (6) and (11) imply

\[
 \int_U|(I-\Pi)\psi|^2\le\frac{2\Delta}{27\gamma}+\eta,
 \qquad
 \int_U|(I-\Pi)\varphi|^2\le\frac{\Delta}{6\gamma}+\eta.   \tag{16}
\]

If also \(q\le F_*^2\) on U, (14) and a two-term squared triangle
inequality imply, after making the approximation arbitrarily accurate,

\[
 \int_U|(I-\Pi)H|^2
 \le\left(\frac1{36\pi^2}
                 +\frac{F_*^2}{216\gamma\pi^2}\right)\Delta. \tag{17}
\]

No derivative or global orientation of the eigenline is asserted.

## 6. The lowest-eigenvalue curvature integral

This subsection does not require q>0 or scalar negativity. Set

\[
 w=\lambda_{\min}(W^+),\qquad b=2s/3+2w,
 \qquad h=(-b)_+=\max(-b,0).
\]

Omit \(D_{\rm eig}\) from (6). Equations (4) and (7) then give

\[
 0\le D_{\rm amp}+D_{\rm quad}+D_{\rm H}
 =\int\left(-\frac23s\rho^2
                 -2\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle-f\rho^3\right).
\]

Since \(\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle\ge w\rho^2\),

\[
 \int f\rho^3\le\int(-b)\rho^2\le\int h\rho^2.           \tag{18}
\]

Choose any smooth positive \(q_\epsilon\ge h\) converging uniformly
to h, and solve the rescaled equations with
\(f_\epsilon=\sqrt{q_\epsilon}\). Put
\(C_\epsilon=\int f_\epsilon^2\rho^2\),
\(Z_\epsilon=\int f_\epsilon\rho^3\), and
\(Q_\epsilon=\int f_\epsilon^4\). Equations (1)--(2) give

\[
 32\pi^2p\le8\|F_A^+\|_2^2=C_\epsilon,
 \qquad Z_\epsilon\le C_\epsilon.
\]

Holder yields
\(C_\epsilon\le Q_\epsilon^{1/3}Z_\epsilon^{2/3}
\le Q_\epsilon^{1/3}C_\epsilon^{2/3}\). Since p>0,
division proves \(C_\epsilon\le Q_\epsilon\). Letting epsilon tend
to zero proves, with \(x_-=\min\{x,0\}\),

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \int(2s/3+2w)_-^2\ge32\pi^2p.\quad}                      \tag{19}
\]

This argument is valid even when h vanishes; no uniform spinor bound
is needed for this particular limiting step. If s<0, then w<=0 and
b<0, so (19) is exactly
\(\int(2s/3+2w)^2\ge32\pi^2p\). It is the lowest-eigenvalue
estimate appearing in the proof of
[LeBrun's Theorem 2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0003068), here
derived directly from the rescaled equations.

For comparison, pairing the ordinary f=1 equation (3) with Phi gives
\(\int\rho^2\le\int(-s)_+\rho\). Cauchy--Schwarz and
\(\int\rho^2=8\|F_A^+\|_2^2\ge32\pi^2p\) give
\(\int s_-^2\ge32\pi^2p\). If scalar curvature is everywhere
nonnegative, (3) forces \(\Phi=0\) and \(F_A^+=0\), contradicting
p>0. The sign obstruction and the spectral estimate are distinct claims.

## 7. A global bounded smooth approximation to W+

Assume henceforth \(\delta=\|g^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}+I\|_{\infty,op}
\le1/64\). Put

\[
 E=\operatorname{Ric}+g,\quad e=s+4,\quad t=s+8\sqrt6u_W,
 \quad G=(2s/3-2\alpha)^2-(2s/3+2w)^2,
\]
\[
 \mathcal D=|E|^2+e^2+t^2+G,\qquad d_{\rm cal}=\int\mathcal D.
\]

One has \(-5\le s\le-3\), \(q\ge3\), and \(G\ge0\).
Set \(z=w+\alpha\). Trace-freeness and the norm inequality imply
\(0\le z\le\alpha\), and direct expansion gives

\[
 G=2z(-4s/3+4\alpha-2z)\ge8z.                              \tag{20}
\]

Let \(U=\{\mathcal D\le1/64\}\) and \(B=M\setminus U\).
Then \(\operatorname{Vol}(B)\le64d_{\rm cal}\). On U,

\[
 \alpha=(t-s)/24,\qquad
 61/384\le\alpha\le67/384,\qquad z\le1/512.
\]

The remaining two eigenvalues are
\(-w/2\pm\sqrt{2u_W^2-3w^2}/2\), so

\[
 \lambda_2-\lambda_1
 =\frac32(\alpha-z)-\frac12\sqrt{6\alpha z-3z^2}
 >1/8.                                                     \tag{21}
\]

The last inequality follows by inserting the preceding rational bounds;
also \(q\le587/128<5\) on U. Choose a measurable lowest-eigenline
projection Pi everywhere, agreeing with the simple eigenline on U, and
put \(y=(I-\Pi)\psi\). The maximum principle gives \(\rho\le3\).
Equation (16) on U and this bound on B yield

\[
 \int|y|^2\le16\Delta/27+576d_{\rm cal}+\eta.              \tag{22}
\]

The amplitude has a global constant target. The exact identity
\(q-9/2=(t-9e)/8\) implies
\(\|q-9/2\|_2^2\le41d_{\rm cal}/32\). Equation (13), q>=3,
and \(|\sqrt q-\sqrt{9/2}|^2\le(q-9/2)^2/12\) give

\[
 \|\rho-\sqrt2\|_2^2
 \le8\Delta/27+41d_{\rm cal}/432+\eta.                    \tag{23}
\]

For the measurable operator \(A_0(\Pi)=I/12-\Pi/4\), one has

\[
\begin{split}
 |W^+-A_0(\Pi)|^2
 &=u_W^2+1/24+w/2\\
 &=\frac32(\alpha-1/6)^2+z/2
 \le(t^2+e^2)/192+G/16\le\mathcal D/16.                  \tag{24}
\end{split}
\]

Now form the smooth tensor, without dividing by rho,

\[
 A_\psi=\rho^2I/24-\psi\otimes\psi/8.
\]

Its eigenvalues are \(-\rho^2/12,\rho^2/24,\rho^2/24\), and
\(|A_\psi|^2=\rho^4/96\). The rank-one identity

\[
 |\psi\otimes\psi-\rho^2\Pi|^2=2\rho^2|y|^2
\]

and a squared triangle inequality give

\[
 |A_\psi-A_0(\Pi)|^2
 \le\rho^2|y|^2/16+(\rho^2-2)^2/48.
\]

Use \(\rho\le3\), \((3+\sqrt2)^2<20\), and (22)--(24):

\[
\begin{split}
 \|W^+-A_\psi\|_2^2
 &\le d_{\rm cal}/8+(9/8)\int|y|^2
                         +(20/24)\|\rho-\sqrt2\|_2^2\\
 &\le\frac{74}{81}\Delta
       +\left(\frac18+648+\frac{205}{2592}\right)d_{\rm cal}
       +\eta\\
 &\le\boxed{\Delta+649d_{\rm cal}+\eta}.                 \tag{25}
\end{split}
\]

Also \(\|A_\psi\|_\infty\le9/(4\sqrt6)\). Equations (12),
(23), and (25) are precisely the quantitative inputs used in the
intrinsic Hessian proof. They hold simultaneously for a sufficiently
accurate smooth approximation on each metric. Neither a global
eigenvalue gap nor a spinor nonvanishing assumption has been introduced.

## 8. Weighted Hodge and the divergence-energy normalization

Here are also the local identities needed for the positive lower bound
on divergence energy in `round3_rigidity.md`. They involve only smooth
self-dual forms and the metric connection. Define

\[
 B_\psi=(s/3)\rho^2-2\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle,
 \qquad C_\psi=\langle\iota_{\nabla\rho}\psi,*d\psi\rangle.
\]

Testing the two-form Hodge equation against \(\rho^2\psi\), with
the nonnegative Hodge Laplacian, gives

\[
 \int\rho^2\bigl(|\nabla\psi|^2+B_\psi-2|d\psi|^2\bigr)
 =4\int\rho C_\psi-2\int\rho^2|d\rho|^2.                 \tag{26}
\]

Indeed, self-duality combines the two Hodge energies to
\(2\langle d\psi,d(\rho^2\psi)\rangle\). Their cross term is
\(4\rho\langle*d\psi,\iota_{\nabla\rho}\psi\rangle\).
On the rough-Laplacian side, differentiating \(\rho^2\psi\)
adds \(2\rho^2|d\rho|^2\) to the main weighted gradient energy.
These two integrations by parts prove (26), including the signs.

For an orthonormal basis \(\epsilon_a\) of \(\Lambda^+\), put
\(\omega_a=\sqrt2\epsilon_a\). The four-tensor corresponding to
a self-adjoint trace-free operator A is

\[
 W(A)_{ijkl}=\tfrac12\sum_{ab}A_{ab}(\omega_a)_{ij}(\omega_b)_{kl}.
\]

Let \(J_\psi=\nabla^iW(A_\psi)_{ijkl}\), with its ordinary
three-index norm. At a point where rho>0 choose a parallel frame at
that point with \(\psi=\rho\epsilon_1\) and write
\(\nabla_m\psi=c_m\epsilon_1+a_m\epsilon_2+b_m\epsilon_3\).
Then \(c_m=\partial_m\rho\), and direct differentiation gives

\[
 \nabla_m A_\psi=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -\rho c_m/6&-\rho a_m/8&-\rho b_m/8\\
 -\rho a_m/8&\rho c_m/12&0\\
 -\rho b_m/8&0&\rho c_m/12
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Taking its Hilbert--Schmidt norm gives

\[
 |\nabla A_\psi|^2
 =\rho^2|\nabla\psi|^2/32+\rho^2|d\rho|^2/96.             \tag{27}
\]

Insert the same matrix into the displayed four-tensor formula and
contract its first index. Using the explicit oriented frame
\(\omega_1=dx^{01}+dx^{23}\),
\(\omega_2=dx^{02}+dx^{31}\),
\(\omega_3=dx^{03}+dx^{12}\) gives

\[
 |\nabla A_\psi|^2-|J_\psi|^2
 =\frac{\rho^2}{64}(|\nabla\psi|^2-2|d\psi|^2)
   +\frac{\rho^2}{32}|d\rho|^2-\frac\rho{16}C_\psi.       \tag{28}
\]

This is a pointwise quadratic identity in the twelve real entries
\((c_m,a_m,b_m)\). The complete contraction is also reproduced in
the preserved local checker `round2_rigidity_check.py`. All terms
extend across zeros; here \(\rho=|\Phi|^2\) is itself smooth.

Eliminating the weighted gradient from (26)--(28) yields the exact
integrated formula

\[
 \int|J_\psi|^2
 =-\frac1{64}\int\rho^2B_\psi
  +\frac1{16}\int\rho^2|d\psi|^2
  -\frac5{96}\int\rho^2|d\rho|^2
  +\frac18\int\rho C_\psi.                               \tag{29}
\]

Since \(A_\psi\psi=-\rho^2\psi/12\), writing
\(W^+=A_\psi+R\) makes its first integrand

\[
 -\rho^2B_\psi/64
 =-s\rho^4/192-\rho^6/384
                       +\rho^2\langle R\psi,\psi\rangle/32.
\]

At \(s=-4\), \(\rho=\sqrt2\), and R=0 this equals 1/16.
The bounded amplitude, (12), (23), and (25) therefore give, with
\(V=\operatorname{Vol}M\) and
\(\varepsilon=\delta^2+(\Delta+d_{\rm cal})/V\),

\[
 \fint|J_\psi|^2
 \ge\frac1{16}-C\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon+\eta/V}
                                    +\varepsilon+\eta/V\right).
                                                               \tag{30}
\]

For the cross term use
\(|C_\psi|\le\rho|d\rho||d\psi|\) and Cauchy--Schwarz.
Polynomial amplitude errors are bounded by
\(C\fint|\rho-\sqrt2|\); the R term is bounded by
\(C\fint|R|\). These estimates require no control of
\(\nabla\psi\) on a varying bad set.

# A scalar-band remainder for LeBrun's Ricci-energy inequality

23 September 2026. This note concerns the equality geometry of LeBrun's
Proposition 4.1: constant-scalar-curvature Kähler metrics, not necessarily
Einstein metrics. It derives a global smooth self-dual form, a polynomial
Weyl approximation, and differential remainder estimates from that
proposition's defect. No Einstein or trace-free-Ricci bound is assumed.

The weighted-monopole identities underlying the argument were already
derived in [the Round 3 monopole proof](round3_monopole_input.md),
Sections 1–4. Their use below is
different from the Einstein-defect argument: the only small energy is the
LeBrun defect, and the target includes non-Einstein cscK metrics. The added
geometric hypothesis is a two-sided negative scalar-curvature band.

## 1. Statement and conventions

Let \((M,g)\) be a connected closed oriented smooth four-manifold. Fix a
Spin\(^c\) structure with real first Chern class \(a\), and assume that its
ordinary unperturbed Seiberg–Witten equations are solvable for every metric
conformal to \(g\). The class may be selected separately for each metric;
it is held fixed while the conformal weight varies. Set

\[
 p=(a_g^+)^2>0,
 \qquad -S\le s_g\le-s_0<0,
 \qquad S\ge s_0>0.
 \tag{1}
\]

This is a metricwise polarized hypothesis, not the assertion that one
fixed class is a monopole class for all metrics on \(M\).

Use the exterior-algebra norm on forms and the Hilbert–Schmidt operator
norm on \(W^+:\Lambda^+\to\Lambda^+\). All integrals use \(g\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 w&=\sqrt6\,|W^+|, &q&=-s+w, &t&=s+2w, &V&=\operatorname{Vol}_g(M),\\
 D&=\frac1{4\pi^2}\int_M
       \left(\frac{s^2}{24}+\frac{|W^+|^2}{2}\right)-\frac p2,
 &\delta&=144\pi^2D,
 &\Delta&=\int_Mq^2-72\pi^2p,\\
 K&=\frac S{\sqrt{s_0}},
 &C_0&=\left(1+\frac{3S}{2s_0}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Here \(q\ge s_0\). We will prove, not assume, that

\[
 \boxed{\delta=\Delta+\frac12\int_Mt^2,\qquad\Delta\ge0.}
 \tag{3}
\]

**Theorem.** For every \(\eta>0\) there is a smooth positive weight
\(f\), a smooth weighted Seiberg–Witten solution \((A,\Phi)\), and its
smooth self-dual two-form

\[
 D_A\Phi=0,\qquad -iF_A^+=f\sigma(\Phi),\qquad
 \psi=2\sqrt2\,\sigma(\Phi),\qquad
 \rho=|\Phi|^2=|\psi|\le K,
 \tag{4}
\]

such that all the following estimates hold simultaneously. Although
\(\psi\) may vanish, \(\rho=|\Phi|^2\) is smooth, so no nonsmooth norm
derivative is involved:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int|d\rho|^2&\le\frac{2\Delta}{27}+\eta,
 &\int|d\psi|^2&\le\frac{2\Delta}{27}+\eta,\\
 \int q\left(\rho-\frac23\sqrt q\right)^2
   &\le\frac{4\Delta}{9}+\eta,
 &\int E_\lambda&\le\frac{2\Delta}{27}+\eta,
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

where

\[
 E_\lambda=\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle+\frac w3\rho^2\ge0.
 \tag{6}
\]

In (5), \(d\psi\) is the exterior derivative, not the covariant
derivative. Define a smooth trace-free symmetric endomorphism of
\(\Lambda^+\) by

\[
 \mathcal B_\psi=\frac{\rho^2}{8}I-\frac38\psi\otimes\psi.
 \tag{7}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \int|W^+-\mathcal B_\psi|^2
 \le\frac1{27}\int t^2+
       \left(\frac{C_0}{27}+\frac1{18}\right)\Delta+\eta
 \le\frac{2C_0+7}{54}\,\delta+\eta.}
 \tag{8}
\]

The full covariant derivative satisfies the stronger retained identity
estimate

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int|\nabla\psi|^2
 &\le\frac{4\Delta}{27}-\frac13\int t\rho^2+\eta\\
 &\le\frac{4\Delta}{27}+
          \frac{K^2}{3}\sqrt{V\int t^2}+\eta\\
 &\le\frac{4\delta}{27}+\frac{K^2}{3}\sqrt{2V\delta}+\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Let \(H\) denote the harmonic self-dual representative of \(a_g^+\),
with the curvature convention
\(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal H^+}(iF_A^+)=2\pi H\). There is also
actual harmonic-projection control:

\[
 \left\|\sqrt q\,\psi+4\sqrt2\pi H\right\|_2^2
 \le\frac{4\Delta}{9}+\eta.
 \tag{10}
\]

If \(\lambda_1(g)>0\) is the first positive scalar Laplace eigenvalue,
then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int(s-\bar s)^2
 &\le\frac13\int t^2+
       \left(\frac{4C_0}{3}+\frac{2K^2}{\lambda_1(g)}\right)\Delta\\
 &\le\left(\frac23+\frac{4C_0}{3}
                   +\frac{2K^2}{\lambda_1(g)}\right)\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

The last estimate is about \(g\) alone and has no \(\eta\): apply the
preceding construction for arbitrarily small \(\eta\). Uniform scalar
oscillation control along degenerating metrics requires control of the
displayed spectral factor; none is implicit here.

If \(D=0\), the weight can be taken exactly as \(f=\sqrt q\), and all
slack terms vanish. The metric is negative cscK, with the complex
structure induced by its nonvanishing positive spinor. No conclusion
\(\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}=0\) follows.

## 2. Weighted solvability and a derivative-free amplitude bound

For any smooth positive \(f\), solve the ordinary equations for
\(\widehat g=f^{-2}g\). Write \(\beta:S_g^+\to S_{\widehat g}^+\)
for the fiberwise isometric conformal spinor identification. The spinor
on \(g\) is

\[
 \Phi=f^{-3/2}\beta^{-1}\widehat\Phi.
 \tag{12}
\]

Dirac conformal covariance gives \(D_A^g\Phi=0\). As differential
two-forms the unscaled quadratic map obeys
\(\sigma_{\widehat g}(\beta\zeta)=f^{-2}\sigma_g(\zeta)\).
Consequently
\(\sigma_g(\Phi)=f^{-1}\sigma_{\widehat g}(\widehat\Phi)\).
The self-dual splitting of two-forms is conformally invariant, giving
(4). This calculation keeps separate the bundle identification, the
spinor weight, and the two-form weight.

Normalize \(|\sigma(\Phi)|^2=|\Phi|^4/8\). The spinor Weitzenböck
equation is

\[
 4\nabla_A^*\nabla_A\Phi+(s+f\rho)\Phi=0.
 \tag{13}
\]

At a maximum of \(\rho\), this implies \(f\rho\le-s\).
Choose smooth \(f_j\ge\sqrt{s_0}\) converging uniformly to
\(\sqrt q\). Such approximants exist on the fixed compact manifold,
including when \(|W^+|\) is nonsmooth at its zeros. Then every solution
chosen for \(f_j\) has \(\rho_j\le S/\sqrt{s_0}=K\), independently
of all derivatives of \(f_j\).

## 3. Four nonnegative defects and the total remainder

For a smooth weight and its solution define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_{\rm amp}&=2\int|d\rho|^2,\\
 D_{\rm quad}&=\int(4\rho|\nabla_A\Phi|^2-|\nabla\psi|^2),\\
 D_H&=\int(|d\psi|^2+|d^*\psi|^2)=2\int|d\psi|^2,\\
 D_{\rm eig}&=2\int E_\lambda,
 \qquad D_0=D_{\rm amp}+D_{\rm quad}+D_H+D_{\rm eig}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

Each term is nonnegative. For \(D_{\rm quad}\), the quadratic map is
the parallel Hopf map, whose differential satisfies

\[
 |d\psi_z(v)|^2
 =4|z|^2|v|^2-4(\operatorname{Im}\langle z,v\rangle)^2
 \le4|z|^2|v|^2.
 \tag{15}
\]

For \(D_{\rm eig}\), the least eigenvalue of a trace-free symmetric
three-by-three matrix is at least
\(-\sqrt{2/3}|W^+|=-w/3\).

Pair (13) with \(\rho\Phi\) and integrate by parts:

\[
 2\int|d\rho|^2+4\int\rho|\nabla_A\Phi|^2
          +\int s\rho^2+\int f\rho^3=0.
 \tag{16}
\]

The self-dual two-form Weitzenböck formula reads

\[
 D_H=\int\left(|\nabla\psi|^2+\frac s3\rho^2
                    -2\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle\right).
 \tag{17}
\]

Combining (16) and (17) gives the exact identity

\[
 D_0=\int\left(\frac23q\rho^2-f\rho^3\right).
 \tag{18}
\]

Let \(u=3\rho/2\) and

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P&=\int(qu^2-fu^3)=\frac{27}{8}D_0,&
 X&=\left(\int q^3f^{-2}\right)^{1/3},\\
 Y&=\left(\int f^4\right)^{1/3},&
 Z&=\int fu^3,\qquad C=\int f^2u^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{19}
\]

Hölder gives two nonnegative remainders

\[
 H_1=XZ^{2/3}-\int qu^2,\qquad H_2=YZ^{2/3}-C.
 \tag{20}
\]

Hodge orthogonality and the actual curvature equation give

\[
 C=18\|F_A^+\|_2^2=72\pi^2p+18J,
 \qquad J=\|iF_A^+-2\pi H\|_2^2.
 \tag{21}
\]

Here \(p>0\) implies \(Z>0\). Direct factorization yields

\[
 YX^2-72\pi^2p
 =18J+H_2+
       Y\frac{X+Z^{1/3}}{Z^{2/3}}(P+H_1).
 \tag{22}
\]

First suppose \(f=\sqrt q\) is smooth. Put
\(E=\int q^2\), \(\theta=(Z/E)^{1/3}\). Then
\(X=Y=E^{1/3}\), \(H_1=H_2=H\), and \(0<\theta\le1\).
Thus

\[
 \Delta=18J+H+\frac{1+\theta}{\theta^2}(P+H),
 \qquad
 J\le\frac\Delta{18},\quad
 P\le\frac\Delta2,\quad
 D_0\le\frac{4\Delta}{27}.
 \tag{23}
\]

This proves the differential and eigenvalue assertions in (5).

For amplitude control use the probability measure
\(d\nu=q^2d\mu/E\), set \(r=u/\sqrt q\), and write
\(c=\int r^2d\nu\), \(z=\int r^3d\nu\), \(m=\int r\,d\nu\).
The inequalities above imply \(z\le c\le z^{2/3}\), so \(c\le1\).
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(c^2\le mz\le mc\), hence \(m\ge c\).
Therefore

\[
 \int q(u-\sqrt q)^2=E(c-2m+1)\le E-C\le\Delta.
 \tag{24}
\]

Finally \(f\psi=-2\sqrt2\,iF_A^+\); (21) gives (10).

For the general approximants \(f_j\), (18)–(22) remain exact. Their
left side in (22) tends to \(\Delta\). If
\(\vartheta_j=Z_j^{1/3}/X_j\), then
\(0<\vartheta_j\le1\) and the last coefficient is at least
\(2Y_j/X_j\to2\). Hence (23) holds with errors tending to zero.
For (24), retain the fixed measure \(q^2d\mu/E\). Uniform convergence
of the weights and \(u_j\le3K/2\) give
\(z_j\le c_j+o(1)\), while Hölder still gives
\(c_j\le z_j^{2/3}\). Moreover
\(c_j\ge72\pi^2p/E-o(1)>0\). It follows that
\(c_j\le1+o(1)\) and \(m_j\ge c_j-o(1)\), proving (24) with
vanishing error. Harmonic-projection control follows in the same way
from the exact curvature equation. Choose a single sufficiently large
\(j\) to make every error below any prescribed allowance.

No convergence of spinors, uniform derivative bounds for the weights,
or common smoothing rate along varying geometries is used. All asserted
forms are smooth actual weighted-monopole forms, not formal algebraic
choices of a Weyl eigenline.

## 4. The defect split and polynomial Weyl approximation

The pointwise scalar identity is

\[
 \frac{s^2}{24}+\frac{|W^+|^2}{2}
 =\frac{q^2}{36}+\frac{t^2}{72}.
 \tag{25}
\]

Integration gives (3); \(\Delta\ge0\) was established in (22)–(23).

For any \(\psi\), without assuming it is an eigenvector or is nonzero,
trace-freeness of \(W^+\) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 |W^+-\mathcal B_\psi|^2
  =\frac16\left(w-\frac34\rho^2\right)^2
                         +\frac34E_\lambda.}
 \tag{26}
\]

For example, \(|\mathcal B_\psi|^2=3\rho^4/32\) and
\(-2\langle W^+,\mathcal B_\psi\rangle
=3\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle/4\); completing the square proves
(26). This avoids eigenline choices and division by spectral gaps.

Set \(a_0=q-u^2\). Because \(q\ge s_0\) and \(u\le3K/2\),

\[
 \int a_0^2
 \le C_0\int q(u-\sqrt q)^2\le C_0\Delta+\eta.
 \tag{27}
\]

The relation \(w-3\rho^2/4=(t+a_0)/3\), (26), (27), and (5)
give (8). All approximation errors can be decreased in advance to
absorb their fixed coefficients into the displayed \(\eta\).

## 5. Covariant derivative, scalar oscillation, and equality

Rearranging (17) using (6) yields the exact identity

\[
 \int|\nabla\psi|^2=D_H+D_{\rm eig}-\frac13\int t\rho^2.
 \tag{28}
\]

The two nonnegative terms on the right are bounded jointly by \(D_0\),
not merely separately. Equations (23), (3), and Cauchy–Schwarz prove
(9). In particular the unconditional conclusion has order
\(\delta+\sqrt{V\delta}\) with the displayed scalar-band factor;
it is not a linear \(C\delta\) estimate. If the additional sign
\(t\ge0\) holds, (28) does give
\(\int|\nabla\psi|^2\le4\Delta/27+\eta\).

To prove (11), Poincaré and \(0\le\rho\le K\) give

\[
 \int(\rho^2-\bar\rho^{\,2})^2
 \le4K^2\int(\rho-\bar\rho)^2
 \le\frac{8K^2}{27\lambda_1(g)}\Delta+\eta.
 \tag{29}
\]

Use
\(s=t/3-3\rho^2/2-2a_0/3\), subtract the constant
\(-3\bar\rho^{\,2}/2\), and apply
\((x+y+z)^2\le3(x^2+y^2+z^2)\). The mean \(\bar s\) minimizes
the squared scalar error over constants. Equations (27) and (29) give
(11) after letting the auxiliary allowance tend to zero.

When \(\delta=0\), (3) gives \(t=0\), so
\(w=-s/2>0\) and \(q=-3s/2\) are smooth. Use the exact weight
\(\sqrt q\). Now \(D_0=0\), \(d\rho=0\),
\(\rho=2\sqrt q/3>0\), and (28) gives \(\nabla\psi=0\).
Thus \(\sqrt2\psi/\rho\) is a parallel self-dual form of norm
\(\sqrt2\), defining a Kähler structure with the chosen orientation.
Its scalar curvature is constant and negative. The everywhere nonzero
positive spinor gives the compatible Spin\(^c\) structure. This is the
classical equality geometry, now visible directly in the retained
defects.

An unequal-curvature product of closed hyperbolic surfaces is an
essential check. With factor curvatures \(-\alpha,-\beta\),
\(\alpha,\beta>0\), its compatible Kähler class has
\(p=(\alpha+\beta)^2V/(8\pi^2)\), whereas
\(s=-2(\alpha+\beta)\), \(w=\alpha+\beta\), and \(D=0\).
But \(|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2=(\alpha-\beta)^2\), which
need not vanish. Therefore this theorem supplies no unqualified
Einstein or trace-free-Ricci remainder.

## 6. Supplying a second-order budget

The preceding theorem has no second-order hypothesis. Such a budget can
also be obtained if one supplies integral Ricci and determinant-curvature
control. This step uses the bundle Hessian estimate established in
`round4_rigidity.md`, Section 2, and rederived in `round21_analytic.md`,
Section 2:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\nabla^2u\|_2^2
 &\le2\|\nabla^*\nabla u\|_2^2
       +32\|u\|_\infty^2\|\operatorname{Ric}\|_2^2
       +33\|u\|_\infty^2\|\mathscr F\|_2^2,\\
 \|\nabla u\|_4^2&\le4\|u\|_\infty\|\nabla^2u\|_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]

Here \(\mathscr F\) is the compatible bundle connection curvature,
with component norm \(\sum_{ij}|\mathscr F_{ij}|_{\rm HS}^2\).
Equation (30) is an integration-by-parts estimate on a closed manifold;
it assumes no Sobolev constant or differentiated-curvature bound.

**Second-order corollary.** In addition to (1), assume

\[
 \fint|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\le R_0,\qquad
 \frac{(-a^2)_+}{V}\le A_0,\qquad
 \frac D V\le D_0^*.
 \tag{31}
\]

For every allowance \(\eta>0\), the solution in the theorem may be
chosen so that it also satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\fint\bigl(|\nabla^2\psi|^2+|\Delta_H\psi|^2\bigr)
       \le C(S,s_0,R_0,A_0,D_0^*).}
 \tag{32}
\]

The constant is independent of volume, injectivity radius, Sobolev
constants, and derivatives of the approximating weight.

**Proof.** To make the dependence explicit, put

\[
 H_W=\frac{S^2+288\pi^2D_0^*}{12},\qquad
 Q_1=S+\sqrt{6H_W}.
 \tag{33}
\]

Since \(w=(t-s)/2\), (3) gives
\(\fint|W^+|^2\le H_W\) and \(\fint q\le Q_1\).
Choose the smooth approximants sufficiently close that
\(f_j^2\le q+s_0\), in addition to \(f_j\ge\sqrt{s_0}\).
The weighted curvature equation and \(\rho\le K\) give

\[
 \fint|F_A^+|^2\le\frac{K^2}{8}(Q_1+s_0),\qquad
 \fint|F_A|^2
 =2\fint|F_A^+|^2-\frac{4\pi^2a^2}{V}
 \le\frac{K^2}{4}(Q_1+s_0)+4\pi^2A_0.
 \tag{34}
\]

The curvature of the positive-spinor connection satisfies the
dimensional representation estimate

\[
 |\mathscr F^{S^+}|^2
 \le C\bigl(|W^+|^2+s^2+|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2
                                      +|F_A|^2\bigr).
 \tag{35}
\]

The \(W^-\) block acts trivially on the positive-spin representation;
it does not enter this estimate. Hence its normalized integral is
bounded by a constant depending only on the parameters in (31).
Equation (13) and \(|\Phi|^2\le K\) give

\[
 \fint|\nabla_A^*\nabla_A\Phi|^2
 \le\frac K8\left[S^2+K^2(Q_1+s_0)\right].
 \tag{36}
\]

Apply (30) to \(\Phi\), whose supremum norm is at most \(\sqrt K\).
Equations (31), (35), and (36) bound
\(\fint|\nabla_A^2\Phi|^2\). The second inequality in (30) then
gives

\[
 \fint|\nabla_A\Phi|^4
 \le16K\,\fint|\nabla_A^2\Phi|^2.
 \tag{37}
\]

Twice differentiating the parallel quadratic map, rather than
differentiating its PDE or the weight, gives

\[
 |\nabla^2\psi|^2
 \le C\bigl(K|\nabla_A^2\Phi|^2+|\nabla_A\Phi|^4\bigr).
 \tag{38}
\]

Finally use
\(\Delta_H\psi=\nabla^*\nabla\psi+(s/3)\psi-2W^+\psi\),
\(|\nabla^*\nabla\psi|^2\le4|\nabla^2\psi|^2\), and
\(|\psi|\le K\). This proves (32). The bounds hold uniformly for
all sufficiently close approximants, so selecting one that meets the
first-order allowances causes no further loss. In particular the
proof never assumes a bound on \(df_j\).

## 7. The realizable negative-Einstein Weyl branch

The same actual form supplies the *allowed* branch in the
[Round 21 two-form criterion](round21_analytic.md). This is a form-production result,
not an additional Einstein obstruction.

Set

\[
 \omega=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\psi,\qquad
 A_{\omega,1/2}=\frac12
       \left(\frac{|\omega|^2}{3}I-\omega\otimes\omega\right)
       =\mathcal B_\psi,
 \qquad r=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2.
 \tag{39}
\]

Writing \(e=s+4\) and retaining \(a_0=q-9\rho^2/4\), one has the
exact amplitude identity

\[
 |\omega|^2-2=\frac t6-\frac e2-\frac{a_0}{3}.
 \tag{40}
\]

Since \(|e|^2\le4|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2\), it follows that

\[
 \int(|\omega|^2-2)^2
 \le\frac1{12}\int t^2+3\int|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2
                                  +\frac{C_0}{3}\Delta+\eta.
 \tag{41}
\]

Consequently the zeroth-order calibration defect obeys

\[
\begin{aligned}
 d_{1/2}
 &:={\fint}\left(|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2
       +|W^+-A_{\omega,1/2}|^2+(|\omega|^2-2)^2\right)\\
 &\le4r+\frac{13}{108V}\int t^2
       +\left(\frac{10C_0}{27}+\frac1{18}\right)\frac\Delta V
       +\eta\\
 &\le4r+\frac{10C_0+8}{27}\frac\delta V+\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]

Here the free approximation allowance was chosen to be \(\eta V\)
before division by volume. Under (31), (32) also provides
\(\fint|\Delta_H\omega|^2\le C(S,s_0,R_0,A_0,D_0^*)\).
Thus both the bounded calibration and its second-order source budget
are supplied by actual monopole equations.

A common sufficient condition for the negative-square budget is

\[
 a^2\ge T:=2\chi(M)+3\tau(M).
 \tag{43}
\]

It must be verified for the selected class; it does not follow merely
from conformal-class solvability. With the current norm conventions,
Chern–Weil gives

\[
 4\pi^2T=\int\left(2|W^+|^2+\frac{s^2}{24}
                                  -\frac{|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2}{2}\right),
 \qquad
 \frac{(-a^2)_+}{V}\le\frac r{8\pi^2}.
 \tag{44}
\]

Also \(\fint|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\le8+2r\). Therefore, in a fixed
scalar band, bounds on \(r\) and \(D/V\), together with (43), are
sufficient for all the budgets in this corollary. There is no separate
uncontrolled negative-square parameter in that special application.

At \(r=D=0\), the form has norm \(\sqrt2\), is parallel, and yields
the Kähler–Einstein Weyl amplitude \(t_{\rm branch}=1/2\) in the
notation of Round 21. This branch is realizable. For small positive
defects, the argument yields the displayed form and curvature estimates,
not a global metric correction to an exact Einstein metric.

## 8. Scaling, relation to prior results, and use

Under \(g\mapsto cg\), the quantities \(s,w,q,S,s_0\) scale by
\(c^{-1}\), \(K\) by \(c^{-1/2}\), \(V\) by \(c^2\), and
\(\lambda_1\) by \(c^{-1}\). The numbers
\(\delta,\Delta,C_0,K^2\sqrt V,K^2/\lambda_1\) are unchanged.
Transporting the weighted solution rescales its differential two-form
\(\psi\) by \(c^{1/2}\), consistently with every displayed energy.

The theorem requires neither \(W^-\) control nor a uniform Sobolev,
injectivity-radius, volume, or diameter bound. It does require the
scalar band and actual weighted-monopole solvability. These hypotheses
are not consequences of merely specifying a cohomology vector.

Relative to Round 17, the new control parameter is \(D_{LB}\) alone:
it retains the full cscK equality set and uses no Ricci-error input.
Relative to `round21_analytic.md`, the form here is produced by actual
monopole equations. Sections 6–7 state the additional hypotheses that
supply its Hodge-Laplacian budget; the first-order theorem alone must not
be used as if that budget were automatic. Pointwise near-negative-Einstein
metrics provide a scalar band, but small LeBrun defect remains a separate
condition to be established.

The useful new remainder is (8), together with the actual global form
and its differential controls (5), (9), and (10). It does not assert
closeness of metrics to a global Kähler metric, compactness of a
degenerating family, or exclusion of all Einstein metrics on the smooth
manifold. Those statements require additional arguments.

# Harmonic Chern representatives at the realizable Einstein calibration

23 September 2026. This application starts with a metric and an actual
conformally solvable Spin\(^c\) structure. No auxiliary form, scalar
band, spectral gap, or second-order budget is assumed. Its conclusion
is not another Einstein obstruction: the specified harmonic Chern
representative is quantitatively close to a bounded almost-parallel
form and to low connection-Laplacian modes.

## 1. Raw hypotheses and statement

Let \((M,g)\) be a closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold,
normalized relative to the target \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\).
Fix a Spin\(^c\) structure with real first Chern class \(a\), whose
unperturbed Seiberg–Witten equations are solvable for every metric in
\([g]\). The structure may depend on \(g\), but remains fixed when
the conformal weight varies. Write
\[
 V=\operatorname{Vol}(g),\quad p=(a_g^+)^2>0,\quad
 r=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2,\quad b=\frac{(-a^2)_+}{V},
 \quad w=\sqrt6|W^+|,
\]
\[
 \delta=\frac32\int(s^2+2w^2)-72\pi^2p\ge0,
 \qquad \kappa=r+\delta/V.
 \tag{1}
\]
Ricci uses tensor norms, forms use exterior-algebra norms, and Weyl
operators use Hilbert–Schmidt norms. The deficit equals
\(144\pi^2D_{\rm LB}\), or the parameter \(\beta=2\) in
`line1_functional_pencil.md`. Assume \(\kappa\le1/2880\).

Let \(H\) be the harmonic self-dual representative of \(a_g^+\),
with \(\|H\|_2^2=p\), and set \(\zeta=-2\pi H\).
Our curvature convention is
\(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal H^+}(iF_A^+)=2\pi H\).
Put \(G(\kappa)=13\kappa+4\sqrt{2\kappa}\).

**Theorem.** For each \(0<\eta\le1/4\), there is a smooth actual
weighted-monopole form \(\omega\in\Omega^+(M)\) such that
\[
 \|\omega\|_\infty\le2\sqrt3,
 \qquad \boxed{\fint|\omega-\zeta|^2\le49\kappa+\eta,}
 \tag{2}
\]
\[
 \boxed{\fint|\nabla\omega|^2\le G(\kappa)+\eta,\qquad
 \fint|d\omega|^2\le\frac{13}{2}\kappa+\eta.}
 \tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
 A_{\omega,1/2}=\frac12
       \left(\frac{|\omega|^2}{3}I-\omega\otimes\omega\right),
\]
\[
 d_\omega=\fint\left(|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2
       +|W^+-A_{\omega,1/2}|^2+(|\omega|^2-2)^2\right),
 \quad B_\omega=\fint|\Delta_H\omega|^2.
\]
The same smooth form satisfies, with universal constants,
\[
 d_\omega\le720\kappa+\eta,\qquad
 B_\omega\le C(1+\kappa+b),\qquad
 (1+B_\omega)d_\omega\le C(1+\kappa+b)(\kappa+\eta).
 \tag{4}
\]
Here \(d\omega\) is exterior differentiation and \(\Delta_H\)
is the nonnegative Hodge Laplacian. The first-order conclusions
(2)–(3) do not depend on \(b\); that cost enters only (4).

Let \(\mathscr L=\nabla^*\nabla\) on \(\Lambda^+\), let
\(\mu_0\ge0\) be its bottom eigenvalue, including zero, and let
\(\Pi_{\le\Lambda}\) project onto eigenvalues at most \(\Lambda>0\).
Then the input harmonic representative satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \mu_0\le\frac{G(\kappa)}{2-\sqrt{720\kappa}},\qquad
 \fint|(I-\Pi_{\le\Lambda})\zeta|^2
       \le98\kappa+\frac{2G(\kappa)}{\Lambda}.}
 \tag{5}
\]
No scalar or Hodge spectral gap, scalar-curvature sign, noncollapse,
Sobolev constant, or full-curvature bound is used. In particular (2)
does not follow from small exterior derivative via a uniform Hodge
inverse; its harmonic class comes from the actual curvature equation.

## 2. Producing the bounded form and its zeroth-order budget

Put \(q=-s+w\), \(e=s+4\), \(z=s+2w\), and
\(q_\pm=\max(\pm q,0)\). The signed monopole estimate gives
\(\Delta_+:=\int q_+^2-72\pi^2p\ge0\). Completing the square,
\[
 \delta=\Delta_++\int q_-^2+\tfrac12\int z^2,
 \qquad q-6=(z-3e)/2,\quad w-2=(z-e)/2.
 \tag{6}
\]
Since \(e^2\le4|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2\),
\[
 \fint(q-6)^2\le\delta/V+18r\le18\kappa,
 \qquad \fint(w-2)^2\le\delta/V+2r\le2\kappa.
 \tag{7}
\]
Set \(Q=\max(q,3)\) and
\(f_0=\max\{\sqrt Q,(-s)_+/4\}\). Choose smooth majorants
\(f_j\ge f_0\) converging uniformly to \(f_0\). Conformal
solvability supplies
\[
 D_A\Phi=0,\quad -iF_A^+=f_j\sigma(\Phi),\quad
 \psi=2\sqrt2\sigma(\Phi),\quad
 \rho=|\Phi|^2=|\psi|,\quad u=3\rho/2,
 \quad \omega=\sqrt3\psi/2.
 \tag{8}
\]
With \(|\sigma(\Phi)|^2=|\Phi|^4/8\), the maximum principle
gives \(\rho\le4\) and \(u\le6\).

We record the moment inputs, including their truncation cost. Write
\[
 \begin{split}
 D_0={}&2\int|d\rho|^2
 +\int(4\rho|\nabla_A\Phi|^2-|\nabla\psi|^2)\\
 &+2\int|d\psi|^2+2\int E_\lambda,\qquad
 E_\lambda=\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle+w\rho^2/3\ge0.
 \end{split}
\]
All its summands are nonnegative, and
\(27D_0/8=\int(qu^2-f_ju^3)\). For
\(C=\int Qu^2\), \(Z=\int\sqrt Q\,u^3\),
\(E_Q=\int Q^2\), and \(C_{\rm act}=\int f_j^2u^2\),
\[
 C-Z\ge27D_0/8,\quad C\le E_Q^{1/3}Z^{2/3},\quad
 C_{\rm act}\ge72\pi^2p,\quad
 C_{\rm act}-C\le\tfrac{81}{4}\int e^2+o(1).
\]
The last estimate uses
\(Q^2-q_+^2\le9e^2\) and \(f_0^2-Q\le9e^2/16\).
Consequently, with
\(S=\Delta_++117\int e^2/4\le117\kappa V\),
\[
 D_0\le4S/27+o(1),\quad
 \int Q(u-\sqrt Q)^2\le S+o(1),\quad
 0\le C_{\rm act}-72\pi^2p\le S+o(1).
 \tag{9}
\]
These are the truncated Hölder moment estimates proved in the
functional-pencil note, Section 3, and Round 4, Section 4. All errors
here concern weight approximation on a fixed \((g,a)\); no limiting
spinor or common smoothing rate for a family is assumed.

The explicit zeroth-order bound follows without an eigenline choice:
\[
 \fint(u^2-6)^2
 \le[234(1+2\sqrt3)^2+36]\kappa+o(1)
 \le4800\kappa+o(1),
 \tag{10}
\]
because \(|u^2-Q|\le(1+2\sqrt3)\sqrt Q|u-\sqrt Q|\), and
clipping does not increase distance from six. Since
\(|\omega|^2=u^2/3\), its amplitude error is at most
\(4800\kappa/9+o(1)\). The exact trace-free matrix identity is
\[
 |W^+-A_{\omega,1/2}|^2
 =\tfrac16(w-u^2/3)^2+\tfrac34 E_\lambda.
 \tag{11}
\]
By (7), (9), and \(\int E_\lambda\le2S/27+o(1)\), its
normalized integral is at most
\((2/3+4800/27+13/2)\kappa+o(1)\). Adding amplitude and Ricci
errors gives \(d_\omega\le720\kappa+o(1)\).

## 3. Differential and harmonic information

The self-dual Weitzenböck formula and (9) give directly
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int|\nabla\psi|^2
 &=2\int|d\psi|^2+2\int E_\lambda-\tfrac13\int z\rho^2\\
 &\le4S/27+(16/3)\sqrt{V\int z^2}+o(1).
 \end{split}
 \tag{12}
\]
Multiplying by \(3/4\) proves (3). This controls the full covariant
derivative, without a Hessian estimate or Chern-square budget.

The actual curvature has harmonic projection
\(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal H^+}(f_j\psi)=-4\sqrt2\pi H\).
Thus
\[
 \|f_j\psi+4\sqrt2\pi H\|_2^2
 =\tfrac49(C_{\rm act}-72\pi^2p)\le4S/9+o(1).
 \tag{13}
\]
Moreover \((f_0-\sqrt Q)^2\le f_0^2-Q\), so
\[
 \fint|f_j-\sqrt6|^2
 \le\tfrac92r+
 \frac{2(\delta/V+18r)}{(\sqrt3+\sqrt6)^2}+o(1)
 \le9\kappa+o(1).
 \tag{14}
\]
Use the identity
\[
 2\sqrt2(\omega+2\pi H)
 =(\sqrt6-f_j)\psi+(f_j\psi+4\sqrt2\pi H)
\]
and \(|\psi|\le4\). The squared triangle inequality gives
\[
 \fint|\omega+2\pi H|^2
 \le4\fint|f_j-\sqrt6|^2+S/(9V)+o(1)
 \le49\kappa+o(1),
 \tag{15}
\]
which identifies the prescribed harmonic Chern representative.

## 4. Second-order budget and smoothing

Equations (6)–(7) bound the normalized squared integrals of
\(\operatorname{Ric},s,W^+\) by \(C(1+\kappa)\), and the cutoff
bounds \(\fint f_j^2\le C(1+\kappa)+o(1)\). Hence
\[
 \int|F_A|^2=2\int|F_A^+|^2-4\pi^2a^2
 \le CV(1+\kappa+b)+o(1).
\]
The positive-spinor connection curvature is controlled by
\(W^+,s,\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ},F_A\); \(W^-\) does not act
on this representation. Apply the inherited bounded-section estimate
\[
 \|\nabla^2v\|_2^2\le2\|\nabla^*\nabla v\|_2^2
 +32\|v\|_\infty^2\|\operatorname{Ric}\|_2^2
 +33\|v\|_\infty^2\|\mathscr F\|_2^2,
 \quad \|\nabla v\|_4^2\le4\|v\|_\infty\|\nabla^2v\|_2
\]
to \(\Phi\), whose rough Laplacian is controlled by
\((s+f_j\rho)\Phi\). Twice differentiating the parallel quadratic
map and using the self-dual Hodge formula gives
\(B_\omega\le C(1+\kappa+b)\). This is the second-order argument
in the functional-pencil note, Section 5, and Round 21, Section 2;
it differentiates neither the weight nor the Ricci error.

For each fixed metric and class, select one sufficiently large \(j\)
so that all normalized approximation errors meet their allotted
fractions of \(\eta\). All estimates hold for the same smooth
actual solution. The second-order bound holds for every sufficiently
close majorant. This proves (2)–(4), with no limiting-solution claim.

## 5. Connection spectrum and smooth approximate projections

The amplitude bound implies
\(\fint|\omega|^2\ge2-\sqrt{720\kappa+\eta}>1\), which gives
the Rayleigh bound on \(\mu_0\). The spectral theorem gives
\[
 \|(I-\Pi_{\le\Lambda})\omega\|_2^2
 \le\|\nabla\omega\|_2^2/\Lambda.
\]
By (2)–(3), the normalized tail of \(\zeta\) is at most
\(98\kappa+2G(\kappa)/\Lambda+2\eta(1+1/\Lambda)\).
The harmonic representative and the spectral projection are fixed
as \(\eta\downarrow0\), proving (5) without convergence of spinors.

For a sequence with \(\kappa_j\to0\), even if \(b_j\to\infty\),
\(\fint|\zeta_j|^2=4\pi^2p_j/V_j\to2\).
Choose \(\Lambda_j\to0\) with \(G(\kappa_j)/\Lambda_j\to0\).
Then all its normalized \(L^2\) mass lies asymptotically in the low
connection-Laplacian subspace. This does not assert a pointwise bound
on \(H_j\), nor is it a scalar-Laplacian cluster statement.

The same form defines \(P_\omega=\omega\otimes\omega/2\). Algebra gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \fint|W^++P_\omega-I/3|^2&\le d_\omega,\\
 \fint|P_\omega^2-P_\omega|^2&\le9d_\omega,\\
 \fint|\nabla P_\omega|^2&\le12(G(\kappa)+\eta).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{16}
\]
Outside \(\{|\omega|^2\le1\}\), of relative volume at most
\(d_\omega\), the endomorphism
\(\omega\otimes\omega/|\omega|^2\) is a rank-one projection
with derivative energy at most \(2V(G(\kappa)+\eta)\).
This does not remove the exceptional set or give an everywhere
nonvanishing closed form. An \(L^2\) approximation also does not
permit substituting the potentially unbounded \(\zeta\) for
\(\omega\) in the quadratic Weyl model.

## 6. An actual family outside every fixed negative scalar band

Take a closed negative Kähler–Einstein surface \((M,h)\), with
\(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\), carrying an actual canonical monopole
class; a product of two closed hyperbolic surfaces is an example.
In normal coordinates centered at \(x_0\), choose
\(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_1(0))\) with
\(\phi(0)=0\), \(d\phi(0)=0\), and
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{Eucl}}\phi(0)>0\), for the nonnegative-Laplacian
sign convention. Set
\[
 f_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon\phi(x/\varepsilon),\qquad
 g_\varepsilon=e^{2f_\varepsilon}h,
\]
extending the bump by zero outside its coordinate ball. In dimension
four,
\[
 \int|\nabla_h^2f_\varepsilon|^2=O(\varepsilon^2),\quad
 \int|df_\varepsilon|^4=O(\varepsilon^4),\quad
 \|f_\varepsilon\|_\infty=O(\varepsilon).
\]
The conformal Ricci formula, and cancellation of conformal factors in
the four-dimensional squared norm integral of a covariant two-tensor,
give \(\int|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_\varepsilon}+g_\varepsilon|^2
=O(\varepsilon^2)\). Volumes converge to \(\operatorname{Vol}(h)\).
Both \(p\) and \(\int|W^+|^2\) are conformally invariant. Since
\[
 s_{g_\varepsilon}=e^{-2f_\varepsilon}
       (-4+6\Delta_hf_\varepsilon-6|df_\varepsilon|^2),
\]
integration of \(\Delta_h f_\varepsilon\) gives
\[
 \int s_{g_\varepsilon}^2-\int s_h^2
 =36\int(\Delta_hf_\varepsilon-|df_\varepsilon|^2)^2
       +48\int|df_\varepsilon|^2=O(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Thus \(\delta_\varepsilon=O(\varepsilon^2)\) and
\(\kappa_\varepsilon\to0\), while
\[
 s_{g_\varepsilon}(x_0)
 =-4+6\varepsilon^{-1}\Delta_{\mathrm{Eucl}}\phi(0)
 \longrightarrow+\infty.
\]
The application therefore genuinely includes metrics outside every
fixed negative scalar band. These are integral-error examples, not
pointwise Ricci-pinched Yau26 inputs, and not new smooth topologies.

The supplied form lies at the allowed Round-21 parameter \(t=1/2\),
where spectral non-saturation gives no contradiction. The moment and
Hessian mechanisms are inherited. Their all-scalar composition removes
the scalar band from the earlier actual-form supply. No scalar spectral

The useful added output is
the canonically identified harmonic alignment and spectral/projection

At zero defect this recovers the realizable negative Kähler–Einstein

# Quantitative product-boundary calibration and its surviving source

## 1. Result and geometric setting

The scalar-energy boundary of the closed shear examples is different from
the forbidden Weyl boundary used in the integral gap theorem. At this
boundary the correct model is realizable: a product of two hyperbolic
surfaces. The result below gives quantitative control of departure from
that model on the original metric, without compactness or smoothing.
It also identifies the term that survives in the weak-Bianchi argument.

Let \(g\) be a smooth metric on a closed oriented four-manifold, with

\[
 s_g=-4\kappa,\qquad \kappa>0,\qquad
 A=2\chi+3\tau>0.
\]

Assume a specified spin-c structure has \(a^2=A\) and an actual
unperturbed Seiberg--Witten solution \((\Phi,\mathfrak A)\) for this
metric. An actual monopole class of this square supplies the hypothesis;
an arbitrary cohomology class does not. Set

\[
 \rho=|\Phi|^2,\quad \psi=2\sqrt2\sigma(\Phi),\quad
 |\psi|=\rho,\quad K=\int|\nabla_{\mathfrak A}\Phi|^2,
\]
\[
 P=\int|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2,\qquad
 D=\kappa^2\operatorname{Vol}(g)-2\pi^2 A,\qquad
 w=4\kappa-\rho.
 \tag{1}
\]

All two-form norms are exterior-algebra norms; Weyl and curvature
operators use Hilbert--Schmidt norms on two-forms. Define the smooth,
globally defined Weyl surrogate

\[
 \mathsf A_\psi=\frac{\rho^2}{48\kappa}I
                       -\frac{\psi\otimes\psi}{16\kappa},
 \qquad \mathsf R=W^+-\mathsf A_\psi.
 \tag{2}
\]

It is a polynomial in the spinor and is smooth even where the spinor
vanishes. Its eigenvalues are

\[
 -\frac{\rho^2}{24\kappa},\quad
 \frac{\rho^2}{48\kappa},\quad
 \frac{\rho^2}{48\kappa}.
\]

**Theorem 1.** One has \(0\leq\rho\leq4\kappa\), \(D\geq0\), and
the exact identities

\[
 \boxed{4D=K+\kappa\int w+2\|F_{\mathfrak A}^-\|_2^2,}
 \tag{3}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathsf R\|_2^2+D+\frac1{8\kappa}\|d\psi\|_2^2
 =\frac P4+
   \frac1{384\kappa^2}\int(16\kappa^2-\rho^2)^2
   +\frac1{16\kappa}\|\nabla\psi\|_2^2.}
 \tag{4}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathsf R\|_2^2+\frac1{8\kappa}\|d\psi\|_2^2
      +\frac\kappa3\int w+2\|F_{\mathfrak A}^-\|_2^2
 \leq \frac P4+3D.}
 \tag{5}
\]

No Einstein hypothesis, diameter bound, Sobolev constant, injectivity
radius, or full-curvature bound is assumed. Constant negative scalar
curvature is an actual hypothesis, not inferred from small Ricci error.

The closed shear manifolds have \(\tau=0\), \(A=2\chi>0\), and
actual canonical monopoles of square \(A\) in both orientations by
[Round 12](round12_global.md). Their scalar-calibrated Round-14 metrics
therefore satisfy the theorem twice. The opposite-orientation class is
not incorrectly treated as a positive-square class in the original
orientation.

## 2. Exact scalar loss decomposition

The standard unweighted equations, in the conventions of
[the monopole input](round3_monopole_input.md), give

\[
 4\nabla_{\mathfrak A}^*\nabla_{\mathfrak A}\Phi
                  +(\rho-4\kappa)\Phi=0,
 \qquad |F_{\mathfrak A}^+|^2=\rho^2/8.
 \tag{6}
\]

The maximum principle yields \(\rho\leq4\kappa\). Pairing with the
spinor and integrating gives

\[
 4K=4\kappa\int w-\int w^2.
 \tag{7}
\]

The determinant connection represents \(2\pi a\), so its full
Chern--Weil identity, not merely a harmonic projection bound, gives

\[
 \int\rho^2=8\|F_{\mathfrak A}^+\|_2^2
          =32\pi^2 A+8\|F_{\mathfrak A}^-\|_2^2.
 \tag{8}
\]

Expanding \(\rho=4\kappa-w\) in (8) and using (7) proves (3).
Every term on its right is nonnegative. In particular

\[
 K\leq4D,\qquad \kappa\int w\leq4D,\qquad
 \int w^2\leq16D,\qquad \|F_{\mathfrak A}^-\|_2^2\leq2D.
 \tag{9}
\]

These statements concern the actual connection of an actual solution.
No derivative of a merely chosen harmonic cohomology representative
has been substituted for a spinor derivative.

The foundational spinor identities and equality characterization are
classical: see LeBrun, [*Ricci Curvature, Minimal Volumes, and
Seiberg--Witten Theory*, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0003068),
and [*Four-Manifolds, Curvature Bounds, and Convex Geometry*,
Proposition 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0611450).
Here the full nonnegative decomposition is retained quantitatively.

## 3. Weyl calibration from the actual spinor

The exact two-form Hodge identity is

\[
 2\|d\psi\|_2^2
 =\|\nabla\psi\|_2^2-\frac{4\kappa}{3}\int\rho^2
                         -2\int\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle.
 \tag{10}
\]

Self-duality gives \(\|d^*\psi\|_2=\|d\psi\|_2\). The quadratic
spinor map satisfies the pointwise differential bound

\[
 |\nabla\psi|^2\leq4\rho|\nabla_{\mathfrak A}\Phi|^2
                     \leq16\kappa|\nabla_{\mathfrak A}\Phi|^2.
 \tag{11}
\]

The operator (2) has squared norm \(\rho^4/(384\kappa^2)\), and
trace-freeness of \(W^+\) gives

\[
 \|\mathsf R\|_2^2
 =\|W^+\|_2^2+\frac1{384\kappa^2}\int\rho^4
                  +\frac1{8\kappa}\int\langle W^+\psi,\psi\rangle.
 \tag{12}
\]

Chern--Weil with \(A=2\chi+3\tau\) reads

\[
 \|W^+\|_2^2
 =2\pi^2A-\frac{\kappa^2}{3}V+\frac P4
 =\frac23\kappa^2V-D+\frac P4.
 \tag{13}
\]

Substitute (10) and (13) into (12). The remaining scalar polynomial
is exactly

\[
 \frac23\kappa^2-\frac{\rho^2}{12}
                         +\frac{\rho^4}{384\kappa^2}
 =\frac{(16\kappa^2-\rho^2)^2}{384\kappa^2}.
\]

This proves (4). Since \(0\leq w\leq4\kappa\),

\[
 \frac{(16\kappa^2-\rho^2)^2}{384\kappa^2}
 \leq \frac{w^2}{6}\leq\frac{2\kappa}{3}w.
 \tag{14}
\]

Equations (11), (14), and (3) yield

\[
 \|\mathsf R\|_2^2+D+\frac1{8\kappa}\|d\psi\|_2^2
 \leq\frac P4+K+\frac{2\kappa}{3}\int w
 =\frac P4+4D-\frac\kappa3\int w-2\|F_{\mathfrak A}^-\|_2^2,
\]

which is (5). All estimates are integral identities or pointwise
polynomial bounds on the original metric.

## 4. Two orientations give a full product-curvature estimate

Let \(\mathscr K_\kappa\) be the Weyl-operator orbit with eigenvalues

\[
 (-2\kappa/3,\kappa/3,\kappa/3).
\]

Choose the distinguished eigenline to be the line of \(\psi\) where
it is nonzero; at its zeros choose any measurable line. There is no
derivative of this choice. The squared distance between (2) and the
chosen orbit element is exactly the polynomial in (14). Consequently

\[
 \int\operatorname{dist}(W^+,\mathscr K_\kappa)^2
 \leq2\|\mathsf R\|_2^2+
       2\int\frac{(16\kappa^2-\rho^2)^2}{384\kappa^2}
 \leq\frac P2+\frac{34}{3}D.
 \tag{15}
\]

Now assume \(\tau=0\) and both orientations have actual monopoles
of square \(A=2\chi>0\). Let \(\mathcal R_g:\Lambda^2\to\Lambda^2\)
be the curvature operator and \(\mathscr P_\kappa\) the oriented
orthogonal orbit of the curvature operator of

\[
 \mathbb H^2_{-\kappa}\times\mathbb H^2_{-\kappa}.
\]

**Corollary 2.** For every constant-scalar metric as above,

\[
 \boxed{
 \int\operatorname{dist}_{\rm HS}(\mathcal R_g,\mathscr P_\kappa)^2
 \leq\frac32 P+\frac{68}{3}D.}
 \tag{16}
\]

**Proof.** The two Weyl blocks independently satisfy (15).
The off-diagonal Ricci blocks of the curvature operator have total
squared norm (P/2). Adding \(s/12=-\kappa/3\) to the diagonal
blocks makes their model spectra \((-\kappa,0,0)\) in both
orientations. Any pair of distinguished unit lines in \(\Lambda^+\)
and \(\Lambda^-\) determines two complementary simple two-planes;
equivalently, \(SO(4)\to SO(3)\times SO(3)\) is onto. Thus the
two independent orbit choices assemble into one product-curvature
operator, rather than two incompatible model choices. Summing proves
(16). The full four-index curvature-norm bound is four times (16).

For an Einstein metric \(P=0\), (16) bounds average deviation from
the local product model by its normalized volume excess. It does not
produce a global splitting or an identification of the fundamental
group. In particular it does not erase the shear topology.

The product metric itself has \(D=P=0\), parallel canonical spinors,
and both model Weyl blocks exactly. Thus it passes the equality test.

## 5. The full surviving weak-Bianchi source

The surrogate in (2) is \(1/(2\kappa)\) times the polynomial model
used at the forbidden \(2/3\) boundary. Retaining that old model
unchanged would impose the wrong curvature scale.

Let \(W(\mathsf A)\) denote its four-index tensor, in the conventions
of [the original first-jet computation](round3_monopole_input.md), and
put

\[
 J=\operatorname{div}W(\mathsf A_\psi),\qquad
 C_\psi=\langle\iota_{\nabla\rho}\psi,*d\psi\rangle.
\]

The exact weighted Hodge/first-jet identity gives

\[
\begin{split}
 \int|J|^2={}&
 \int\frac{\rho^4(16\kappa^2-\rho^2)}{3072\kappa^3}
 +\frac1{128\kappa^2}\int\rho^2\langle\mathsf R\psi,\psi\rangle\\
 &+\frac1{64\kappa^2}\int\rho^2|d\psi|^2
 -\frac5{384\kappa^2}\int\rho^2|d\rho|^2
 +\frac1{32\kappa^2}\int\rho C_\psi.
\end{split}
 \tag{17}
\]

This identity includes the remainder/source term, the amplitude term,
and all differential terms. In particular the algebraic term is

\[
 \frac{\rho^4(4\kappa-\rho)(4\kappa+\rho)}{3072\kappa^3},
 \qquad 0\leq\int\text{this term}\leq\frac83\kappa D.
 \tag{18}
\]

It vanishes at the product calibration \(\rho=4\kappa\). There is
no residual positive constant analogous to \(1/16\) in the forbidden
model. This is the precise cancellation, not just a numerical difference
between \(T/A=1\) and \(2/3\).

The pointwise polynomial derivative gives

\[
 |\nabla\mathsf A_\psi|^2
 =\frac{\rho^2}{128\kappa^2}|\nabla\psi|^2
  +\frac{\rho^2}{384\kappa^2}|d\rho|^2.
 \tag{19}
\]

Equations (9), (11), and the fixed-dimensional divergence contraction
give \(\int|J|^2\leq C\kappa D\). The differential terms in
(17) have the same bound, so the *integrated* remainder contraction
also satisfies

\[
 \left|\frac1{128\kappa^2}
       \int\rho^2\langle\mathsf R\psi,\psi\rangle\right|
 \leq C\kappa D.
 \tag{20}
\]

This does not make that contraction pointwise nonnegative or remove it
from the equation. For Einstein \(g\), weak Bianchi says

\[
 J=-\operatorname{div}W(\mathsf R),\qquad
 \int\langle J,T\rangle
   =\int\langle W(\mathsf R),\nabla T\rangle
 \tag{21}
\]

for smooth tests, with the stated divergence convention. Both sides of
the self-test may now tend to zero with \(D\). The actual equations
therefore retain the possibility of product-like Einstein degeneration;
the earlier forbidden-spectrum contradiction cannot be transplanted by
discarding this source.

## 6. A quantitative necessary condition for exact output

Let

\[
 \mu_\pm(g)=\inf_{0\ne\omega\in C^\infty(\Lambda^\pm)}
                    \frac{\|\nabla\omega\|_2^2}{\|\omega\|_2^2}
 \tag{22}
\]

be the lowest connection-Laplacian eigenvalues, not Hodge-Laplacian
eigenvalues. Taking the two spinor forms as tests, (8), (9), and (11)
prove

\[
 \boxed{
 \mu_\pm(g)\leq\frac{2\kappa D}{\pi^2 A}
        =4\kappa\left(\frac{V}{V_*}-1\right),
 \qquad V_*:=\frac{2\pi^2A}{\kappa^2}.}
 \tag{23}
\]

The same metric supplies both estimates. For a non-Kähler smooth type
in both orientations, each \(\mu_\pm(g)>0\): on a fixed compact
metric a zero eigenvalue would give a nonzero parallel self-dual or
anti-self-dual form, hence a compatible Kähler structure. Thus every
putative Einstein output obeys

\[
 \frac{V}{V_*}-1
 \geq\frac{\max\{\mu_+(g),\mu_-(g)\}}{4\kappa}>0.
 \tag{24}
\]

This is a metric-dependent quantitative necessary condition. Positivity
for every metric is not a uniform lower bound over a degenerating
Einstein family. The two explicit collapsing eigenvalue tests below
show why that distinction is necessary.

## 7. The actual shear supplies the source at cubic order

For the pure-shear family of [Round 14](round14_global.md), use
\(\kappa=3\), \(k=\sqrt3\), and

\[
 G_\ell=dt^2+a^2\theta^2+h_F,\quad
 a=c\cosh(kt),\quad \theta=d\phi+z(t)\beta,\quad
 c=\ell/(2\pi),\quad Q=\tfrac12a^2z'^2|\beta|^2.
\]

The global symplectic forms already constructed in Round 11 are

\[
 \Omega_\pm=a\,dt\wedge\theta\ \pm\ \omega_F.
\]

They are closed, of norm \(\sqrt2\), and respectively self-dual and
anti-self-dual. For the pure-shear metric their covariant derivatives
satisfy the **exact pointwise identity**

\[
 \boxed{|\nabla\Omega_+|^2=|\nabla\Omega_-|^2=2Q.}
 \tag{25}
\]

To see this, choose an orthonormal frame \(T,U,X,Y\) with

\[
 T=\partial_t,\quad U=a^{-1}\partial_\phi,\quad
 \beta=|\beta|X^*,\quad q=az'|\beta|/2.
\]

At the point, eliminate the fiber Levi-Civita connection by a normal
frame. The electric terms are

\[
 \nabla_TU=qX,\quad \nabla_TX=-qU,\quad
 \nabla_UT=(a'/a)U+qX,\quad \nabla_UX=-qT,
 \quad \nabla_XT=qU,\quad\nabla_XU=-qT.
\]

The ordinary \(T,U\) hyperbolic rotation preserves their area form.
The \(T\)- and \(U\)-derivatives of either \(\Omega_\pm\) each have
squared norm \(2q^2\); the \(X,Y\) derivatives vanish. Their sum is

\[
 4q^2=a^2z'^2|\beta|^2=2Q.
\]

The formula extends through zeros of \(\beta\) and through profile
cutoffs; outside the shear, the forms are parallel. Thus it holds on
the actual closed manifold, not only the infinite model cylinder.

Writing \(B_\beta=\int_F|\beta|^2\), the optimized profile yields

\[
 \int Q=\frac{kB_\beta}{4\pi^3}\ell^3+O(\ell^6),\qquad
 \frac{\|\nabla\Omega_\pm\|_2^2}{\|\Omega_\pm\|_2^2}
 =\frac1{V_*}\int Q.
 \tag{26}
\]

Here \(V_*=4\pi^2\chi/9\) in the normalization \(\kappa=3\).
Scalar calibration gives \(\widehat G_\ell=v_\ell^2G_\ell\).
Self-duality, closedness, and the \(L^2\) norm of two-forms are
conformally unchanged. Round 14 gives

\[
 \|v_\ell-1\|_\infty=O(\ell^2),\qquad
 \|dv_\ell\|_2=O(\ell^{5/2}).
\]

The difference of the two Levi-Civita connections acting on a two-form
is bounded by \(C|d\log v|\,|\Omega_\pm|\). Thus Cauchy--Schwarz
and (25) show

\[
 \|\widehat\nabla\Omega_\pm\|_{2,\widehat G_\ell}^2
 =2\int Q+O(\ell^4),\qquad
 \mu_\pm(\widehat G_\ell)
 \leq\frac{kB_\beta}{4\pi^3V_*}\ell^3+O(\ell^4).
 \tag{27}
\]

For these scalar-calibrated metrics, simultaneously,

\[
 D_\ell=9(\operatorname{Vol}(\widehat G_\ell)-V_*)
     =\frac{3kB_\beta}{8\pi^3}\ell^3+O(\ell^5),
 \qquad P_\ell=O(\ell^5).
 \tag{28}
\]

Equation (16) therefore gives an \(O(\ell^3)\) full product-curvature
calibration even though the integral Einstein defect is \(O(\ell^5)\).
The scalar-energy surplus and the failure of the canonical plane forms
to be parallel record the shear at a different scale from tracefree
Ricci. They cannot be silently replaced by the smaller Ricci error.

## 8. Einstein outputs approaching the sharp volume cannot form curvature bubbles

There is a stronger consequence when the metric is exactly Einstein.
Fix the normalization \(\operatorname{Ric}=-3g\), and suppose
\(\tau=0\), \(A=2\chi>0\), with the actual canonical monopole
input in both orientations. The constants in the next statement do not
depend on \(A\).

**Theorem 3.** There are universal \(D_0>0\) and \(C_m<\infty\)
such that every such exact Einstein metric with

\[
 0\leq D=9\operatorname{Vol}(g)-2\pi^2A\leq D_0
\]

satisfies

\[
 \sup_M|\nabla^m\operatorname{Rm}_g|\leq C_m
 \quad(m=0,1,2,\ldots).
 \tag{29}
\]

In particular a sequence of exact Einstein metrics on a fixed closed
shear manifold whose volumes approach the sharp scalar-volume bound
\(V_*=2\pi^2A/9\), if it exists, cannot develop unbounded curvature,
even in collapsing regions. This does not address a sequence minimizing
Einstein volume when its infimum lies strictly above this bound.

**Proof.** For exact Einstein metrics \(P=0\), (16) bounds the total
curvature-operator model error by \(68D/3\). The product model has
four-index squared norm \(8\kappa^2=72\). Therefore every ball satisfies

\[
 \int_{B_r(p)}|\operatorname{Rm}|^2
 \leq8\int_M\operatorname{dist}_{\rm HS}
                     (\mathcal R_g,\mathscr P_3)^2
       +144\operatorname{Vol}B_r(p)
 \leq\frac{544}{3}D+144C_{\rm BG}r^4,
 \quad 0<r\leq1.
 \tag{30}
\]

The volume upper bound is Bishop--Gromov with \(\operatorname{Ric}=-3g\);
it does not assume a volume lower bound. The four-dimensional
Cheeger--Tian theorem gives universal \(\epsilon_{\rm CT},C_{\rm CT}\)
such that, for an exact Einstein metric with Einstein constant of
absolute value at most three,

\[
 \int_{B_r(p)}|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\leq\epsilon_{\rm CT},
 \quad r\leq1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sup_{B_{r/2}(p)}|\operatorname{Rm}|\leq C_{\rm CT}r^{-2}.
 \tag{31}
\]

There is no noncollapse condition in this theorem. It is stated directly
by Cheeger in his [2018 lecture, slides 2--3](https://sites.duke.edu/scshgap/files/2016/08/dim4dukebeamer.pdf),
explicitly with an integral not normalized by volume. Its precise statement
is also reproduced as Theorem 1.1 in Ge--Jiang,
[*Epsilon-regularity for shrinking Ricci solitons and Ricci flows*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.05511),
which attributes it to Cheeger--Tian, *JAMS* 19 (2006), 487--525.
Choose once and for all \(r_0\leq1\) so that
\(144C_{\rm BG}r_0^4\leq\epsilon_{\rm CT}/2\), then choose
\(D_0\leq3\epsilon_{\rm CT}/1088\).
Equations (30)--(31) now apply at every point, proving the global
curvature bound. Fixed-order derivative bounds follow from bounded-
curvature Einstein regularity. Equivalently apply the standard complete
bounded-curvature Ricci-flow derivative estimates to the explicit
self-similar flow \(g(t)=(1+6t)g\) on a fixed positive time interval,
and scale back. This route also needs no injectivity-radius hypothesis.

The absolute integral in (30) is intentional. The argument does not
divide by the volume of a collapsing ball, and does not assert that
small absolute model error implies pointwise closeness to the product
at every collapsed point.

**Corollary 4 (the remaining degeneration).** Fix a closed shear manifold
with the non-Kähler conclusion in both orientations proved in Round 11.
If exact metrics \(g_j\) with \(\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}=-3g_j\)
satisfy \(\operatorname{Vol}(g_j)\to V_*\), then

\[
 \operatorname{diam}(M,g_j)\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{32}
\]

Every smooth pointed noncollapsed limit of such a sequence is locally
isometric to \(\mathbb H^2_{-3}\times\mathbb H^2_{-3}\).

**Proof.** A bounded-diameter subsequence would, by the lower total
volume \(V_*>0\) and relative Bishop--Gromov comparison, have uniform
lower volumes for fixed-radius balls. The curvature bound (29) then
gives a uniform injectivity radius and smooth Einstein compactness on
the original smooth type. Pull back the actual quadratic spinor forms
\(\psi_j^+\), not their determinant connections or spin-c structures,
by the same compactness diffeomorphisms. Equations (9) and (11) make
their covariant gradient energies tend to zero and their lengths tend
to \(12\) in \(L^2\). Rellich compactness on the fixed compact limit
then gives a global nonzero parallel two-form, hence a Kähler structure
in one of the two orientations. This contradicts the topology and
proves (32). No identification of the pulled-back spin-c structures is
needed.

For the pointed assertion it is enough to consider any smooth
noncollapsed limit charts. Choose actual spinor solutions in both
orientations on \(g_j\). Equations (9) and (11) give
\(\int|\nabla\psi_j^\pm|^2\to0\) and
\(\int(|\psi_j^\pm|-12)^2\to0\).
After transport to a fixed smooth limit chart, local Rellich compactness
and the uniform amplitude bound give nonzero parallel self-dual and
anti-self-dual limiting forms. A diagonal subsequence works on every
compact subset. The two associated parallel complex structures commute
and give complementary parallel two-plane distributions. Local product
splitting and \(\operatorname{Ric}=-3g\) make both surface factors
have curvature \(-3\). This proves the claimed local geometry without
assuming that the original smooth shear splitting is respected.

The corollary neither asserts noncollapse everywhere nor transfers
absolute error to local covers of unbounded multiplicity. Its conclusion
is precise: any remaining exact sequence with \(V\to V_*\) must have
bounded-curvature long geometry, with product geometry in its smooth
noncollapsed limits, rather than curvature bubbles.

In fact at least one such pointed limit exists. A second classical
Cheeger--Tian estimate, stated in [Cheeger's lecture, slides 8--9](https://sites.duke.edu/scshgap/files/2016/08/dim4dukebeamer.pdf),
says that a complete Einstein four-manifold with
\(\operatorname{Ric}=\pm3g\) and total curvature energy bounded by \(C\)
has some point \(p\) for which
\(\operatorname{Vol}B_1(p)/\operatorname{Vol}M\geq w(C)>0\).
On the fixed closed \(M\), exact Einstein Chern--Gauss--Bonnet gives
\(\int|\operatorname{Rm}|^2=32\pi^2\chi(M)\) in our full four-index
norm; the slides use the curvature-operator convention, with energy
\(8\pi^2\chi(M)\). Rescaling the fixed constant converts between them.
Since \(\operatorname{Vol}(g_j)\geq V_*>0\), choose basepoints \(p_j\)
with a uniform unit-ball volume lower bound. Together with (29),
pointed smooth compactness produces a complete limit after a subsequence.
The preceding proof makes this limit locally
\(\mathbb H^2_{-3}\times\mathbb H^2_{-3}\), and exhaustion by compact
sets gives total volume at most \(V_*\). The noncollapse estimate is a
classical input, not a new theorem of this round.

This complete limit is noncompact. Otherwise smooth pointed convergence
would eventually embed the entire compact limit into the connected
\(M\). The embedding is a local diffeomorphism with compact, hence
closed, image; its image is also open, so it is onto. The limiting
global parallel two-form would then give a Kähler structure on \(M\),
contrary to the assumed topology. The two parallel plane distributions
lift to the complete simply connected cover. The de Rham theorem gives
that cover as \(\mathbb H^2_{-3}\times\mathbb H^2_{-3}\). No identification
of the limit's ends with the original shear collars follows from this
argument.

## 9. Consequence for the next exact-equation step

At \(T/A=1\), actual gauge equations do yield a quantitative theorem:
small scalar-volume excess and small tracefree Ricci energy force both
canonical curvature blocks toward a genuine product model, with explicit
control on the original metric. For an exact output approaching the
sharp volume, its two connection-Laplacian gaps must also vanish.

For the current shear topology the equality \(D=0\) is excluded by
the established non-Kähler theorem. But the actual constructed sequence
has \(D\to0\), collapsing parallel-form gaps, and the source (25).
Thus topology cannot be converted into a uniform \(D\)-gap solely by
claiming that a nonparallel canonical form costs a fixed amount of
energy. Any such claim needs new control of the global matching or of
the exact Einstein equation; it is not supplied by (3)--(21).

This result is a quantitative stability statement at a *realizable*
calibration, complementary to the earlier integral obstruction at the
unrealizable \(2/3\) calibration. It neither creates a Yau26 counterexample
nor limits future arguments to monopole-budget refinements.

`round15_rigidity_check.py` independently checks the scalar and Weyl
identities, the two-orientation curvature normalization, the corrected
Weyl-source contraction, and the electric-shear parallel-form energy.
The classical gauge-existence input is not certified by these algebra
checks; it is the stated actual-monopole hypothesis.

# Product-profile propagation into collapsing Einstein regions

## 1. A quantitative extension of the product-boundary theorem

Let \(M\) be a closed oriented four-manifold with \(\tau(M)=0\),
\(A=2\chi(M)>0\), and actual monopole classes of square \(A\) in
both orientations, as in [Round 15](round15_rigidity.md).
Consider an exact Einstein metric

\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_g=-3g,\qquad
 D=9\operatorname{Vol}_g(M)-2\pi^2A\geq0.
 \tag{1}
\]

Let \(\mathscr P_3\) be the orthogonal orbit of the curvature operator
of \(\mathbb H^2_{-3}\times\mathbb H^2_{-3}\), and write

\[
 f(p)=\operatorname{dist}_{\rm HS}
              (\mathcal R_g(p),\mathscr P_3),\qquad
 v(p)=\operatorname{Vol}_g B_1(p).
 \tag{2}
\]

Curvature-operator norms are Hilbert--Schmidt norms on two-forms;
the squared full four-index curvature norm is four times the squared
operator norm.

**Theorem 1 (relative local product estimate).** There are universal
constants \(D_0,\varepsilon_0>0\) and \(C<\infty\) such that

\[
 D\leq D_0,\qquad \frac D{v(p)}\leq\varepsilon_0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\sup_{B_{1/4}(p)}
       \bigl(f+|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|\bigr)
       \leq C\left(\frac D{v(p)}\right)^{1/2}.}
 \tag{3}
\]

There is no positive lower bound on \(v(p)\) among the hypotheses.
In particular, (3) remains effective on collapsing balls whenever
their volume decays more slowly than the absolute excess \(D\).
The proof takes place on the original manifold; no absolute integral
is lifted to a local cover.

The analytic statement is not specific to monopoles: on a complete
Einstein four-manifold with \(\operatorname{Ric}=-3g\) and
\(|\operatorname{Rm}|\leq K\), replace \(D\) throughout (3) by
\(E=\int f^2<\infty\), with constants depending on \(K\).
The Einstein derivative bound follows from the homothetically
expanding complete Ricci flow and the usual curvature derivative
estimate. Sections 3--6 prove this version too. Monopoles enter
the stated application through the quantitative input (7) and the
uniform curvature bound (8).

For fixed \(M\), Round 15 supplies a point \(x\) with
\(v(x)\geq v_0(M)>0\). Bishop--Gromov then gives the following.

**Corollary 2 (logarithmic propagation).** There are \(c_M,C_M>0\)
such that, whenever \(D e^{3d_g(x,p)}\leq c_M\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{B_{1/4}(p)}(f+|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|)
 \leq C_M D^{1/2}e^{3d_g(x,p)/2}.}
 \tag{4}
\]

Consequently, for every fixed \(0<\theta<1/3\), as \(D\downarrow0\)
through positive values,

\[
 \sup_{B_{\theta\log(1/D)}(x)}
       (f+|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|)
 \leq C_M D^{(1-3\theta)/2}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{5}
\]

For each fixed \(\epsilon>0\), every point with
\(f(p)+|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|(p)\geq\epsilon\) satisfies

\[
 v(p)\leq C_\epsilon D,\qquad
 d_g(x,p)\geq\frac13\log(1/D)-C_{M,\epsilon}.
 \tag{6}
\]

Thus any fixed departure from product curvature or local symmetry
must move into quantitatively small-volume regions at least
logarithmically far from the thick point. This strengthens the
previous qualitative pointed-limit statement. It does not rule out
a long, bounded-curvature, deeply collapsed region.

## 2. Inputs already established in Round 15

Two-orientation spinor calibration gives

\[
 \int_M f^2\,d\mu\leq\frac{68}{3}D.
 \tag{7}
\]

For \(D\leq D_0\), exact Einstein epsilon-regularity and
Bishop--Gromov give universal bounds

\[
 |\operatorname{Rm}|\leq C_0,\qquad
 |\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|\leq C_1.
 \tag{8}
\]

In particular \(f\) is uniformly Lipschitz: the model orbit is
parallel-transport invariant, so its distance has Lipschitz constant
at most a fixed multiple of \(C_1\). The derivation of (8), including
the absolute-energy Cheeger--Tian input and derivative estimates, is
in Round 15, Theorem 3. No local lower volume bound was used there.

The new issue is that (7) alone does not control a point in a very
small ball. We first use (8) and relative volume comparison to enter
a fixed algebraic neighborhood of the product orbit, and then use
the exact Einstein equation to obtain the square-root estimate (3).

## 3. A polynomial measuring the product Weyl orbit

For the algebraic calculations retain \(\kappa>0\), put
\(s=-4\kappa\), and let

\[
 \mathscr K_\kappa
 =SO(3)\cdot\operatorname{diag}(-2\kappa/3,\kappa/3,\kappa/3).
\]

For a symmetric trace-free \(3\times3\) operator \(W\), define

\[
 N(W)=W^2+\frac\kappa3W-\frac{2\kappa^2}{9}I.
 \tag{9}
\]

Its zero set on the trace-free subspace is precisely
\(\mathscr K_\kappa\). Indeed, the roots are
\(-2\kappa/3\) and \(\kappa/3\), and trace zero forces their
multiplicities to be one and two.

In a sufficiently small fixed tubular neighborhood of this orbit,

\[
 c_\kappa\operatorname{dist}(W,\mathscr K_\kappa)
 \leq |N(W)|
 \leq C_\kappa\operatorname{dist}(W,\mathscr K_\kappa).
 \tag{10}
\]

To check this directly, diagonalize \(W\), ordering its simple
negative eigenvalue first. Its three eigenvalues remain close to
the corresponding model roots. Each component of \(N\) is the
product of the eigenvalue's displacement from its nearby root and
a factor bounded above and below by positive multiples of
\(\kappa\). The nearest orbit element has the same eigenspaces
and the model roots.

For an Einstein metric, both Weyl blocks are divergence-free. Their
equation, with \(\Delta=\operatorname{tr}\nabla^2\), is

\[
 \delta W^\pm=0,\qquad
 \Delta W^\pm=Q(W^\pm),\qquad
 Q(W)=-2\kappa W-6W^2+2|W|^2I.
 \tag{11}
\]

The Laplacian has nonpositive eigenvalues on a closed manifold.
The product orbit consists of zeros of \(Q\).
The operator identity is also displayed directly in
Biquard--Gauduchon--LeBrun,
[*Gravitational Instantons, Weyl Curvature, and Conformally Kähler
Geometry*, equation (2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.14387).
The full tensor formula and its covariant-derivative version are
given in Catino--Mastrolia,
[*Bochner type formulas for the Weyl tensor on four dimensional
Einstein manifolds*, Lemma 4.1, Proposition 5.1, Theorem 1.1](
https://cvgmt.sns.it/media/doc/paper/3250/Einstein4D_final.pdf).
These are classical inputs, used with the norm convention above.

## 4. Full first-jet coercivity from the divergence equation

The derivative of \(N\) annihilates tangent directions to the model
orbit. It is therefore essential to use all components of
\(\delta W=0\), not just ellipticity for jets of the form
\(\xi\otimes H\).

At

\[
 W_0=\operatorname{diag}(-2\kappa/3,\kappa/3,\kappa/3),
\]

the tangent space consists of symmetric matrices whose only
possibly nonzero entries are \(12,21,13,31\).
On its orthogonal complement in the trace-free symmetric matrices,

\[
 |DN_{W_0}H|=\kappa|H|,\qquad
 DN_W(H)=WH+HW+\frac\kappa3H.
 \tag{12}
\]

Take an arbitrary four-direction tangent jet \(T=(T_i)_{i=1}^4\).
Write its eight free components as
\((x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_4,y_4)\).
Using an orthonormal self-dual basis
\(\omega_a/\sqrt2\), with

\[
 \omega_1=e^{12}+e^{34},\quad
 \omega_2=e^{13}+e^{42},\quad
 \omega_3=e^{14}+e^{23},
\]

its four-index divergence is

\[
 (\mathcal DT)_{jkl}
 =\frac12\sum_{i,a,b}(T_i)_{ab}(\omega_a)_{ij}(\omega_b)_{kl}.
 \tag{13}
\]

The \(64\times8\) matrix in (13) has Gram matrix

\[
 G=\operatorname{diag}(B,B),\qquad
 B=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  2&0&0&1\\
  0&2&-1&0\\
  0&-1&2&0\\
  1&0&0&2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus its Gram eigenvalues are
\(1,1,1,1,3,3,3,3\), whereas
\(|T|^2=2\sum_i(x_i^2+y_i^2)\). Thus

\[
 \boxed{|\mathcal DT|^2\geq\tfrac12|T|^2
       \quad\text{for every tangent jet}.}
 \tag{14}
\]

This exact finite-dimensional identity is independently evaluated
by [the accompanying check](round16_rigidity_check.py).
An orientation-reversing orthogonal map gives the identical result
on \(\Lambda^-\).

For any full jet \(T=T_{\rm tan}+T_{\rm nor}\) with
\(\mathcal DT=0\), the elementary contraction estimate is
\(|\mathcal DT_{\rm nor}|\leq4|T_{\rm nor}|\).
Here four-index conversion has norm factor two, and contraction
of a four-direction jet has norm at most two. Therefore

\[
 |T_{\rm tan}|\leq4\sqrt2|T_{\rm nor}|,\qquad
 |T|\leq\frac{\sqrt{33}}{\kappa}|DN_{W_0}T|.
 \tag{15}
\]

If \(|W-W_0|\leq\kappa/(4\sqrt{33})\), then

\[
 |(DN_W-DN_{W_0})T|\leq2|W-W_0||T|
\]

can be absorbed in (15). Pointwise application to
\(T=\nabla W\), using \(\delta W=0\), proves

\[
 \boxed{|\nabla W|^2
      \leq\frac{132}{\kappa^2}|\nabla N(W)|^2.}
 \tag{16}
\]

The comparison is pointwise. No differentiable choice of a nearest
orbit element is required in this argument.

## 5. A scalar subsolution and a local derivative-energy estimate

On the same tube, the chain rule for the actual matrix polynomial
gives

\[
 \Delta N(W)=DN_W[Q(W)]+2\sum_i(\nabla_iW)^2.
 \tag{17}
\]

Since \(Q\) vanishes on the orbit, (10) and boundedness of \(DN\)
give
\(|DN_WQ(W)|\leq C_\kappa|N(W)|\).
Writing \(u_W=|N(W)|^2\), it follows that

\[
 \Delta u_W
 \geq2|\nabla N(W)|^2-C_\kappa u_W
                  -4|N(W)|\,|\nabla W|^2.
 \tag{18}
\]

Shrink the fixed tube once more. Equation (16) absorbs the last
term, yielding

\[
 \Delta u_W\geq|\nabla N(W)|^2-C_\kappa u_W.
 \tag{19}
\]

For \(u=|N(W^+)|^2+|N(W^-)|^2\), with \(\kappa=3\), this gives

\[
 \Delta u\geq
  |\nabla N(W^+)|^2+|\nabla N(W^-)|^2-Cu.
 \tag{20}
\]

If the tube condition holds on \(B_1(p)\), choose a Lipschitz
cutoff supported there, equal to one on \(B_{3/4}(p)\), with
uniformly bounded gradient. Multiply (20) by its square and
integrate by parts. The inequality

\[
 |\nabla u|\leq2\sqrt u\,
       (|\nabla N(W^+)|^2+|\nabla N(W^-)|^2)^{1/2}
\]

and Young's inequality absorb half the derivative term. By (16),

\[
 \int_{B_{3/4}(p)}|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|^2
 \leq C\int_{B_1(p)}u\leq CD.
 \tag{21}
\]

The last step uses (7), (10), and the exact Einstein curvature
decomposition. This argument requires only first derivatives of
the distance cutoff, not an injectivity radius or a smooth radial
coordinate system.

The Einstein derivative Bochner identity and the global curvature
bound (8) also give, for \(z=|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|^2\),

\[
 \Delta z\geq-Cz.
 \tag{22}
\]

For example, the cited Catino--Mastrolia Theorem 1.1 has
\(\tfrac12\Delta|\nabla W|^2=
|\nabla^2W|^2+\tfrac{13}{12}s|\nabla W|^2
-10W_{ijkl}W_{ijpq,t}W_{klpq,t}\).
Bound the cubic term by \(C|W||\nabla W|^2\);
\(\nabla\operatorname{Rm}=\nabla W\) because the metric is Einstein.

## 6. Entering the tube and proving the relative estimate

Fix the tube radius \(a_0>0\) required above, and let \(L\) be a
universal Lipschitz constant for \(f\) from (8). Suppose some
\(y\in B_1(p)\) has \(f(y)\geq a_0\). Set

\[
 r_0=\min\{1/2,a_0/[2(L+1)]\}.
\]

Then \(f\geq a_0/2\) on \(B_{r_0}(y)\).
Since \(B_1(p)\subset B_2(y)\), relative Bishop--Gromov gives

\[
 \operatorname{Vol}B_{r_0}(y)
 \geq\frac{\mathcal V_{-1}(r_0)}{\mathcal V_{-1}(2)}
                  \operatorname{Vol}B_2(y)
 \geq c(a_0)v(p),
 \tag{23}
\]

where \(\mathcal V_{-1}\) is the ball volume in hyperbolic
four-space of sectional curvature \(-1\).
Combining with (7) contradicts \(D/v(p)\leq\varepsilon_0\)
when \(\varepsilon_0\) is sufficiently small.
Consequently the entire ball \(B_1(p)\) lies in the required tube.
This preliminary step uses only the already-established derivative
bound, and so does not assume the desired refined estimate.

The needed analytic input is the **normalized** local mean-value
inequality: under a fixed Ricci lower bound, a nonnegative function
satisfying \(\Delta h\geq-Ch\) on a fixed-radius ball obeys

\[
 \sup_{B_{r/2}(p)}h
 \leq\frac{C'}{\operatorname{Vol}B_r(p)}
                          \int_{B_r(p)}h.
 \tag{24}
\]

No lower volume assumption occurs. This follows from the local
subharmonic mean-value inequality of Li--Schoen,
[*\(L^p\) and mean value properties of subharmonic functions on
Riemannian manifolds*, Theorem 2.1 and equation (2.3)](
https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/6362-11511_2006_Article_BF02392380.pdf).
For the zeroth-order term, apply their subharmonic estimate on
\(M\times\mathbb R\) to \(h(q)e^{\sqrt C\,t}\). Its Laplacian is
nonnegative. Product-ball volumes are comparable to
\(r\,\operatorname{Vol}B_r(p)\), by inclusions and
Bishop--Gromov; integration over the real coordinate costs only a
fixed factor. Use smaller concentric balls if needed to supply the
buffer in the local form of their theorem.

Apply (24) first to \(u\), using (20), and then to \(z\) on a
smaller ball, using (21)--(22). Bishop--Gromov compares every
fixed-fraction concentric ball volume with \(v(p)\). This proves

\[
 \sup_{B_{1/4}(p)}(u+z)\leq C D/v(p).
\]

Equation (10), the two Weyl blocks, and zero trace-free Ricci
complete the proof of (3).

## 7. Distance, volume, and the remaining thin region

For \(d=d_g(x,p)\), the inclusion \(B_1(x)\subset B_{d+1}(p)\)
and Bishop--Gromov give

\[
 v(p)\geq
 v_0(M)\frac{\mathcal V_{-1}(1)}{\mathcal V_{-1}(d+1)}
 \geq c_M e^{-3d}.
 \tag{25}
\]

Substitution in (3) proves (4)--(5). If the defect at \(p\) is
at least \(\epsilon\), either \(D/v(p)>\varepsilon_0\), or (3)

# Scalar-energy stability on the original metric

## 1. Question and result

The product-boundary estimate in [Round 15](round15_rigidity.md) assumed
constant negative scalar curvature. That hypothesis supplied a pointwise
bound for the monopole spinor; it was not a harmless normalization of an
arbitrary metric. Here it is removed. A smooth radial truncation gives a
bounded global two-form while retaining its first-order energy and its
relation to an actual Chern class. Hodge theory then controls the Weyl
profile without a conformal change, a scalar-curvature upper bound, a
Sobolev constant, or a geometric compactness assumption.

This concerns a **realizable** calibration: negative Kähler--Einstein
geometry in one orientation, and the product of two hyperbolic surfaces
in two orientations. It is different from the forbidden calibration at
topological ratio \(2/3\) in [Round 4](round4_rigidity.md) and
[Round 5](round5_rigidity.md).

Let \(g\) be any smooth metric on a closed connected oriented
four-manifold \(M\).
Assume a specified spin-c structure with first Chern class \(a\) has an
actual unperturbed Seiberg--Witten solution for this metric, and write

\[
 A=a^2>0,\qquad T=2\chi(M)+3\tau(M),\qquad V=\operatorname{Vol}(M,g).
 \tag{1}
\]

An actual monopole class supplies this hypothesis for every metric.
A formal cohomology class or a convex combination of classes does not
supply the spinor used below. For any constant \(\kappa>0\), define

\[
 B=\int_M s^2\,d\mu-32\pi^2 A,\qquad
 P=\int_M|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2\,d\mu,\qquad
 L=\int_M(s+4\kappa)^2\,d\mu.
 \tag{2}
\]

Two-form norms are exterior-algebra norms. Weyl and curvature operators
use Hilbert--Schmidt norms on two-forms; Ricci uses the ordinary
two-tensor norm. Let \(\mathscr K_\kappa\) be the Weyl-operator orbit with
eigenvalues \((-2\kappa/3,\kappa/3,\kappa/3)\).

**Theorem 1 (arbitrary-scalar calibration).** One has \(B\ge0\).
There is a globally smooth self-dual two-form \(\varphi\) such that

\[
 \begin{split}
 |\varphi|&\le8\kappa,\\
 \int|\nabla\varphi|^2&\le4\kappa B,\\
 \int\bigl(|\varphi|-4\kappa\bigr)^2&\le2(B+L).
 \end{split}
 \tag{3}
\]

If \(\mathcal F=iF_{\mathfrak A}\) is the real determinant curvature
of the monopole solution, with the quadratic-map sign chosen so that
\(\mathcal F^+=\psi/(2\sqrt2)\), then

\[
 d\mathcal F=0,\qquad [\mathcal F/(2\pi)]=a,\qquad
 \left\|\mathcal F-\frac{\varphi}{2\sqrt2}\right\|_2^2
 \le\frac38B+\frac14L .
 \tag{4}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{\;
 \int\operatorname{dist}_{\rm HS}(W^+,\mathscr K_\kappa)^2
 \le\frac12P+\frac{39}{8}B+\frac{14}{3}L
                  +4\pi^2(T-A)_+ .
 \;}
 \tag{5}
\]

The topological remainder is absent whenever \(a^2\ge2\chi+3\tau\).
In particular, \(a^2=T>0\) is sufficient, but is not needed for the
exact identity in Section 4.

All constants are absolute. These are integral estimates on the
original metric; they do not assert pointwise curvature bounds, a
nowhere-zero \(\varphi\), or that \(\varphi\) is closed.

## 2. The scalar defect controls the actual connection

Use the conventions in [Round 3](round3_monopole_input.md). For the
solution \((\Phi,\mathfrak A)\), set

\[
 \rho=|\Phi|^2,\qquad
 \psi=-2\sqrt2\,\sigma(\Phi),\qquad
 |\psi|=\rho,\qquad
 K=\int|\nabla_{\mathfrak A}\Phi|^2.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus \(-iF_{\mathfrak A}^+=\sigma(\Phi)\), as in Round 3, and
\(\mathcal F^+=\psi/(2\sqrt2)\). The sign of \(\psi\) is immaterial
to its norm, derivative estimates, and quadratic Weyl surrogate.

The integrated spinor Weitzenböck formula and the determinant
Chern--Weil formula are

\[
 4K+\int s\rho+\int\rho^2=0,\qquad
 \int\rho^2=32\pi^2 A+8\|\mathcal F^-\|_2^2.
 \tag{7}
\]

Expanding \((s+\rho)^2\) proves the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{\;
 B=\|s+\rho\|_2^2+8K+8\|\mathcal F^-\|_2^2.
 \;}
 \tag{8}
\]

Consequently \(B\ge0\), \(K\le B/8\), and
\(\|\mathcal F^-\|_2^2\le B/8\). In addition,

\[
 \|\rho-4\kappa\|_2^2
 =\|(s+\rho)-(s+4\kappa)\|_2^2\le2(B+L).
 \tag{9}
\]

The use of the *full* determinant connection matters: (8) controls its
anti-self-dual curvature, not just the harmonic anti-self-dual
projection of \(a\). No derivative of scalar curvature is involved.

The Seiberg--Witten equations, the scalar-energy lower bound, and the
negative Kähler--Einstein equality case are classical inputs. See
LeBrun, [*Ricci Curvature, Minimal Volumes, and Seiberg--Witten
Theory*, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0003068), and
[*Four-Manifolds, Curvature Bounds, and Convex Geometry*,
Proposition 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0611450).
Identity (8) keeps all the nonnegative losses needed for stability.

## 3. A bounded smooth calibration with controlled derivative

A scalar \(L^2\) estimate does not bound \(\rho\) pointwise. Directly
using the quartic Weyl surrogate of \(\psi\) would therefore require
an uncontrolled \(\int\rho^4\). The following truncation avoids it.

Choose once and for all a smooth function
\(\chi:\mathbb R\to[0,1]\), equal to \(1\) on \((-\infty,0]\) and
to \(0\) on \([1,\infty)\). Define, for \(r\ge0\),

\[
 \beta_\kappa(r)=
 \int_0^r\chi\!\left(\frac{t-4\kappa}{4\kappa}\right)\,dt.
 \tag{10}
\]

Then

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \beta_\kappa(r)=r\quad(0\le r\le4\kappa),\qquad
 0\le\beta_\kappa'\le1,\qquad
 \beta_\kappa'=0\quad(r\ge8\kappa),\\
 0\le\beta_\kappa(r)\le\min(r,8\kappa),\qquad
 \beta_\kappa(r)\ge4\kappa\quad(r\ge4\kappa).
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{11}
\]

Put

\[
 \varphi=\frac{\beta_\kappa(\rho)}{\rho}\,\psi ,
 \tag{12}
\]

using multiplier \(1\) near \(\rho=0\). This is globally smooth:
\(\rho=|\Phi|^2\) is smooth, and the multiplier is identically \(1\)
on a neighborhood of its only potentially singular denominator.
Write \(\beta=\beta_\kappa(\rho)=|\varphi|\).

The inequalities
\[
 |\beta-4\kappa|\le|\rho-4\kappa|,\qquad
 |\rho-\beta|\le|\rho-4\kappa|
 \tag{13}
\]
follow separately on \(r\le4\kappa\) and \(r\ge4\kappa\).
They prove the amplitude part of (3) and give
\(\|\psi-\varphi\|_2^2\le2(B+L)\).
Because the two terms in
\[
 \mathcal F-\frac{\varphi}{2\sqrt2}
 =\mathcal F^-+\frac{\psi-\varphi}{2\sqrt2}
\]
have opposite duality, their squared norms add. Equations (8)--(9)
now prove (4).

For the derivative, the quadratic spinor map satisfies

\[
 |\nabla\psi|^2\le4\rho|\nabla_{\mathfrak A}\Phi|^2.
 \tag{14}
\]

This is the differential estimate for the Hopf map with the
determinant gauge direction retained. On the set \(\rho>0\), write
\(\psi=\rho n\), \(|n|=1\). Orthogonality yields

\[
 \begin{split}
 |\nabla\psi|^2&=|d\rho|^2+\rho^2|\nabla n|^2,\\
 |\nabla\varphi|^2
 &=\beta_\kappa'(\rho)^2|d\rho|^2
                       +\beta_\kappa(\rho)^2|\nabla n|^2.
 \end{split}
 \tag{15}
\]

Both coefficients after multiplication by \(\rho\) are bounded:

\[
 \rho\,\beta_\kappa'(\rho)^2\le8\kappa,\qquad
 \frac{\beta_\kappa(\rho)^2}{\rho}
                  \le\beta_\kappa(\rho)\le8\kappa.
 \tag{16}
\]

Combining (14)--(16) gives
\[
 |\nabla\varphi|^2
 \le32\kappa|\nabla_{\mathfrak A}\Phi|^2 .
 \tag{17}
\]
At spinor zeros this holds by smooth continuation. Integrating and
using \(K\le B/8\) proves the derivative bound in (3).
There is no derivative bound on \(s\), no maximum principle with a
uniform scalar minimum, and no rescaling-weight derivative hidden
in this argument.

## 4. An exact Hodge--Chern--Weil identity

Define the smooth trace-free symmetric endomorphism of \(\Lambda^+\)

\[
 \mathsf A_\varphi=
 \frac{\beta^2}{48\kappa}I-\frac{\varphi\otimes\varphi}{16\kappa},
 \qquad \mathsf R=W^+-\mathsf A_\varphi.
 \tag{18}
\]

Its squared norm is \(\beta^4/(384\kappa^2)\). The self-dual Hodge
formula and Chern--Weil identity read

\[
 2\|d\varphi\|_2^2
 =\|\nabla\varphi\|_2^2+\frac13\int s\beta^2
                       -2\int\langle W^+\varphi,\varphi\rangle ,
 \tag{19}
\]
\[
 \|W^+\|_2^2=2\pi^2T-\frac1{48}\int s^2+\frac14P.
 \tag{20}
\]

Expanding (18), using trace-freeness, and substituting (19)--(20)
gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{split}
 \|\mathsf R\|_2^2+\frac1{8\kappa}\|d\varphi\|_2^2
 ={}&2\pi^2(T-A)+\frac14P-\frac1{16}B
                    +\frac1{16\kappa}\|\nabla\varphi\|_2^2\\
 &+\frac1{384\kappa^2}
                       \int(\beta^2+4\kappa s)^2 .
 \end{split}}
 \tag{21}
\]

This identity holds before imposing \(A\ge T\), and does not discard
the exterior-derivative term. In particular, the topology cannot be
silently removed when a class of the wrong square is substituted.

To estimate its last term, note that \(\beta\le8\kappa\) and (3) give

\[
 \begin{split}
 \int(\beta^2-16\kappa^2)^2
 &\le144\kappa^2\int(\beta-4\kappa)^2\\
 &\le288\kappa^2(B+L).
 \end{split}
 \tag{22}
\]

Since
\(\beta^2+4\kappa s=(\beta^2-16\kappa^2)+4\kappa(s+4\kappa)\),

\[
 \int(\beta^2+4\kappa s)^2
 \le576\kappa^2B+608\kappa^2L.
 \tag{23}
\]

Thus (21) implies the useful intermediate estimate

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathsf R\|_2^2+\frac1{8\kappa}\|d\varphi\|_2^2
 \le2\pi^2(T-A)+\frac14P+\frac{27}{16}B+\frac{19}{12}L.}
 \tag{24}
\]

Where \(\varphi\ne0\), choose the element of
\(\mathscr K_\kappa\) whose simple eigenspace is
\(\mathbb R\varphi\). Its squared distance from
\(\mathsf A_\varphi\) is

\[
 \frac{(\beta^2-16\kappa^2)^2}{384\kappa^2}.
 \tag{25}
\]

At \(\varphi=0\) the same distance holds for every choice of line.
The pointwise triangle inequality squared, (22), and (24) give

\[
 \int\operatorname{dist}(W^+,\mathscr K_\kappa)^2
 \le4\pi^2(T-A)+\frac12P+\frac{39}{8}B+\frac{14}{3}L.
 \tag{26}
\]

Replacing the signed topological term by its positive part proves
(5). Keeping the signed version (26) is sometimes sharper.
This completes the proof of Theorem 1.

## 5. Two orientations: the full product curvature profile

**Theorem 2.** Suppose \(\tau(M)=0\), \(A=2\chi(M)>0\), and the
two orientations each have a specified actual monopole class of
square \(A\). For every smooth metric and every \(\kappa>0\), let
\(B,P,L\) be as in (2). If \(\mathscr P_\kappa\) is the full
curvature-operator orbit of
\(\mathbb H^2_{-\kappa}\times\mathbb H^2_{-\kappa}\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 \int\operatorname{dist}_{\rm HS}
                (\mathcal R_g,\mathscr P_\kappa)^2
 \le\frac32P+\frac{39}{4}B+\frac{75}{8}L.}
 \tag{27}
\]

There are two globally smooth forms
\(\varphi_\pm\in\Gamma(\Lambda^\pm)\), each satisfying (3)--(4)
with its corresponding actual determinant connection.

**Proof.** Apply Theorem 1 in both orientations. The curvature
operator decomposes as

\[
 \mathcal R=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 W^++sI/12 & \operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}_{\Lambda}\\
 (\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}_{\Lambda})^* & W^-+sI/12
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{28}
\]

The two off-diagonal blocks have total squared norm
\(|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2/2\). The scalar displacement from
\(-4\kappa\) has squared norm \((s+4\kappa)^2/24\); this is
orthogonal to the two trace-free Weyl displacements. The action
\(\mathrm{SO}(4)\to\mathrm{SO}(3)\times\mathrm{SO}(3)\) on the
two Weyl blocks is onto. Consequently their two orbit minimizations
can be realized simultaneously by a product curvature operator.
Adding the two instances of (5), \(P/2\), and \(L/24\) proves (27).
\(\square\)

This hypothesis is not a shear-specific geometric ansatz. Products
of hyperbolic surfaces supply exact equality tests. The closed shear
examples are additional applications once their two actual classes
have been established by [Round 12](round12_global.md); a new
manifold must supply its own class-existence argument.

## 6. Mean-scalar normalization and the Yau26 input

Suppose only that the mean scalar curvature is negative, and choose

\[
 \kappa=-\frac1{4V}\int s,\qquad
 D=\kappa^2V-2\pi^2A .
 \tag{29}
\]

Then \(L=\int(s-\fint s)^2\) is the scalar variance and

\[
 \boxed{B=16D+L\ge0.}
 \tag{30}
\]

Unlike in the constant-scalar theorem, \(D\) need not be nonnegative:
the exact restriction is \(D\ge-L/16\). Substituting (30) in (27)
gives

\[
 \int\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal R,\mathscr P_\kappa)^2
 \le\frac32P+156D+\frac{153}{8}L
 \le\frac32P+156D_++\frac{153}{8}L.
 \tag{31}
\]

Thus any sequence in this actual-monopole category with

\[
 \frac{P+L+D_+}{\kappa^2V}\longrightarrow0
 \tag{32}
\]

has a relative \(L^2\) product-curvature profile on its **original**
metrics. At the same time,
\(\int|\nabla\varphi_\pm|^2/(\kappa^3V)\to0\), their amplitudes
approach \(4\kappa\) in normalized \(L^2\), and (4) retains their
actual topological representatives. No conformal scalar-curvature
repair or pullback from another metric occurs.

The pointwise near-Einstein input directly controls two errors:
if
\(\delta_\kappa=\|g^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}+\kappa I\|_{\infty,\rm op}\),
then

\[
 P+\frac14L
 =\int|\operatorname{Ric}+\kappa g|^2
 \le4\delta_\kappa^2 V
 \tag{33}
\]

whenever \(L\) is measured against this same \(\kappa\). A
near-Einstein sequence whose normalized volume also approaches the
sharp scalar-energy value therefore satisfies the hypotheses of
(32). If one first fixes the input normalization rather than the
mean normalization, the difference between the two \(\kappa\)'s is
at most \(\delta_\kappa\).

This is a necessary geometric structure theorem near a realizable
boundary, not an Einstein-existence theorem. Negative product
Einstein metrics meet all estimates with zero right side. In
particular, this calibration cannot itself furnish the contradiction
used at the forbidden \(2/3\) boundary.

## 7. What topological and spectral information survives

The cap does not merely produce a small vector in an unrelated
bundle. It produces a bounded almost-parallel self-dual form close
to the curvature of an actual line bundle. Two elementary
consequences make that content explicit.

First,
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}\{|\varphi|<2\kappa\}
 \le\frac{B+L}{2\kappa^2}.
 \tag{34}
\]
This gives an amplitude-exceptional volume estimate, not a
pointwise nonvanishing theorem.

Second, let \(\mu_+(g)\) be the bottom eigenvalue of the connection

\(4\kappa\sqrt V>\sqrt{2(B+L)}\), its Rayleigh principle and (3)

\[
 \mu_+(g)\le
 \frac{4\kappa B}
 {\bigl(4\kappa\sqrt V-\sqrt{2(B+L)}\bigr)^2}.
 \tag{35}
\]

For example, if \(B+L\le2\kappa^2V\), then
\(\mu_+(g)\le B/(\kappa V)\).
The opposite-orientation conclusion is analogous. No uniform lower

Kähler--Einstein equality case. In the two-orientation category,
both chiral parallel forms split the tangent bundle into parallel
two-planes, giving the local product model. This is an equality
check, not a new classification theorem.

# Actual monopole intersections and global phase mixing

## 1. A global consequence of the bounded calibration

The bounded forms of [Round 17](round17_integral.md) approximate
curvatures of actual determinant line bundles. This permits intersection
pairings to be computed by global \(L^2\) wedge products, without
restricting forms to collapsing cycles. A common Weyl tensor forces all
these forms into approximately the same *unoriented* line. The signs may
vary over the manifold; their joint distribution, however, is constrained
by the intersection form.

This note proves two consequences:

1. the normalized intersection matrix approaches the convex hull of
   rank-one sign matrices;
2. nonextremal intersections force small scalar-Laplacian
   eigenvalues, with their multiplicity controlled by an intersection
   covariance matrix, through globally smooth phase observables.

The second conclusion is a quantitative global cost of phase mixing,
not a claim that the phase cannot switch. It is valid on the original
metric and needs neither noncollapse nor a lower spectral bound.

### Assumptions and normalized errors

Let \(M\) be a closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold.
For a smooth metric \(g\), suppose the specified classes
\(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) have actual unperturbed Seiberg--Witten solutions,
and

\[
 a_i^2=A>0,\qquad A\ge T:=2\chi(M)+3\tau(M).
 \tag{1}
\]

Actual monopole classes make this assumption available for all metrics.
The classes need not have nonzero ordinary Seiberg--Witten invariants.
No sum or difference of them is assumed to be a monopole.

Suppose the mean scalar curvature is negative and set

\[
 \kappa=-\frac1{4V}\int s>0,\qquad
 B=\int s^2-32\pi^2 A,\quad
 P=\int|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2,\quad
 L=\int(s+4\kappa)^2,
 \tag{2}
\]
\[
 b=\frac{B}{\kappa^2V},\quad
 p=\frac{P}{\kappa^2V},\quad
 \ell=\frac{L}{\kappa^2V},\quad
 \epsilon=b+p+\ell,\quad
 r=\frac{2\pi^2 A}{\kappa^2V}.
 \tag{3}
\]

Round 17 gives \(B\ge0\). The mean-scalar normalization gives exactly

\[
 r=1+\frac{\ell-b}{16}.
 \tag{4}
\]

All estimates below assume \(\epsilon\le1\); thus
\(15/16\le r\le17/16\).
Write \(\mathbb E f=V^{-1}\int_M f\,d\mu\).
Let \(G\) denote the metric-independent intersection matrix

\[
 G_{ij}=\frac{a_i\cdot a_j}{A},\qquad G_{ii}=1.
 \tag{5}
\]

The constants are not optimized.

## 2. The joint intersection theorem

Define the finite sign-correlation polytope

\[
 \mathcal C_m
 =\operatorname{conv}\{zz^{\mathsf T}:z\in\{-1,1\}^m\}.
 \tag{6}
\]

This definition, rather than any classification of its facets, is all
that is used.

**Theorem 1.** Under (1)--(3), there is \(C\in\mathcal C_m\) such that

\[
 \boxed{\max_{i,j}|G_{ij}-C_{ij}|\le24\sqrt\epsilon.}
 \tag{7}
\]

The constant is independent of the number of classes. More concretely,
there are measurable signs \(\sigma_i:M\to\{-1,1\}\), with
\(\sigma_1=1\), for which

\[
 C_{ij}=\mathbb E(\sigma_i\sigma_j).
 \tag{8}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \left|
 \frac{\operatorname{Vol}\{\sigma_i\sigma_j=1\}}{V}
            -\frac{1+G_{ij}}2
 \right|\le12\sqrt\epsilon.
 \tag{9}
\]

The signs are used only measurably. Their derivatives, the
orientability of a Weyl eigenline bundle, and continuity of a global
unit eigenvector are not assumptions.

### Consequence: integral odd-sum constraints

For \(n=(n_1,\ldots,n_m)\in\mathbb Z^m\) with \(\sum_i n_i\) odd,
every sign vector satisfies \((n\cdot z)^2\ge1\). Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{(\sum_i n_i a_i)^2}{A}
 \ge1-24\|n\|_1^2\sqrt\epsilon.}
 \tag{10}
\]

If the left side is \(1-\eta<1\), every metric covered by (1)--(3)
has

\[
 \epsilon\ge
 \min\!\left\{1,\frac{\eta^2}{576\|n\|_1^4}\right\}.
 \tag{11}
\]

This is an intersection criterion, not an assertion that a particular
violating lattice configuration is realized by monopoles.

## 3. Proof: from actual curvature to common measurable signs

Apply Round 17 separately to each actual solution. Let \(\varphi_i\)
be its smooth capped self-dual form and let
\(\mathcal F_i=iF_{\mathfrak A_i}\), so
\([\mathcal F_i/(2\pi)]=a_i\).
Normalize

\[
 u_i=\frac{\varphi_i}{4\kappa},\qquad
 f_i=\frac{\mathcal F_i}{\sqrt2\,\kappa},\qquad
 Q(u)=u\otimes u-\frac{|u|^2}{3}I.
 \tag{12}
\]

The following estimates follow directly from Round 17:

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 |u_i|\le2,\qquad
 \mathbb E(|u_i|-1)^2\le\alpha:=\frac{b+\ell}{8},\\
 \mathbb E|\nabla u_i|^2\le\frac{\kappa b}{4},\qquad
 \mathbb E|f_i-u_i|^2
       \le z_0:=\frac{3b}{16}+\frac{\ell}{8},\\
 \mathbb E|W^+/\kappa+Q(u_i)|^2
       \le e_0:=\frac p4+\frac{27b}{16}+\frac{19\ell}{12}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{13}
\]

The last inequality uses \(T-A\le0\) in the exact capped
Hodge--Chern--Weil identity, not equality of these numbers.
Also, the exact determinant-curvature formula gives

\[
 \mathbb E|f_i|^2
 =r+\frac{\|\mathcal F_i^-\|_2^2}{\kappa^2V}
 \le r+\frac b8\le\frac{19}{16}.
 \tag{14}
\]

In particular all the full two-forms \(f_i\), including their
anti-self-dual parts, have a controlled global norm.

Choose a measurable unit vector \(v_i\) along \(u_i\) where it is
nonzero and any measurable unit vector at its zeros. Such a
measurable choice in \(\Lambda^+\) exists using finitely many local
trivializations. Then

\[
 \mathbb E|u_i-v_i|^2\le\alpha,\qquad
 \mathbb E|Q(u_i)-Q(v_i)|^2\le6\alpha.
 \tag{15}
\]

Indeed \(Q(u_i)=|u_i|^2Q(v_i)\),
\(|Q(v_i)|^2=2/3\), and \(|u_i|+1\le3\).
The common tensor \(W^+\) in (13) gives

\[
 \mathbb E|Q(u_i)-Q(u_1)|^2\le4e_0.
 \tag{16}
\]

Combining the three differences in (15)--(16),

\[
 \mathbb E|Q(v_i)-Q(v_1)|^2\le36\alpha+12e_0.
 \tag{17}
\]

For unit vectors \(v,w\),

\[
 |Q(v)-Q(w)|^2=2(1-\langle v,w\rangle^2),\qquad
 \min_{\sigma=\pm1}|v-\sigma w|^2
       =2(1-|\langle v,w\rangle|)
       \le|Q(v)-Q(w)|^2.
 \tag{18}
\]

Set \(n=v_1\), and choose \(\sigma_i\) by this minimum, resolving
zero scalar products measurably. Take \(\sigma_1=1\). Equations
(15)--(18) imply

\[
 \mathbb E|u_i-\sigma_i n|^2
 \le74\alpha+24e_0
 \le50\epsilon .
 \tag{19}
\]

This treats low-amplitude and zero sets by their amplitude cost,
not by deleting them without an error estimate.

The full determinant curvatures satisfy

\[
 \mathbb E|f_i-\sigma_i n|^2
 \le2z_0+100\epsilon\le101\epsilon .
 \tag{20}
\]

Using the Hodge star and the actual Chern classes,

\[
 \mathbb E\langle f_i,*f_j\rangle
 =\frac1V\int f_i\wedge f_j
 =\frac{2\pi^2}{\kappa^2V}\,a_i\cdot a_j
 =rG_{ij}.
 \tag{21}
\]

Because \(n\) is self-dual, (14), (20), and Cauchy--Schwarz yield

\[
 |rG_{ij}-\mathbb E(\sigma_i\sigma_j)|
 \le\sqrt{101\epsilon}
                 \left(\sqrt{19/16}+1\right)
 \le22\sqrt\epsilon .
 \tag{22}
\]

The distribution of the finite-valued vector
\((\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_m)\) under normalized volume defines
\(C\in\mathcal C_m\). Since \(|C_{ij}|\le1\), (4) gives

\[
 |G_{ij}-C_{ij}|
 \le\frac{22\sqrt\epsilon+\epsilon/16}{15/16}
 \le24\sqrt\epsilon .
 \tag{23}
\]

This proves Theorem 1. Pairing with \(nn^{\mathsf T}\) proves (10).
For (11), if \(\epsilon\le1\), rearrange (10); if
\(\epsilon>1\), the displayed minimum is automatic.

## 4. A smooth observable makes switching cost measurable

Projective alignment alone does not stop signs occupying different
large regions. Its new differential consequence comes from the
*smooth* function

\[
 h_{ij}=\langle u_i,u_j\rangle.
 \tag{24}
\]

Let \(\lambda_1(g)>0\) be the first positive eigenvalue of the scalar
Laplacian on the connected closed manifold, and let
\(q=G_{ij}\) with \(|q|\le1\).

**Theorem 2 (intersection-forced small eigenvalue).**

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\lambda_1(g)}{\kappa}
       \bigl(1-q^2-64\sqrt\epsilon\bigr)_+
 \le4b .}
 \tag{25}
\]

In particular, if \(|q|<1\) and
\(\epsilon\le(1-q^2)^2/128^2\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\lambda_1(g)}{\kappa}
 \le\frac{8}{1-q^2}\,
       \frac{\int s^2-32\pi^2A}{\kappa^2V}.}
 \tag{26}
\]

Thus normalized scalar-energy saturation with uniformly nonextremal
intersection forces the scalar spectral gap to vanish. A lower
spectral bound is a possible additional obstruction, not a hidden
premise of the theorem.

**Proof.** The bounds \(|u_i|,|u_j|\le2\) and (13) give

\[
 |dh_{ij}|^2
 \le8(|\nabla u_i|^2+|\nabla u_j|^2),\qquad
 \mathbb E|dh_{ij}|^2\le4\kappa b.
 \tag{27}
\]

Equation (19) and the triangle inequality in normalized \(L^2\) give

\[
 \|h_{ij}-\sigma_i\sigma_j\|_{L^2(d\mu/V)}
 \le3\sqrt{50\epsilon}.
 \tag{28}
\]

In particular,
\(\mathbb E h_{ij}^2\ge1-2\sqrt{450\epsilon}\).
The comparison with the actual intersection, using (13)--(14)
without the measurable signs, is

\[
 |\mathbb E h_{ij}-rq|
 \le\sqrt{z_0}\left(\sqrt{19/16}+2\right)
 \le2\sqrt\epsilon .
 \tag{29}
\]

Since \(|q|\le1\), (4) implies
\(|\mathbb E h_{ij}-q|\le3\sqrt\epsilon\).
Also \(|h_{ij}|\le4\); hence
\[
 |(\mathbb E h_{ij})^2-q^2|\le15\sqrt\epsilon .
 \tag{30}
\]
It follows that
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(h_{ij})
 \ge1-q^2-(2\sqrt{450}+15)\sqrt\epsilon
 \ge1-q^2-64\sqrt\epsilon.
 \tag{31}
\]

The variational definition of \(\lambda_1\), applied to the globally
smooth function \(h_{ij}-\mathbb E h_{ij}\), now gives (25).
There is no differentiation of \(n\), \(v_i\), or \(\sigma_i\).
Equation (26) follows by absorbing half of \(1-q^2\). \(\square\)

A useful equivalent consequence is that if
\(\lambda_1(g)/\kappa\ge\lambda_*>0\) and \(|q|<1\), then

\[
 \epsilon\ge
 \min\left\{1,\frac{(1-q^2)^2}{128^2},
                     \frac{\lambda_*(1-q^2)}8\right\}.
 \tag{32}
\]

The spectral input in (32) must be proved for the metrics in question;
it is not inferred from non-virtual-Kähler topology.

### Several independent phase modes

Relabel a collection of \(n+1\) actual classes as
\(a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_n\), retaining (1)--(5). Set
\[
 \Sigma_{ij}=G_{ij}-G_{0i}G_{0j}\qquad(1\le i,j\le n).
 \tag{32a}
\]
This is a specified topological matrix. At finite error it is not
assumed positive semidefinite.

**Theorem 3 (intersection-forced spectral cluster).** Suppose
\(\Sigma\) has at least \(r_0\) eigenvalues at least \(s_0>0\).
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\lambda_{r_0}(g)}{\kappa}
          (s_0-160n\sqrt\epsilon)_+\le4nb .}
 \tag{32b}
\]
In particular, if \(\epsilon\le(s_0/(320n))^2\), then
\[
 \boxed{\frac{\lambda_{r_0}(g)}{\kappa}\le\frac{8n}{s_0}b.}
 \tag{32c}
\]
Here \(\lambda_j\) are the positive scalar-Laplacian eigenvalues,
counted with multiplicity.

**Proof.** Let \(h_i=\langle u_0,u_i\rangle\). These are globally
smooth. Take the common signs from Theorem 1 with \(\sigma_0=1\).
Equations (27)--(29) apply to every \(h_i\), giving
\[
 \mathbb E|dh_i|^2\le4\kappa b,\qquad
 \|h_i-\sigma_i\|_{L^2(d\mu/V)}\le\sqrt{450\epsilon}.
 \tag{32d}
\]
In addition, (14) and (21) imply
\[
 |G_{ij}|\le1+\frac{b}{8r}\le\frac{17}{15}.
 \tag{32e}
\]
The proof of (29), (4), and (32e) give
\(|\mathbb Eh_i-G_{0i}|\le3\sqrt\epsilon\) without the extra
assumption \(|G_{0i}|\le1\).
Since \(|h_i|\le4\) and \(|\sigma_i|=1\), (32d) yields
\[
 |\mathbb E(h_ih_j)-\mathbb E(\sigma_i\sigma_j)|
        \le5\sqrt{450\epsilon}.
 \tag{32f}
\]
Using (7), and then comparing the products of the means, gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 |\mathbb E(h_ih_j)-G_{ij}|
       &\le(5\sqrt{450}+24)\sqrt\epsilon,\\
 |(\mathbb Eh_i)(\mathbb Eh_j)-G_{0i}G_{0j}|
       &\le16\sqrt\epsilon.
 \end{split}
 \tag{32g}
\]
Thus the actual covariance matrix \(\Gamma\) of the smooth
functions \(h_i\) satisfies
\[
 \max_{ij}|\Gamma_{ij}-\Sigma_{ij}|
      \le160\sqrt\epsilon,\qquad
 \|\Gamma-\Sigma\|_{\rm op}\le160n\sqrt\epsilon.
 \tag{32h}
\]

Let \(E\subset\mathbb R^n\) be an \(r_0\)-dimensional spectral
subspace of \(\Sigma\) on which \(\Sigma\ge s_0I\).
For \(t\in E\), set
\(H_t=\sum_i t_i(h_i-\mathbb E h_i)\).
If \(s_0-160n\sqrt\epsilon>0\), then
\[
 \mathbb E H_t^2\ge(s_0-160n\sqrt\epsilon)|t|^2,\qquad
 \mathbb E|dH_t|^2\le4n\kappa b\,|t|^2.
 \tag{32i}
\]
The first estimate makes \(t\mapsto H_t\) injective. Its image is
therefore an \(r_0\)-dimensional space of mean-zero smooth
functions. The scalar min--max principle proves (32b); when the
positive part vanishes, (32b) is automatic. Equation (32c) follows.
\(\square\)

This conclusion counts genuine scalar low modes. It is stronger
than an upper bound on a connection-Laplacian eigenvalue of a
two-form: the test functions are obtained from pairings of
different actual classes and have topologically forced variance.

## 5. Actual connected-sum application outside the shear category

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be compact almost-complex four-manifolds with
\(b_1=0\), \(b_+\equiv3\pmod4\), odd canonical Seiberg--Witten
invariant, and positive canonical squares \(A_X,A_Y\).
The Bauer--Furuta connected-sum theorem gives actual monopoles

\[
 a=c_1(X)+c_1(Y),\qquad
 c=c_1(X)-c_1(Y)
 \tag{33}
\]

on \(M=X\#Y\). Their squares and pairing are

\[
 A=A_X+A_Y,\quad T=A-4,\quad
 q=\frac{a\cdot c}{A}
       =\frac{A_X-A_Y}{A_X+A_Y},\quad
 1-q^2=\frac{4A_XA_Y}{(A_X+A_Y)^2}.
 \tag{34}
\]

The actual-class input, including independent signs, is classical;
see [Ishida--LeBrun, *Curvature, Connected Sums, and
Seiberg--Witten Theory*, Proposition 6, Theorem 9 and
Proposition 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0111228).
For two summands the fourth-summand congruence in their common
statement is unnecessary; alternatively it can be met by adding
auxiliary almost-complex examples before applying the theorem with
\(m=2\).

Equations (25)--(26) apply to every metric of negative mean scalar
curvature on this actual smooth connected sum. If both components
contribute a uniformly positive fraction of \(A\), any sequence
with \(\epsilon\to0\) must have \(\lambda_1/\kappa\to0\).
This identifies a genuine global low-frequency mode; it is not a
claim that the connected sum has already been constructed with
pointwise Ricci error tending to zero.

### Explicit growing family and several small eigenvalues

Take a smooth degree \(d\) hypersurface
\(X_d\subset\mathbb{CP}^3\), where \(d\ge8\) is divisible by four,
and let \(M_d=X_d\#X_d\).
Adjunction and the hyperplane class \(H\) give
\(K_{X_d}=(d-4)H|_{X_d}\), \(H^2[X_d]=d\), so

\[
 A_X=d(d-4)^2,\qquad
 A_d=2d(d-4)^2,\qquad T_d=A_d-4.
 \tag{35}
\]

The hypersurface sequence and
\(H^1(\mathbb{CP}^3,\mathcal O(-d))=
 H^2(\mathbb{CP}^3,\mathcal O(-d))=0\)
give \(q(X_d)=0\). Its Kähler Hodge decomposition gives
\[
 p_g(X_d)=\binom{d-1}{3},\qquad
 b_+(X_d)=1+2\binom{d-1}{3}\equiv3\pmod4 .
 \tag{36}
\]
For \(d=4j\), the binomial coefficient equals
\((4j-1)(2j-1)(4j-3)/3\), an odd integer.
The canonical invariant is \(\pm1\), by
[Taubes, *More Constraints on Symplectic Forms from Seiberg--Witten
Invariants*, Theorem 1](https://archive.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/mrl/1995/0002/0001/MRL-1995-0002-0001-a002.pdf).
Thus (33) applies. One can literally meet the four-factor
version of Theorem 9 with \(X_d,X_d,K3,K3\):
their \(b_+\)-sum is \(4\pmod8\).

For the two classes in (33), \(q=0\). Hence every metric on \(M_d\)
with \(\epsilon\le1/128^2\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\frac{\lambda_1(g)}{\kappa}\le8b.}
 \tag{37}
\]

Here \(A_d\to\infty\) and \(T_d/A_d\to1\). This is the natural
regime in which normalized errors could vanish without pretending
that the fixed connected-sum topological loss disappears.

More generally take
\[
 M_{m,d}=\#_{j=1}^{m}X_d,\qquad m\in\{2,3,4\},\qquad
 A_{m,d}=m\,d(d-4)^2,\quad T_{m,d}=A_{m,d}-4(m-1).
 \tag{37a}
\]
Four identical \(X_d\) satisfy the total \(b_+\) congruence in the
four-factor Bauer theorem, since \(4b_+(X_d)\equiv4\pmod8\).
Thus all the signed sums of the \(c_j=c_1(X_d)\) are actual
monopoles. Choose
\[
 a_0=\sum_jc_j,\qquad a_i=a_0-2c_i\quad(1\le i\le m).
 \tag{37b}
\]
Their intersection covariance is exactly
\[
 \Sigma=4\left(\frac1m I-\frac1{m^2}\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\right).
 \tag{37c}
\]
It has rank \(m-1\), with every positive eigenvalue \(4/m\).
Theorem 3, with \(n=m\), therefore gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \epsilon\le\frac1{(80m^2)^2}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \frac{\lambda_{m-1}(g)}{\kappa}\le2m^2b.}
 \tag{37d}
\]
In particular, relative scalar/Ricci saturation on the growing
three- or four-summand families forces, respectively, at least two
or three scalar low modes. The argument does not assume that the
unknown metrics have visible necks, respect the connected-sum
presentation geometrically, or converge to separate components.

### The relative-saturation hypothesis is realized by actual metrics

For fixed \(m\in\{2,3,4\}\), these growing connected sums do admit
metrics with
\[
 B=O_m(1),\qquad P=O_m(1),\qquad L=O_m(1),\qquad
 \kappa\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 \epsilon=O_m(A_{m,d}^{-1}).
 \tag{37e}
\]
Here the norms are integral, not pointwise.

To see this, endow each \(X_d\) with its negative
Kähler--Einstein metric normalized by \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\).
Its canonical bundle is ample because \(d>4\), so the classical
negative Kähler--Einstein existence theorem applies. Its volume is
\(2\pi^2d(d-4)^2\).
Choose finitely many distinct attachment points. For each \(d\),
choose a common radius \(t_d\to0\) so small that on each attachment
ball the rescaled normal-coordinate metric is as close to Euclidean
as desired in \(C^2\) on a fixed annulus. No uniform original
curvature bound as \(d\) varies is required for this choice.

Remove the small balls and join adjacent components by a fixed
smooth two-ended spherical connector scaled by \(t_d^2\).
Choose that connector to equal Euclidean radial annuli near its
ends. A fixed cutoff interpolates the rescaled original annular
metric to the Euclidean end metric. The rescaled transition and
connector metrics have uniform positive definiteness and \(C^2\)
bounds on fixed compact domains. In dimension four their
\(\int|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\) and \(\int s^2\) are invariant under
constant scaling, so the total cost of these finitely many regions
is \(O_m(1)\). Their volume is \(O_m(t_d^4)\), and their absolute
scalar integral is \(O_m(t_d^2)\).

Off these regions the original metrics are unchanged and Einstein.
Thus
\[
 \begin{split}
 V&=2\pi^2 A_{m,d}+O_m(t_d^4),\\
 \int s&=-4V+O_m(t_d^2),\\
 \int s^2&=32\pi^2A_{m,d}+O_m(1),\qquad P=O_m(1).
 \end{split}
 \tag{37f}
\]
Equation (8) of Round 17 ensures \(B\ge0\). The middle line gives
\(\kappa=1+O_m(t_d^2/V)\). On the unchanged region the contribution
to \(L\) is \(16(\kappa-1)^2\) times its volume; on the surgery
region the preceding \(L^2\) estimates give \(O_m(1)\). This proves
(37e).

This is the classical scale-critical connected-sum energy
construction, used only to show nonvacuity. It does **not** give a
Yau26 input with small \(L^\infty\) Ricci error: the connector can
have curvature of order \(t_d^{-2}\).
The new all-metric conclusion is that **any** sequence on these
smooth types with the same relative saturation, not just this
explicit neck sequence, must have at least \(m-1\) small scalar
eigenvalues. For the metrics in (37e), (37d) gives
\(\lambda_{m-1}/\kappa=O_m(A_{m,d}^{-1})\).

For a fixed \(M_d\), absolute simultaneous saturation is already
impossible. The signed identity of Round 17 gives
\[
 \frac14P+\frac{27}{16}B+\frac{19}{12}L\ge8\pi^2,
 \tag{38}
\]
because \(T_d-A_d=-4\). The new assertion is the spectral
restriction on *relative* saturation, not a rediscovery of a fixed
connected-sum curvature-energy gap. Ishida--LeBrun's same paper
already establishes sharp absolute Ricci-energy bounds for these
connected sums (Theorem C).

## 6. What is additional to the earlier Hodge and Walsh arguments?

The scalar inequalities alone bound projected class lengths and
imply pairwise Cauchy--Schwarz constraints. They do not algebraically
force membership in \(\mathcal C_m\). For example,

\[
 G_*=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&-1/2&-1/2\\
 -1/2&1&-1/2\\
 -1/2&-1/2&1
 \end{pmatrix}
 \tag{39}
\]

is positive semidefinite and satisfies every pairwise bound
\(|G_{ij}|\le1\), but \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}G_*\mathbf1=0\),
whereas every \(C\in\mathcal C_3\) has
\(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}C\mathbf1\ge1\).
This is a linear-algebra separation, not a claim that \(G_*\) is
realized by actual monopole classes.

[Round 2](round2_rigidity.md) obtained measurable projective
alignment and spatial phase separation at the different, forbidden
calibration, including a full monopole sign cube. This note neither
recounts that as new nor assumes its sign-cube hypothesis. It uses
the realizable calibration and the actual determinant curvatures to
produce a joint intersection distribution, and then uses bounded
first derivatives to obtain the scalar spectral estimate (25).
This latter estimate quantifies the previously uncontrolled global
phase-switching alternative.

For the closed shear manifolds, the known ordinary basic set in
one orientation is only \(\{\pm a\}\), so \(q=\pm1\); (25) is
correctly uninformative. The opposite-orientation class cannot be
inserted as another self-dual class. This theorem does not
manufacture additional monopoles on those manifolds.

Finally, (21) integrates over all of \(M\). No \(L^2\)-to-cycle
trace estimate is used. For a closed test two-form \(\theta\),
Round 17 also gives the direct period-duality estimate

\[
 \left|2\pi\,a_i\cdot[\theta]
       -\frac1{2\sqrt2}\int\varphi_i\wedge\theta\right|
 \le\sqrt{\frac38B+\frac14L}\,\|\theta\|_2.
 \tag{40}
\]

If one wants periods on a degenerating family of cycles, one must
still supply controlled dual forms \(\theta\); their norms do not
become bounded merely because the cycles are topologically fixed.

## 7. Scope and verification

Theorems 1--3 concern any metrics satisfying the stated actual
monopole assumptions. They do not require an Einstein equation or
a geometric ansatz. For a near-negative-Einstein input,
\(P+L/4=\int|\operatorname{Ric}+\kappa g|^2\);
scalar-energy saturation controls the remaining \(B\).
Thus they constrain a possible Yau26 input sequence in a different
topological category from the closed shear examples.

A small \(\lambda_1\) is not itself a contradiction. The next
mathematical question is whether a proposed topology and Ricci
pinching admit the required low-frequency phase mixing, or whether
an independently proved spectral, capacity, or geometric bound
prevents it. Neither its prevention nor a new sign-branch solution
is claimed here.

[The accompanying exact checks](round18_periods_check.py) verify
the projective-distance identities, all coefficient bounds,
normalization of the wedge pairing, the finite sign constraints,
the separating Gram example, and the hypersurface/congruence
application. The measurable-choice and analytic Rayleigh arguments
are proved above; finite computation does not replace them.

# What an integral monopole gap can control in the period domain

22 September 2026. This note gives an all-metric period bound without
scalar-energy saturation, states the exact linear-algebra condition that turns it
into compact confinement, and tests that condition on actual exact
Einstein metrics. The compactness mechanism is classical finite-dimensional
geometry combined with classical monopole estimates. Round 4 improves its
radius but does not supply the missing directions. No general bound on the
rank of the actual monopole set is proved.

## 1. A projection bound valid away from scalar saturation

Let \(M\) be a closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold, \(b^+\ge2\),
and put \(T=2\chi+3\tau\). For a metric with

\[
 R_2=\int|\operatorname{Ric}|^2>0,
 \qquad
 e=4-\frac{(\int s)^2}{VR_2}
   =\frac{4P+L}{R_2},
 \quad
 P=\int|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2,
 \quad L=\int(s-\bar s)^2,
 \tag{1}
\]

write \(H=H_g^+\subset H^2(M;\mathbb R)\) and \(p_H(a)=(a_H^+)^2\).
An actual monopole class is the real Chern class of a spin-c structure
solvable for every smooth metric, not merely an element of a formal
convex hull.

**Proposition 1 (classical radius and integral-gap radius).** If \(e<1\),
then \(T>0\), and every actual monopole class satisfies

\[
 p_H(a)\le U_{\rm cl}(e,T)
 :=\frac{3(4-e)}{8(1-e)}T.                         \tag{2}
\]

Fix \(C\ge0\), and let \(\varepsilon(C)\in(0,1/4]\) be an all-sign
constant from Round 4/5: if \(p_H(a)>0\) and \(a^2\ge-Cp_H(a)\), then

\[
 e+\left(\frac{T}{p_H(a)}-\frac23\right)_+
       \ge\varepsilon(C).                              \tag{3}
\]

For any collection \(\mathcal A\) of actual classes with

\[
 a^2\ge-CT\quad(a\in\mathcal A),\qquad
 e<\varepsilon(C),
\]

one has the simultaneous bound

\[
 \boxed{p_H(a)\le U(e,T,C)
 :=\frac{T}{\,2/3+\varepsilon(C)-e\,}
 \quad(a\in\mathcal A).}                              \tag{4}
\]

In particular the same bound with \(C=0\) holds for every actual special
class \(a^2\ge T\). These statements apply to every possible non-Ricci-flat
Einstein output, by setting \(e=0\); they are not tied to an input metric
or a construction ansatz. A nonzero actual class with positive square
also excludes Ricci-flat metrics by the scalar inequality.

**Proof.** Chern--Weil and \(\int s^2=4(R_2-P)\) give

\[
 4\pi^2T=2\|W^+\|_2^2+\frac{R_2}{6}-\frac{2P}{3}
       \ge\frac{R_2-4P}{6}
       \ge\frac{1-e}{6}R_2.                           \tag{5}
\]

Thus \(T>0\) and \(R_2\le24\pi^2T/(1-e)\). The classical projected
monopole estimate is

\[
 p_H(a)\le\frac32T+\frac{3P}{16\pi^2}.                \tag{6}
\]

It follows directly from [LeBrun, *Ricci Curvature, Minimal Volumes, and
Seiberg--Witten Theory*, Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0003068)
and Chern--Weil. Substituting \(P\le eR_2/4\) proves (2).
For (4), if \(p_H(a)<T\), the claim follows because its denominator is
positive and less than one. Otherwise \(a^2\ge-CT\ge-Cp_H(a)\), so (3)
applies; its positive part must be positive because \(e<\varepsilon(C)\).
Rearrangement proves (4). Zero projection causes no division. \(\square\)

This is not a new curvature estimate: (4) is a reformulation of the
completed integral gap, and (2) is entirely classical. It records exactly
what can be used without the additional scalar error

\[
 B_a=\int s^2-32\pi^2a^2
\]

being small. In particular no Round 18 phase-correlation or spectral-cluster
conclusion has been used.

## 2. The extra hypothesis for confinement of the whole period plane

Let \(Q\) be the intersection form, of signature \((b^+,b^-)\), and let
\(\mathcal D\) be its space of maximal positive subspaces. For finite nonempty
\(\mathcal A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\) put

\[
 W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\mathcal A,
 \qquad
 K_U=\{H\in\mathcal D:p_H(a_i)\le U\text{ for all }i\}.
 \tag{7}
\]

**Theorem 2 (the exact properness criterion).** The height
\(H\mapsto\max_i p_H(a_i)\) on \(\mathcal D\) is proper if and only if
\(W\) contains a maximal positive subspace or a maximal negative subspace
for \(Q\). Equivalently, all \(K_U\) are compact precisely under that
condition. If the condition fails, every nonempty \(K_U\) is noncompact.
Under this criterion, (2), or the stronger (4) under its square-cost hypothesis,
confines all the corresponding metric period planes on this fixed marked
manifold to an explicitly bounded compact subset of \(\mathcal D\).

Here is an explicit bound when \(W\) contains a maximal positive subspace
\(P_0\). Choose a \(Q\)-orthonormal basis

\[
 u_j=\sum_i c_{ji}a_i\quad(1\le j\le b^+),\qquad
 D=U\sum_j\left(\sum_i|c_{ji}|\right)^2.                 \tag{8}
\]

Every \(H\in K_U\) is the graph of \(X:P_0\to P_0^{\perp_Q}\), and

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|X\|_{\rm op}^2\le1-\frac1{D-b^++1}.
 \quad}                                                   \tag{9}
\]

If \(D<b^+\), \(K_U\) is empty. All norms in (9) come from \(Q\) on
\(P_0\) and \(-Q\) on its orthogonal complement.

**Proof.** Projection is linear, so the triangle inequality on \(H\)
gives \(p_H(u_j)\le U(\sum_i|c_{ji}|)^2\). In the graph coordinates
with \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(I,-I)\), solve the projection equation to get

\[
 p_H(u)=u^{\mathsf T}(I-X^{\mathsf T}X)^{-1}u,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(I-X^{\mathsf T}X)^{-1}\le D.           \tag{10}
\]

Each eigenvalue on the left is at least one. Its largest is therefore
at most \(D-b^++1\), proving (9). Thus \(X\) stays a fixed distance
from the boundary \(X^{\mathsf T}X=I\). The constraints defining \(K_U\)
are closed inside this bounded coordinate set, so \(K_U\) is compact.

For the negative version apply the same argument to \(H^{\perp_Q}\)
and \(-Q\), using

\[
 |a_{i,H}^-|^2=p_H(a_i)-a_i^2\le U-a_i^2.               \tag{11}
\]

If some right side is negative, \(K_U\) is empty. Otherwise these finitely
many bounds control the negative projections of a basis for a maximal
negative subspace contained in \(W\).

For necessity, first suppose \(W\) is nondegenerate and contains neither
kind of maximal definite subspace. Then \(W^{\perp_Q}\) has both signs.
Its orthogonal group has noncompact one-parameter boosts fixing every
\(a_i\). Starting from any \(H_0\in K_U\), their orbit stays in \(K_U\)
and escapes compact subsets of \(\mathcal D\): the orthogonal group's
action on the positive-plane domain has compact stabilizer and is proper.

If \(W\) is degenerate, choose
\(0\ne l\in W\cap W^{\perp_Q}\). Let \(J_0\) be the involution acting
as \(+1\) on \(H_0\) and \(-1\) on \(H_0^{\perp_Q}\), and put

\[
 d=Q(l,J_0l)>0,\qquad m=J_0l/d,\qquad
 Z=\operatorname{span}(l,m)^{\perp_Q}.
 \tag{11a}
\]

Both \(l,m\) are null, \(Q(l,m)=1\), and \(Z\) is \(J_0\)-invariant.
The decomposition \(\mathbb Rl\oplus\mathbb Rm\oplus Z\) is orthogonal
for the positive Hodge inner product \(Q(\,\cdot,J_0\,\cdot)\).
Since \(Q(l,w)=0\) for all \(w\in W\), every such \(w\) is \(cl+z\).
Let \(A_t\in O(Q)\) scale \(l\) by \(e^{-t}\), \(m\) by \(e^t\), and
fix \(Z\). For \(H_t=A_t^{-1}H_0\),

\[
 \|cl+z\|_{H_t}^2=e^{-2t}c^2d+\|z\|_{H_0}^2,
 \qquad
 \|m\|_{H_t}^2=e^{2t}/d.                              \tag{11b}
\]

Because \(2p_H(w)=\|w\|_H^2+Q(w,w)\), all the heights of the \(a_i\)
decrease while the norm of the fixed vector \(m\) diverges. Thus
\(H_t\in K_U\) and escapes compact subsets. Finally, a degenerate \(W\)
cannot contain either kind of maximal definite subspace: its nonzero
radical would lie in that subspace's definite orthogonal complement.
This completes both directions. \(\square\)

The compactness theorem itself also follows from the classical radius
(2). The integral gap supplies a strictly smaller allowed radius at
\(e=0\), not a new compactness mechanism. Neither statement is geometric
compactness of Einstein metrics.

## 3. Exact Einstein calibration: degeneration survives all special classes

The following test is genuine degeneration, not just pulling back one
metric by unbounded mapping classes.

**Proposition 3.** Fix \(M=\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h\), \(g,h\ge2\), with
the product orientation, and let \(a=c_1(M)\). There is a family of
product hyperbolic metrics \(G_\ell\), \(\ell\downarrow0\), for which

\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_{G_\ell}=-G_\ell,\qquad
 e=0,\qquad a^2=T=p_{H_{G_\ell}}(a)>0,\qquad
 \int s^2=32\pi^2T.                                   \tag{12}
\]

The full set of real special monopole classes of \(M\) is exactly
\(\{a,-a\}\). Nevertheless its period planes escape every compact
subset even of the quotient \(O(Q;\mathbb Z)\backslash\mathcal D\).
In particular quotienting by orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms
does not repair this failure.

**Proof of the class statement.** The canonical classes are actual by
the symplectic Seiberg--Witten theorem. The product has

\[
 V=16\pi^2(g-1)(h-1),\quad T=8(g-1)(h-1),
 \quad b^+=b^-=1+2gh.
\]

For any real special monopole \(c\), the scalar inequality gives

\[
 T\le c^2\le p_{H_G}(c)\le\frac{\int s^2}{32\pi^2}=T.
 \tag{13}
\]

All are equalities. In particular \(c^-=0\), and equality in the
scalar estimate gives a parallel nonzero self-dual form representing
\(c\). This is the equality proof of [LeBrun, *Four-Manifolds, Curvature
Bounds, and Convex Geometry*, Proposition 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0611450).
The product's restricted holonomy is \(SO(2)\times SO(2)\); its parallel
self-dual two-forms form the single line spanned by the sum of the two
area forms. Thus \(c=ta\), and \(c^2=a^2>0\) gives \(t=\pm1\).
This argument handles all actual special classes, not merely those with
nonzero ordinary invariants. It makes no assertion about nonspecial
ordinary monopoles.

**Proof of quotient-period escape.** Choose a fixed nonseparating curve
\(\gamma\subset\Sigma_g\). Hyperbolic pants gluing with its boundary
length set to \(\ell\) gives hyperbolic metrics \(k_\ell\) in which its
geodesic has length \(\ell\). Keep the second metric \(k_h\) fixed and
set \(G_\ell=k_\ell\oplus k_h\). The collar theorem supplies, for small
\(\ell\), an embedded fixed-width cylinder around \(\gamma\), with

\[
 k_\ell=dr^2+\ell^2\cosh^2r\,d\theta^2,
 \quad -1<r<1,\quad\theta\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z.
 \tag{14}
\]

The classical collar lemma in equivalent conformal coordinates is recalled
in [Rupflin--Topping, *Horizontal curves of hyperbolic metrics*, Lemma
A.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.06691).

Fix \(f\in C_c^\infty(-1,1)\) with \(\int f\,dr=1\). The form
\(\eta_\ell=f(r)dr\), extended by zero, is smooth and closed. It represents
the fixed primitive integral class \(u=\operatorname{PD}[\gamma]\);
its integral on a transverse loop is its algebraic intersection with
\(\gamma\). Its energy is

\[
 \|\eta_\ell\|_2^2
  =\ell\int_{-1}^1f(r)^2\cosh r\,dr=C_f\ell.            \tag{15}
\]

The harmonic representative \(\alpha_\ell\) of \(u\) has no larger
energy. Choose a fixed nonzero primitive integral class
\(v\in H^1(\Sigma_h;\mathbb Z)\) and its harmonic representative
\(\beta\). The product harmonic form \(\alpha_\ell\wedge\beta\)
represents the fixed primitive integral two-class \(x=u\otimes v\ne0\).
Product norms give

\[
 \|x\|_{H_{G_\ell}}^2
 =\|\alpha_\ell\|_2^2\|\beta\|_2^2
 \le C_f\|\beta\|_2^2\ell\longrightarrow0,              \tag{16}
\]

where \(\|x\|_H^2=Q(x_H^+,x_H^+)-Q(x_H^-,x_H^-)\) is the
positive-definite Hodge norm on \(H^2\).

For the fixed integral lattice \(\Lambda=H^2(M;\mathbb Z)/\mathrm{tors}\),
the function

\[
 \sigma(H)=\min_{0\ne z\in\Lambda}\|z\|_H
 \tag{17}
\]

is positive and continuous on \(\mathcal D\), and invariant under
\(O(Q;\mathbb Z)\). Positivity follows from lattice discreteness;
continuity follows because on a neighborhood of any positive-definite
norm only finitely many lattice vectors can realize its minimum.
It therefore descends to a positive continuous function on the quotient,
bounded below on every compact subset. Equation (16) implies
\(\sigma(H_{G_\ell})\to0\), proving the claimed escape. \(\square\)

All local curvature tensors in this example are those of
\(\mathbb H^2\times\mathbb H^2\); neither curvature blow-up nor a change
of smooth topology is used. The injectivity radius collapses. The class
\(x\) detecting escape is not asserted to be a monopole. It lies in the
indefinite mixed Kunneth summand, invisible to the special-class line.

For the forbidden Round 4 Weyl orbit
\(\mathcal O_0=\operatorname{Orb}\operatorname{diag}(-1/6,1/12,1/12)\),
these exact Einstein metrics give, pointwise,

\[
 W^+=\operatorname{diag}(-2/3,1/3,1/3),\quad
 \operatorname{dist}(W^+,\mathcal O_0)^2=\frac38,
 \quad q=-s+\sqrt6|W^+|=6.                              \tag{18}
\]

Thus \(\Delta_+=\int q^2-72\pi^2p_H(a)=0\) and the negative
Chern-square cost vanishes, yet the calibration defect in Round 4 is
\(3/8\). Its Weyl-orbit term cannot be discarded in favor of relative
Einstein defect. The ratio \(T/p_H(a)=1\) keeps this example outside the
forbidden (2/3) band, exactly as it must.

## 4. What this settles, and what it does not

Equations (2)--(4) are honest constraints on all output period planes
without scalar saturation. Theorem 2 gives the exact algebraic
criterion for properness of these simultaneous class-height constraints.
Proposition 3 shows that even the
entire actual special-class set, zero Ricci defect, zero negative-square
cost, and exact scalar saturation need not satisfy that condition; the
failure persists after the natural integral marking quotient.

There is no contradiction with Round 18: on this example the monopole
span is one-dimensional and its nontrivial phase covariance has rank zero.
Round 18 controls fields arising from the specified actual classes, not
the full harmonic self-dual space.

For completeness, the elementary packing bound for any finite actual
set with \(a^2\ge-CT\) and (4) is

\[
 \#\mathcal A\le
 \left(1+\frac{2\sqrt{2U+CT}}{\sigma(H)}\right)^{b_2(M)}.
 \tag{19}
\]

Indeed \(\|a\|_H^2=2p_H(a)-a^2\le2U+CT\), and disjoint Hodge balls
of radius \(\sigma(H)/2\) about these distinct real lattice classes fit
inside the ball of radius \(\sqrt{2U+CT}+\sigma(H)/2\). This counts
real classes, not spin-c structures distinguished only by torsion.
The dependence on the lattice systole and dimension is explicit. Neither
the preceding proof nor the exact calibration yields a bound on the
number of linearly independent actual classes solely from \(e\) and \(C\).
The example disproves an unrestricted period-compactness inference; it
does not disprove an as-yet-unproved gauge-theoretic rank theorem.

The contribution here is a completed consequence and a decisive test of
the proposed extension, not a new general Einstein obstruction. The
classical ingredients and the inherited gap have been kept separate.
The accompanying checker verifies (2), the graph projection identities,
and the exact Einstein/Weyl calibration. The collar, lattice compactness,
and monopole existence arguments are analytic proofs above, not claims
certified by finite computation.

# Simultaneous endpoint saturation and the positive inertia of Chern periods

23 September 2026. Baseline: `8113dd1`. This note deduces a global
constraint on actual Chern classes from the bounded-form endpoint theorem.
The conclusion concerns the positive part of the intersection form. No
equal-square, nonnegative-square, topological-square, or negative-square
budget is imposed on the classes.

## 1. Statement from actual geometric inputs

Let `(M,g)` be a closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold, with the
Einstein target normalized to `Ric = -g`. Let `a_1,...,a_m`, `m >= 2`, be
real first Chern classes of specified Spin^c structures whose unperturbed
Seiberg--Witten equations are solvable for every metric conformal to `g`.
Each structure is held fixed during conformal variation. The classes need
not be linearly independent or have equal squares. Put

\[
 V=\operatorname{Vol}(g),\quad
 p_i=(a_{i,g}^+)^2>0,\quad
 r=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2,\quad w=\sqrt6|W^+|,
\]
\[
 \delta_i=\frac32\int(s^2+2w^2)-72\pi^2p_i\ge0,
 \qquad \kappa=\max_i\{r+\delta_i/V\}\le1/2880.
 \tag{1}
\]
Let `lambda_1>0` be the first positive scalar-Laplacian eigenvalue and set

\[
 G(\kappa)=13\kappa+4\sqrt{2\kappa},\qquad
 J=\left(300\sqrt\kappa+
                  12\sqrt{G(\kappa)/\lambda_1}\right)^2.
 \tag{2}
\]
For `kappa=0`, set `J=0`.

**Theorem (signed periods and positive inertia).** There are constant
signs `s_1=1,s_2,...,s_m` such that, for every `i`,

\[
 \boxed{\frac{2\pi^2}{V}
             \bigl((a_i-s_i a_1)_g^+\bigr)^2\le J.}
 \tag{3}
\]
Consequently, for every real vector `t` with `sum_i t_i s_i=0`,

\[
 \boxed{\frac{2\pi^2}{V}\left(\sum_i t_i a_i\right)^2
       \le J\left(\sum_{i=2}^m|t_i|\right)^2
       \le(m-1)J\sum_i t_i^2.}
 \tag{4}
\]
The first square in (3) is the positive Hodge norm; the square in (4)
is the full, indefinite cup-product square.

The following matrix form records more of the structure. Define the
normalized cup and positive-period matrices

\[
 Q_{ij}=\frac{2\pi^2}{V}a_i\cdot a_j,\qquad
 P_{ij}=\frac{2\pi^2}{V}a_{i,g}^+\cdot a_{j,g}^+,
 \qquad q_1=P_{11}.
\]
There are positive semidefinite matrices `R,N` and a real vector `alpha`
such that

\[
 \boxed{P=q_1\alpha\alpha^{\mathsf T}+R,
 \quad Q=q_1\alpha\alpha^{\mathsf T}+R-N,
 \quad \operatorname{tr}R\le(m-1)J,}
 \tag{5}
\]
\[
 \alpha_1=1,\qquad
 |\alpha_i-s_i|\le\sqrt{J/q_1},\qquad
 \frac12\le q_1\le\frac32.
 \tag{6}
\]
The positive-period matrix itself has the sharper entrywise estimate

\[
 \boxed{\max_{i,j}|P_{ij}-q_1s_i s_j|\le\kappa/36+2J.}
 \tag{6a}
\]
This uses the exact common-functional relation among the class lengths,
not merely Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the `L^2` approximation.
In particular, if `nu_2(Q)` denotes the second largest eigenvalue,

\[
 \boxed{\nu_2(Q)\le(m-1)J.}
 \tag{7}
\]
No smallness of `J` is needed for these finite inequalities. They become
restrictive when `kappa` and `G(kappa)/lambda_1` are small. In particular,
`lambda_1 >> sqrt(kappa)` is sufficient along a sequence; a fixed positive
lower bound for `lambda_1` is stronger than necessary.

## 2. Two useful cup-product obstructions

For two classes `a,c`, (3) immediately gives

\[
 \boxed{\frac{2\pi^2}{V}
       \bigl(a^2+c^2-2|a\cdot c|\bigr)\le J.}
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus positive cup square for both `a-c` and `a+c` obstructs simultaneous
saturation when the scalar gap is large relative to the error. This
criterion does not require either class individually to have positive
square. It may be vacuous, which is correctly reported by a nonpositive
left side.

More generally, if `nu_2(Q)>=theta>0` and

\[
 \kappa\le\frac{\theta}{360000(m-1)},
\]
then (7) gives the explicit alternative

\[
 \boxed{\lambda_1\le\frac{576(m-1)G(\kappa)}{\theta}.}
 \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, (7) implies
`sqrt(theta/(m-1)) <= 300 sqrt(kappa)+12 sqrt(G/lambda_1)`;
the first summand is at most half the left side. If `kappa=0`, a
positive `theta` is impossible. These statements obstruct a proposed
metric and its specified actual classes; they do not rule out all
Einstein metrics on an arbitrary smooth manifold.

For fixed classes and bounded volume, a positive second eigenvalue of
their unnormalized cup matrix therefore excludes a sequence with
`kappa -> 0` and `G(kappa)/lambda_1 -> 0`. If volume grows, the necessary
normalization in (7) must be retained: positive inertia alone, without
a quantitative positive eigenvalue divided by volume, is insufficient.

## 3. An elementary phase lemma with a squared error

All expectations below use normalized volume. Let `u,v` be smooth
sections of the same Euclidean rank-three bundle, with `|u|,|v|<=sqrt(6)`.
Write

\[
 a=|u|^2,\quad b=|v|^2,\quad h=\langle u,v\rangle,\quad
 \mathcal Q(u)=u\otimes u-\frac{|u|^2}{3}I,
 \quad \sigma=\operatorname{sign}h\in\{-1,1\},
\]
resolving `h=0` by `sigma=1`. Pointwise,

\[
 |\mathcal Q(u)-\mathcal Q(v)|^2
       =\frac23(a-b)^2+2(ab-h^2).                 \tag{10}
\]
Two useful estimates, including at zeros, are

\[
 |u-\sigma v|^2
 \le2\bigl((a-1)^2+(b-1)^2\bigr)
                    +2|\mathcal Q(u)-\mathcal Q(v)|^2,\tag{11}
\]
\[
 |h-\sigma|^2
 \le2\bigl((a-1)^2+(b-1)^2\bigr)
                       +|\mathcal Q(u)-\mathcal Q(v)|^2.\tag{12}
\]

For (11), if `a+b>=1`, use

\[
 |u-\sigma v|^2
 =\frac{(a-b)^2+4(ab-h^2)}{a+b+2|h|}
 \le2|\mathcal Q(u)-\mathcal Q(v)|^2.
\]
If `a+b<1`, the left side is at most `a+b`, whereas
`2((a-1)^2+(b-1)^2)>=(a+b-2)^2>=a+b`.
For (12), let `z=sqrt(ab)` and, when `z>0`, `t=|h|/z`.
Then `0<=t<=1` and

\[
 (zt-1)^2\le2(z-1)^2+2z^2(1-t)^2,
 \quad (z-1)^2\le(a-1)^2+(b-1)^2,
 \quad 2z^2(1-t)^2\le2(ab-h^2).
\]
The middle inequality follows because `sqrt(ab)` lies between `a` and
`b`; the zero case follows directly. Equation (10) completes the proof.

Suppose `E|h-sigma|^2<=T` and `E|dh|^2<=D`. Poincare and the
triangle inequality for orthogonal projection off the constants give

\[
 \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\sigma}
 \le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}h}+\|h-\sigma\|_2
 \le\sqrt{D/\lambda_1}+\sqrt T.                  \tag{13}
\]
There is a constant sign `s` with

\[
 \mathbb E|\sigma-s|^2
 =2(1-|\mathbb E\sigma|)
 \le2\operatorname{Var}\sigma.                  \tag{14}
\]
If `E|u-sigma v|^2<=F`, it follows that

\[
 \|u-sv\|_2
 \le\sqrt F+\sqrt{12}\bigl(\sqrt{D/\lambda_1}+\sqrt T\bigr).
 \tag{15}
\]
All norms here are normalized `L^2` norms. No derivative of the
measurable sign, unit eigenvector, or normalized form is taken. The
argument only needs the Poincare estimate for these finitely many
smooth scalar functions `h`; a gap on the whole scalar spectrum is a
convenient sufficient condition.

## 4. Applying the lemma to the actual endpoint forms

Let `H_i` represent `a_{i,g}^+` harmonically and put
`xi_i=-sqrt(2) pi H_i`. The actual bounded-form theorem in
[the endpoint application](line1_composed_application.md), equations
(2)--(4), gives smooth `u_i=omega_i/sqrt(2)` with arbitrarily small
simultaneous smoothing allowances. Suppressing those allowances until
the limiting step, it gives

\[
 \begin{split}
 |u_i|&\le\sqrt6,\qquad
 \mathbb E|u_i-\xi_i|^2\le49\kappa/2,\\
 \mathbb E(|u_i|^2-1)^2&\le180\kappa,\qquad
 \mathbb E|W^++\mathcal Q(u_i)|^2\le720\kappa,\\
 \mathbb E|\nabla u_i|^2&\le G(\kappa)/2.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{16}
\]
The same Weyl tensor occurs for every class, so

\[
 \mathbb E|\mathcal Q(u_i)-\mathcal Q(u_1)|^2\le2880\kappa.
 \tag{17}
\]
Equations (11)--(12), and the differential estimate
`|d<u_i,u_1>|^2 <=12(|nabla u_i|^2+|nabla u_1|^2)`, give

\[
 F=6480\kappa,\qquad T=3600\kappa,\qquad D=12G(\kappa).
 \tag{18}
\]
Thus (15), followed by the two harmonic-alignment errors, gives a
constant sign `s_i` with

\[
 \|\xi_i-s_i\xi_1\|_2
 \le\left(7\sqrt2+36\sqrt5+120\sqrt3\right)\sqrt\kappa
                  +12\sqrt{G(\kappa)/\lambda_1}
 \le300\sqrt\kappa+12\sqrt{G(\kappa)/\lambda_1}.       \tag{19}
\]
The coefficient estimate is elementary: use `sqrt(2)<3/2`,
`sqrt(5)<9/4`, and `sqrt(3)<26/15`, giving an upper bound `299.5`.

To restore the allowances rigorously, choose a sequence of positive
allowances tending to zero, obtaining one finite sign vector for each
choice. Pass to a subsequence with constant sign vector. The harmonic
forms, their norms, and the scalar eigenvalue are fixed. All the errors
in (16)--(19) tend to their displayed bounds. No convergence of spinors,
connections, or measurable sign functions is asserted. This proves (3).
Since a full cup square is at most its positive Hodge square, summing
the signed differences proves (4).

For completeness, (16) implies

\[
 \left(\sqrt{1-\sqrt{180\kappa}}-7\sqrt{\kappa/2}\right)^2
 \le q_1\le
 \left(\sqrt{1+\sqrt{180\kappa}}+7\sqrt{\kappa/2}\right)^2.
 \tag{20}
\]
The lower parenthesis is positive on `kappa<=1/2880`, and this interval
gives `1/2<=q_1<=3/2`.

## 5. The matrix decomposition and its precise limitation

Use the Hilbert space of self-dual forms with normalized `L^2` inner
product. Orthogonally split

\[
 \xi_i=\alpha_i\xi_1+\eta_i,\qquad
 \alpha_i=\frac{\langle\xi_i,\xi_1\rangle_{L^2(d\mu/V)}}{q_1},
 \quad \langle\eta_i,\xi_1\rangle=0.
\]
Here `alpha_1=1`, `eta_1=0`, and (3) gives
`||eta_i||_2^2<=J` and `|alpha_i-s_i|<=sqrt(J/q_1)`.
Set `R_ij=<eta_i,eta_j>` and let `N` be the normalized positive Gram
matrix of the anti-self-dual harmonic representatives. Then `R,N>=0`,
`tr R<= (m-1)J`, and Hodge decomposition gives (5) exactly. On the
codimension-one subspace perpendicular to `alpha`, the quadratic form
of `Q` is at most `||R||_op<=tr R`. The min--max principle proves (7).

To prove (6a), put `q_i=P_ii`. Subtracting the definitions of the
deficits in (1) gives the exact identity

\[
 q_i-q_1=-\frac{\delta_i-\delta_1}{36V},\qquad
 |q_i-q_1|\le\kappa/36.                            \tag{21}
\]
The signed forms `s_i xi_i` are each within `sqrt(J)` of `xi_1`, so
the squared distance between any two is at most `4J`. Polarization
therefore gives

\[
 q_1-\kappa/36-2J\le s_i s_jP_{ij}
                       \le q_1+\kappa/36,
\]
proving (6a). No extra square root of `J` is lost in the positive Gram
matrix estimate.

It would be incorrect to delete `N` or replace (5) by closeness of `Q`
to a rank-one sign matrix. The bounded-form estimates control the
positive projections. For example, in an abstract intersection space
of signature `(1,m)`, choose an orthonormal positive vector `e_0` and
negative vectors `e_i`, and let `a_i=e_0+L e_i`. Their positive Gram
matrix is exactly `11^T`, while their full Gram matrix is
`11^T-L^2 I`. This illustrates the missing negative information even
when positive-period alignment is exact. It is an algebraic test of
the implication, **not** an assertion that these classes are actual
monopoles on a metric saturating (1). Classical gauge equality
rigidity can impose further restrictions at exact saturation.

Similarly, distinct Spin^c labels, torsion differences, or the pair
`a,-a` do not by themselves supply a positive period obstruction.
An integral lattice alone gives no lower bound on the positive Hodge
norm of all nonzero vectors; one must use a verified positive cup
square, a quantitative positive eigenvalue, or additional period data.

## 6. Exact increment over the earlier arguments

[Round 18](round18_periods.md), Sections 1--4, assumes common positive
square `a_i^2=A>=2chi+3tau` and uses scalar-energy saturation to control
the **full** determinant curvatures. It obtains an intersection
sign-correlation polytope and low scalar eigenvalues from covariance.
Those remain stronger in their particular regime and are not new here.
[Round 2](round2_rigidity.md), Sections 3--4, already handles measurable
line alignment and Walsh phase separation at a different calibration.

The added result is (3)--(7) for the actual all-scalar endpoint input,
with arbitrary individual squares and no negative-square budget. It
extracts a positive-inertia obstruction from harmonic Chern periods,
while preserving the uncontrolled negative Gram matrix. The squared
phase estimates (11)--(14) yield an `O(kappa+G(kappa)/lambda_1)` error,
rather than losing a square root simply by estimating the mean of the
inner product. The common-functional identity (21) preserves that order
in the positive Gram matrix. No growing low-mode cluster theorem is
being claimed.

The classical scalar--Weyl curvature bound and its equality geometry

# Kähler controls for LeBrun equality, Ricci energy, and Einstein defect

This note supplies exact tests for the corrected first scientific line. The
identities below are elementary consequences of classical Kähler geometry,
Hodge theory, and Chern–Weil theory; no priority is claimed. They identify a
missing hypothesis in an Einstein-stability assertion, not a new solution of
Yau26. In particular, closeness of an integral residual does not by itself
give pointwise Ricci pinching, metric compactness, or an exact Einstein metric
on the same smooth manifold.

## 1. Definitions and the exact Kähler decomposition

Let \((M^4,J,g,\omega)\) be a closed connected Kähler surface, with its complex
orientation. Use real Riemannian tensor norms, \(|\omega|^2=2\), and the Weyl
endomorphism norm for which \(|W^+|^2=s^2/24\). Set

\[
 a=c_1(K_M),\quad A=a^2=2\chi(M)+3\tau(M),\quad
 p=(a_g^+)^2,\quad V=\operatorname{Vol}(g),\quad
 \bar s=V^{-1}\int_M s\,d\mu,
\]
\[
 L=\int_M(s-\bar s)^2\,d\mu,\qquad
 P=\int_M|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2\,d\mu,\qquad
 R_2=\int_M|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\,d\mu.
\]

Here \(a_g^\pm\) denote harmonic representatives of the two Hodge projections;
the square in \(p\) is the intersection square. Define the LeBrun remainder

\[
 D=\frac1{4\pi^2}\int_M
       \left(\frac{s^2}{24}+\frac12|W^+|^2\right)d\mu-\frac p2.
 \tag{1}
\]

Changing \(a\) to \(-a=c_1(M)\) does not change any formula here. Whenever
LeBrun's polarized monopole hypotheses apply to this canonical pair, this
is his Proposition 4.1 remainder. Its equality set is constant-scalar
Kähler, not just Kähler–Einstein. His Proposition 4.2 and equation (17)
give the associated Ricci-energy identity. These are classical inputs.
[LeBrun, arXiv:math/0003068v1, Propositions 4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0003068).

For every Kähler metric as above, without needing a monopole argument,

\[
 \boxed{
 p=\frac{(a\cdot[\omega])^2}{[\omega]^2}
   =\frac{\bar s^2V}{32\pi^2},\qquad
 p-A=\|a_g^-\|_{L^2}^2\ge0,\qquad
 D=\frac{L}{64\pi^2}.}
 \tag{2}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 P=8\pi^2(p-A)+\frac L4
   =8\pi^2(p-A)+16\pi^2D,}
 \tag{3}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 R_2=8\pi^2(2p-A)+\frac L2
    =8\pi^2(2p-A)+32\pi^2D.}
 \tag{4}
\]

### Proof, including the primitive component

The Ricci form \(\rho\) represents \(-2\pi a\), and

\[
 \rho=\frac{s}{4}\omega+\rho_0,
 \qquad *\rho_0=-\rho_0,
 \qquad |\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2=2|\rho_0|^2.
 \tag{5}
\]

Since \(a\) has type \((1,1)\), its harmonic self-dual component is a multiple
of \(\omega\); its other component is primitive and anti-self-dual. Also
\([\omega]^2=2V\) and
\(\int s\,d\mu=2\int\rho\wedge\omega=-4\pi a\cdot[\omega]\).
This proves the first two identities in (2).

The harmonic representative of \([\rho]\) is

\[
 \rho_H=\frac{\bar s}{4}\omega+\eta,
 \qquad \eta=-2\pi a_g^-.
\]

Write \(\zeta=\rho-\rho_H\). It is exact, so
\(\int\zeta\wedge\zeta=0\), and its two Hodge components have equal squared
\(L^2\) norms. Its self-dual component is
\(\zeta^+=(s-\bar s)\omega/4\), giving

\[
 \|\zeta^-\|_2^2=\|\zeta^+\|_2^2=L/8.
\]

The harmonic form \(\eta\) is orthogonal to the exact form \(\zeta\), and
therefore to \(\zeta^-\). Consequently

\[
 \int|\rho_0|^2=\|\eta+\zeta^-\|_2^2
 =4\pi^2(p-A)+L/8.
\]

Equation (5) proves (3). The Kähler Weyl identity turns (1) into
\(D=\int s^2/(64\pi^2)-p/2=L/(64\pi^2)\). Finally,
\(R_2=P+\int s^2/4\) proves (4).

A separate Chern–Weil check is

\[
 4\pi^2 A=\int\left(2|W^+|^2+\frac{s^2}{24}-\frac12
 |\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2\right)
 =\frac18\int s^2-\frac P2.
 \tag{6}
\]

Thus \(P=\int s^2/4-8\pi^2A\), consistently with (3).

## 2. A sharp extra condition for integral Einstein stability

Assume \(\bar s<0\), and choose the mean-scalar normalization
\(\kappa=-\bar s/4>0\). Let

\[
 Q_\kappa=\int|\operatorname{Ric}+\kappa g|^2.
\]

Orthogonality of trace and trace-free parts gives the exact identities

\[
 \boxed{Q_\kappa=P+\frac L4
 =8\pi^2(p-A)+32\pi^2D,\qquad
 \kappa^2 V=2\pi^2p.}
 \tag{7}
\]

The relative Einstein defect used in the earlier rounds is

\[
 \mathcal E(g)=\inf_{\lambda\ne0}
 \frac{\int|\operatorname{Ric}-\lambda g|^2}{\lambda^2 V}.
\]

Minimizing this quadratic in \(1/\lambda\), with
\(S=\int s<0\), gives \(\lambda=R_2/S\), and hence

\[
 \mathcal E=4-\frac{S^2}{V R_2}
 =\frac{4P+L}{R_2}=\frac{4Q_\kappa}{R_2}
 =\boxed{\frac{4\bigl((p-A)+4D\bigr)}{2p-A+4D}}.
 \tag{8}
\]

The optimizer of this relative functional need not equal \(-\kappa\);
\(-\kappa\) instead minimizes the unnormalized squared residual.

In particular, putting
\(\theta=((p-A)+4D)/p\ge0\), one has

\[
 \frac{Q_\kappa}{\kappa^2V}=4\theta,
 \qquad \mathcal E=\frac{4\theta}{1+\theta}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Thus a sequence of negative-mean Kähler metrics has
\(\mathcal E\to0\) if and only if both

\[
 \frac{p-A}{p}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \frac{D}{p}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{10}
\]

This is an exact integral statement. The extra condition measures the
harmonic primitive canonical component and cannot be dropped.

When \(A>0\), the first condition is equivalently normalized-volume
saturation:

\[
 \frac{\kappa^2V}{2\pi^2 A}=\frac pA\longrightarrow1.
 \tag{11}
\]

At the exact canonical ray \([\omega]=t a\), \(t>0\), one has \(p=A\), and

\[
 P=16\pi^2D,\quad Q_\kappa=32\pi^2D,\quad
 \mathcal E=\frac{16D}{A+4D}.
 \tag{12}
\]

Conversely, under \(\bar s<0\), equality \(p=A\) forces this ray condition,
by (2). Therefore \(D=0\) together with \(p=A\) is equivalent to negative
Kähler–Einstein. Requiring only \(D=0\) gives merely negative cscK.

All of \(P,L,R_2,Q_\kappa,p,D\) are unchanged by constant rescaling in four
dimensions, with \(\kappa\) rescaled accordingly. An averaged curvature
error must be divided by \(\kappa^2\): the dimensionless quantity is
\(Q_\kappa/(\kappa^2V)\), not \(Q_\kappa/V\). Pointwise normalized Ricci
control implies this average control, since

\[
 \frac{Q_\kappa}{\kappa^2V}
 \le4\left(\frac{\|g^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}+\kappa I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}}
 {\kappa}\right)^2.
 \tag{13}
\]

There is no converse in this note without additional regularity hypotheses.

## 3. Unequal negative products: exact failure of a Ricci remainder

Fix closed hyperbolic surfaces of genera \(r,s\ge2\), with curvature \(-1\)
metrics \(h_1,h_2\). For \(\alpha,\beta>0\), put

\[
 g_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha^{-1}h_1\oplus\beta^{-1}h_2,
 \qquad m=(r-1)(s-1),\qquad A=8m.
\]

These are metrics on one fixed smooth product, with

\[
 V=\frac{16\pi^2m}{\alpha\beta},\quad
 s=-2(\alpha+\beta),\quad
 |\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2=(\alpha-\beta)^2,\quad D=L=0,
\]
\[
 p=A\frac{(\alpha+\beta)^2}{4\alpha\beta},\qquad
 p-A=A\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^2}{4\alpha\beta},\qquad
 \mathcal E=\frac{2(\alpha-\beta)^2}{\alpha^2+\beta^2}.
 \tag{14}
\]

To fix the scalar scale, take \(\alpha=1+t\), \(\beta=1-t\), \(0<t<1\).
Then \(s=-4\), \(\kappa=1\), and

\[
 p=\frac A{1-t^2},\qquad
 V=\frac{2\pi^2 A}{1-t^2},\qquad
 \|g^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}=t,
 \qquad \mathcal E=\frac{4t^2}{1+t^2}>0.
 \tag{15}
\]

Every such metric is locally symmetric: \(\nabla\operatorname{Rm}=0\), and
its Kähler form is parallel. Its self-dual Weyl spectrum is exactly
\((-2/3,1/3,1/3)\), independent of \(t\). Thus exact LB equality,
constant scalar curvature, a parallel Kähler form, and the exact Kähler
Weyl profile all coexist with nonzero Einstein defect. Fixing any
\(t\in(0,1)\) already refutes every proposed estimate
\(D\ge c\int|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2\), \(c>0\), even on this one
nondegenerate smooth metric.

For a different normalization, take \(\alpha=u\), \(\beta=u^{-1}\).
The volume is fixed, while
\(P=16\pi^2m(u-u^{-1})^2\to\infty\) and \(D=0\). This version does not have
a fixed scalar scale or a uniform full-curvature bound. Neither version
is a counterexample to Yau26: the same smooth product also admits its
equal-curvature Einstein metric.

## 4. Minimal Kähler surfaces: sharp energy comparisons

Suppose \(M=X\) is a minimal complex surface of general type, and write
\(A=c_1^2(X)>0\). LeBrun's Theorem 4.3 identifies the absolute Ricci-energy
infimum over all smooth metrics as \(8\pi^2 A\).
[LeBrun, Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0003068).
For a Kähler metric of negative mean scalar curvature, define

\[
 \Delta=R_2-8\pi^2 A
       =16\pi^2(p-A)+32\pi^2D.
 \tag{16}
\]

Equations (7) and (16) imply

\[
 \boxed{Q_\kappa\le\Delta\le2Q_\kappa,\qquad
 \frac{2\Delta}{R_2}\le\mathcal E\le\frac{4\Delta}{R_2}.}
 \tag{17}
\]

Both constants are sharp. Unequal negative products have \(D=0\) and
\(\Delta=2Q_\kappa\). A small Kähler-potential perturbation of a negative
Kähler–Einstein metric, within its canonical Kähler class and with
nonconstant scalar curvature, has \(p=A\), \(D>0\), and
\(\Delta=Q_\kappa\). Such perturbations exist: a smooth potential outside
the kernel of the scalar-curvature linearization changes scalar curvature
nontrivially, and sufficiently small perturbations preserve positivity.

Thus in this particular minimal Kähler category, approaching the absolute
Ricci-energy infimum does force vanishing relative integral Einstein
defect. It does not establish pointwise or geometric stability.

## 5. Blow-ups: actual minimizing sequences with a positive limiting defect

Let \(X\) be a minimal complex surface of general type and
\(\pi:M\to X\) the complex blow-up at \(k\) distinct points. Write

\[
 A_0=c_1^2(X)>0,\qquad a=c_1(K_M),\qquad
 T=a^2=A_0-k,\qquad
 \Delta=R_2-8\pi^2(A_0+k).
\]

Only actual complex blow-ups are considered here; no Kähler construction
on an additional \(S^1\times S^3\) connected sum is asserted.

For every Kähler class \(\Omega\) on \(M\), put \(u=\pi^*c_1(K_X)\).
The minimal canonical class is nef and big, so \(u\cdot\Omega>0\),
\(u^2=A_0\), and Hodge index gives
\((u\cdot\Omega)^2\ge A_0\Omega^2\). Since
\(a=u+\sum E_i\) and each effective exceptional divisor has
\(E_i\cdot\Omega>0\), one obtains \(a\cdot\Omega>0\) and

\[
 p=\frac{(a\cdot\Omega)^2}{\Omega^2}\ge A_0,
 \qquad \bar s<0.
 \tag{18}
\]

The inequality is strict when \(k>0\). Substituting \(A=T=A_0-k\) in
(3)–(4) yields

\[
 \boxed{\Delta=16\pi^2(p-A_0)+\frac L2
                   =16\pi^2(p-A_0)+32\pi^2D\ge0,}
 \tag{19}
\]
\[
 \boxed{P=8\pi^2k+\frac\Delta2,\qquad
 Q_\kappa=8\pi^2k+\frac\Delta2+\frac L4,}
 \tag{20}
\]
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal E
 =\frac{32\pi^2k+2\Delta+L}{8\pi^2(A_0+k)+\Delta}.}
 \tag{21}
\]

In particular, there is no extra \(L/4\) in the formula for \(P\) in (20).
It has already been included in \(\Delta/2\). Since (19) gives
\(0\le L\le2\Delta\), every Kähler sequence with \(\Delta\to0\) satisfies

\[
 P\to8\pi^2 k,\qquad
 Q_\kappa\to8\pi^2 k,\qquad
 \mathcal E\longrightarrow\frac{4k}{A_0+k}.
 \tag{22}
\]

The scale-aware average and its pointwise consequence are equally explicit:

\[
 \frac{Q_\kappa}{\kappa^2V}\longrightarrow\frac{4k}{A_0},\qquad
 \liminf\frac{\|g^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}+\kappa I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}}
 {\kappa}\ge\sqrt{\frac{k}{A_0}}.
 \tag{22a}
\]

The second statement is only a lower bound, obtained from (13), not an
assertion that the pointwise defect stays bounded or approaches that value.

These limits are realized on fixed smooth blow-ups, not just formally.
For example, take \(X=\Sigma_r\times\Sigma_s\) with its equal-curvature
negative product metric \(\omega_0\). It has no nonzero holomorphic vector
fields. For any distinct blow-up points and positive weights \(b_i\), the
Arezzo–Pacard theorem supplies negative cscK metrics in the classes

\[
 \Omega_\varepsilon=\pi^*[\omega_0]-\varepsilon^2\sum_{i=1}^k b_i E_i
\]

for all sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\).
[Arezzo–Pacard, Theorem 1.1](https://perso.pages.math.cnrs.fr/users/frank.pacard/Publications/43.pdf).
The complex surface and the blow-up points stay fixed. Here
\([\omega_0]=t\,c_1(K_X)\), \(t>0\), and direct intersection arithmetic gives

\[
 p_\varepsilon=
 \frac{(t A_0+\varepsilon^2\sum b_i)^2}
 {t^2 A_0-\varepsilon^4\sum b_i^2}\longrightarrow A_0.
 \tag{23}
\]

Since \(L=D=0\) exactly, (19) gives \(\Delta\to0\), whereas (22) gives
a positive limiting Einstein defect whenever \(k>0\). This approaches
the global all-metrics Ricci-energy infimum given by LeBrun's theorem.
It does not approach Einstein in the relative integral sense, and a
fortiori cannot be a sequence with vanishing normalized pointwise Ricci
defect. The obstruction in (22) is a fixed topological contribution,
not scalar variance or failure of LB equality.

## 6. What is inherited and what this calculation clarifies

The identity connecting LB remainder and Ricci-energy excess is already
classical, and is explicitly retained in the first-line correction.
This note does not rebrand it as a new estimate.

[Round 17, §6](round17_integral.md) already distinguishes scalar variance
\(L\), trace-free Ricci energy \(P\), and the mean-normalized volume gap.
Its equation (33) is the first identity in (7). In its notation
\(B=\int s^2-32\pi^2 A\), the Kähler specialization is simply
\(P=B/4\), by (6), while its volume gap is
\(\kappa^2V-2\pi^2 A=2\pi^2(p-A)\). This is not the LB remainder \(D\).

[Round 18](round18_periods.md) uses the separate \(B,P,L\) controls and
does not identify LB equality with Einstein equality.
[Round 21, equation (3) and §5](round21_comparison.md) already separates
the relative defect from absolute Ricci energy. The present formulas
give exact Kähler models for that distinction, including its different
behavior on minimal surfaces and fixed blow-ups.

The precise usable extra hypothesis is control of
\(\|a_g^-\|_2^2=p-a^2\), equivalently canonical-ray or normalized-volume
control in the positive-square setting, in addition to scalar variance.
For a blow-up one must not confuse \(a^2=A_0-k\) with the minimal-model
square \(A_0\): approaching \(p=A_0\) leaves primitive canonical energy
\(p-a^2\to k\), exactly as the actual metrics demonstrate.

# Ricci-energy selection, scalar coherence, and Einstein defect

## 1. The question this note resolves

The preceding Kähler calculation identified the limiting relative Einstein
defect of a constant-scalar-curvature blow-up sequence. Here we remove the
Kähler assumption and describe **every** sequence approaching the sharp
Ricci-energy bound. The remaining term is the scalar variance. An actual
conformal construction shows that this term cannot be dropped, even on a
fixed manifold admitting Kähler–Einstein metrics and with zero primitive
period cost. Conversely, choosing the negative Yamabe representative removes
this term without a spectral-gap assumption.

The finite estimates below follow from classical Seiberg–Witten and
Chern–Weil inequalities. They do not require the project’s integral
calibration lemma, its second-order estimates, or the Round21 spectral
criterion. This is a new deduction recorded in this research package, not
a claim of priority over the literature.

All manifolds are closed, connected, oriented, and four-dimensional.
Ricci norms are ordinary tensor norms; Weyl norms are endomorphism norms
on two-forms. Write
\[
 T=2\chi+3\tau,\quad V=\operatorname{Vol}(g),\quad
 S_2=\int s^2,\quad R=\int|\operatorname{Ric}|^2,\quad
 P=\int|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2,\quad
 L=\int(s-\bar s)^2.
 \tag{1}
\]
Integrals use the current metric.

## 2. Precise topological input and the finite theorem

Fix numbers \(A>0\), \(d\ge0\), with \(T=A-d\). For the metric in question,
assume an actual polarized monopole Spin\(^c\) structure whose class \(a\)
satisfies \(p=(a_g^+)^2\ge A\). Its equations must be solvable throughout
the conformal class, so that the classical scalar and scalar–Weyl
inequalities apply. A formal intersection vector is not enough.

The class may depend on \(g\). No class-square upper bound, scalar band,
Sobolev constant, injectivity-radius bound, or diameter bound is assumed.

In the standard application,
\[
 M=X\#k\overline{\mathbb {CP}}^{\,2}
       \#m(S^1\times S^3),\qquad
 A=c_1^2(X)>0,\quad d=k+4m,
 \tag{2}
\]
where \(X\) is a minimal complex surface of general type. The polarized
class selection and its \(b_+=1\) interpretation are as in LeBrun’s
Proposition 3.2 and the [application closure](lebrun_stability_closure.md).
LeBrun’s Theorem 4.3 identifies the lower bound below as the actual
all-metrics infimum in this application.

Put
\[
 C=8\pi^2A,\quad H=8\pi^2d,\quad
 J=R-(C+H).
 \tag{3}
\]

**Theorem 1 (finite energy-selection bound).** Under the stated input,
\(J\ge0\), and
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 H-\sqrt{J(C+J)}\ \le P\le H+J .
 \quad} \tag{4}
\]
The lower bound may of course be replaced by its positive part.
With
\[
 \mathcal E(g)=\inf_{\lambda\ne0}
 \frac{\int|\operatorname{Ric}-\lambda g|^2}{\lambda^2 V},
 \tag{5}
\]
one has the exact formula
\[
 \mathcal E(g)=\frac{4P+L}{R}
 \tag{6}
\]
and consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 -\frac{4\sqrt{J(C+J)}}{C+H+J}
 \le
 \mathcal E(g)-\frac{4H+L}{C+H+J}
 \le\frac{4J}{C+H+J}.}
 \tag{7}
\]

Thus the Einstein defect is not determined by absolute Ricci energy
alone. Near its minimum, its two contributions are the topological
quantity \(4H\) and the scalar variance \(L\).

### Proof

Define the classical LeBrun remainder
\[
 D=\frac1{4\pi^2}\int
       \left(\frac{s^2}{24}+\frac12|W^+|^2\right)-\frac p2.
 \tag{8}
\]
The required classical inputs are
\[
 S_2\ge32\pi^2p,\qquad
 \int(-s+\sqrt6|W^+|)^2\ge72\pi^2p,\qquad D\ge0.
 \tag{9}
\]
These are the scalar estimate, Theorem 2.3, and Proposition 4.1 of
[LeBrun, arXiv:math/0003068v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0003068).
No assertion about the equality set of a different curvature functional
is used.

Chern–Weil gives the exact identity
\[
 R=8\pi^2(2p-T)+32\pi^2D,
 \qquad J=16\pi^2(p-A)+32\pi^2D.
 \tag{10}
\]
In particular
\[
 0\le p-A\le J/(16\pi^2),\qquad 0\le D\le J/(32\pi^2).
 \tag{11}
\]
Let
\[
 q=-s+\sqrt6|W^+|,\quad t=s+2\sqrt6|W^+|,\quad
 \delta=144\pi^2D,\quad
 \Delta=\int q^2-72\pi^2p,\quad U=\int t^2.
 \tag{12}
\]
Here \(q\) need not be positive. The unsigned inequality in (9) suffices.
Pointwise expansion and integration yield
\[
 \delta=\Delta+\tfrac12 U,\qquad \Delta\ge0,\qquad
 s=(t-2q)/3.
 \tag{13}
\]
For the scalar surplus \(B_p=S_2-32\pi^2p\ge0\), this implies
\[
 9B_p=4\delta-U-4\int qt.
 \tag{14}
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz and \(U\le2\delta\) give
\[
 B_p\le\frac49
       \left[\delta+\sqrt{2\delta(72\pi^2p+\delta)}\right].
 \tag{15}
\]
Using (11), \(\delta\le9J/2\), and the convenient bound
\(72\pi^2p+\delta\le72\pi^2A+9J\), we obtain
\[
 \boxed{0\le B_p\le2J+4\sqrt{J(C+J)}.}
 \tag{16}
\]
There is room to sharpen these numerical constants; their optimality
is not asserted.

Since \(P=R-S_2/4\), (10) also gives
\[
 P=H+J-8\pi^2(p-A)-B_p/4.
 \tag{17}
\]
The upper bound in (4) follows immediately. For the lower bound, (11)
and (16) give
\[
 P-H\ge J-J/2-\bigl(J/2+\sqrt{J(C+J)}\bigr)
          =-\sqrt{J(C+J)}.
 \tag{18}
\]
Finally, expansion in \(1/\lambda\) yields
\[
 \mathcal E=4-\frac{(\int s)^2}{VR}
            =\frac{4P+L}{R}.
 \tag{19}
\]
Here \(R\ge C>0\). If \(\int s=0\), the infimum is \(4\), approached
as \(|\lambda|\to\infty\); otherwise the minimizer is
\(\lambda=R/\int s\). This covers both cases without falsely treating a
Ricci-flat metric as zero relative defect. Substitution proves (7).

## 3. All minimizing sequences, not only Kähler sequences

**Corollary 2.** In the setting of Theorem 1, if \(J(g_j)\to0\), then
\[
 p_j\to A,\quad D_j\to0,\quad
 \int s_j^2\to32\pi^2A,\quad P_j\to8\pi^2d.
 \tag{20}
\]
Moreover
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal E(g_j)
   =\frac{4d}{A+d}
       +\frac{L_j}{8\pi^2(A+d)}+o(1).}
 \tag{21}
\]
In particular
\[
 \liminf\mathcal E(g_j)\ge\frac{4d}{A+d},
 \tag{22}
\]
and \(\mathcal E(g_j)\to4d/(A+d)\) is equivalent to \(L_j\to0\).
For \(d=0\), vanishing relative Einstein defect is equivalent to
vanishing scalar variance along such a sequence.

Indeed (11) and (16) give (20). Since \(0\le L_j\le\int s_j^2\),
the variance is bounded; (7) therefore gives (21).
This assertion imposes no Kähler condition, no constant-scalar-curvature
condition, and no geometric compactness hypothesis.

For varying topologies the uniform formulation is (7). For example,
write \(r=d/A\), \(j=J/(8\pi^2A)\), and
\(\ell=L/(8\pi^2A)\). Then the difference between
\(\mathcal E\) and \((4r+\ell)/(1+r+j)\) lies between
\(-4\sqrt{j(1+j)}/(1+r+j)\) and \(4j/(1+r+j)\).
Small *relative* energy excess, rather than small unnormalized excess,
is the appropriate hypothesis when \(A\) varies.

### A direct scalar-coherence criterion on the original metric

For an ordinary monopole solution, put \(\rho=|\Phi|^2\) and let
\(\mathcal F\) be its real determinant curvature. The integrated spinor
identity and Hodge projection give
\[
 B_p=\|s+\rho\|_2^2+8\|\nabla\Phi\|_2^2+
        8\|\mathcal F^+-(\mathcal F^+)_H\|_2^2.
 \tag{22a}
\]
This retains the harmonic-projection loss in the classical scalar
inequality. It also follows from the [Round17 identity](round17_integral.md)
by subtracting the harmonic anti-self-dual contribution.

If \(s\ge-S\), the maximum principle yields \(0\le\rho\le S\).
Since \(|d\rho|^2\le4S|\nabla\Phi|^2\), Poincaré gives
\[
 \sqrt L\le\|s+\rho\|_2+
       \sqrt{4S/\lambda_1}\,\|\nabla\Phi\|_2.
\]
Joint Cauchy–Schwarz in the two displayed losses of (22a) proves
\[
 \boxed{L\le\left(1+\frac{S}{2\lambda_1}\right)B_p.}
 \tag{22b}
\]
Thus bounded \(S/\lambda_1\) is one sufficient condition for scalar
coherence along a minimizing sequence. It requires no weighted equations,
strict negative scalar band, or prior bound on Ricci error. The stronger
weighted variance estimate in the companion converse theorem instead
controls \(L\) linearly by \(D\), under its stated negative-band assumptions.
Neither argument supplies a spectral gap for an arbitrary degenerating
family.

## 4. Removing scalar variance by conformal normalization

The variance in (21) is essential on the original metric. It can nevertheless
be removed by a mathematically legitimate change of representative.

**Corollary 3 (Yamabe representatives of all minimizing sequences).**
In the standard application (2), or whenever Theorem 1's input holds in
the conformal class, let \(\widehat g\) be its negative constant-scalar-
curvature Yamabe representative. Then
\[
 \widehat J\le J,\qquad
 R(g)-R(\widehat g)
       =\tfrac13\bigl(S_2(g)-S_2(\widehat g)\bigr),\qquad
 P(g)-P(\widehat g)
       =\tfrac1{12}\bigl(S_2(g)-S_2(\widehat g)\bigr).
 \tag{23}
\]
Consequently every Ricci-energy-minimizing sequence can be replaced,
within its own conformal classes, by one satisfying
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal E(\widehat g_j)\longrightarrow
                  \frac{4d}{A+d}.}
 \tag{24}
\]
For \(d=0\) there is also the finite bound
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal E(\widehat g)
          \le\frac{4J}{8\pi^2A+J}.}
 \tag{25}
\]

### Proof of the conformal step

A nonzero actual monopole projection rules out nonnegative scalar
curvature in this conformal class by the spinor Weitzenböck formula.
The Yamabe theorem therefore provides a representative with negative
constant scalar curvature. Four-dimensional Hodge star on two-forms,
\(p\), and \(\int|W^+|^2\) are conformally invariant.

For completeness, the scalar-energy minimization in a negative conformal
class has a direct verification. Rescale \(\widehat g\) to
\(s_{\widehat g}=-b<0\), and write \(g=u^2\widehat g\).
With \(\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla\), conformal covariance gives
\[
 \int s_g^2\,d\mu_g
 =\int\left(-b-6u^{-1}\Delta_{\widehat g}u\right)^2d\mu_{\widehat g}.
 \tag{26}
\]
Integration by parts yields
\[
 \int u^{-1}\Delta u=\int u^{-2}|du|^2\ge0.
 \tag{27}
\]
Expanding (26) shows \(S_2(g)\ge b^2V_{\widehat g}=S_2(\widehat g)\).
The Chern–Weil identity
\[
 R=4\int|W^+|^2+\tfrac13 S_2-8\pi^2T
 \tag{28}
\]
and \(P=R-S_2/4\) prove (23).
The lower bound \(R(\widehat g)\ge C+H\) remains valid for the same
polarization and actual class, so \(0\le\widehat J\le J\).
Now (21) with \(\widehat L=0\) proves (24).
When \(d=0\), (4) gives \(\widehat P\le\widehat J\), whence
\[
 \mathcal E(\widehat g)=
 \frac{4\widehat P}{C+\widehat J}
 \le\frac{4\widehat J}{C+\widehat J}
 \le\frac{4J}{C+J}.
 \tag{29}
\]

This does not estimate the distance between \(g\) and \(\widehat g\).
The conformal scale-splitting example shows that such an estimate cannot
be inferred from \(J\to0\). Nor does an integral estimate for \(\widehat g\)
give a pointwise Ricci estimate.

[Round14](round14_global.md) already carried out Yamabe normalization for
a particular shear construction. The assertion here concerns arbitrary
input conformal classes and all Ricci-energy-minimizing sequences; it does
not count the act of Yamabe normalization as a new method.

## 5. An explicit energy exclusion for all Einstein candidates

If a metric in this topological range is Einstein, then \(P=0\).
Theorem 1 forces
\[
 J(C+J)\ge H^2,\qquad
 \boxed{
 J\ge\frac{\sqrt{C^2+4H^2}-C}{2}
   =4\pi^2\bigl(\sqrt{A^2+4d^2}-A\bigr).}
 \tag{30}
\]
For \(d>0\), any Einstein metric must therefore stay a positive
absolute-energy distance above the Ricci-energy infimum.
This is a consequence of the classical curvature inequalities, not
a new nonexistence theorem.

More generally, if \(0\le P/R\le\alpha\), then
\[
 H\le\alpha(C+H+J)+\sqrt{J(C+J)}.
 \tag{31}
\]
For the negative Yau26 normalization
\(\delta_-=\|\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}<1\),
\[
 P\le4\delta_-^2V,\quad R\ge4(1-\delta_-)^2V,\quad
 \alpha=\frac{\delta_-^2}{(1-\delta_-)^2}
 \tag{32}
\]
is admissible.

Thus, at any fixed positive blow-up/handle ratio \(d/A\), a pointwise
near-Einstein sequence cannot simultaneously be a relative Ricci-energy-
minimizing sequence. This is a usable restriction on a construction
strategy. It does not exclude inputs whose energy stays above that
minimum, and it does not decide whether the smooth manifold admits an
Einstein metric elsewhere.

## 6. The resulting first-line picture

Three statements now have different, explicit roles.

1. The existing integral gap near the forbidden \(2/3\) topology uses
   the project's integral calibration proof. It remains a theorem about
   exclusion of near-Einstein metrics in its actual-monopole range.
2. The present theorem describes every sequence minimizing the classical
   *absolute* Ricci energy. It identifies the topological contribution to
   the relative defect and the independent scalar-coherence term.
3. The [fixed-metric converse](line1_non_kahler_bridge.md) uses the earlier
   actual-form remainder and a scalar spectral gap. It starts from
   curvature-functional and period data, not from an assumed Ricci-small
   input. The [scale-splitting example](line1_conformal_neck.md)
   explains why the scalar-coherence hypothesis has content.

The prior actual-form supply uses \(t=1/2\), already an allowed negative
Kähler–Einstein root of Round21. Substituting that value into
\(\operatorname{dist}(t,\{0,1/2\})\) alone gives zero and proves no new
obstruction. The additional conclusions here come from curvature-energy
identities, actual topology, and control or removal of scalar variance.

The remaining Yau26 task is not concealed by these deductions:
small integrated Ricci error must still be connected to the required
pointwise input or to existence of an exact Einstein metric on the same
smooth manifold. Neither follows merely from minimizing the classical
energy. No new Yau26 branch is declared solved here.

# Einstein-defect bridges without a Kähler assumption

23 September 2026. Two conclusions are distinguished: control of the
**original metric** uses the scalar-band variance estimate and its spectral
factor; control of a **conformal Yamabe representative** needs neither.
The metrics in both statements need not initially be Kähler. The
all-metric absolute-energy selection theorem is in the companion
[energy-selection paper](line1_energy_selection.md), not repeated here.

## 1. Common hypotheses and exact accounting

Let \((M,g)\) be a closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold. A
specified Spin\(^c\) class \(a\) is assumed to have ordinary
Seiberg–Witten solutions for every metric conformal to \(g\). The class
may be selected separately for each metric, but is fixed as its conformal
weight varies. Write
\[
 p=(a_g^+)^2>0,\quad A=a^2,\quad T=2\chi+3\tau,\quad V=\operatorname{Vol}(g),
\]
\[
 S_2=\int s^2,\quad P=\int|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2,\quad
 L=\int(s-\bar s)^2,\quad R_2=P+S_2/4,
\]
\[
 D=\frac1{4\pi^2}\int\left(\frac{s^2}{24}+\frac{|W^+|^2}{2}\right)-\frac p2.
 \tag{1}
\]
Weyl norms are Hilbert–Schmidt operator norms. Classical monopole
inequalities give \(D\ge0\) and \(B_p:=S_2-32\pi^2p\ge0\).
Chern–Weil then gives the exact identities
\[
 R_2=8\pi^2(2p-T)+32\pi^2D,\qquad
 P+B_p/4=8\pi^2(p-T)+32\pi^2D.
 \tag{2}
\]
These identities and the scalar inequality are classical inputs, not new
existence results. Normalized weighted derivations are given in
[normalized monopole derivation](round3_monopole_input.md), Section 6.

## 2. The original-metric bridge

Assume \(-S\le s\le-s_0<0\), and put
\[
 K=S/\sqrt{s_0},\quad C_0=(1+3S/(2s_0))^2,\quad
 C_{\rm var}=\frac23+\frac{4C_0}{3}+\frac{2K^2}{\lambda_1(g)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\lambda_1(g)>0\) is the first positive scalar Laplace eigenvalue.
The actual-form remainder proved in
[scalar-band remainder](line1_scalar_band_remainder.md),
equation (11), gives \(L\le144\pi^2C_{\rm var}D\).

Let \(\kappa=-\bar s/4>0\) and
\(Q_\kappa=\int|\operatorname{Ric}+\kappa g|^2=P+L/4\).
**Theorem 1.** Without assuming any Ricci bound,
\[
 Q_\kappa\le8\pi^2(p-T)+(32\pi^2+36\pi^2C_{\rm var})D.
 \tag{4}
\]
In particular, define the nonnegative supply
\[
 \mathcal P=(p-A)+(A-T)_+,\quad
 U=8\pi^2\mathcal P+(32\pi^2+36\pi^2C_{\rm var})D.
 \tag{5}
\]
For the relative integral Einstein defect one has
\[
 \boxed{Q_\kappa\le U,\qquad
 \mathcal E(g):=\inf_{\lambda\ne0}
 \frac{\int|\operatorname{Ric}-\lambda g|^2}{\lambda^2V}
 \le\frac{4U}{4\kappa^2V+U}\le\frac{16U}{s_0^2V}.}
 \tag{6}
\]
**Proof.** Drop the nonnegative \(B_p/4\) in (2), and add the
scalar-variance bound divided by four. Also
\(p-A=\|a_g^-\|_2^2\ge0\), hence \(p-T\le\mathcal P\).
Direct minimization in \(1/\lambda\) gives
\(\mathcal E=4Q_\kappa/(4\kappa^2V+Q_\kappa)\);
\(\kappa\ge s_0/4\) completes the proof.

For a sequence in a fixed scalar band, sufficient conditions are
\(\mathcal P/V\to0\), \(D/V\to0\), and
\(D/(\lambda_1V)\to0\). A uniform spectral gap suffices for the last
condition but is not mandatory. It cannot simply be deleted. At
\(D=\mathcal P=0\), the conclusion is negative Kähler–Einstein;
\(D=0\) alone gives only cscK. Unequal-curvature hyperbolic products
have \(D=0\) and \(p-A>0\).

## 3. Supplying an actual form and its joint analytic budget

Constant-rescale to \(\bar s=-4\), and use the scalar-band parameters
of that normalization. Then \(r:=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2\le U/V\).
Suppose \(U/V\le r_0\), \(D/V\le d_0\), and
\((-a^2)_+/V\le a_0\). Sections 6–7 of the scalar-band note supply a
smooth actual weighted-monopole form \(\omega\), for every prescribed
\(\eta>0\), with
\[
 \|\omega\|_\infty\le\frac{\sqrt3}{2}K,\quad
 B_\omega:=\fint|\Delta_H\omega|^2
       \le C(S,s_0,8+2r_0,a_0,d_0),
 \tag{7}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_{\omega,1/2}&=\frac12\left(\frac{|\omega|^2}{3}I-\omega\otimes\omega\right),\\
 d_{1/2}&:=\fint\left(|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2
       +|W^+-A_{\omega,1/2}|^2+(|\omega|^2-2)^2\right)\\
 &\le\frac{4U}{V}+\frac{16\pi^2(10C_0+8)}3\frac D V+\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently
\[
 (1+B_\omega)d_{1/2}\le C\left(\frac U V+\frac D V+\eta\right).
 \tag{9}
\]
The constant in (9) depends only on the displayed budgets. To check the
inputs, \(\fint|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\le8+2r_0\); the bounded-spinor
Hessian estimate controls \(\Delta_H\omega\) without derivatives of
the conformal weight or \(W^-\). This is an explicit invocation of the
inherited Hessian lemma, not a consequence of finite algebra checks.
If the chosen class additionally satisfies \(a^2\ge T\), Chern–Weil
gives \((-a^2)_+/V\le r_0/(8\pi^2)\), so no independent square-cost
bound is needed in this special case.

Unlike a circular composition, Ricci control in (7)–(9) is an **output**
of Theorem 1. However, \(1/2\) is the realizable Kähler–Einstein root
in [Round21](round21_analytic.md); (9) is a calibrated-form and source-budget
statement, not a forbidden-root contradiction or an exact metric repair.

## 4. A conformal bridge without scalar-band or spectral assumptions

Return to only the hypotheses of Section 1. Define
\[
 \theta(g)=\frac{p-T+4D(g)}p\ge0.
 \tag{10}
\]
Nonnegativity follows from (2). **Theorem 2.** The conformal class has a
negative constant-scalar-curvature Yamabe representative \(\widehat g\)
satisfying
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal E(\widehat g)
       \le\frac{4\theta(g)}{1+\theta(g)}.}
 \tag{11}
\]
**Proof.** The Yamabe theorem supplies a minimizer. Its scalar curvature
cannot be nonnegative because the same conformal Spin\(^c\) structure
has \(p>0\) and an actual solution. Thus its Yamabe constant \(Y<0\).
Normalize \(\widehat g=u^2g\) by \(\int u^4=1\). Then
\[
 Y=\int(6|du|^2+su^2)\ge-\|s_-\|_2,
 \quad S_2(\widehat g)=Y^2\le S_2(g).
 \tag{12}
\]
The quantities \(p\) and \(\int|W^+|^2\) are conformally invariant,
so \(D(\widehat g)\le D(g)\). Since \(\widehat g\) has constant
scalar curvature, \(\mathcal E(\widehat g)=4P(\widehat g)/R_2(\widehat g)\).
By (2) and \(B_p(\widehat g)\ge0\), this is at most
\[
 \frac{4[p-T+4D(\widehat g)]}{2p-T+4D(\widehat g)}.
\]
The expression increases with \(D\), its derivative having the sign of
\(p>0\). Replacing \(D(\widehat g)\) by \(D(g)\) proves (11).

Theorem 2 changes the metric conformally; it does not remove the
spectral term from Theorem 1 on the original metric. Neither conclusion
upgrades integral approximation to pointwise Ricci pinching or to an
Einstein metric. Relative to Round 17, the direction is reversed:
Ricci and scalar errors are derived here from actual period/topological
data and LeBrun saturation instead of being supplied as hypotheses.

Sources: [LeBrun, Propositions 4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0003068),
the classical Yamabe theorem, the scalar-band remainder cited above, and
the [normalized monopole inequalities](round3_monopole_input.md).
The [algebra checker](line1_ricci_remainder_check.py) checks finite algebra, including the
companion energy-selection identities; it does not certify these PDE inputs.
