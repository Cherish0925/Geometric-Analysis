\section{Setting and exact deficits}
Let $M$ be a fixed critical Ishida--LeBrun manifold, with
\[
 D=A/3,\qquad
 \delta_i=\|g_i^{-1}(\Ric_{g_i}+g_i)\|_{L^\infty}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For a selected sign monopole class $a$ (fixed after taking a subsequence),
the quantitative decomposition in
\texttt{workspace/codex\_loop/work/quantitative\_deficit\_analysis.md}
gives
\[
 R_i=O(A\delta_i^2),\qquad
 \Delta_{a,i}=O(A\delta_i^2),
\]
and therefore
\[
 D_{\rm amp,i}+D_{\rm quad,i}+D_{\rm Hodge,i}+D_{\rm eig,i}\to0,
 \qquad J_{A,i}\to0.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Writing $w_i=|W^+_i|$, $q_i=-s_i+\sqrt6w_i$, and using the worker's
rescaled variables $\psi_i,\varphi_i$, one also has
\[
 \|f_i\varphi_i+6\sqrt2\pi H_i\|_2\to0,
 \qquad
 \left\||2\pi H_i|-\frac{q_i}{3\sqrt2}\right\|_2\to0,    \tag{2}
\]
where $H_i$ is the harmonic self-dual representative of $a^+$ and
$f_i\to\sqrt{q_i}$.  The curvature completion gives
\[
 s_i\to-4\quad\hbox{uniformly},\qquad
 w_i\to\frac1{2\sqrt6}\quad\hbox{in }L^2,
 \qquad q_i\to\frac92\quad\hbox{in }L^2.                  \tag{3}
\]
The rescaled spinor normalization in (1)--(2) is not the same as the
unrescaled Seiberg--Witten normalization sometimes used in the literature;
constants must not be mixed between them.

\section{A rigorous regular-patch extraction statement}
Suppose that a sequence of unit balls $B_1(p_i)$ is noncollapsed:
\[
 \Vol_{g_i}B_1(p_i)\ge v>0.                                \tag{4}
\]
Cheeger--Naber Theorem~1.13 gives, in dimension four and under bounded
Ricci curvature,
\[
 \Vol\!\left(T_r\{x\in B_1(p_i):r_h(x)\le r\}\right)
 \le C(v)r^4.                                               \tag{5}
\]
This concerns harmonic radius, not the stronger curvature regularity scale.
Choose $\rho=\rho(v)>0$ so small that the right side of (5), with $2\rho$,
is less than half a Bishop--Gromov lower bound for $\Vol B_{1/2}(p_i)$.
Then a subset of uniformly positive volume in $B_{1/2}(p_i)$ is covered by
balls $B_\rho(x_i)$ on which the harmonic radius stays uniformly positive.
Thus (4) yields positive-measure regular patches, not a uniform harmonic
radius on the whole ball or the whole manifold.

In harmonic coordinates on a smaller patch,
\[
 g_i\to g_\infty\quad\hbox{strongly in }C^{1,\beta}_{\rm loc},
 \qquad g_i\rightharpoonup g_\infty\quad\hbox{in }W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}
\]
for every finite $p$ and suitable $\beta<1$.  The harmonic-coordinate
Ricci equation is
\[
 (\Ric g_i)_{ab}=-\frac12g_i^{kl}\partial_k\partial_l(g_i)_{ab}
                 +Q_{ab}(g_i,\partial g_i).                  \tag{6}
\]
Since $E_i:=\Ric(g_i)+g_i\to0$ in $L^\infty$, (6) passes to the limit in
the distributional sense and gives
\[
 \Ric(g_\infty)=-g_\infty.                                  \tag{7}
\]
Elliptic regularity then makes $g_\infty$ smooth.  Interior $W^{2,p}$
estimates applied after identifying the smooth limit yield strong
$W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}$ convergence, enough to pass algebraic curvature
expressions in $L^p$.  One must not instead assert that $E_i\to0$ in
$C^\alpha$ or that $g_i\to g_\infty$ in $C^{2,\alpha}$; no derivatives of
$E_i$ are controlled.

For the fixed critical manifold the deficits above are absolute
$O(A\delta_i^2)$ quantities, so they restrict to every such patch.  Strong
$W^{2,p}$ convergence therefore passes (3) and the nonnegative algebraic
spectral defect to the limit.  In particular,
\[
 s_\infty=-4,\qquad |W^+_\infty|=\frac1{2\sqrt6}.             \tag{8}
\]
Local elliptic compactness for the harmonic forms $H_i$, together with
(2), gives a harmonic self-dual limit $H_\infty$ satisfying
\[
 |H_\infty|=\frac{3}{4\sqrt2\pi}>0.                          \tag{9}
\]
The limiting SW quadratic form is consequently nonzero.  The equality
$D_{\rm eig}=0$ says it spans the lowest eigenspace and saturates the sharp
trace-free $3\times3$ estimate
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(W^+)\ge-\sqrt{\frac23}|W^+|.
\]
Hence
\[
 \lambda_{\min}=-\frac16,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Spec}(W^+_\infty)
   =\left(-\frac16,\frac1{12},\frac1{12}\right).             \tag{10}
\]
Thus the worker's amplitude, Hodge projection, and nonnegative eigenvalue
defects really do force the full calibrated spectrum on every regular
patch to which they pass.

\section{Superseded Step-5 branch: do not use as a conclusion}
The spectrum (10) has a simple negative eigenvalue and a double positive
eigenvalue.  In the notation $\alpha\ge\beta\ge\gamma$ it lies at
\[
 \alpha=\beta=\frac1{12},\qquad \gamma=-\frac16,
 \qquad \rho=\beta/\alpha=1,                                \tag{11}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{\det W^+}{|W^+|^3}=-\frac1{3\sqrt6}.                  \tag{12}
\]
This is the opposite boundary from the simple-largest, $\det W^+>0$
branch used by many modern conformal-Kaehler theorems.  In particular, the
formula $\alpha^{2/3}g$ associated with a simple positive largest
eigenline cannot be imported into (11).

Derdzinski's original local two-eigenvalue theorem instead implies that,
after orienting the simple lowest eigenline on a local double cover, there
is a positive function $f$ such that
\[
 \widetilde g=f^2g_\infty
\]
is Kaehler with negative scalar curvature $\widetilde s$.  Weyl conformal
weight and (10) give
\[
 f^2\widetilde s=-1.                                         \tag{13}
\]
Using the div--grad Laplacian, the four-dimensional scalar-curvature
transformation, $s_{g_\infty}=-4$, and (13) give
\[
 \Delta_{g_\infty}f=-\frac12f.                               \tag{14}
\]
Positive solutions of (14) are locally possible and need not be constant
on noncompact pointed limits.  Thus a smooth Einstein regular patch with
(10) is locally conformally Kaehler, not contradictory.

For reference, the standard Einstein--Weyl Weitzenbock identity in these
endomorphism conventions is
\[
 \Delta|W^+|^2=2|\nabla W^+|^2+s|W^+|^2-36\det W^+.          \tag{15}
\]
At (10), $|W^+|^2=1/24$, $\det W^+=-1/864$, and $s=-4$.
Since the norm is constant, (15) yields
\[
 |\nabla W^+|^2=\frac1{16},                                  \tag{16}
\]
not $1/24$.  The model is therefore nonparallel and is compatible with the
Bochner identity.

On a closed manifold, by contrast, integrating (14) would give
$0=-\frac12\int f<0$.  Equivalently, LeBrun's exact saturation produces a
global almost-Kaehler form, and Armstrong then makes an Einstein saturated
metric Kaehler--Einstein.  A Kaehler metric with $s=-4$ has spectrum
$(-2/3,1/3,1/3)$ and $|W^+|^2=2/3$, contradicting (8).  The obstruction is
therefore global and compact, not local.

\section{Weak Bianchi control and compactness distinctions}
The identity relating $\delta W^+$ to the Cotton tensor contains derivatives
of $\Ric^\circ$ and $s$.  The estimate $\|\Ric+g\|_\infty\le\delta$ gives no
pointwise or $L^2$ estimate for these derivatives.  What follows directly is
only a distributional bound after moving derivatives to a compactly
supported test tensor $T$:
\[
 |\langle\delta W^+,T\rangle|
 \le C\delta\bigl(\|\nabla T\|_{L^1}+\|T\|_{L^1}\bigr),      \tag{17}
\]
up to the conventional algebraic projection constants.  Any strong Cotton
or $\nabla W^+$ estimate requires additional regularity or an elliptic
identity; it cannot be assumed from pinching.

A single noncollapsed ball plus (5) produces regular patches even if
curvature concentrates elsewhere.  It does not recover the smooth topology,
remove orbifold points, control ALE bubble trees, bound the diameter, or stop
collapse escaping to infinity.  Thin regions of vanishing volume may violate
the calibrated Weyl profile at negligible total cost.  Likewise, the
unconditional Walsh argument gives $L^1$ spatial orthogonality; phase
separation additionally requires spectral/eigenline control.

The same distinction applies to the positive branch.  Myers gives a diameter
bound, but a volume lower bound does not by itself remove curvature
concentration or identify the original smooth topology.  Moreover no
universal normalized volume lower bound can hold: round lens-space quotients
have exact positive Einstein metrics and volumes tending to zero.  Such
collapse already has the desired conclusion and should be handled by lifting,
equivariant descent, or classification rather than excluded as a prerequisite.

\section{Step-6 correction}
The conclusion above was overturned by retaining the full product-rule terms in
$\delta(\omega\otimes\omega)=0$.  For constant distinct eigenvalues, projection
first along $\omega$ gives $\delta\omega=0$, and projection orthogonal to
$\omega$ gives $\omega_{ab}\nabla^a\omega_{cd}=0$; nondegeneracy then forces
$\nabla\omega=0$.  This contradicts the Einstein--Weyl identity, which requires
$|\nabla W^+|^2=1/16$ at the calibrated spectrum.  Derdzi\'nski Proposition 5
independently fixes the conformal K\"ahler factor as $(24|W^+|^2)^{1/3}$, hence
constant here.  The corrected theorem and collapse reduction are in
\texttt{workspace/notes/step6\_local\_projector\_obstruction\_and\_collapse\_reduction.tex}.
