## 1. The strict subthreshold metric lies in the closure of smooth symplectic pairs

Choose a smooth monotone cutoff \(\chi\), identically one near the bolt and identically zero near the exact-CH collar. Rotate the diagonal eigenframe in the collapsing/fibre plane by \(\epsilon\chi\):
\[
H_\epsilon=R(\epsilon\chi)H_*R(\epsilon\chi)^T.
\]
Because the rotation is constant in a neighborhood of the bolt, it is the pullback of the smooth bolt metric by a fixed right translation there; because it is the identity near the collar, the marked CH boundary germ is unchanged. Thus \(g_\epsilon\) is a smooth exact-collar metric on the same marked blow-up cell.

Let \(\Omega_\epsilon\) be the invariant closed self-dual form obtained by integrating the self-duality ODE inward from the unchanged host Kähler form on the collar. Step 40's variation formula becomes, after integrating the pure-rotation formula by parts,
\[
p'(0)=-\pi\int\chi'(r)\Phi(r)\,dr,
\qquad \Phi=\psi_+(0)\frac{\phi_f}{\psi_+}>0
\quad (r>0).
\]
The derivative of \(\chi\) is nonpositive and is strictly negative on a nonempty interior interval; hence
\[
p'(0)>0
\]
(up to reversing the sign convention for the rotation). This proof uses no numerical sign of \(H_{33}-H_{11}\). By the invariant ODE uniqueness lemma, any nonzero invariant self-dual solution with \(p\ne0\) is nowhere zero. Therefore \(\Omega_\epsilon\) is symplectic for every sufficiently small nonzero \(\epsilon\). Finally, the Ricci action is continuous in the smooth metric two-jet, so the certified strict margin implies \(J(g_\epsilon)<1\) for all sufficiently small \(|\epsilon|\).

This proves the existence statement needed for the global construction: the harmonic-zero cell is in the closure of genuinely symplectic, conformally almost-Kähler, exact-collar subthreshold cells. Inserting them at disjoint marked exact-CH sites on compact ball-quotient covers preserves the pure-blow-up strict sub-four upper construction. Transfer to a merely CH-close ramified Kähler–Einstein site would additionally require a metric–form pair interpolation, not only the metric interpolation already established. The result does not extend to handle additions merely from this local argument.

The floating cutoff used in the script has \(\chi=1\) for \(u\le0.12\), \(\chi=0\) for \(u\ge0.62\). It gives \(p/\epsilon=-0.175376+o(1)\). The tested original actions remain below one through \(|\epsilon|=0.02\):

## 2. Correct period angle

On a closed pure-blow-up host, in \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\) normalization, use the **pulled-back host** canonical class and write
\[
[\psi]=\pm2\pi K_X-\sum_i s_i\,\mathrm{PD}(E_i),
\qquad s_i=\int_{E_i}\psi .
\]
Orthogonality and \(E_i^2=-1\) give
\[
[\psi]^2=4\pi^2A-\sum_i s_i^2,
\qquad
\eta=\frac{|2\pi K_X\cdot[\psi]|}{\sqrt{4\pi^2A[\psi]^2}}
=\left(1-\frac{\sum_i s_i^2}{4\pi^2A}\right)^{-1/2}.
\]
Thus the host-pairing numerator is fixed, but \(\eta\) changes at quadratic order when exceptional periods open. The Step-40 statement that it remains exactly one is corrected. Here \(s_i=6p_i\) relative to the profile-scale periods used below; if one instead pairs with the canonical class of the blown-up symplectic manifold, its exceptional components contribute linearly, so the two pairings must not be conflated. This quadratic change occurs at the same order as the local period stiffness and must be retained in AKΓ comparisons.

## 3. Smooth bolt-frame expansion

Let \(r\) be normal distance to the bolt and let \((\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3)\) be the bolt frame, with \(\sigma_1\) the collapsing connection direction. Smoothness gives
\[
H_* = \operatorname{diag}(r^2,b^2,b^2)+O(r^2),
\quad b^2=x_0,
\]
with the transverse anisotropy of order \(r^4\). The regular SD exponents \((0,2,2)\) give
\[
\phi_1(r,p)=\frac p\pi+O(pr^2),\qquad
\phi_2=O(pr^2),\qquad
\phi_3=c\,r^2+O(r^4)+O(pr^2).
\]
For \(\Omega=\tfrac12d(\phi_i\sigma_i)\), put
\[
f_p:=\frac{|\Omega_p|_{g_p}}{\sqrt2}.
\]
In an orthonormal smooth bolt frame, the constant SD component generated by \(\phi_1(0)\) is orthogonal to the first normal component of the zero-period form. Consequently
\[
\boxed{f_p(r)^2=a^2p^2+\kappa^2r^2+O(p^2r^2+r^4)},
\qquad a=\frac1{\pi b^2}>0,
\]
where \(\kappa>0\) because the zero-period form has a transverse first-order zero. Hence
\[
f_p(r)=\sqrt{a^2p^2+\kappa^2r^2}\,(1+o(1)).
\]
The resolving layer has \(r\asymp|p|\), not \(r\asymp\sqrt{|p|}\). The false square-root layer comes from confusing the potential coefficient \(\phi_3=O(r^2)\) with the orthonormal form component \(\phi_3/(a_1a_2)=O(r)\). Separately, one must distinguish \(f=|\Omega|/\sqrt2\) from the conformal scale \(v=\sqrt f\), since \(\widehat g=v^2g=f g\).

For the certified profile the script obtains
\[
a=10.61032954,\qquad \kappa=6.50226623.
\]
The exact value \(a=1/(\pi x_0)\) follows from the bolt frame; \(\kappa\) is a floating profile coefficient.

## 4. Matched divergence of the almost-Kähler scalar variance

Put
\[
\widehat g_p=f_pg_p=v_p^2g_p,\qquad v_p=\sqrt{f_p}.
\]
In dimension four,
\[
\widehat s_p=v_p^{-3}(-6\Delta_{g_p}v_p+s_{g_p}v_p),
\qquad
\widehat s_p^2d\mu_{\widehat g_p}
=\left(s_{g_p}-6\frac{\Delta v_p}{v_p}\right)^2d\mu_{g_p}.
\]
To leading normal order, with \(A=ap\),
\[
v_p=(A^2+\kappa^2r^2)^{1/4},
\qquad
\frac{\Delta v_p}{v_p}
=\frac{\kappa^2(4A^2+\kappa^2r^2)}{4(A^2+\kappa^2r^2)^2}+O(1).
\]
After orbit integration, the bolt volume form is \(dV_{\mathrm{rad}}=2\pi^2b^2r\,dr+O(r^3)dr\); equivalently \(\operatorname{Area}(E)=\pi b^2\) and the normal angular factor is \(2\pi r\,dr\). Direct integration gives
\[
\int_0^\infty\left(\frac{\Delta v_p}{v_p}\right)^2r\,dr
=\frac{7\kappa^2}{32a^2p^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\int\widehat s_p^2d\mu_{\widehat g_p}
=\frac{C_E}{p^2}+O(|\log|p||)},
\qquad
C_E=36(2\pi^2b^2)\frac{7\kappa^2}{32a^2}>0.
\]
The exponent is \(p^{-2}\). A \(p^{-1}\) prediction arises if one mistakes the potential coefficient \(\phi_3=O(r^2)\) for the orthonormal form component \(\phi_3/(a_1a_2)=O(r)\), thereby replacing the true model \(f^2\sim p^2+r^2\) by the false model \(p^2+r^4\). The numerical coefficient for the certified profile is
\[
C_E=1.75135145.
\]
Resolved computations give
\[

1.74336,\ 1.74916,\ 1.75073,\ 1.75103
\]
for \(p=-0.0035073,-0.0017537,-0.00087688,-0.00043844\), converging to the local prediction. The integral \(\int\widehat s_p\,d\mu_{\widehat g_p}\) and \(V(\widehat g_p)\) remain bounded; its variance, not its mean, carries the singular growth. The integration-by-parts identity
\[
\int\widehat s_p d\mu_{\widehat g_p}
=\int(6f_p|\nabla\log v_p|^2+s_{g_p}f_p)d\mu_{g_p}
\]
was reproduced to relative error below \(2\times10^{-10}\).

At fixed topology, conformal invariance of Weyl energy and Chern–Gauss–Bonnet imply exactly
\[
P(\widehat g_p)-P(g_p)=\frac{S(\widehat g_p)-S(g_p)}{12}.
\]
Thus \(P(\widehat g_p)-P(g_p)\sim C_E/(12p^2)\), while the original smooth metrics satisfy \(J(g_p)<1\), equivalently \(J_6(g_p)<8\pi^2\) and converge smoothly to the certified zero-period metric. The almost-Kähler scalar-variance blow-up is a degeneration of the chosen conformal representative, not an additional payment in the original Einstein-defect problem. Any AKΓ estimate evaluated on \(\widehat g_p\) cannot be transferred to \(g_p\) without retaining this conformal conversion.

## 6. A direct application of a positive trace-free Ricci gap

**Proposition.** Let \(M\) be a closed smooth four-manifold with
\[
Y(M)<0,\qquad p_0:=\inf_gP(g)>0.
\]
Assuming family 351 Theorem 1.1, every maximal volume-normalized Ricci flow
\[
\partial_tg=-2\operatorname{Ric}+\frac12\bar s\,g
\]
has unbounded scalar curvature on its maximal interval.

If the maximal time is finite and scalar curvature were bounded, the usual scale/time conversion would produce an unnormalized flow with bounded scalar curvature at finite time; family 351 extends it, and scaling back contradicts maximality.

If the normalized flow is immortal and scalar bounded, Ishida Proposition 5 gives \(\min s\le Y(M)V^{-1/2}<0\) uniformly. The minimum principle makes the scalar variance time-integrable. In dimension four the exact identity is
\[
\bar s'=\frac{2P}{V}-\frac12\fint(s-\bar s)^2.
\]
Since \(\bar s\) stays bounded, \(\int_0^\infty P(g(t))dt<\infty\), contradicting \(P\ge p_0\). This strengthens the Einstein-gap theory by turning an all-metric positive \(P\)-gap into a dynamical scalar blow-up conclusion. A lower bound involving an auxiliary \(D_2\) suffices only if that same quantity is known to be time-integrable along the hypothetical bounded-scalar flow. In particular, when \(D_2=\int(s-\bar s)^2\), an all-metric positive lower bound for \(P+D_2\) does suffice because both terms are time-integrable; a differently defined field defect does not enter automatically.
