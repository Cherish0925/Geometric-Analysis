## Deep-Reasoning

This Agent4 step was a critical review of the positive Gate-3 framework after Step97 reframed the product-orbifold problem as a variational finite-dimensional obstruction rather than a frozen coefficient comparison. The active Yau--26 target remains far from resolved: the full proposition asks for a dimension-by-dimension existence theorem or a strict counterexample manifold carrying Ricci-pinched metrics but no Einstein metric, with Lean formalization at completion. The four-dimensional product-orbifold line is valuable because it probes the strict positive branch and exposes how Ricci pinching, Einstein gluing, topology, and Kähler versus non-Kähler smoothings interact; it is not itself a global no-Einstein theorem and must not be allowed to replace the user's universal target.

I first tested the Strategist's stronger compatible-locus calibration against actual sources and prior artifacts. Reading the Step96 correction note, the patched Step94 three-gate note, Step97's gradient audit data, and OSS lines 300--328 confirmed that the current A02 notes still contained now-dangerous formulations: the equal-phase comparison \(|q_{\rm diag}|\) versus \(|2q_{\rm adj}+q_{\rm diag}|\) was still presented too much like the Gate-3 criterion, and Step94/Step96 outcome logic even contained a linear-algebra error by treating \(|Q|>|P|\) as producing a kernel of \(z\mapsto Pz+Q\bar z\). The correct linear algebra is \(\ker(Pz+Q\bar z)\ne0\) iff \(|P|=|Q|\); both strict inequalities give an invertible real-linear map, with orientation change when the determinant is negative. This matters because a sign or magnitude bias is not a solvability theorem.

The source request and source reading clarified the local geometric calibration. OSS establishes that degree-4 del Pezzo moduli have a Zariski-open smooth locus, polystable intersections of two quadrics have only smooth/two-node/four-node possibilities, and the unique four-\(A_1\) surface is \((\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1)/\mathbb Z_2\) with the quotient KE metric. Biquard--Rollin supplies the missing period geometry: near canonical singularities the smoothing parameters lie in \(\mathfrak h_{\mathbb C}/W\), the real cohomology of fibers is identified with \(\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}\), the local deformation-with-Kähler-class is encoded by \(\zeta=(\zeta_r,\zeta_c)\), and \(\zeta_r\) represents the restriction of the Kähler class. Their Lemma 6 states that when the real/Kähler component lies in \(\ker\theta\), the root class is Lagrangian, while when the complex component lies in \(\ker\theta\), the root is represented by a holomorphic cycle. This supports the essential interpretation: Kähler smoothings of an \(A_1\) node use periods in the plane orthogonal to the local Kähler form, i.e. in the product-orbifold kernel plane \(K=\omega^\perp=\ker R^\pm\), whereas the holomorphic resolution direction is the Kähler exceptional-curve direction. Biquard--Rollin also explicitly distinguishes smoothings from resolutions and notes that in the positive KE case desingularizations/resolutions containing holomorphic \((-2)\)-spheres generally cannot carry KE metrics unless \(c_1=0\), while smoothings can.

I then made a grounded TTCS verifier call with the A02 Gate-3 notes/data, A01's Step97 AB physical-cone note, and the OSS, Biquard--Rollin, and Ozuch sources. The independent verification is important because the Strategist note asserted a stronger pairwise vanishing conclusion than the sources alone obviously imply. The integrated result `Sessions/S0910A02/trace/ttcs/results/step98_ttcs01_verifier1_integration.md` supports the local period calibration and conditionally supports a variational Lyapunov--Schmidt picture: if one has a genuine fixed-volume, slice-compatible LS reduction of the Einstein equation to a real reduced potential \(\varphi=\varphi(0)+f_4+o(|x|^4)\), then any exact KE branch tangent to direction \(a\) forces \(df_4(a)=0\), and Euler homogeneity then gives \(f_4(a)=0\). But TTCS correctly refused the stronger inference that individual same-chirality self or pair coefficients vanish: nontrivial quartics such as \((|z_1|^2-|z_2|^2)^2\) vanish with zero gradient on a sublocus while having nonzero self and pair coefficients. Thus the honest mathematical standard is that the full, calibrated, coordinate-consistent LS gradient vanishes on the compatible KE tangent cone or KE orbit quotient; partial raw sectors need not vanish individually. TTCS also emphasized the necessary slice assumptions: fixed volume, Bianchi/Ebin slice, equivariant projection, automorphism quotient/Luna or Kuranishi coordinates at the product quotient, lifted \(A_1\) periods with \(\xi\sim-\xi\), uniform EH normalization and frame transport, complement/Gram/projection/global deformation terms, and an error estimate strong enough for both potential and first derivative. This tightened rather than closed Gate 3.

However, as Agent4 I do not yet accept A01's theorem-grade conclusion without further audit.
