## Deep-Reasoning

This step repaired a serious mathematical error in the restricted four-dimensional negative Ishida--LeBrun cutoff-flow route and sharpened the next analytic target. The Step 37 continuation argument failed exactly at the cutoff-degenerate interface. With \(U=aT=a(\operatorname{Rm}+G)\), \(a=\chi^2\), and \(\operatorname{Ric}(U)=aE\), a bound on \(U\) only gives \(|aE|\le C|U|\). It does not give an additional \(\chi^2\)-small velocity in the transition region. Hence the proposed split into \(\{\chi\ge\delta\}\cup\{\chi<\delta\}\) cannot recover unweighted curvature derivatives near the vanishing cutoff. Even small \(C^0\) metric displacement would not control curvature derivatives, and fixed coordinate derivatives of the cutoff cannot replace evolving covariant cutoff jets because \(\nabla^j\chi\) depends on the evolving connection through \(\partial_t\Gamma=g^{-1}*\nabla\operatorname{Ric}(U)\). This correction changes the analytic route from a purported closed platform back into an active Wang-style coupled PDE problem.

The repaired structure is now clearer. One must evolve the weighted shifted tensor \(U=a(\operatorname{Rm}+G)\) in the spirit of Wang Lemma 4.5, so the top cutoff terms involve derivatives of \(U\), not derivatives of unweighted curvature. In parallel, one must prove a separate Wang Proposition 4.13-style unweighted curvature-derivative module for \(T=\operatorname{Rm}+G\), without dividing by \(a\). The cutoff jets form the third coupled object through \(\partial_t\Gamma=g^{-1}*\nabla\operatorname{Ric}(U)\). Terminal smoothness, if the derivative estimates are obtained, should be recovered from \(\partial_tg=-2\operatorname{Ric}(U)\) and the connection evolution in a fixed background connection, not from \(U/a\) in the transition region. This is a real repair of the mathematical architecture rather than a cosmetic change.

Independent TTCS reading confirmed the correction and accepted the support-normalization direction, while identifying concrete remaining analytic work: derive the full shifted \(U\) equation including \(G\) and normalization terms, prove the shifted zero-order \(L^p(T)\) and higher unweighted \(T\)-derivative estimates, keep all bounds uniform in time on a positive-time interval, repair the \(m=2\) high-cutoff product, and formulate terminal recovery in fixed coordinates. A03 Step 40 then supplied a genuine first nontrivial repair: for \(V=\nabla^2U\), \(Z=\nabla^3\chi=\nabla\Theta\), \(W=\nabla U\), and \(\Theta=\nabla^2\chi\), the bad direct estimate of \(\int\chi ZW|V|^{p-2}V\) would require the current unknown \(Z\in L^{2p}\). The correct move is another signed integration by parts off \(Z\), producing \(d\chi\Theta W P(V)\), the signed Hessian term \(\chi\Theta V P(V)\), and \(\chi\Theta W\nabla P(V)\), which is paid by weighted dissipation plus \(\int\Theta^2W^2|V|^{p-2}\) using finite base-pair exponents. A03 also supplied the order-one unweighted \(\nabla T\) mechanism by expanding \(\nabla^2a=2\chi\nabla^2\chi+2d\chi\otimes d\chi\) and \(\nabla^3a=2\chi\nabla^3\chi+C\,d\chi*\nabla^2\chi\), integrating only the \(\chi\nabla^3\chi\) part. This gives a strong base case for the arbitrary-order continuation repair, but not the all-order theorem.

The support-selection front was also repaired. The correct object is the volume-average maximal function \(\mathcal M_R f(x)=\sup_{0<s\le R}\fint_{B_s(x)}f\), not \(r^{-4}\int\) or \(r^4\int\). A large threshold retains a fixed fraction of total volume; a small fixed radius then gives \(r^4\fint_{B_r}|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\) small. If the downstream smoothing input uses the unnormalized four-dimensional quantity \(\int_{B_r}|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\), Bishop--Gromov upper volume supplies that smallness as well, without assuming local noncollapse. The Sobolev-volume product still cancels: \(\mathcal S(K_2^\eta)^{1/2}\le Cr^2(1+\Lambda\overline K_0)^{1/2}\). The conversion from retained total-volume fraction to \(A_i\)-proportional plateau volume must come from calibration, \(\operatorname{Vol}(M_i)/A_i\to32\pi^2/9\), not from an Euler upper bound. This keeps the support construction productive while preventing it from certifying the still-unproved continuation theorem.

The broader Yau 26 goal remains unresolved. The repaired negative route concerns only the near-critical Ishida--LeBrun four-dimensional laboratory. A02’s positive toric work is a separate stress test: it identified the diagonal toric sector with alternating labels and found strong evidence of rigidity and rod/polygon mismatch for \(m\ge6\), but this does not decide the positive branch because the Yau antecedent has no symmetry and the output Einstein metric may be non-toric. The session now has a more reliable local analytic target and a corrected support normalization, but no complete proof, no disproof of the full proposition, and no Lean-formalizable final theorem.
