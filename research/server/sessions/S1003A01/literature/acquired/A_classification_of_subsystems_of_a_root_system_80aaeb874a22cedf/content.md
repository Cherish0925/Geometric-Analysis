## Abstract

We classify isomorphic classes of the homomorphisms of a root system Ξ\\XiΞ to a root system Σ\\SigmaΣ which do not change Cartan integers. We examine several types of isomorphic classes defined by the Weyl group of Σ\\SigmaΣ, that of Ξ\\XiΞ and the automorphisms of Σ\\SigmaΣ or Ξ\\XiΞ etc. We also distinguish the subsystem generated by a subset of a fundamental system. We introduce the concept of the dual pair for root systems which helps to study the action of the outer automorphism of Ξ\\XiΞ on the homomorphisms.

## Introduction to Root Subsystems

Root systems are fundamental geometric and algebraic configurations that describe the symmetries of various mathematical objects, most notably Lie algebras. A root system is a finite set of vectors, called roots, in a Euclidean space that satisfy strict geometric conditions regarding reflections and integrity. These structures were first introduced in the late 19th century by Wilhelm Killing and Élie Cartan as part of their effort to classify semisimple Lie algebras.

Within a larger root system Σ\\SigmaΣ, one can identify smaller sets of roots that themselves form a root system. Such a subset is called a subsystem Ξ\\XiΞ. Understanding how these subsystems are embedded into the larger parent system is a problem of significant depth in algebra. While early work by mathematicians like Eugene Dynkin focused on specific types of subsystems—namely those that are "S-closed"—modern research has sought a more comprehensive and unified classification.

This overview explores the research by Toshio Oshima, which provides a systematic classification of all subsystems of any given root system. By leveraging the graphic combinatorics of extended Dynkin diagrams, Oshima provides a method to calculate equivalence classes of these subsystems under the actions of various symmetry groups, such as the Weyl group WΣW\_\\SigmaWΣ​ and the automorphism group Aut(Σ)\\text{Aut}(\\Sigma)Aut(Σ).

## Classification Methodology

The research adopts a recursive and unified approach to handle both classical and exceptional root systems:

1. **Reduction to Irreducible Components:** If a root system is reducible (can be split into smaller, independent systems), the problem is broken down. The classification of homomorphisms between reducible systems is derived from the homomorphisms between their irreducible components.
2. **Imbedding Analysis:** For irreducible systems, the paper provides explicit rules (Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.5) for counting distinct embeddings. This includes identifying the structure of the "orthogonal complement" Ξ⊥\\Xi^\\perpΞ⊥, which consists of all roots in Σ\\SigmaΣ that are orthogonal to every root in Ξ\\XiΞ.
3. **Refined Orbit Counting:**The paper distinguishes between several types of equivalence classes:

   - WΣW\_\\SigmaWΣ​-orbits (denoted by #\\##): Subsystems that can be mapped to each other by Weyl group reflections.
   - Aut(Ξ)\\text{Aut}(\\Xi)Aut(Ξ)-orbits (denoted by #Ξ\\#\_{\\Xi}#Ξ​): Subsystems identified if they differ only by an internal symmetry of the subsystem itself.
   - Aut(Σ)\\text{Aut}(\\Sigma)Aut(Σ)-orbits (denoted by #Σ\\#\_{\\Sigma}#Σ​): Subsystems identified if they can be mapped to each other by any symmetry of the larger parent system.

This multi-layered counting allows researchers to determine, for instance, if an outer automorphism of a subsystem can be realized by a reflection in the larger system.

## Structural Properties: Closures and Dual Pairs

A significant portion of the work is dedicated to defining how "closed" a subsystem is. This is important for understanding the relationship between Lie algebras and their subalgebras.

- **S-closed:** A subsystem is S-closed if, whenever the sum of two roots in the subsystem is a root in the parent system Σ\\SigmaΣ, that sum is also in the subsystem. These correspond to "regular" subalgebras in Lie theory.
- **L-closed:** A subsystem is L-closed if it contains every root of Σ\\SigmaΣ that lies in its linear span. Every L-closed subsystem is S-closed, but the reverse is not always true.
- **⊥\\perp⊥-closed:** A subsystem is ⊥\\perp⊥-closed if it is the orthogonal complement of its own orthogonal complement, i.e., Ξ=Ξ⊥⊥\\Xi = \\Xi^{\\perp\\perp}Ξ=Ξ⊥⊥. This is the strongest form of closure.

The research also explores "dual pairs." A pair of subsystems (Ξ1,Ξ2)(\\Xi\_1, \\Xi\_2)(Ξ1​,Ξ2​) is a dual pair if Ξ1⊥=Ξ2\\Xi\_1^\\perp = \\Xi\_2Ξ1⊥​=Ξ2​ and Ξ2⊥=Ξ1\\Xi\_2^\\perp = \\Xi\_1Ξ2⊥​=Ξ1​. Identifying these pairs helps in understanding how the symmetries of one subsystem influence the symmetries of the other.

## Addressing the Core Questions

Oshima uses the developed framework to answer seven fundamental questions regarding subsystem embeddings. Some of the key findings include:

- **Realization of Automorphisms:** One of the most complex questions is whether an outer automorphism of a subsystem Ξ\\XiΞ can be realized by an element of the Weyl group WΣW\_\\SigmaWΣ​ of the larger system. The paper shows that this is possible if and only if the count of WΣW\_\\SigmaWΣ​-orbits equals the count of Aut(Ξ)\\text{Aut}(\\Xi)Aut(Ξ)-orbits (i.e., #=#Ξ\\# = \\#\_{\\Xi}#=#Ξ​). The classification identifies specific cases in E7E\_7E7​ and E8E\_8E8​ where this fails, meaning the larger system cannot "see" or perform certain internal symmetries of its own subsystems.
- **Equivalence under Aut(Σ)\\text{Aut}(\\Sigma)Aut(Σ):** The work clarifies when two subsystems that are equivalent under a general symmetry of Σ\\SigmaΣ are also equivalent under the more restrictive Weyl group WΣW\_\\SigmaWΣ​. This happens if #Σ=1\\#\_{\\Sigma} = 1#Σ​=1. The paper notes rare exceptions, such as in certain DnD\_nDn​ type systems.
- **Fundamental Subsystems:** The paper investigates whether every subsystem can be generated by a subset of the fundamental roots Ψ\\PsiΨ. While many are, Oshima identifies specific "non-fundamental" subsystems in BnB\_nBn​ and DnD\_nDn​ types that cannot be generated this way.

## Significance of the Unified Classification

The results of this research are compiled into extensive tables covering every irreducible root system (classical types An,Bn,Cn,DnA\_n, B\_n, C\_n, D\_nAn​,Bn​,Cn​,Dn​ and exceptional types E6,E7,E8,F4,G2E\_6, E\_7, E\_8, F\_4, G\_2E6​,E7​,E8​,F4​,G2​). These tables serve as a comprehensive reference for the properties of every possible subsystem embedding.

The significance of this work lies in its completeness. Prior to this, classifying subsystems of exceptional root systems was an arduous task often done on a case-by-case basis. By reducing the problem to diagrammatic combinatorics, Oshima provided an algorithmic way to explore the structural landscape of Lie theory.

Furthermore, the concepts of LLL-closure and SSS-closure provide a bridge between the geometric properties of root systems and the algebraic properties of Lie algebra subalgebras. This classification is useful not only for pure mathematicians studying group theory and representation theory but also for theoretical physicists who utilize the symmetries of Lie groups (like E8E\_8E8​) in unified field theories and string theory. By providing the exact counts and properties of these embeddings, the research offers a definitive map of the internal architecture of root systems.
