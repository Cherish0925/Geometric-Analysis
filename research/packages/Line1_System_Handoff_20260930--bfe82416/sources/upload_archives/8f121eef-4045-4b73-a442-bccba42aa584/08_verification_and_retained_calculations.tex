\hypertarget{mathematical-changes}{%
\subsection{Mathematical changes}\label{mathematical-changes}}

I50 connects the actual determinant line to a whole smooth core with its
specified cyclic boundary marking. It controls the full relative charge,
including its integer part and the boundary correction. The endpoint
application gives charge tightness on small-volume marked cores without
assuming a nonzero rescaled spinor in their interiors. Harmonic
replacement preserves the same integral line and relative charge. Finite
tree cancellation is independent of depth, with sums taken over
frontiers rather than all ancestors.

This is not torsion-index tightness. The relative characteristic
identities give explicit canonical-resolution models with unbounded
opposite-spinor index and bounded determinant charge. The new X07
construction strengthens this distinction within the actual local gauge
equation: hyperbolic balls have torsion index d-squared, zero
determinant charge and vanishing specified subcritical costs, while
quartic unit-torsion direction energy grows like d-to-the-fourth. Every
radial cut in a fixed-ratio round collar retains the index. The
construction has no prescribed exterior matching or closed global class
supply.

The torsion trace estimate retains the phase of the actual unit section.
Under the explicit whole-core decomposition and exterior amplitude
routing, an endpoint escape must have divergent normalized quartic
direction energy, at least a constant times \texttt{(H/V)\^{}(-1/3)}.
Neither this decomposition nor an upper bound on that critical energy
follows from the original weak input. In particular, trivial-boundary
torsion cores must not be discarded because their determinant charge
vanishes.

For balanced small-h families, a separate exact localization uses one
ordinary actual SW solution throughout. A coefficient below four
requires exterior ASD determinant energy or positive transition flux.
Below 28/9, positive first-order flux and a critical mixed-current
contribution are necessary. The value 28/9 is conditional on the absence
of positive leading flux, not an unconditional improvement of the
all-metric coefficient.

\subsection{Mathematical increment}\label{mathematical-increment}}

I51 proves the unrestricted all-metric estimate

\[
(1+b_a)(r+D_\beta/(\beta V))\ge c(\beta-2)^2/\beta^4.
\]

The square-cost factor disappears under
\texttt{a\^{}2\textgreater{}=T+}. This improves the compactified factor
from \texttt{t\^{}3(1-2t)\^{}2} to \texttt{t\^{}2(1-2t)\^{}2} and the
inherited upper energy wall from a cubic to a quadratic margin at
\texttt{h=1/4}. It does not impose pointwise Ricci, Euler density,
diameter, noncollapse, Sobolev or stationarity assumptions. The core new
identity decomposes the entire parameter pencil using one actual
constant-weight solution; its full Weyl square has a stronger parameter
weight. Weak Bianchi uses that weight separately from the Ricci error.
Negative Yamabe comparison returns the estimate to every input metric
with the original actual supplier.

This is stronger than the prior unrestricted theorem. The linear
compactified factor in I45 still has geometric hypotheses; the earlier
boundary-compatibility proposal with that factor is conditional. Neither
already proves I51. The quadratic power is not proved optimal, and the
nondegenerate O10 gap is not established. This comparison concerns the
preserved project lineage, not a claim of worldwide priority.

The same proof continues the two global tests rather than reporting only
the square completion. Amplitude-controlled actual high-index cores have
explicit derivative costs and an extension Dirac/curvature alternative.
Splitting the zeros lowers their selected critical-shell cost to linear
in total index. Thus neither the monomial's superlinear growth nor I50's
frontier bound without \texttt{H/V-\textgreater{}0} excludes all escape.
Closed matching also requires the exterior's nonzero kinetic and
harmonic energies. No closed actual escape sequence has been
constructed.

Continuing the direct equation also closes the natural curvature and
harmonic signed-test errors to o(V), using a bounded ASD model for the
same constant solution. Their two principal endpoint terms cancel
exactly. This is recorded in Section 2.2 so that this signed test is not
repeatedly mistaken for a gap; the stronger derivative test still
contains a critical torsion-curvature term.

The balanced ordinary-solution calculation preserves the cancellation of
trace-free Ricci and ASD determinant energy through their action on the
spinor. It supplies an exact signed target and an order-Ah bound, not
the little-o estimate needed to settle the coefficient. I40/I41 linear
order and scalar-variance removal remain inherited results.

\hypertarget{what-changed-mathematically}{%
\subsection{What changed
mathematically}\label{what-changed-mathematically}}

I52 is one result family, not a separate discovery count for each lemma.
Its final statement concerns actual, further-selected complex-hyperbolic
shrinking-surgery recovery metrics on the established balanced family.
Theorem 9 allows every globally vanishing C0 amplitude in either an
exact TT slice followed by negative Yamabe normalization, or an exact
Bianchi slice with scalar-minus-four endpoints. It assumes no derivative
bound, amplitude rate in h, or competitive endpoint energy.

The proof gives a quantitative inequality, not just a contradiction:

\[
P_{\rm final}\ge P_0+DP_0[k]+c\|\mathcal A_0(k)\|_2^2-Ca^2Q,
\qquad a=\|k\|_\infty,\quad Q=Ah.
\]

The identified derivative includes the metric and volume changes. The
opposite-chirality budget then gives \(\mathcal E/h\ge4-C'a^2-o(1)\). In
the stated vanishing-amplitude slices, coefficient four is equivalent to
the identified derivative square being o(Q). The raw second-order graph
norm is then o(sqrt Q). Exact conformal identities give low-order
control of the actual final Yamabe factor without an assumed supremum
bound. A nonzero diffuse TT family attains this first-order equality
without being isometric to the bases.

Corollary 11 replaces exact Bianchi gauge on constant-scalar endpoints
by the verifiable condition \[
a\,\|\delta^*\beta k\|_2=o(\sqrt Q).
\] It keeps the complete quantitative gauge error. Pure diffeomorphism
oscillations verify that this is weaker than an unweighted small gauge
error, and that neither scalar curvature nor competitive energy alone
automatically supplies a gauge estimate. For nonconstant-scalar inputs
followed by Yamabe normalization, equation (4.44) instead retains the
additional actual scalar-linear error \(\|Ds_0[k]\|_2^2\). The
