For single-Kähler limits (like CP² with det R₋ = (Λ/3)³ > 0), reversed bubbles face genuine R₋ obstruction — the chirality lever is real in this case. But for bi-Kähler limits (product S²×S² type), both blocks are degenerate and the first-order test is vacuous for all bubble orientations. The bi-Kähler case is precisely where the stress family topology is achievable.

But Cheeger-Naber gives only a positive Einstein orbifold, not necessarily Kähler. The assertion 'positive Einstein 4-orbifold implies conformally Kähler' is not a theorem — it is essentially the LeBrun-Ozuch admissibility hypothesis. The clean analysis works directly with (det R₊, det R₋) without Kähler input.
