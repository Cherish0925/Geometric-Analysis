## Point-by-point

One integration by parts on the closed manifold (the divergence theorem contributes nothing extra, and no curvature commutator is generated by a single divergence) sheds one derivative, leaving $\Theta$ in every resulting term. The product rule on $\nabla(|W|^{p-2}W\cdot\chi U)$ produces exactly the three factors: derivative on $|W|^{p-2}W$ ($I_{1a}$), on $\chi$ ($I_{1b}$), on $U$ ($I_{1c}$, with $\nabla U=W$). The count and structure are right. This IBP is what lowers the cutoff order from $\nabla^3\chi$ to $\Theta$ and matches the coupled norm.

**(b) $\chi^2/a=1$ across $\{a=0\}$ — valid.** The Young split of $I_{1a}$ pairs the diffusion factor $a^{1/2}|\nabla W||W|^{(p-2)/2}$ against $\chi a^{-1/2}|U||\Theta||W|^{(p-2)/2}$; the residual coefficient is $\chi^2/a=1$ on $\{a>0\}$, never a genuine division of a numerator vanishing slower than the denominator. On $\{\chi=0\}$ the entire integrand vanishes: at each fixed $t<T$ the metric is smooth, so $W=\nabla(aT)=2\chi\,d\chi\,T+a\nabla T$ has a factor $\chi$ and vanishes on $\{\chi=0\}$, as do $U$ and $\Theta$. Restricting the integral to $\{a>0\}$ is therefore rigorous, and (3.6)–(3.7) hold with $\int|\Theta|^2|W|^{p-2}\le\|\Theta\|_p^2\|W\|_p^{p-2}$ by Hölder at $(p/2,\,p/(p-2))$.

**(c) $R_4$ does not lose a derivative — correct.** $R_4=\chi\,d\chi*\nabla^2U=\chi\,d\chi*\nabla W$; the factor $\chi=|a|^{1/2}$ pairs with the diffusion weight, giving $|I_4|\le\varepsilon\int a|\nabla W|^2|W|^{p-2}+C_\varepsilon r^{-2}\int|W|^p$. ## Central problem: the $R_3$ term (§3.3, eq. 3.11)

$R_3=\chi\,\Theta*W$ arises from $\nabla^2a*\nabla U$, with $\nabla^2a=2\chi\Theta+2d\chi\otimes d\chi$. In the $L^p$ energy,
$$I_3=\int|W|^{p-2}\langle W,\chi\,\Theta*W\rangle\,d\mu\ \le\ \int\chi\,|\Theta|\,|W|^{p}\,d\mu,$$
which carries **$p$ powers of $W$**, one more than $I_{1c}$ (which has $|W|^{p-1}$). Two statements in §3.3 are wrong:

1. "**same structure as $I_{1c}$**" — false; $I_3$ has an extra $|W|$.
2. "$|I_3|\le C\|\Theta\|_p\|W\|_p^p$ (Hölder with $(p,p/(p-1))$)" — Hölder at $(p,p/(p-1))$ gives $\|\Theta\|_p\,\|W\|_{p^2/(p-1)}^p$, a supercritical $W$-norm, not $\|W\|_p^p$. Reaching $\|W\|_p^p$ would require $\|\Theta\|_\infty$, which is not available at this level.

The scaling shows why this matters. Write $Y_1=\|W\|_p^p+\|\Theta\|_p^p$ (degree $p$). The other reaction outputs are degree $p$ and hence linear in $Y_1$ after Young: $I_{1a}\to\|\Theta\|_p^2\|W\|_p^{p-2}$ (degree $p$), $I_{1b},I_2,I_{1c}\to\|\Theta\|_p\|W\|_p^{p-1}$ (degree $p$). But $\|\Theta\|_p\|W\|_p^p$ is degree $p+1$, i.e. $\sim Y_1^{1+1/p}$. A term of that size in $Y_1'$ produces $Y_1'\lesssim Y_1^{1+1/p}$, which blows up in finite time and cannot yield a uniform bound on $[0,T)$. Notably, **this offending term does not appear in (3.15)** — (3.15) lists only $\|\Theta\|_p^2\|W\|_p^{p-2}$, $\|W\|_p^p$, $\|\Theta\|_p\|W\|_p^{p-1}$, $\|T\|_p^p$. So the note's own endpoint is inconsistent with its stated bound (3.11): (3.15) tacitly used a better treatment of $R_3$ than §3.3 describes.

### The correct treatment (restores closure, needs no Hessian norm)

$R_3$ should be integrated by parts, exactly as $R_1$ was and exactly as Step 27 (5.4) handled $\nabla^2a*T$. Treat $\nabla^2a*W=\nabla(\nabla a)*W$ and shed the derivative:
$$\int|W|^{p-2}W*\nabla^2a*W=-\int\nabla\!\big(|W|^{p-2}W*W\big)*\nabla a
\ \lesssim\ \int|W|^{p-1}|\nabla W|\,\chi\,|d\chi|,$$
using $\nabla a=2\chi\,d\chi$ (no $\Theta$ reappears). Young with $\chi=|a|^{1/2}$ then gives
$$|I_3|\ \le\ \varepsilon\int a|\nabla W|^2|W|^{p-2}+C\,r^{-2}\!\int|W|^p,$$
identical in form to the $I_4$ output, absorbed into dissipation and into the benign $C_p(1+K)\|W\|_p^p$ slot already present in (3.15). No $\|\Theta\|_p$, no $\|\Theta\|_\infty$, no superlinear term. With this replacement, (3.15)–(3.16), (5.1)–(5.3), and the finiteness in (f) all stand.

So: the theorem's conclusion survives, but §3.3 must be rewritten. The coupled $\Theta$-equation is still genuinely needed — $I_{1a},I_{1b},I_2,I_{1c}$ really do couple to $\|\Theta\|_p$ and cannot be integrated by parts away — but $R_3$ is not one of those; it is a $\nabla^2a$ term that must be integrated by parts, not bounded by a Hessian $L^p$ norm.
