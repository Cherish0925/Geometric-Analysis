# Step 39: the general-$p$ homogeneous defect power inequality

Session: S0910A03. Agent2 / Constructor. Phase: Execute.

## 0. What this note proves and why it was the blocking object

Step 35 proved persistence of the *fixed* defect moment
$J_6=\int\eta^2a^4|E|^6$ without regularization. Both Step 38 TTCS branches
established that this fixed seed does **not** supply the differential
inequalities at the subsequent Moser exponents
$$
p_k=6\Bigl(\tfrac32\Bigr)^k=6,\;9,\;13.5,\;20.25,\dots
$$
so the defect endpoint $\|aE(t)\|_\infty\le C\delta(r^2/t)^{1/3}$ and the
integrable metric drift were resting on an unproved input.

This note derives that input. Two things come out of the derivation that were
not visible before:

1. The inequality holds for **every real $p\ge6$** with no
   $E$-independent additive source, no norm regularization, and no division by
   $a$. The mechanism is that $s=|E|^2$ is smooth, $s^{p/2}$ is $C^2$ for
   $p\ge4$, and the Hessian-$a$ contraction tensor $B_{\rm def}$ is quadratic in
   $E$.
2. The two-factor absorption used at $p=6$ **cannot** be used for the ladder.
   It is exact and elegant at fixed $p$, but its coefficient is *linear* in the
   dissipation with a factor $p$ in front, so it would demand
   $\gamma\lesssim p^{-1}$, which fails as $p\to\infty$. The ladder must use the
   three-factor identity, whose Young step converts the reaction into a
   *Grönwall coefficient* $Cp^N\mathcal S^2Y$ with only polynomial loss.

Point 2 is a genuine structural finding: it explains why the defect ladder is
necessarily coupled to the curvature quantity $Y=\int\eta^2aF^3$ even though the
$p=6$ seed is not. The dependency graph of the smoothing module is therefore

$$
K_2\;\Longrightarrow\;J_6\quad(\text{two-factor, }Y\text{-free}),
\qquad
K_2\;\Longrightarrow\;Y\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathcal S^2Y
\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{both ladders},
$$

and the defect endpoint requires *both* branches, not just the first.

## 1. Setting, weights, and the single-support convention

Fix the closed four-manifold $M$ and the flow
$$
\partial_tg=-2aE,\qquad a=\chi^2,\qquad E=\operatorname{Ric}_g+g,
\tag{1.1}
$$
assumed smooth on a provisional first-exit interval $[0,T]$ with the moving
Sobolev, metric and support comparability of Step 34 §5. Set
$$
s=|E|^2_{g(t)},\qquad F=(1+|\operatorname{Rm}|^2)^{1/2},\qquad q=\frac p2 .
\tag{1.2}
$$

**Single-support convention.** The auxiliary cutoff $\eta$ satisfies
$\eta\equiv1$ on a neighbourhood of $\operatorname{supp}\chi$ and
$a=da=0$ on $\operatorname{supp}d\eta$ (Step 23, Step 30 coloured supports).
Consequently every integrand below carrying a positive power of $a$ is
unaffected by the value of $\eta$, so all $\eta$-powers may be redistributed
freely inside Hölder products. Every $d\eta$-term vanishes identically. This is
used silently from §5 on.

For real $p\ge6$ define
$$
H_p=\int\eta^2a^{p-2}s^{q},
\qquad
X_p=\int\eta^2a^{p-1}s^{q-1}|\nabla E|^2,
\qquad
X_p'=\int\eta^2a^{p-1}s^{q-2}|\nabla s|^2,
\tag{1.3}
$$
$$
R_p=\int\eta^2a^{p-1}Fs^{q},
\qquad
z_p=\eta\,a^{(p-1)/2}s^{q/2},
\qquad
Y=\int\eta^2aF^3,
\qquad
K_2=\int\eta^2F^2 .
\tag{1.4}
$$
Note $z_p^2=\eta^2a^{p-1}s^q$, so
$$
R_p=\int F\,z_p^2 ,
\qquad
\|z_p\|_2^2\le H_p\quad(a\le1).
\tag{1.5}
$$
At $p=6$: $H_6=J_6$, $X_6$ and $z_6=\eta\chi^5|E|^3=w_6$ agree with Step 35.
The Sobolev inequality on the auxiliary ball is the squared form
$$
\|u\|_4^2\le\mathcal S\bigl(\|\nabla u\|_2^2+r^{-2}\|u\|_2^2\bigr),
\qquad
\mathcal S\simeq C(4,Kr^2)\,\frac{r^2}{\sqrt V},
\qquad
\gamma=\mathcal SK_2^{1/2}.
\tag{1.6}
$$

## 2. Regularity of the tested powers for real exponents

Two exponents are real, not integral: $s^{q}$ with $q=p/2\ge3$, and
$a^{p-2}=\chi^{2(p-2)}$ with $2(p-2)\ge8$.

**Lemma 2.1.** Let $\varphi_\beta(t)=t^\beta$ on $[0,\infty)$ with $\beta\ge2$.
Then $\varphi_\beta\in C^2([0,\infty))$ with $\varphi_\beta''(0)=0$ for
$\beta>2$. Hence if $u\ge0$ is smooth, $u^\beta$ is $C^2$ and
$$
\Delta(u^\beta)=\beta u^{\beta-1}\Delta u+\beta(\beta-1)u^{\beta-2}|\nabla u|^2
\tag{2.1}
$$
holds classically at every point, the second term being interpreted as $0$
where $u=0$ (legitimate because $\beta-2\ge0$ and $\nabla u=0$ at interior
minima of $u\ge0$ with $u=0$).

Applied with $u=s$, $\beta=q\ge3$: $s^q$ is $C^3$, and the chain rule
$$
\boxed{\;(\partial_t-a\Delta)s^q
=q\,s^{q-1}(\partial_t-a\Delta)s
-a\,q(q-1)\,s^{q-2}|\nabla s|^2\;}
\tag{2.2}
$$
is an exact pointwise identity. **No Kato regularization is used anywhere in
this note**, so no $\varepsilon$-remainder and no additive source can be
generated by a limit passage; this is exactly the failure mode identified in
Step 38 TTCS §9.

Applied with $u=\chi$, $\beta=2(p-2)\ge8$: the weight $a^{p-2}$ is $C^8$, and
only *first* derivatives of $a^{p-1}$ ever occur below, with the everywhere-valid
no-division identities
$$
|\nabla a|=2\chi|d\chi|=2a^{1/2}|d\chi|,
\qquad
\nabla(a^{p-1})=(p-1)a^{p-2}\nabla a .
\tag{2.3}
$$
If one prefers all real powers to be $C^\infty$, choose $\chi$ flat (vanishing
to infinite order) at $\partial\{\chi>0\}$; the only quantitative input used is
$\|d\chi\|_\infty\le C/r$ and $\|\nabla^2\chi\|_\infty\le C/r^2$, both available
for flat cutoffs.

## 3. The scalar defect equation and its Hessian-$a$ tensor

From (1.1) the defect satisfies
$$
\partial_tE=a(\Delta_LE-2E)+\mathcal C_a(E),
\tag{3.1}
$$
where $\mathcal C_a(E)$ is the cutoff operator collecting all terms carrying
$\nabla a$ or $\nabla^2a$ (Step 34 eq. 2.2). Contracting with $E$ and including
the metric variation of $|E|^2$ gives
$$
(\partial_t-a\Delta)s=-2a|\nabla E|^2+\Sigma_s,
\tag{3.2}
$$
with
$$
\Sigma_s=\underbrace{\Sigma_s^{\rm reac}}_{|\cdot|\le C(aFs+as)}
+\underbrace{\Sigma_s^{(1)}}_{|\cdot|\le C|\nabla a|s^{1/2}|\nabla E|}
+\underbrace{2(\nabla_i\nabla_ja)B_{\rm def}^{ij}}_{\text{Hessian-}a},
\tag{3.3}
$$
$$
B_{\rm def}^{ij}=s\,g^{ij}+\tau E^{ij}-2(E^2)^{ij},
\qquad \tau=\operatorname{tr}E .
\tag{3.4}
$$
The reaction group $\Sigma_s^{\rm reac}$ contains the Lichnerowicz curvature
term $4aR_{ikjl}E^{ij}E^{kl}$, the normalization terms, and the metric-variation
term $4a\,E*E*E$ (using $|E|\le CF$ so that $a|E|^3\le CaFs$).

The tensor (3.4) is A01's index-level result (A01 Step 35, cross-checked in
A03 Step 35 §3.5). Its two structural properties are the whole reason the
argument below is homogeneous:
$$
\boxed{\;B_{\rm def}\big|_{E=0}=0,\qquad
|B_{\rm def}|\le Cs,\qquad
|\nabla B_{\rm def}|\le Cs^{1/2}|\nabla E| .\;}
\tag{3.5}
$$
The gradient bound is sharp: $\nabla\tau=\operatorname{tr}\nabla E$ contributes
no extra term (A01's TTCS correction).

Throughout we use the elementary Kato-type inequality, valid pointwise with no
regularization because $s=|E|^2$,
$$
|\nabla s|=2|\langle E,\nabla E\rangle|\le2s^{1/2}|\nabla E|
\;\Longrightarrow\;
X_p'\le4X_p .
\tag{3.6}
$$

## 4. Energy identity: every term

Differentiate $H_p$, using $\partial_td\mu=-a\tau\,d\mu$ and the fact that
$\eta,a$ are time-independent:
$$
H_p'=\int\eta^2a^{p-2}\,\partial_t(s^q)
-\int\eta^2a^{p-1}\tau\,s^{q}.
\tag{4.1}
$$

**(M) Measure.** Since $|\tau|\le2|E|=2s^{1/2}\le CF$,
$$
\Bigl|\int\eta^2a^{p-1}\tau s^q\Bigr|\le C\!\int\eta^2a^{p-1}Fs^q=CR_p .
\tag{4.2}
$$

**(D) Dissipation.** Insert (2.2) and (3.2). The tensor dissipation and the
chain-rule term give
$$
-2q\,X_p-q(q-1)X_p'\;=:\;-\mathcal D_p ,
\qquad
\mathcal D_p=2qX_p+q(q-1)X_p'\ge0 .
\tag{4.3}
$$
Both are kept: the first pays derivatives of $B_{\rm def}$, the second pays the
$q^2$ loss in $\nabla z_p$ (§5).

**(C1) Cutoff cross term from $a\Delta s^q$.** Integrating by parts,
$$
\int\eta^2a^{p-2}\,a\Delta s^q
=-\int\bigl\langle d(\eta^2a^{p-1}),\,d(s^q)\bigr\rangle
=-(p-1)\!\int\eta^2a^{p-2}q\,s^{q-1}\langle\nabla a,\nabla s\rangle,
\tag{4.4}
$$
the $d\eta$ piece vanishing by §1. Using (2.3) and (3.6),
$$
\Bigl|\,(4.4)\,\Bigr|
\le Cpq\!\int\eta^2a^{p-3/2}|d\chi|\,s^{q-1/2}|\nabla E| .
\tag{4.5}
$$
Split the integrand as
$$
\bigl(\eta a^{(p-1)/2}s^{(q-1)/2}|\nabla E|\bigr)\cdot
\bigl(\eta a^{(p-2)/2}|d\chi|\,s^{q/2}\bigr),
\tag{4.6}
$$
whose weight exponents add correctly:
$\tfrac{p-1}2+\tfrac{p-2}2=p-\tfrac32$ and
$\tfrac{q-1}2+\tfrac q2=q-\tfrac12$. Young with a parameter gives
$$
\Bigl|\,(4.4)\,\Bigr|\le\varepsilon\,q\,X_p
+C\varepsilon^{-1}p^2q\,r^{-2}H_p .
\tag{4.7}
$$

**(C2) First-derivative cutoff terms of $\Sigma_s^{(1)}$.** Tested with
$q\eta^2a^{p-2}s^{q-1}$ these have exactly the structure (4.6), hence
$$
\le\varepsilon\,qX_p+C\varepsilon^{-1}q\,r^{-2}H_p .
\tag{4.8}
$$

**(H) Hessian-$a$ term: signed integration by parts.** The tested term is
$$
T_H=2q\!\int(\nabla_i\nabla_ja)\,\eta^2a^{p-2}s^{q-1}B_{\rm def}^{ij}.
\tag{4.9}
$$
Apply the Step 38 signed lemma to the vector field
$X^i=\nabla_ja\,\eta^2a^{p-2}s^{q-1}B_{\rm def}^{ij}$: on the closed manifold
$$
T_H=-2q\!\int\nabla_ja\;\nabla_i\bigl[\eta^2a^{p-2}s^{q-1}B_{\rm def}^{ij}\bigr].
\tag{4.10}
$$
The derivative hits four factors. The $\eta^2$ branch vanishes ($\nabla a=0$ on
$\operatorname{supp}d\eta$). The remaining three, using
$|\nabla a|=2a^{1/2}|d\chi|$ and (3.5):

* on $a^{p-2}$: integrand $\le Cpq\,a^{p-2}|d\chi|^2s^{q}$, so
  $\le Cpq\,r^{-2}H_p$;
* on $s^{q-1}$: integrand
  $\le Cq^2a^{p-3/2}|d\chi|s^{q-1/2}|\nabla E|$ — the structure (4.6) — so
  $\le\varepsilon qX_p+C\varepsilon^{-1}q^3r^{-2}H_p$;
* on $B_{\rm def}$: by $|\nabla B_{\rm def}|\le Cs^{1/2}|\nabla E|$, integrand
  $\le Cq\,a^{p-3/2}|d\chi|s^{q-1/2}|\nabla E|$ — again (4.6) — so
  $\le\varepsilon qX_p+C\varepsilon^{-1}q\,r^{-2}H_p$.

Hence
$$
|T_H|\le\varepsilon\,qX_p+C\varepsilon^{-1}p^3r^{-2}H_p .
\tag{4.11}
$$
No norm of $\nabla^2\chi$ and no quotient by $a$ appears; this is the point of
keeping the Hessian indices contracted with the actual tensor $B_{\rm def}$
instead of passing to $\chi|\nabla^2\chi|$.

**(R) Reaction.** Testing $\Sigma_s^{\rm reac}$ with $q\eta^2a^{p-2}s^{q-1}$,
$$
\Bigl|\int\eta^2a^{p-2}q\,s^{q-1}\Sigma_s^{\rm reac}\Bigr|
\le Cq\,R_p+Cq\,H_p .
\tag{4.12}
$$

Collecting (4.2)–(4.12) with $\varepsilon$ a small fixed fraction:
$$
\boxed{\;
H_p'+\tfrac12\mathcal D_p
\le Cp^3\bigl(1+r^{-2}\bigr)H_p+Cq\,R_p .\;}
\tag{4.13}
$$
Every term on the right is homogeneous in $E$: **there is no
$E$-independent additive source**, because each entry of $\Sigma_s$ carries $s$,
$s^{1/2}|\nabla E|$, or $|\nabla E|^2$, and $B_{\rm def}$ vanishes at $E=0$.

## 5. The Sobolev variable is controlled by the dissipation, uniformly in $p$

$$
\nabla z_p
=\underbrace{(d\eta)a^{(p-1)/2}s^{q/2}}_{=0}
+\eta\tfrac{p-1}2a^{(p-3)/2}(\nabla a)s^{q/2}
+\eta a^{(p-1)/2}\tfrac q2s^{q/2-1}\nabla s .
\tag{5.1}
$$
The middle term has magnitude $(p-1)\eta a^{(p-2)/2}|d\chi|s^{q/2}$; the last
has magnitude $\tfrac q2\eta a^{(p-1)/2}s^{(q-2)/2}|\nabla s|$. Therefore
$$
\|\nabla z_p\|_2^2
\le\frac{q^2}{2}X_p'+Cp^2r^{-2}H_p .
\tag{5.2}
$$
Compare with the available chain-rule dissipation $q(q-1)X_p'$ inside
$\mathcal D_p$. For $q\ge3$,
$$
\frac{q^2/2}{q(q-1)}=\frac{q}{2(q-1)}\le\frac34 ,
\tag{5.3}
$$
so
$$
\boxed{\;\|\nabla z_p\|_2^2\le\tfrac34\mathcal D_p+Cp^2r^{-2}H_p
\quad\text{for all }p\ge6 .\;}
\tag{5.4}
$$
The constant $\tfrac34$ is **$p$-independent**. This is exactly why the
chain-rule term $-aq(q-1)s^{q-2}|\nabla s|^2$ must be retained rather than
discarded as it was at the fixed exponent $p=6$: at large $q$ the tensor
dissipation alone ($2qX_p\ge\tfrac q2X_p'$) is not enough to dominate the
$q^2/2$ in (5.2).

## 6. Why the $p=6$ two-factor absorption fails on the ladder

At $p=6$, Step 35 absorbed the reaction by the two-factor Hölder
$$
R_p=\int Fz_p^2\le\|F\|_2\|z_p\|_4^2
\le\gamma\bigl(\|\nabla z_p\|_2^2+r^{-2}H_p\bigr),
\qquad\gamma=\mathcal SK_2^{1/2}.
\tag{6.1}
$$
This is correct for every $p$, and it needs no curvature quantity beyond
$K_2$. But (4.13) carries the factor $q$ in front of $R_p$, so with (5.4)
$$
Cq\,R_p\le Cq\gamma\bigl(\tfrac34\mathcal D_p+Cp^2r^{-2}H_p\bigr),
\tag{6.2}
$$
and the bound is **linear** in $\mathcal D_p$ with no free parameter to trade.
Absorption into $\tfrac12\mathcal D_p$ therefore requires
$$
Cq\gamma\le\tfrac13
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\gamma\lesssim p^{-1},
\tag{6.3}
$$
which cannot hold along $p_k=6(3/2)^k\to\infty$ for a fixed geometry. So the
two-factor route is a genuinely *fixed-exponent* device: it gives the $J_6$
seed with a time-independent Grönwall coefficient (Step 35), and it is
unavailable for the ladder. This resolves the ambiguity left open in
Step 38 §5 and Step 35 §7.1, which cited "the general defect power inequality"
without distinguishing the two absorptions.

## 7. Three-factor absorption: the ladder inequality

Because $\eta\equiv1$ on $\operatorname{supp}a$ (§1), all $\eta$-powers may be
distributed freely. Split
$$
\eta^2a^{p-1}Fs^q
=\bigl(\eta^2aF^3\bigr)^{1/3}
\cdot\bigl(\eta^2a^{p-2}s^q\bigr)^{1/3}
\cdot\bigl(\eta^2a^{2(p-1)}s^{2q}\bigr)^{1/3}.
\tag{7.1}
$$
The weight and defect exponents check exactly:
$$
\tfrac13+\tfrac{p-2}3+\tfrac{2(p-1)}3=p-1,
\qquad
0+\tfrac q3+\tfrac{2q}3=q,
\qquad
F:\ \tfrac33=1 .
\tag{7.2}
$$
The third factor is $z_p^{4/3}$. Hölder with exponents $(3,3,3)$ gives
$$
\boxed{\;R_p\le Y^{1/3}H_p^{1/3}\|z_p\|_4^{4/3}.\;}
\tag{7.3}
$$
Insert the Sobolev inequality (1.6) with $u=z_p$ and write
$\mathcal W_p=\|\nabla z_p\|_2^2+r^{-2}H_p$, so $\|z_p\|_4^2\le\mathcal S\mathcal W_p$:
$$
q\,R_p\le
\bigl(q\,Y^{1/3}H_p^{1/3}\mathcal S^{2/3}\bigr)\mathcal W_p^{2/3}.
\tag{7.4}
$$
Young's inequality with exponents $(3,\tfrac32)$, i.e.
$A\mathcal W^{2/3}\le\varepsilon\mathcal W+C\varepsilon^{-2}A^3$, gives
$$
q\,R_p\le\varepsilon\,\mathcal W_p
+C\varepsilon^{-2}q^3\,\mathcal S^2Y\,H_p .
\tag{7.5}
$$
The cube of the prefactor is exactly $q^3\mathcal S^2YH_p$: the coefficient is a
*Grönwall multiplier*, not a fraction of the dissipation, so no $p$-dependent
smallness is required. Combining (4.13), (5.4) and (7.5), and moving
$\mathcal W_p$'s $H_p$-part to the right:

> **Theorem 7.1 (general-$p$ homogeneous defect power inequality).**
> Let $g(t)$, $t\in[0,T]$, be a smooth solution of (1.1) on a first-exit
> interval with the single-support convention of §1 and Sobolev constant
> $\mathcal S(t)$ as in (1.6). Then for every real $p\ge6$ and $q=p/2$,
> $$
> \boxed{\;
> H_p'+c\,\|\nabla z_p\|_2^2
> \le C\,p^{4}\bigl(1+r^{-2}+\mathcal S^2Y\bigr)H_p\;}
> \tag{7.6}
> $$
> with $c>0$ and $C$ depending only on dimension and $\|d\chi\|_\infty r$. There
> is no $E$-independent additive term, no norm regularization, no
> $\nabla^2\chi$ norm, and no division by $a$.

*Proof.* (4.13) with (7.5) and $\varepsilon$ a small multiple of the constant in
(5.4); the residual $\mathcal W_p$-part of $H_p$ contributes
$\varepsilon r^{-2}H_p$, and the losses accumulated in (4.7), (4.11) and (7.5)
are $p^2q$, $p^3$ and $q^3$, all bounded by $Cp^4$ once $q=p/2$. $\square$

This is precisely the input assumed at Step 38 (5.2) for $f=|E|$, now proved.

## 8. Consequences: the defect Moser endpoint and the drift

Theorem 7.1 has exactly the shape required by the mixed spacetime recurrence
established in Step 34/A01 Step 32,
$$
H_{3p/2}\le H_p^{1/2}\|z_p\|_4^2,
\qquad
\tfrac{p-2}2+(p-1)=\tfrac{3p}2-2,
\qquad
\tfrac p2+p=\tfrac{3p}2 .
\tag{8.1}
$$
With the dyadic slab coefficient
$\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{I_k}\mathcal S^2Y\le C\gamma^3/t$ from the $q=3$
curvature propagation (Step 38 §4.9), and the Step 35 seed
$J_6(t)\le C\delta^6V$, the standard nested-time iteration from $p_0=6$ gives
$$
\|aE(t)\|_\infty
\le C\mathcal S^{1/3}t^{-1/2}\Bigl(\int_{t/2}^tH_6\Bigr)^{1/6}
\le C\mathcal S^{1/3}t^{-1/2}(t\delta^6V)^{1/6},
\tag{8.2}
$$
and with $\mathcal S\lesssim r^2V^{-1/2}$, so that
$\mathcal S^{1/3}V^{1/6}\lesssim r^{2/3}$,
$$
\boxed{\;\|aE(t)\|_\infty\le C\delta\Bigl(\frac{r^2}{t}\Bigr)^{1/3},
\qquad
\int_0^{\theta r^2}\|aE(t)\|_\infty\,dt\le C\delta\,\theta^{2/3}r^2 .\;}
\tag{8.3}
$$
The drift is integrable and linear in $\delta$, which is what the moving
Sobolev/metric comparison of the first-exit argument consumes.

## 9. What is now established and what is not

Established, conditional only on a smooth first-exit interval with the stated
support and comparability hypotheses:

1. Theorem 7.1, for all real $p\ge6$, unregularized and homogeneous.
2. The $p$-uniform gradient comparison (5.4), which requires retaining the
   chain-rule dissipation $q(q-1)X_p'$.
3. The structural dichotomy of §6: two-factor absorption is a fixed-exponent
   device; the ladder is necessarily coupled to $\mathcal S^2Y$.
4. Consequently (8.3), given the already-established curvature chain
   $K_2\Rightarrow Y\Rightarrow\mathcal S^2Y\le C\gamma^3/t$ and the Step 35
   seed.

Not established here, and not to be quoted as such:

* existence and continuation of the degenerate flow to the target time — the
  transported-coefficient system of Step 37 opens the flow, and the
  higher-order induction of the companion note is required for smoothness;
* the simultaneous first-exit closure of
  $(\mathcal S,K_2,Y,J_6,Q,\text{support geometry})$ as one self-improving
  argument;
* the terminal geometric passage to a calibrated Einstein germ;
* any Ricci-pinching gap, any Einstein-existence statement, and anything about
  the positive branch, arbitrary four-manifold topology, $n\ge5$,
  dimension-only constants in Yau Problem 26, or Lean formalization.
