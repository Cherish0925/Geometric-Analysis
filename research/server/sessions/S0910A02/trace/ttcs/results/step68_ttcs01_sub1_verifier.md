## Overall assessment

Items (1), (2), (3), and (5) are mathematically sound after adding equivariance and normalization qualifications. Item (4) has the correct kernel dimensions, but its analytic consequence needs refinement: Ozuch’s obstruction space is the kernel of \(P\) on all symmetric \(2\)-tensors, not merely the TT kernel. For this orbifold it is four-dimensional, and only half of it is visibly generated by diffeomorphisms.

## 1. Reflection and chirality exchange

For the round product metric,
\[
c(z,w)=(\overline z,w)
\]
is an isometry. Since
\[
c\rho(z,w)=(-\overline z,-w)=\rho c(z,w),
\]
it descends to the quotient by \(\rho(z,w)=(-z,-w)\). Complex conjugation fixes \(0,\infty\) on the first \(\mathbb P^1\), so the four points
\[
(0,0),\ (0,\infty),\ (\infty,0),\ (\infty,\infty)
\]
are fixed individually.

The derivative \(D c\) reverses the product orientation. Consequently it induces orthogonal maps
\[
D c:\Lambda^+\longrightarrow\Lambda^-,
\qquad
D c:\Lambda^-\longrightarrow\Lambda^+.
\]
Thus an EH datum parametrized by \(\zeta^+\) is carried to an EH datum parametrized by \(A\zeta^-\) for a node-dependent orthogonal map \(A\in O(3)\). The metric germ, its Bianchi-gauge corrections, and the Einstein equation are transported isometrically.

The precise all-order statement is conjugacy rather than coefficientwise equality. If \(\mathcal F_{i,+}\) and \(\mathcal F_{i,-}\) denote obstruction maps at node \(i\), then equivariant choices of charts, cutoffs, kernel bases, and projections give
\[
\mathcal F_{i,-}(A_i\zeta)=B_i\,\mathcal F_{i,+}(\zeta)
\]
for orthogonal changes of parameter and obstruction bases. Hence their zero sets, ranks, and all scalar invariants agree. Arbitrarily fixed bases can introduce signs or permutations.

This argument applies to a genuinely single-node contribution. In a multi-node configuration, \(c\) reverses the chirality at every node simultaneously; it does not justify reversing one node while leaving all others unchanged.

## 2. Pattern complement symmetry

If \(S\) is the set of reversed insertions relative to the fixed product orientation, pullback by \(c\) gives
\[
S\longmapsto S^c.
\]
Therefore the full finite-dimensional systems satisfy an equivariance relation of the form
\[
\mathcal F_{S^c}(T\zeta,Tv)=T'\mathcal F_S(\zeta,v).
\]
It follows that:

- \(r=0\) and \(r=4\) have equivalent solvability and multiplicity data;
- \(r=1\) and \(r=3\) have equivalent solvability and multiplicity data;
- for \(r=2\), complement preserves the adjacent and diagonal types separately.

Thus a computation giving different zero-set behavior for \(r=1\) and \(r=3\) is inconsistent with the isometry. Raw component arrays need not be identical unless all bases are transported by \(c\).

The additional sentence that the \(r=0\) row is realized by a known Kähler–Einstein smoothing is not a consequence of this reflection alone. It needs a separate cited identification of the smoothing and its Einstein metric. Once existence for \(r=0\) is established, reflection transfers it to \(r=4\).

## 3. Operator conventions and the relative scaling mode

Ozuch’s convention is
\[
P_g=\frac12\nabla^*\nabla-\mathring R,
\]
while Kröncke uses
\[
\Delta_E=\nabla^*\nabla-2\mathring R=2P_g.
\]
These agree exactly on an Einstein background when the same curvature convention is used.

For
\[
h_{\mathrm{rel}}=g_1-g_2
\]
on \(S^2\times S^2\), with \(\operatorname{Ric}_{g_i}=\Lambda g_i\),
\[
\nabla h_{\mathrm{rel}}=0,\qquad
\mathring R(g_i)=\Lambda g_i.
\]
Hence
\[
P h_{\mathrm{rel}}=-\Lambda h_{\mathrm{rel}},
\qquad
\Delta_Eh_{\mathrm{rel}}=-2\Lambda h_{\mathrm{rel}}.
\]
The Step-68 normalization is correct. A value \(-4\Lambda\) is not compatible with either of these two stated operators.

## 4. TT kernel and full \(P\)-kernel

### TT kernel

The conclusion
\[
\ker(P|_{\mathrm{TT}})=0
\]
is correct, including on the quotient.

Let \(u\) be a first spherical harmonic on the first factor:
\[
\Delta u=2\Lambda u,\qquad
\nabla^2u=-\Lambda u\,g_1.
\]
A scalar-sector tensor has the form
\[
h=A\,u g_1+B\,u g_2+C\,\nabla^2u
  =(A-C\Lambda)u g_1+B u g_2.
\]
Its TT equations are
\[
(A-C\Lambda)+B=0,\qquad A-C\Lambda=0,
\]
so \(B=0\) as well. The same argument applies to first harmonics on the second factor.

The remaining sectors do not contribute zero modes:

- \(S^2\) has no factorwise TT Einstein deformation;
- on mixed one-form tensors \(\mathring R=0\), while the lowest rough one-form eigenvalue on each factor is \(\Lambda\), giving \(P\geq\Lambda\);
- the relative scaling tensor has eigenvalue \(-\Lambda\), not zero.

Any quotient TT tensor lifts to an invariant TT tensor on the cover, so the quotient kernel is also zero.

Kröncke’s general product multiplicity formulas require special care here because on the round \(S^2\),
\[
\nabla^2u=-\Lambda u g
\]
makes the Hessian and conformal tensor summands linearly dependent. His discussion around Lemma 4.3 explicitly notes this spherical overlap. The direct calculation above is the appropriate argument for \(S^2\times S^2\).

### Full kernel

The four-dimensional full kernel on the quotient is also correct. Let \(u\) and \(v\) be the axial \(l=1\) harmonics on the first and second factors. These are the unique invariant first harmonics under the half-turn on each sphere. Directly,
\[
P(u g_1)=P(u g_2)=P(v g_1)=P(v g_2)=0.
\]
Thus
\[
\ker P_{\mathrm{full}}^\rho
=\operatorname{span}\{u g_1,u g_2,v g_1,v g_2\}.
\]

This space has a useful exact decomposition. Since
\[
\mathcal L_{\nabla u}g=2\nabla^2u=-2\Lambda u g_1,
\]
the tensors \(u g_1\) and \(v g_2\) are infinitesimal diffeomorphism directions. The tensors
\[
u(g_1+g_2),\qquad v(g_1+g_2)
\]
are conformal scalar directions and are not generated by those diffeomorphisms. Moreover, using \(B=\delta+\frac12d\operatorname{tr}\),
\[
B(u g_1)=0,\qquad B(u(g_1+g_2))=du,
\]
and similarly for \(v\).

Therefore “scalar/conformal/automorphism-type” should be replaced by the sharper description:

- two residual diffeomorphism modes;
- two conformal scalar modes.

Ozuch’s Definition 1.8 defines \(\mathbf O(g)\) using the full \(L^2\)-kernel of \(P\). Hence the JSON sentence saying that no kernel variable enters because the TT kernel vanishes is incorrect in Ozuch’s full formulation. Proposition 2.10 explicitly includes the \(\nu_l v_l\) projections against \(\mathbf O(g_o)\). The axial modes have nontrivial constant and quadratic Taylor coefficients at the four nodes, so their pairings with an EH multipole are not automatically zero.

A TT-only Green operator is valid only after proving that the singular datum, global extension, normalization, and all source terms remain in the TT subspace. Otherwise the four-dimensional full projection must be retained. A Bianchi slice can exclude the conformal modes, while a reduced diffeomorphism quotient handles the residual Lie-derivative modes; the precise slice and its invertibility need to appear explicitly.

## 5. Distant interaction as a multipole-to-two-jet map

The Step-68 correction is correct and important.

The EH asymptotic tensor \(H_i^4\sim r^{-4}\) is a quadrupolar singular datum for a second-order tensor operator in four dimensions. If \(\mathbb G(x,y)\) is the tensor Green kernel of the projected operator \(P^\perp\), its response is schematically
\[
h_i(x)
  \sim Q_i\!\contr\nabla_y^2\mathbb G(x,p_i),
\]
where \(Q_i\) records the EH angular and chiral tensor. At another node \(p_j\),
\[
h_i=H_{ij,0}+H_{ij,2}+O(r_j^4),
\]
and the interaction relevant to curvature is the normalized quadratic coefficient
\[
H_{ij,2}\sim
\frac12\nabla_x^2
\left[
Q_i\!\contr\nabla_y^2\mathbb G(x,y)
\right]_{(x,y)=(p_j,p_i)}.
\]

In a symmetric-tensor eigenbasis \(\Psi_\alpha\), the corresponding spectral object has the schematic form
\[
\sum_{\lambda_\alpha\neq0}
\frac{
(\nabla^2\Psi_\alpha)(p_j)\,
\big\langle(\nabla^2\Psi_\alpha)(p_i),Q_i\big\rangle
}{\lambda_\alpha},
\]
with all tensor indices, chiral maps, and gauge projections contracted appropriately.

Two refinements matter on the curved product:

1. The constant term \(H_{ij,0}\) is removed by a linear coordinate normalization \(\phi_j\). That normalization also changes the representation of the ambient quadratic jet, so the effective receiver is the full normalized two-jet, not merely the raw coordinate Hessian.

2. The Green inverse is the pseudo-inverse after projection off the relevant full kernel unless a TT reduction has already been proved.

Ozuch’s equations (35)–(39), the torus construction (60)–(62), and Lemma 4.2 support precisely this structure: the metric contribution is regular at the receiving node, while its curvature is obtained by taking two derivatives there. A sum
\[
\sum_\alpha\frac{\Psi_\alpha(p_i)\Psi_\alpha(p_j)}{\lambda_\alpha}
\]
is a monopole value kernel and misses both the source quadrupole and the receiving two-jet.

## Execution-relevant conclusions

- The reflection gives exact conjugacy tests for \(r=0\leftrightarrow4\) and \(r=1\leftrightarrow3\), including kernel projections and obstruction bases.
- The operator normalization and the \(h_{\mathrm{rel}}\) eigenvalue are correct.
- The TT inverse has no zero mode, but Ozuch’s full operator has four quotient-invariant zero modes. The full-kernel projection cannot be omitted without a proved TT/Bianchi reduction.
- The interaction matrix must be built from tensor-harmonic derivatives at both nodes, with constant-term normalization and chiral contractions. A distance-only or value-only Green matrix can serve as a symmetry model but not as the Ozuch coefficient.
