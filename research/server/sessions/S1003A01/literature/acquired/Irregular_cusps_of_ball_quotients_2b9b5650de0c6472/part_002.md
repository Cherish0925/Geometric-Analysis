[Part 2/2 of: Irregular cusps of ball quotients]

Let $L_{U \oplus U}$ be an even unimodular Hermitian lattice of signature $(1,1)$ over $\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})}$ defined by the matrix
$$\frac{1}{2 \sqrt{-2}}\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & 1 \\
-1 & 0
\end{array}\right) .$$
Then its associated quadratic lattice $\left(L_{U \oplus U}\right)_{Q}$ is $U \oplus U$.
Let $L_{E_{8}(-1)}$ be an even unimodular Hermitian lattice of signature $(0,4)$ over $\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})}$ defined by the matrix
$$-\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}{cccc}
2 & 0 & \sqrt{-2}+1 & \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{-2} \\
0 & 2 & \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{-2} & 1-\sqrt{-2} \\
1-\sqrt{-2} & -\frac{1}{2} \sqrt{-2} & 2 & 0 \\
-\frac{1}{2} \sqrt{-2} & \sqrt{-2}+1 & 0 & 2
\end{array}\right) .$$
Then its associated quadratic lattice $\left(L_{E_{8}(-1)}\right)_{Q}$ is $E_{8}(-1)$.
Let $L_{\langle-2\rangle \oplus\langle-4\rangle}$ be an even unimodular Hermitian lattice of signature $(0,1)$ over $\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})}$ defined by the matrix
$$(-1) .$$
Then its associated quadratic lattice $\left(L_{\langle-2\rangle \oplus\langle-4\rangle}\right)_{Q}$ is $\langle-2\rangle \oplus\langle-4\rangle$. We define $L_{(\langle-2) \oplus\langle-4\rangle)^{\perp}}$ be the orthogonal complement of $L_{\langle-2\rangle \oplus\langle-4\rangle}$ in $L_{E_{8}(-1)}$. Let $L:=L_{U \oplus U} \oplus L_{E_{8}(-1)} \oplus L_{E_{8}(-1)} \oplus L_{(\langle-2\rangle \oplus\langle-4\rangle)}{ }^{\perp}$ be a Hermitian lattice of signature $(1,12)$ over $\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})}$ whose associated quadratic lattice is $U \oplus U \oplus$ $E_{8}(-1) \oplus E_{8}(-1) \oplus(\langle-2\rangle \oplus\langle-4\rangle)^{\perp}$.

For $I I_{2,26}:=U \oplus U \oplus E_{8}(-1) \oplus E_{8}(-1) \oplus E_{8}(-1)$, we embed $L_{Q} \hookrightarrow I I_{2,26}$ by Nikulin's theorem. On the Hermitian symmetric domain $\mathscr{D}_{I I_{2,26}}$, there exists the Borcherds form $\Phi_{12}$, a modular form of weight 12 with respect to $\mathrm{O}^{+}\left(I I_{2,26}\right)$ with character det. This is obtained by using the Borcherds lift of the inverse of Ramanujan's tau function.

Proposition 7.1. There exists a non-zero cusp form $\Psi_{13}$ of weight 13 with respect to $\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(L)$ with character det.

Proof. Since the complement of $L_{Q}$ in $I I_{2,26}$ has exactly two (-2)-vectors, by [9, Theorem 8.2], the quasi-pull back $f_{13}$ of $\Phi_{12}$ is a cusp form of weight $12+2 / 2=13$ with respect to $\widetilde{\mathrm{O}}^{+}\left(L_{Q}\right)$ with character det. Then by restricting $f_{13}$ to $D_{L}$, we obtain a cusp form $\Psi_{13}:=\iota^{\star} f_{13}$ of weight 13 with respect to $\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(L)$ with character det on a 12-dimensional ball $D_{L}$. \(\square\)

Therefore, since the canonical bundle on $D_{L}$ is isomorphic to $\mathscr{O}(-13)$, by Freitag's criterion [5], we have the following.

Proposition 7.2. The ball quotient $\mathscr{F}_{L}(\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(L))$ has non-negative Kodaira dimension.

\section*{8. Examples}

In this section, we give, as examples, the irregular cusps with any branch indices for any imaginary quadratic fields with class number 1.
8.1. Case of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})$. Let $\eta:=1+\sqrt{-1}$.

Example 8.1. Let $a=2 b+1$ be an integer with $b \geqslant 0$ and $L$ be a Hermitian lattice of signature $(1, b+1)$ defined by
$$\langle-1\rangle^{\oplus b} \oplus\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & \eta^{a} \\
\bar{\eta}^{a} & 0
\end{array}\right) .$$
Then, we have
$$A_{L} \cong\left(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})} / \eta^{2}\right)^{\oplus b} \oplus\left(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})} / \eta^{a+2}\right)^{\oplus 2} .$$
We put
$$M:=\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & \eta^{a} \\
\bar{\eta}^{a} & 0
\end{array}\right) .$$
We take a generator $e_{1}, \ldots, e_{b}$ of $\langle-1\rangle^{b}$ and $v, w$ of $M$. In other words, $\left\langle e_{i}, e_{j}\right\rangle=-\delta_{i j}$ and $\langle v, v\rangle=$ $\langle w, w\rangle=0,\langle v, w\rangle=\eta^{a}$. We define $A_{v}$ to be the subgroup of $A_{M}$ generated by $v / \eta^{a+2}$.

Now we take an isotropic vector
$$\ell:=e_{1}+\cdots+e_{b}+v+w .$$

Let
$$\Gamma:=\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(L)^{v}:=\left\{g \in \mathrm{U}(L)(\mathbb{Z})|g|_{A_{v}}=\mathrm{id}\right\}$$
. Then, we have
$$\begin{cases}-\mathrm{id} \in \Gamma, \sqrt{-1} \mathrm{id} \notin \Gamma & (a=-1) \\ -\mathrm{id}, \sqrt{-1} \mathrm{id} \notin \Gamma & (a \geqslant 0) .\end{cases}$$

Now for $\lambda:=1 / 2^{b+1}$, we can show
$$-\sqrt{-1} T_{\lambda \sqrt{-1}(\ell \otimes \ell)} \in \Gamma$$
by our assumption on $a$ and $b$, that is,
$$\begin{gathered}
-\sqrt{-1} T_{\lambda \sqrt{-1}(\ell \otimes \ell)}\left(\frac{v}{\eta^{a+2}}\right)=\frac{v}{\eta^{a+2}} \in A_{v}, \\
-\sqrt{-1} T_{\lambda \sqrt{-1}(\ell \otimes \ell)}\left(e_{i}\right) \in L,-\sqrt{-1} T_{\lambda \sqrt{-1}(\ell \otimes \ell)}(w) \in L .
\end{gathered}$$
Hence, $\ell$ defines an irregular sublattice of $L$ with index 4.
Example 8.2. Let $L$ be a Hermitian lattice of signature $(1,3)$ defined by
$$\left\langle-\frac{1}{2}\right\rangle^{\oplus 2} \oplus\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & \frac{\bar{\eta}}{2} \\
\frac{\eta}{2} & 0
\end{array}\right) .$$
Then we have
$$A_{L} \cong\left(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})} / \eta\right)^{\oplus 2} .$$

We put
$$M_{1}:=\left\langle-\frac{1}{2}\right\rangle^{\oplus 2}, M_{2}:=\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & \frac{\bar{\eta}}{2} \\
\frac{\eta}{2} & 0
\end{array}\right) .$$

We take a generator $e, f$ of $M_{1}$ and $v, w$ of $M_{2}$. We define $A_{v}$ to be the subgroup of $A_{L}$ generated by $v / \eta$.

Now we take an isotropic vector
$$\ell:=e+f+v+w .$$

Let
$$\Gamma:=\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(L)^{v}:=\left\{g \in \mathrm{U}(L)(\mathbb{Z})|g|_{A_{v}}=\mathrm{id}\right\} .$$

We put $\lambda:=-1$. Then, we have
$$-\mathrm{id} \in \Gamma, \sqrt{-1} \mathrm{id} \notin \Gamma, \sqrt{-1} T_{-\sqrt{-1}}(\ell \otimes \ell) \in \Gamma .$$
Hence, $\ell$ defines an semi-irregular sublattice of $L$ with index 2.
8.2. Case of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$. Let $\omega:=(-1+\sqrt{-3}) / 2$.

Example 8.3. Let $L$ be a Hermitian lattice of signature $(1,2)$ defined by
$$\langle-1\rangle \oplus\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & \omega \\
\bar{\omega} & 0
\end{array}\right) .$$
Then we have
$$A_{L} \cong\left(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})} / \sqrt{-3}\right)^{\oplus 3} .$$

We take a generator $e, v, w$ of $L$ with $\langle e, e\rangle=-1,\langle v, v\rangle=\langle w, w\rangle=0$ and $\langle v, w\rangle=\omega$. We define $A_{w}$ to be the subgroup of $A_{L}$ generated by $w / \sqrt{-3}$.

Now we take an isotropic vector
$$\ell:=e+v+w .$$
Let
$$\Gamma:=\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(L)^{w}:=\left\{g \in \mathrm{U}(L)(\mathbb{Z})|g|_{A_{w}}=\mathrm{id}\right\} .$$
Then, we have
$$\omega \operatorname{id} \notin \Gamma .$$

Now for $\lambda:=-1 / 2$, we can show
$$\omega T_{\lambda \sqrt{-3}(\ell \otimes \ell)} \in \Gamma,-\omega T_{\lambda \sqrt{-3}(\ell \otimes \ell)} \notin \Gamma .$$
Hence, $\ell$ defines an irregular sublattice of $L$ with index 3.
Example 8.4. Let $L$ be a Hermitian lattice of signature $(1,4)$ defined by
$$\langle-1\rangle^{\oplus 3} \oplus\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & \frac{3+\sqrt{-3}}{2} \\
\frac{3-\sqrt{-3}}{2} & 0
\end{array}\right) .$$
We have
$$A_{L} \cong\left(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})} / \sqrt{-3}\right)^{\oplus 3} \oplus\left(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})} / 3\right)^{\oplus 2} .$$

We take a generator $e_{1}, e_{2}, e_{3}, v, w$ of $L$ with $\left\langle e_{i}, e_{j}\right\rangle=-\delta_{i j},\langle v, v\rangle=\langle w, w\rangle=0$ and $\langle v, w\rangle=$ $(3+\sqrt{-3}) / 2$. We define $A_{v}$ to be the subgroup of $A_{L}$ generated by $v / 3$.

Now we take an isotropic vector
$$\ell:=e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}+f+v+w .$$
Let
$$\Gamma:=\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(L)^{v}:=\left\{g \in \mathrm{U}(L)(\mathbb{Z})|g|_{A_{v}}=\mathrm{id}\right\} .$$
Then, we have
$$-\mathrm{id}, \omega \mathrm{id} \notin \Gamma .$$

Now for $\lambda:=-1 / 6$, we can show
$$-\omega T_{\lambda \sqrt{-3}(\ell \otimes \ell)} \in \Gamma .$$
Hence, $\ell$ defines an irregular sublattice of $L$ with index 6.
8.3. General case. In this subsection, let $F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ be an imaginary quadratic field with $d \neq-1$ and $\eta:=\sqrt{d}$.

Example 8.5. Let $L$ be a Hermitian lattice of signature $(1,1)$ defined by
$$\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & \eta \\
\bar{\eta} & 0
\end{array}\right) .$$

We take a generator $v, w$ of $L$. We define $A_{v}$ to be the subgroup of $A_{L}$ generated by
$$\begin{cases}\frac{v}{2 \eta^{2}} & (d \equiv 2,3 \bmod 4) \\ \frac{v}{\eta^{2}} & (d \equiv 1 \bmod 4) .\end{cases}$$

Now we take an isotropic vector
$$\ell:=e+f+v+w .$$
Let
$$\Gamma:=\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(L)^{v}:=\left\{g \in \mathrm{U}(L)(\mathbb{Z})|g|_{A_{v}}=\mathrm{id}\right\} .$$
Then, we have
$$-\mathrm{id} \notin \Gamma$$
if $d \neq-1$.
Now for $\lambda:=-1 / d$, we can show
$$-T_{\lambda \sqrt{d}(\ell \otimes \ell)} \in \Gamma .$$
Hence, $\ell$ defines an irregular sublattice of $L$ with index 2.

A.5. Other cases. Let $F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$, where $d=-11,-19,-43,-67$ or $-163, \eta:=\sqrt{d}, \delta:=2$ and $a, b$ be non-negative integers. Let $I$ be an irregular isotropic sublattice of $L$ with index 2 with respect to $\widetilde{\mathrm{U}}(L)$. Then, by Proposition 4.7 (1), we have $\operatorname{div}(I) \equiv 1 / \sqrt{d}, 2 / \sqrt{d}, 1$ or 2 modulo $\mathscr{O}_{F}^{\times}$.

If $\operatorname{div}(I) \equiv 1 / \sqrt{d}$, then $A_{L}$ is isomorphic to $a \cdot \delta \oplus \eta$ as $\mathscr{O}_{F}$-modules.
If $\operatorname{div}(I) \equiv 2 / \sqrt{d}$, the candidates are
$$a \cdot \delta \oplus \delta^{c}, \quad a \cdot \delta \oplus 2 \cdot \delta^{2}, \quad a \cdot \delta \oplus \eta^{2}, \quad a \cdot \delta \oplus 2 \cdot \eta$$
where $c=0,2,3$.
If $\operatorname{div}(I) \equiv 1$, the candidates are
$$a \cdot \delta \oplus \eta^{2}, \quad a \cdot \delta \oplus 2 \cdot \eta .$$

If $\operatorname{div}(I) \equiv 2$, the candidates are
$$a \cdot \delta \oplus \delta^{c} \oplus \eta^{2}, \quad a \cdot \delta \oplus \delta^{c} \oplus 2 \cdot \eta, \quad a \cdot \delta \oplus 2 \cdot \delta^{2} \oplus \eta^{2}, \quad a \cdot \delta \oplus 2 \cdot \delta^{2} \oplus 2 \cdot \eta$$
where $c=2,3$.
