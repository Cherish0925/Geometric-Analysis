The condition that $\Pi_{1} \mathbf{w}<\Pi_{1} \mathbf{v}$, along with Inequality 11.6 implies that $b_{\ell}<c_{\ell}$. Let
$$\mathbf{u}=\sum_{j<\ell} c_{j} \mathbf{v}_{j}+\left(b_{\ell}+1\right) \mathbf{v}_{\ell} .$$
Clearly, $\mathbf{u} \preceq \mathbf{w}$ as required. Moreover, by Inequality 11.6 once again, we have that $\Pi_{1} \mathbf{u}<\Pi_{1} \mathbf{v}$. The existence of some $k$ with $b_{k}>c_{k}$, along with the ordering property Inequality 11.3 forces $\Pi_{2}(\mathbf{u})<\Pi_{2}(\mathbf{v})$. Thus, $\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u} \in \Lambda_{+}$. \(\square\)

The space $\widehat{C}_{p, q}$ is covered by $m+1$ affine coordinate patches $\left\{U_{i}\right\}_{i=0}^{m}$, where
$$U_{i}=\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{C}\left[\chi\left(-\mathbf{v}_{i}\right), \chi\left(\mathbf{v}_{i+1}\right)\right]$$
corresponds to the submonoid $\Lambda_{i}=\left\langle-\mathbf{v}_{i}, \mathbf{v}_{i+1}\right\rangle$ of $\Lambda$ generated by $-\mathbf{v}_{i}$ and $\mathbf{v}_{i+1}$. Moreover, the set
$$U_{i-1} \cup U_{i}$$
is the total space of a line bundle over
$$S_{i}=\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{C}\left[\chi\left(-\mathbf{v}_{i}\right)\right] \cup \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{C}\left[\chi\left(\mathbf{v}_{i}\right)\right],$$
which is a projective line. Its transition function can be read off from the relation in Equation 11.4, so we see that $c_{1}$ of the line bundle is $-a_{i}$.

The ring of global functions on this variety is given by
$$\bigcap_{i} \mathbb{C}\left[\chi\left(-\mathbf{v}_{i}\right), \chi\left(\mathbf{v}_{i+1}\right)\right]=\mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{+}\right],$$
inducing the map
$$r: \widehat{C}_{p, q} \rightarrow C_{p, q} .$$

A module over the monoid $\Lambda_{+}$naturally induces a module over the ring $\mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{+}\right]$. We claim that for all $j$, the modules $\Gamma\left(C_{p, q}, \mathcal{O}_{j}\right)$ over the coordinate ring $\mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{+}\right]$arise in this way.

Lemma 11.4. The module
$$M_{j}=[-j,+\infty) \times[0, \infty) \cap \Lambda \subset \Lambda$$
over $\Lambda_{+}$induces a module over $\mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{+}\right]$which is isomorphic to $\Gamma\left(C_{p, q}, \mathcal{O}_{j}\right)$.

Proof. From its description, $\Gamma\left(C_{p, q}, \mathcal{O}_{j}\right)$ is an $\mathbb{A}\left[C_{p, q}\right]$-submodule of of the module $\mathbb{C}[w, z]$. Multiplication by $z^{-j}$ is an $\mathbb{A}\left[C_{p, q}\right]$ automorphism of $\mathbb{C}\left[w, z, w^{-1}, z^{-1}\right]$, taking the module $\Gamma\left(C_{p, q}, \mathcal{O}_{j}\right)$ to the $\mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{+}\right]$-module $M_{j}$. \(\square\)

Let $\mathbf{w}_{j}$ be the unique vector in $\Lambda$ with
$$\Pi_{1} \mathbf{w}_{j}=-j \text { and } 0 \leq \Pi_{2} \mathbf{w}_{j} \leq p .$$

Lemma 11.5. The module $M_{j}$ over the monoid $\Lambda_{+}$is generated by all vectors $\mathbf{u} \in \Lambda_{-}$with $\mathbf{u} \preceq \mathbf{w}_{j}$.

Proof. By Inequality 11.2, any $\mathbf{u}$ with $\mathbf{u} \preceq \mathbf{w}_{j}$ must be contained in $M_{j}$. This fact, together with Corollary 11.3 implies the lemma. \(\square\)

Since $\Lambda_{+}$is a submonoid of $\Lambda_{i}=\left\langle-v_{i}, v_{i+1}\right\rangle$, we can consider the monoid $\Lambda_{i} M_{j}$, the $\Lambda_{i}$-submodule of $\Lambda$ generated by $M_{j}$.

Lemma 11.6. The $\Lambda_{i}$ module $\Lambda_{i} M_{j} \subset \Lambda$ is free and generated by the single element $\sum_{k \leq i} x_{k} \mathbf{v}_{k}$. Equivalently, the $\mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{i}\right]$-module $M_{j} \otimes_{\mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{+}\right]} \mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{i}\right]$ is free and generated by the single element $\chi\left(\sum_{k \leq i} x_{k} \mathbf{v}_{k}\right)$.

Proof. We must verify that the map
$$\Lambda_{i} \rightarrow \Lambda_{i} M_{j}$$
taking
$$\lambda \mapsto \lambda+\sum_{k \leq i} x_{k} \mathbf{v}_{k}$$
is surjective (as it is clearly injective). By the ordering of the $\mathbf{v}_{k}$ (Inequality 11.2, 11.3), we have that
$$\begin{array}{rll}
-\mathbf{v}_{k} \in \Lambda_{i} & \text { if } & k \leq i, \\
\mathbf{v}_{k} \in \Lambda_{i} & \text { if } & k>i .
\end{array}$$

By Lemma 11.5, it suffices to show that any vector $\mathbf{u} \preceq \mathbf{w}_{\mathbf{j}}$ lies in the image. Writing
$$\mathbf{u}=\sum_{k} y_{k} \mathbf{v}_{k},$$
where
$$y_{k} \leq x_{k},$$
we can write
$$\mathbf{u}=\sum_{k \leq i} x_{k} \mathbf{v}_{k}+\sum_{k>i} y_{k} \mathbf{v}_{k}+\sum_{\ell \leq i}\left(x_{k}-y_{k}\right)\left(-\mathbf{v}_{k}\right),$$
where the second two terms are in $\Lambda_{i}$, by the ordering property. \(\square\)

Remark 11.7. Recall (see for example [16]) that under the composition
$$U_{i} \subset \widehat{C}_{p, q},$$
the restriction of $r^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{j}\right)$ corresponds to the module $M_{j} \otimes_{\mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{+}\right]} \mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{i}\right]$.
Lemma 11.8. The module $M_{j}$ is precisely the intersection of the $\Lambda_{i} M_{j}$;
$$M_{j}=\bigcap_{i=0}^{m} \Lambda_{i} M_{j} .$$

Proof. Clearly,
$$M_{j} \subset \bigcap_{i=0}^{m} \Lambda_{i} M_{j} .$$
Given $\mathbf{v} \in \bigcap_{i=0}^{m} \Lambda_{i} M_{j}$, as $\Pi_{2} \lambda \geq 0$ for any $\lambda \in \Lambda_{0}$, we must have
$$\Pi_{2} \mathbf{v} \geq 0 .$$
Similarly, since $\Pi_{1} \lambda \geq 0$ for any $\lambda \in \Lambda_{m}$, we have that
$$\Pi_{1} \mathbf{v} \geq-j .$$
These conditions force $\mathbf{v} \in M_{j}$. \(\square\)

Proposition 11.1 follows rather easily from these lemmas.
Proof of Proposition 11.1. We have exhibited an affine cover, the
$$\left\{\text { Spec } \mathbb{C}\left[\Lambda_{i}\right]\right\}_{i=0}^{r},$$
of $\widehat{C}_{p, q}$ over which $r^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{j}\right)$ is free by Lemma 11.6. The statement that $\mathcal{O}_{i} \mapsto$ $r_{*} r^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}\right)$ follows from Lemma 11.8.

Restricting to $S_{i}$, we have that the generator of $r^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{j}\right)$ from Lemma 11.6 over $U_{i} \cap S_{i}$ differs from the generator of $r^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{j}\right)$ over $U_{i+1} \cap S_{i}$ by multiplication by $\chi\left(\mathbf{v}_{i}\right)^{x_{i}}$. Thus, it follows immediately that
$$\left\langle c_{1}\left(r^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{j}\right)\right), S_{i}\right\rangle=x_{i} .$$ \(\square\)

Suppose now that $R$ is a complex orbifold with finitely many $\mathbb{C}^{2} /(\mathbb{Z} / p \mathbb{Z})$ singular points, and $\mathcal{F}$ is a reflexive sheaf over $R$ which is locally free away from the singular points, and is isomorphic to $\mathcal{O}_{j(x)}$ near the singular point $x \in R$. Consider the minimal resolution
$$r: \widehat{R} \rightarrow R,$$
obtained by locally performing the procedure discussed above. We obtain the following precise statement of Theorem 3 from Section 1:

Theorem 7. The sheaf $r^{*}(\mathcal{F})$ is a locally free sheaf over $\widehat{R}$, with Chern classes determined as in Proposition 11.1. Moreover, $r$ induces an isomorphism between the moduli space of divisors in $r^{*}(\mathcal{F})$ over $\widehat{R}$ with the moduli space of divisors in $\mathcal{F}$ over $R$.

Proof. The fact that $r^{*}(\mathcal{F})$ is locally free follows directly from Proposition 11.1, after passing to germs near the singularity. Similarly, we have that the natural map
$$\mathcal{F} \rightarrow r_{*} r^{*} \mathcal{F}$$
is an isomorphism. This gives us the fact that the map
between moduli spaces is, as a map of sets, bijective.
To check that the local models agree, take any nonzero section
$$s \in H^{0}\left(\widehat{R}, r^{*} \mathcal{F}\right)-0 .$$
This section induces a short exact sequence of sheaves
$$0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{\widehat{R}} \xrightarrow{s} r^{*} \mathcal{F} \longrightarrow r^{*} \mathcal{F}_{s^{-1}(0)} \longrightarrow 0,$$
whose direct image (via the rationality of the singularities, and the fact that Natural Map 11.7 is an isomorphism) is the short exact sequence
$$0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{R} \xrightarrow{r(s)} \mathcal{F} \longrightarrow \mathcal{F}_{r s^{-1}(0)} \longrightarrow 0 .$$

Rationality of the singularities implies that all
$$\mathrm{R}^{j} r_{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{R}}\right)=0$$
for $j>0$, so by the Leray spectral sequence
$$H^{i}\left(R, \mathrm{R}^{j} r_{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{R}}\right)\right) \Rightarrow H^{i+j}\left(\widehat{R}, \mathcal{O}_{\widehat{R}}\right)$$
(see [11]), we have natural isomorphisms
$$H^{i}\left(\widehat{R}, \mathcal{O}_{\widehat{R}}\right) \cong H^{i}\left(R, \mathcal{O}_{R}\right)$$
Similarly, we have natural identifications between
$$H^{i}\left(\widehat{R}, r^{*} \mathcal{F}\right) \cong H^{i}(R, \mathcal{F}),$$
because of the vanishing
$$\mathrm{R}^{j} r_{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{R}}\right)=0$$
for $i>0$, which we see as follows. The fibers of $r$ have (complex) dimension at most 1 , so the $\mathrm{R}^{j} r_{*}$ must vanish for all $j>1$, by a standard result of sheaf theory (see [11]). The fact that
$$\mathrm{R}^{1} r_{*}\left(r^{*}(\mathcal{F})\right)=0$$
follows from Grauert's comparison theorem (see for example [12]), together with the fact that
$$H^{1}\left(\widehat{r^{-1}(x)},\left.r^{*}(\mathcal{F})\right|_{r^{-1}(x)}\right)=0$$
for any $x \in R$. This is true because the fiber is zero-dimensional over each non-singular point, and over each singular point, it is a wedge of projective lines, and the sheaf restricts over each line is a locally free sheaf with non-negative first Chern class (as in the Proposition 11.1), so its $H^{1}$ must vanish. Alternatively, the vanishing of $\mathrm{R}^{1} r_{*}$ follows from a more general argument in [13] (see also [2]). \(\square\)

\section*{12. The Dimension Formula.}

In Section 11, we gave a correspondence between divisors in the ruled surface $R$ with divisors in its desingularization $\widehat{R}$. The purpose of this section is to use this correspondence, together with the Riemann-Roch formula, to
derive the explicit formula from Section 1 (Corollary 1.4) for the expected dimension of the space of divisors in $R$ in terms of Seifert data.

Let $E_{1}, E_{2}$ be a pair of orbifold line bundles over $\Sigma$. Theorem 7 gives an identification between the moduli space of effective divisors connecting $E_{1}$ with $E_{2}, \mathcal{D}\left(E_{1}, E_{2}\right)$, with the moduli space of divisors in a line bundle we will denote by $\widehat{E}_{1,2}$ over $\widehat{R}$. Letting $\mathcal{D}\left(\widehat{E}_{1,2}\right)$ denote this latter moduli space, the ordinary Riemann-Roch theorem gives us that
$$\mathrm{e}-\operatorname{dim} \mathcal{D}\left(E_{1}, E_{2}\right)=\mathrm{e}-\operatorname{dim} \mathcal{D}\left(\widehat{E}_{1,2}\right)=\left\langle c_{1}\left(\widehat{E}_{1,2}\right)^{2}+c_{1}\left(K_{\widehat{R}}\right) c_{1}\left(\widehat{E}_{1,2}\right),[\widehat{R}]\right\rangle .$$
Since $\operatorname{deg}(Y) \neq 0$, the intersection form of $\widehat{R}$ splits (over $\mathbb{Q}$ ) intc the summands corresponding to the resolutions of the line bundles $Y \times_{S^{1}} \mathbb{C}$ and $Y^{*} \times_{S^{1}} \mathbb{C}$, so we can naturally decompose
$$\mathrm{e}-\operatorname{dim} \mathcal{D}\left(E_{1}, E_{2}\right)=\operatorname{dim}_{Y}\left(E_{1}\right)+\operatorname{dim}_{Y^{*}}\left(E_{2}\right),$$
where $\operatorname{dim}_{Y}(E)$ for any Seifert fibered space $Y$ and orbifold line bundle $E$ over $\Sigma$ is given by the formula
$$\operatorname{dim}_{Y}(E)=\left\langle c_{1}(\widehat{E})^{2}+c_{1}\left(K_{X}\right) c_{1}(\widehat{E}),[\widehat{X}]\right\rangle,$$
where $\widehat{X}$ is the smooth, non-compact complex surface obtained as the minimal resolution of $X=Y \times_{S^{1}} \mathbb{C}$, and $\widehat{E}$ is the desingularization of $E$, as given by the procedure of Section 11.

We will now derive a concrete formula for $\operatorname{dim}_{Y}(E)$ in terms of Seifert data. Let $g$ denote the genus of $\Sigma$, and suppose that $\Sigma$ has $n$ singularities with multiplicities $\left(\alpha_{1}, \ldots, \alpha_{n}\right)$. Let $\left(b, \beta_{1}, \ldots, \beta_{n}\right)$ be the Seifert invariants of $Y$. Let $\widehat{\Sigma} \subset \widehat{X}$ denote the proper transform of the zero-section $\Sigma \subset X$, and for $i=1, \ldots, n, j=1, \ldots, m_{i}$, let $S_{i}^{j}$ denote the $j^{t h}$ sphere in the configuration of spheres lying over the $i^{\text {th }}$ singular point in $\Sigma \subset X$. The homology classes induced by the curves
$$\widehat{\Sigma}, S_{1}^{1}, \ldots S_{m_{1}}^{1}, S_{1}^{2}, \ldots, S_{m_{2}}^{2}, \ldots, S_{1}^{n}, \ldots, S_{m_{n}}^{n}$$
form a basis $\mathfrak{B}$ for $H_{2}(\widehat{X})$. With respect to the Poincaré dual basis $\mathfrak{B}^{*}$, the intersection form for $\widehat{X}$ can be written as a sparse matrix $M$ (with zeros in all the entries not marked):
$$\left(\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
b & 1 & & & & 1 & & & & \ldots & 1 & & & \\
1 & -a_{1}^{1} & 1 & & & & & & & & & & & \\
& 1 & -a_{2}^{1} & \ddots & & & & & & & & & & \\
& & \ddots & \ddots & 1 & & & & & & & & & \\
& & & 1 & -a_{m_{1}}^{1} & & & & & & & & & \\
1 & & & & & -a_{1}^{2} & 1 & & & & & & & \\
& & & & & 1 & -a_{2}^{2} & \ddots & & & & & & \\
& & & & & & \ddots & \ddots & 1 & & & & & \\
& & & & & & & 1 & -a_{m_{2}}^{2} & & & & & \\
\vdots & & & & & & & & & \ddots & & & & \\
1 & & & & & & & & & & -a_{1}^{n} & 1 & & \\
& & & & & & & & & & 1 & -a_{2}^{n} & \ddots & \\
& & & & & & & & & & & \ddots & \ddots & 1 \\
& & & & & & & & & & & & 1 & -a_{m_{n}}^{n}
\end{array}\right)$$
where the $a_{1}^{j}, \ldots, a_{m_{j}}^{j}$ are the coefficients appearing in the Hirzebruch-Jung continued fraction expansion for $\alpha_{j} / \beta_{j}$.

Let $d_{i}^{j}$ be given by the denominator Hirzebruch-Jung continued fraction
$$\frac{d_{i}^{j}}{d_{i+1}^{j}}=\left\langle a_{i}^{j}, \ldots, a_{m_{j}}^{j}\right\rangle,$$
so that
$$a_{i}^{j}=\frac{d_{i-1}^{j}}{d_{i}^{j}}+\frac{d_{i+1}^{j}}{d_{i}^{j}} .$$
Let $E$ be an orbifold bundle over $\Sigma$ with Seifert invariants $\left(e_{0}, \epsilon_{1}, \ldots, \epsilon_{n}\right)$, and let $\xi_{i}^{j}$ be the coefficients appearing in the minimal expansion of $\epsilon_{j}$ with respect to $\left(d_{1}^{j}, \ldots, d_{m_{j}}^{j}\right)$,
$$\epsilon_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j}} \xi_{i}^{j} d_{i}^{j} .$$
It follows from Section 11, that the first Chern class of $\widehat{E}$ over $\widehat{X}$, the resolution of $\pi^{*}(E)$, is uniquely characterized by its evaluations
$$\begin{aligned}
& \left\langle c_{1}(\widehat{E}),[\widehat{\Sigma}]\right\rangle=e_{0}, \\
& \left\langle c_{1}(\widehat{E}),\left[S_{i}^{j}\right]\right\rangle=\xi_{i}^{j} .
\end{aligned}$$

Thus, if we let $\Xi$ be the vector formed by concatenating $e_{0}, \xi_{1}^{1}, \ldots, \xi_{m_{1}}^{1}$, $\xi_{1}^{2}, \ldots, \xi_{m_{1}}^{2}, \ldots, \xi_{1}^{n}, \ldots, \xi_{m_{n}}^{n}$, we can write $c_{1}(\widehat{E})$, in the basis $\mathfrak{B}^{*}$ as the vector
$$\mathbf{x}=M^{-1} \Xi .$$
We will write this vector more explicitly, in the following Lemma.
Lemma 12.1. Let
$$\mathbf{x}=\left(x^{0}, x_{1}^{1}, \ldots, x_{m_{1}}^{1}, x_{1}^{2}, \ldots, x_{m_{1}}^{2}, \ldots, x_{1}^{n}, \ldots, x_{m_{n}}^{n}\right)$$
denote the coefficients of $c_{1}(\widehat{E})$ in terms of the basis $\mathfrak{B}^{*}$ for $H^{2}(\widehat{X})$. Then,
$$x^{0}=\frac{\operatorname{deg}(E)}{\operatorname{deg}(Y)} .$$
and
$$x_{\ell}^{j}=d_{\ell}^{j}\left(\frac{x^{0}}{d_{0}^{j}}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell} \frac{1}{d_{i-1}^{j} d_{i}^{j}} \sum_{k=i}^{m_{j}} d_{k}^{j} \xi_{k}^{j}\right) .$$

Remark 12.2. In the above formulas, we have let $d_{0}^{j}=\alpha_{j}$.

Proof. The equation $M \mathbf{x}=\Xi$ can be written as a system of equations:
$$b x^{0}+x_{1}^{1}+x_{1}^{2}+\ldots x_{1}^{n}=e_{0},$$
and, for $j=1, \ldots, n, \ell=1, \ldots, m_{j}-1$
$$\begin{aligned}
& x_{\ell-1}^{j}-a_{\ell}^{j} x_{\ell}^{j}+x_{\ell+1}^{j}=\xi_{\ell}^{j}, \\
& x_{m_{j}-1}^{j}-a_{m_{j}}^{j} x_{m_{j}}^{j}=\xi_{m_{j}}^{j},
\end{aligned}$$
where we declare $x_{0}^{j}=x^{0}$ for $j=1, \ldots, n$. This is equivalent to the system of equations:
$$\begin{aligned}
\left(b+\frac{d_{1}^{1}}{d_{0}^{1}}+\ldots+\frac{d_{1}^{n}}{d_{0}^{n}}\right) x^{0} & =e_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m_{1}} d_{k}^{1} \xi_{k}^{1}+\ldots+\sum_{k=1}^{m_{n}} d_{k}^{n} \xi_{k}^{n} \\
x_{\ell-1}^{j}-\frac{d_{\ell-1}^{j}}{d_{\ell}^{j}} x_{\ell}^{j} & =\frac{1}{d_{\ell}^{j}} \sum_{k=\ell}^{m_{j}} d_{k}^{j} \xi_{k}^{j} .
\end{aligned}$$

By Equation 12.3, the first equation is equivalent to Equation 12.4. The other can be reexpressed as Equation 12.5. \(\square\)

The canonical class can be described (via the adjunction formula) as the cohomology class with
$$\begin{gathered}
\left\langle c_{1}(K),[\widehat{\Sigma}]\right\rangle=-b+2 g-2, \\
\left\langle c_{1}(K),\left[S_{i}^{j}\right]\right\rangle=a_{i}^{j}-2,
\end{gathered}$$
so, letting $\kappa$ denote the vector obtained by concatenating the above values, the vector $M^{-1} \kappa$ represents the coefficients of $c_{1}(K)$ in our basis. Now, we can rewrite Equation 12.1 as:
$$(\Xi-\kappa)^{\dagger} M^{-1} \Xi .$$
We can combine this formula with Equation 12.2 and the results of Lemma 12.1 to get that the dimension is given by
$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{dim}_{Y}(E)= & \sum_{j=1}^{n} \sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}}\left\{\left(\frac{x^{0}}{d_{0}^{j}}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell} \frac{1}{d_{i-1}^{j} d_{i}^{j}} \sum_{k=i}^{m_{j}} d_{k}^{j} \xi_{k}^{j}\right)\right)\right. \\
& \left.\left(d_{\ell}^{j} \xi_{\ell}^{j}-d_{\ell-1}^{j}-d_{\ell+1}^{j}+2 d_{\ell}^{j}\right)\right\} \\
& +x^{0}\left(e_{0}+b+2-2 g\right) .
\end{aligned}$$
We will rewrite this formula after a few observations.
First, notice that for a fixed $j$,
$$\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}}\left(d_{\ell}^{j} \xi_{\ell}^{j}-d_{\ell-1}^{j}-d_{\ell+1}^{j}+2 d_{\ell}^{j}\right) & =\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}} d_{\ell}^{j} \xi_{\ell}^{j}\right)+\left(d_{1}^{j}-d_{0}^{j}\right)-\left(d_{m_{j}}^{j}\right) \\
& =\epsilon_{j}+d_{1}^{j}-d_{0}^{j}+1
\end{aligned}$$
Thus, dividing this last expression by $d_{0}^{j}$, summing over $j$, and adding ( $e_{0}+$ $b+2-2 g$ ) we see that
$$\begin{aligned}
& \left(\sum_{j=1}^{n} \frac{1}{d_{0}^{j}} \sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}}\left(d_{\ell}^{j} \xi_{\ell}^{j}-d_{\ell-1}^{j}-d_{\ell+1}^{j}+2 d_{\ell}^{j}\right)\right)+\left(e_{0}+b+2-2 g\right) \\
& \quad=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{j}}{d_{0}^{j}}+\frac{d_{1}^{j}}{d_{0}^{j}}-1+\frac{1}{d_{0}^{j}}\right)\right)+\left(e_{0}+b+2-2 g\right) \\
& \quad=\operatorname{deg}(E)+\operatorname{deg}(Y)-\operatorname{deg}\left(K_{\Sigma}\right)
\end{aligned}$$

Moreover, by the discrete analogue of integration by parts, applied twice,
$$\begin{aligned}
& \sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}}\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell} \frac{1}{d_{i-1}^{j} d_{i}^{j}} \sum_{k=i}^{m_{j}} d_{k}^{j} \xi_{k}^{j}\right)\left(\left(d_{\ell+1}^{j}-d_{\ell}^{j}\right)-\left(d_{\ell}^{j}-d_{\ell-1}^{j}\right)\right)\right\} \\
& \quad=\left(d_{m+1}^{j}-d_{m}^{j}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j}} \frac{1}{d_{i-1}^{j} d_{i}^{j}} \sum_{k=i}^{m_{j}} d_{k}^{j} \xi_{k}^{j}\right)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{d_{\ell}^{j}}-\frac{1}{d_{\ell-1}^{j}}\right) \sum_{k=\ell}^{m_{j}} d_{k}^{j} \xi_{k}^{j}\right) \\
& \quad=-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j}} \frac{1}{d_{i-1}^{j} d_{i}^{j}} \sum_{k=i}^{m_{j}} d_{k}^{j} \xi_{k}^{j}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{d_{0}^{j}} \sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}} d_{\ell}^{j} \xi_{k}^{j}\right)+\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}} \xi_{\ell}^{j}\right) \\
& \quad=-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j}} \frac{1}{d_{i-1}^{j} d_{i}^{j}} \sum_{k=i}^{m_{j}} d_{k}^{j} \xi_{k}^{j}\right)-\frac{\epsilon_{j}}{d_{0}^{j}}+\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}} \xi_{\ell}^{j}\right)
\end{aligned}$$
Substituting Equations 12.7, 12.8, and 12.4 back into Equation 12.6, we get:
$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{dim}_{Y}(E)= & \sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\{\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}} \xi_{\ell}^{j}\right)-\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{j}} d_{\ell}^{j} \xi_{\ell}^{j} \sum_{i=1}^{\ell} \frac{1}{d_{i-1}^{j} d_{i}^{j}}\right)\right. \\
& \left.-\left(\sum_{\ell, k=1}^{m_{j}} d_{k}^{j} \xi_{k}^{j} d_{\ell}^{j} \xi_{\ell}^{j} \sum_{i=1}^{\min (k, \ell)} \frac{1}{d_{i-1}^{j} d_{i}^{j}}\right)\right\} \\
& +e_{0}+\frac{\operatorname{deg}(E)}{\operatorname{deg}(Y)}\left(\operatorname{deg}(E)-\operatorname{deg}\left(K_{\Sigma}\right)\right)
\end{aligned}$$

\section*{13. Examples.}

In this section, we give some examples of the theory. We restrict for simplicity to the case of rational homology spheres, i.e. those Seifert fibrations
$$\pi: Y \rightarrow \Sigma$$
which have non-zero degree, and whose base orbifold has genus zero. These are the manifolds for which the reducible locus consists of isolated points.

If the base orbifold has less than three singular points (so that the total space $Y$ is a lens space), then the Euler characteristic of the base orbifold is positive, so that there are no irreducible critical points. This agrees with the calculations which can be made for the critical points of the equations using the Levi-Civita connection for a metric of positive curvature (see [34]).

Definition 13.1. An orbifold is called simply-connected if its genus is zero and its multiplicities are pairwise relatively prime.

Remark 13.2. A more natural definition is to say that an orbifold is simply-connected if it admits no non-trivial, connected orbifold covering spaces. This condition is equivalent to the above definition, according to the general theory of orbifolds (see [9], [30]).

For simply-connected orbifolds, the isomorphism class of an orbifold line bundle is uniquely specified by its degree. According to Corollary 5.17, if
$$\pi: Y \rightarrow \Sigma$$
is a Seifert fibered space over a simply-connected orbifold $\Sigma$, the the reducible locus for any $\operatorname{Spin}_{\mathrm{c}}(3)$ structure is smooth. According to Theorem 2.13, these simply-connected orbifolds are precisely those which arise as the base spaces of integral homology Seifert fibered spaces. In fact, given a simply-connected orbifold $\Sigma$, there are exactly two Seifert fibered spaces over $\Sigma$ whose underlying three-manifold is an integral homology sphere. The two fibrations correspond to the two generators of the topological Picard group of $\Sigma$, and the two underlying three-manifolds are naturally (orientation-reversing) diffeomorphic. Since $\Sigma$ is uniquely specified by its (relatively prime, positive, integral) multiplicities $\left(\alpha_{1}, \ldots, \alpha_{n}\right)$ this three-manifold can be unambiguously be described by the same data. It is typically denoted $\Sigma\left(\alpha_{1}, \ldots, \alpha_{n}\right)$. The convention that it correspond to a line bundle with negative degree (unambiguouisly) gives $\Sigma\left(\alpha_{1}, \ldots, \alpha_{n}\right)$ the additional structure of a Seifert fibration.

The quantity
$$\left\lfloor-\frac{\chi(\Sigma)}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor g-1+\frac{1}{2} \sum\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{i}}-1\right)\right\rfloor$$
gives the maximum dimension of any irreducible critical manifold. In particular, since for any homology sphere each $\alpha_{i} \geq 2$, the above result says that a Seifert fibered homology sphere with fewer than five singular five singular fibers has a discrete critical manifold. (When we allow five singular fibers, some homology spheres, e.g. $\Sigma(2,3,5,7,11)$, still have discrete critical manifolds while others, e.g. $\Sigma(3,5,7,11,13)$ do not.)

Note that these critical manifolds are always rational; indeed they are projective spaces. (It is interesting to compare this with the analogous statement for $S U(2)$ representation varieties, see [9].)

When all the critical manifolds are isolated points we can proceed as in the classical Floer picture (see for example [7]), to form a relatively graded chain complex, the Seiberg-Witten Floer complex which depends on the Seifert fibration
$$\pi: Y \rightarrow \Sigma$$
and the $\operatorname{Spin}_{\mathrm{c}}(3)$ structure on $Y,\left(\mathrm{CF}_{*}(Y, W), \partial\right)$. The underlying group is a free Abelian group generated by the irreducible critical points $\{\mathfrak{b}\}$ of cs. The relative grading is defined by
$$\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{a})-\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{b})=\mathrm{e}-\operatorname{dim} \mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}),$$
the expected dimension of the moduli space of parameterized flow lines from $\mathfrak{a}$ to $\mathfrak{b}$. The moduli spaces of flows always have even expected dimension, since they are identified with moduli spaces of divisors. This says that any two critical points have even relative grading, so the boundary map must be identically zero, so that the homology groups of this complex the irreducible Seiberg-Witten Floer homology, denoted $\mathrm{HF}_{*}^{i r r}(Y, W)$ is, as a graded group, isomorphic to the Seiberg-Witten Floer chain complex. In general, these homology groups depend the auxiliary data - the metric on $Y$ and the choice of connection used on $T Y$ (choices which we have made in this paper); in particular they are not topological invariants of the three-manifold underlying $Y$. However, these groups enjoy certain functorial properties (product formulas) useful for calculating Seiberg-Witten invariants for four-manifolds glued along Seifert fibered spaces. According to recent work ([18] and [26]), they can also be applied to the problem of studying the fillable contact structures on these three-manifolds.
