## Deep-Reasoning

This step achieved a genuine structural advance and then a consequential self-correction. The original construction derived a four-step chain for the cutoff flow: (1) q=2 energy with critical BG-transferred Sobolev absorption, (2) 4D parabolic Sobolev embedding giving spacetime L³, (3) q=3 with time cutoff giving time-uniform spatial L³, and (4) local boundedness. Steps 1-3 are mathematically valid: the BG energy transfer cancels χ₀ exactly (ℓ⁴ fint|Rm|² ≤ CK₀r⁴), the a₀ factor cancels in the critical absorption making the threshold γ = Cr²√(1+K₀) independent of the cutoff level, the parabolic Sobolev exponent 2(n+2)/n = 3 is specific to dimension 4, and the time-cutoff source control uses the bounded spacetime L³ from Step 2. All verified numerically for χ₀ from 1 to 10⁻⁶. However, Step 4 (Stampacchia on the rescaled ball) contained a decisive scaling error: under metric rescaling g̃ = ℓ⁻²g, curvature transforms as |Rm(g̃)| = ℓ²|Rm(g)|, so F is NOT invariant. The raw Stampacchia output is ℓ²F ≤ C, equivalently |aRm| ≤ Cr⁻², which is weaker than claimed. The correct mechanism is A03's q→3q/2 weighted-moment Moser iteration (their Sections 10-11), which avoids metric rescaling entirely. The convergent infinite product yields |aRm| ≤ Cγ/t = O(1) at the smoothing time and |aE| ≤ Cδ(r²/t)^{1/3}. This is the correct output for the continuation argument: on the plateau χ₀=1, it gives |Rm|=O(1) and controlled charts; in the transition, the metric barely moves so |Rm| stays near its initial O(1) value. A03's derivative-free refinement (message #294) further simplifies the zero-order first-exit: no quantitative pointwise control of χ∇(aE) is needed because all Hessian-a terms integrate by parts and cutoff gradients transfer under bilipschitz comparability. The remaining load-bearing derivation is the weak nested time-space estimate (A03 eq. 11.1) uniformly with finite-neighbor terms. A02's CZ audit confirms the weighted estimates are robust against concentrated initial data. The full Yau 26 problem remains open: this addresses only the four-dimensional negative LeBrun laboratory.
