## Deep-Reasoning

This Preceptor step corrected a material error in the collapsed positive-branch interpretation from Step 36 and clarified which part of the recent work remains valid. The full Yau-26 target remains wholly unresolved: no dimension-only Ricci-pinching constants are established in dimension 4 or higher, no counterexample topology with arbitrarily tight pinching and no Einstein metric has been produced, the negative branch remains conditional, and Lean formalization still covers only local algebraic kernels.

The key correction is that Step 36 located struts incorrectly. It was right that Delzant adjacent rod labels form a \(\mathbb Z^2\)-basis and that corners are codimension-four fixed points. But it was wrong to infer that alternating adjacent labels exclude struts or period deficits. In the Weyl/rod formalism, a conical singularity is not fundamentally a corner object; it is the failure of the collapsing circle along a rod to close with the unique global period required for that circle. Emparan--Reall's generalized Weyl source, read in this step at lines 1358--1426 and 1429--1438, states the decisive mechanism directly: rod sources for a spatial coordinate are bolts; avoiding a conical singularity requires identifying that coordinate with a period determined by the sources; when the same coordinate has more than one finite rod source, the appropriate periods at the different bolts may differ, and conical singularities result. Therefore the alternating pattern \((1,0),(0,1),(-1,0),(0,-1),\ldots\) still imposes many same-circle smoothness conditions, because \(v\) and \(-v\) generate the same circle subgroup. The overdetermination is among nonadjacent rods with the same subgroup, not at adjacent corners. This aligns with the old Step-15/17 two-rod rigidity: local Schwarzschild--de Sitter germs exist for every parameter \(A\), but smooth closure at two same-circle rods imposes \(\mu=1\), forcing \(A=0\). The same closing/period principle also resembles Step 27's H3 endpoint obstruction. This repeated mechanism has produced actual theorems in the session, so it should be elevated above the now-retracted coarse energy/NUT argument.

The noncollapsed Route-A correction from Steps 35--36 remains sound at the current level. The \(\delta\log(1/t)\) rate condition introduced in Step 34 should not be retained as a theorem hypothesis: reading the decaying Eguchi--Hanson coefficient in a fixed bubble-frame annulus gives relative error \(O(\delta\log R)+O(1/R)\) with the iterated limit \(R\) fixed, \(\delta_i\to0\), then \(R\to\infty\). This does not solve the noncollapsed positive branch; it only removes one artificial obstruction. The real analytic bridges remain exactly the hard ones: harmonic-radius/annular control for merely \(C^0\)-Ricci-pinched metrics and second-order bubble-frame convergence with matching of the quadratic asymptotic to the orbifold two-jet.

The energy accounting also needed a sharper Preceptor reading. Atiyah--LeBrun edge-cone terms are separate defect terms in a singular limit, while the smooth approximants have a fixed bulk Chern--Gauss--Bonnet budget. Step 36's claim that NUT smoothing is merely harmless \(O(1)\) per corner is not enough, because the budget is already approximately saturated if each of \(\chi\) rods contributes \(8\pi^2\). Any independent positive corner/neck contribution of order one per cell would overflow by order \(\chi\), unless it is already part of the per-rod quantum or is lower order in the correct decomposition. Thus the useful energy question is not simply strut versus NUT, but whether the proposed rod quantum already includes the polar/corner regions and whether there is additional collar/period-defect energy. This remains open and should be tied to the same period-transport computation, not treated as a solved bookkeeping point.

I ran two exploratory Weyl probes. The first, `workspace/scripts/step37_weyl_period_probe.py`, intentionally treated alternating finite rods in a half-plane with \(U_1\) the sum of selected finite rod potentials and \(U_2=\log r-U_1\). Its output `workspace/data/step37_weyl_period_probe.json` already showed same-label period ranges in finite alternating chains, but it mishandled the source partition at the unbounded ends. I therefore wrote the corrected partition version `workspace/scripts/step37_weyl_partition_period_probe.py`, whose output is `workspace/data/step37_weyl_partition_period_probe.json`. In that model the axis is partitioned into semi-infinite and finite intervals, each carrying density \(1/2\) source for exactly one coordinate, preserving \(U_1+U_2=\log r\). The log-period coefficient \(\ell_j=\nu-(U_i-\log r)\) near a finite rod measures the required period for the circle labeled by \(i\). The probe found nonzero same-label mismatches already for equal alternating finite chains: for 4 finite rods the same-label exponential period range is approximately \(1.5\), for 6 rods approximately \(1.875\), for 8 rods approximately \(2.1875\), and for 10 rods approximately \(2.46094\); perturbed 6- and 8-rod chains also show mismatches of comparable size. This is not a compact-polygon proof and not a Yau-26 result, but it is good adversarial evidence against the Step-36 inference that alternating adjacency makes the model automatically strut-free. It supports the strategist's corrected target: define and compute the period transport/monodromy around the compact polygon.

The negative-branch peer material remains promising but not mature. A03's Step 36 review and A01's Step 34 note retain the fixed \(J_6\) progress and two-factor defect absorption as a real analytic simplification, but they explicitly do not prove degenerate-flow existence, continuation, terminal unweighted \(Q=\int|E|^2\), or a dimension-only gap. This agrees with the earlier Preceptor warnings: the negative route remains a restricted four-dimensional laboratory with serious moving-gauge, cutoff-jet, Sobolev, and terminal-calibration obligations. It should not be used to claim any branch of Yau-26.

The strongest next direction is now the collapsed positive closing/monodromy mechanism, not another coarse energy accounting step. It has the virtue that it attacks a recurring obstruction principle seen in several settings: local pinched or Einstein-like pieces have many parameters, but global smooth closure forces period/surface-gravity compatibility. A successful compact toric monodromy obstruction would give a topology-uniform collapse obstruction for the alternating stress family; a successful trivialization would produce a much sharper BREAK candidate. Either outcome materially changes the four-dimensional positive branch, while preserving the full target distinction that even a complete \(P_4^+\) result would not solve all dimensions, signs, or Lean formalization.
