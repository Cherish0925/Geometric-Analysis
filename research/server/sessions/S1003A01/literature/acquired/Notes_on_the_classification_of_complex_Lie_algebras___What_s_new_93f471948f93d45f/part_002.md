for all ![{v \in V_\lambda^{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv+%5Cin+V_%5Clambda%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{x \in{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). When this occurs we call ![{v}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) a _weight vector_ with weight ![{\lambda}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

**— 2\. Engel’s theorem and Lie’s theorem —**

In the introduction we gave the two basic examples of nilpotent and solvable Lie algebras, namely the strictly upper triangular and upper triangular matrices. The theorems [of Engel](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engel%27s_theorem) and [of Lie](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie%27s_theorem#Lie.27s_theorem) assert, roughly speaking, that these examples (and subalgebras thereof) are essentially the only type of solvable and nilpotent Lie algebras that can exist, at least in the concrete setting of subalgebras of ![{{\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Among other things, these theorems greatly clarify the representation theory of nilpotent and solvable Lie algebras.

We begin with Engel’s theorem.

> **Theorem 3 (Engel’s theorem)** Let ![{{\mathfrak g} \subset {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a concrete Lie algebra such that every element ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent as a linear transformation on ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).
>
> - (i) If ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-trivial, then there is a non-zero element ![{v}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) which is annihilated by every element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).
> - (ii) There is a basis of ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for which all elements of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are strictly upper triangular. In particular, ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent.

_Proof:_ We begin with (i). We induct on the dimension of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The claim is trivial for dimensions ![{0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so suppose that ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has dimension greater than ![{1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and that the claim is already proven for smaller dimensions.

Let ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a maximal proper subalgebra of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has dimension strictly between zero and ![{\hbox{dim} \mathfrak{g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bdim%7D+%5Cmathfrak%7Bg%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (since all one-dimensional subspaces are proper subalgebras). Observe that for every ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) acts on both the vector spaces ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and thus also on the quotient space ![{{\mathfrak g}/{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2F%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). As ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent, all of these actions are nilpotent also. In particular, by induction hypothesis, there is ![{v \in {\mathfrak g}/{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2F%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) which is annihilated by ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Let ![{w}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a representative of ![{v}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then ![{[w, {\mathfrak h}] \subset {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Bw%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and so ![{\hbox{span}(w, {\mathfrak h})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bspan%7D%28w%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a subalgebra and is thus all of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

By induction hypothesis again, the space ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of vectors in ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) annihilated by ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-trivial; as ![{[w, {\mathfrak h}] \subset {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Bw%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), it is preserved by ![{w}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). As ![{w}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent, there is a non-trivial element of ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) annihilated by ![{w}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and hence by ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), as required.

Now we prove (ii). We induct on the dimension of ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The case of dimension zero is trivial, so suppose ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has dimension at least one, and the claim has already been proven for dimension ![{\hbox{dim}(V)-1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bdim%7D%28V%29-1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By (i), we may find a non-trivial vector ![{v}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) annihilated by ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and so we may project ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) down to ![{{\mathfrak{gl}}(V / \hbox{span}(v))}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V+%2F+%5Chbox%7Bspan%7D%28v%29%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By the induction hypothesis, there is a basis for ![{V/\hbox{span}(v)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%2F%5Chbox%7Bspan%7D%28v%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on which the projection of any element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is strictly upper-triangular; pulling this basis back to ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and adjoining ![{v}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we obtain the claim. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

As a corollary of this theorem and the short exact sequence [(6)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#central) we see that an abstract Lie algebra ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent iff ![{\hbox{ad} {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent iff ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent in ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for every ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (i.e. every element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is _ad-nilpotent_).

Engel’s theorem is in fact valid over every field. The analogous theorem of Lie for solvable algebras, however, relies much more strongly on the specific properties of the complex field ![{{\bf C}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

> **Theorem 4 (Lie’s theorem)** Let ![{{\mathfrak g} \subset {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a solvable concrete Lie algebra.
>
> - (i) If ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-trivial, there exists a non-zero element ![{v}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) which is an eigenvector for every element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).
> - (ii) There is a basis for ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that every element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is upper triangular.

Note that if one specialises Lie’s theorem to abelian ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) then one essentially recovers the abelian theory of the previous section.

_Proof:_ We prove (i). As before we induct on the dimension of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The dimension zero case is trivial, so suppose that ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has dimension at least one and that the claim has been proven for smaller dimensions.

Let ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a codimension one subalgebra of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); such an algebra can be formed by taking a codimension one subspace of the abelianisation ![{{\mathfrak g}/[{\mathfrak g}, {\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2F%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (which has dimension at least one, else ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) will not be solvable) and then pulling back to ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Note that ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is automatically an ideal.

By induction, there is a non-zero element ![{v}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that every element of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has ![{v}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as an eigenvector, thus we have

![\displaystyle xv = \lambda(x) v](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+xv+%3D+%5Clambda%28x%29+v&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for all ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and some linear functional ![{\lambda: {\mathfrak h} \rightarrow {\bf C}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda%3A+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). If we then set ![{W = V_\lambda^{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW+%3D+V_%5Clambda%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to be the simultaneous eigenspace

![\displaystyle W := \{ w \in V: xv = \lambda(x) v \hbox{ for all } x \in {\mathfrak h} \}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+W+%3A%3D+%5C%7B+w+%5Cin+V%3A+xv+%3D+%5Clambda%28x%29+v+%5Chbox%7B+for+all+%7D+x+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5C%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

then ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a non-trivial subspace of ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Let ![{y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be an element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that is not in ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and let ![{w \in W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw+%5Cin+W%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Consider the space spanned by the orbit ![{w, yw, y^2 w, \ldots}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw%2C+yw%2C+y%5E2+w%2C+%5Cldots%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By finite dimensionality, this space has a basis ![{w, yw, y^2 w, \ldots, y^{n-1} w}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw%2C+yw%2C+y%5E2+w%2C+%5Cldots%2C+y%5E%7Bn-1%7D+w%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for some ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By induction and definition of ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we see that every ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) acts on this space by an upper-triangular matrix with diagonal entries ![{\lambda(x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda%28x%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in this basis. Of course, ![{y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) acts on this space as well, and so ![{[x,y]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Bx%2Cy%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has trace zero on this space, thus ![{n \lambda([x,y]) = 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn+%5Clambda%28%5Bx%2Cy%5D%29+%3D+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and so ![{\lambda([x,y])=0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda%28%5Bx%2Cy%5D%29%3D0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (here we use the characteristic zero nature of ![{{\bf C}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)). From this we see that ![{y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) fixes ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). If we let ![{v'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be an eigenvector of ![{y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (which exists from the Jordan decomposition of ![{y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)), we conclude that ![{v'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a simultaneous eigenvector of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as required.

The claim (ii) follows from (i) much as in Engel’s theorem. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

**— 3\. Characterising semisimplicity —**

The objective of this section will be to prove Theorem [1](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#semisimple).

Let ![{{\mathfrak g} \subset {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be an concrete Lie algebra, and ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be an element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then the components ![{x_{ss}, x_n: V \rightarrow V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%2C+x_n%3A+V+%5Crightarrow+V%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) need not lie in ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). However they behave “as if” they lie in ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for the purposes of taking Lie brackets, in the following sense:

> **Lemma 5** Let ![{{\mathfrak g} \subset {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and let ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) have Jordan decomposition ![{x = x_{ss} + x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%3D+x_%7Bss%7D+%2B+x_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then ![{[x_{ss},{\mathfrak g}] \subset [{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Bx_%7Bss%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D+%5Csubset+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{[\overline{x_{ss}},{\mathfrak g}] \subset [{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D+%5Csubset+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{[x_n,{\mathfrak g}] \subset [{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Bx_n%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D+%5Csubset+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

_Proof:_ As ![{x_{ss}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are semisimple and nilpotent on ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and commute with each other, ![{\hbox{ad} x_{ss}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x_%7Bss%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\hbox{ad} x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are semisimple and nilpotent on ![{{\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and also commute with each other (this can for instance by using Lie’s theorem (or the Jordan normal form) to place ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in upper triangular form and computing everything explicitly). Thus ![{\hbox{ad} x = \hbox{ad} x_{ss} + \hbox{ad} x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x+%3D+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x_%7Bss%7D+%2B+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the Jordan-Chevalley decomposition of ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and in particular ![{\hbox{ad} x_{ss} = Q(\hbox{ad} x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x_%7Bss%7D+%3D+Q%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for some polynomial ![{Q}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BQ%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with zero constant coefficient. Since ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) maps ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to the subalgebra ![{[{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we conclude that ![{\hbox{ad} x_{ss} = Q(\hbox{ad} x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x_%7Bss%7D+%3D+Q%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) does also, thus ![{[x_{ss}, {\mathfrak g}] \subset [{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Bx_%7Bss%7D%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D+%5Csubset+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as required. Similarly for ![{\overline{x_{ss}}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (note that ![{\hbox{ad} \overline{x_{ss}} = \overline{ \hbox{ad} x_{ss} }}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D+%3D+%5Coverline%7B+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x_%7Bss%7D+%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)). ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

We can now use this (together with Engel’s theorem and the test [(7)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#test) for nilpotency) to obtain a part of Theorem [1](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#semisimple):

> **Proposition 6** Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a simple Lie algebra. Then the Killing form ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate.

_Proof:_ As ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is simple, its center ![{Z({\mathfrak g})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BZ%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is trivial, so by [(6)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#central)![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is isomorphic to ![{\hbox{ad} {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). In particular we may assume that ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a concrete Lie algebra, thus ![{{\mathfrak g} \subset {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for some vector space ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Suppose for contradiction that ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is degenerate. Using the skew-adjointness identity

![\displaystyle K( [z,x], y ) = - K( x, [z,y] )](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+K%28+%5Bz%2Cx%5D%2C+y+%29+%3D+-+K%28+x%2C+%5Bz%2Cy%5D+%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for all ![{x,y,z \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%2Cy%2Cz+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (which comes from the cyclic properties of trace), we see that the kernel ![{\{ x \in {\mathfrak g}: K(x,y)=0 \hbox{ for all } y \in {\mathfrak g} \}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5C%7B+x+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%3A+K%28x%2Cy%29%3D0+%5Chbox%7B+for+all+%7D+y+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a non-trivial ideal of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and is thus all of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is simple. Thus ![{K(x,y)=0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%28x%2Cy%29%3D0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all ![{x, y \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%2C+y+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Now let ![{x,y,z \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%2Cy%2Cz+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By Lemma [5](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#so), ![{\overline{x_{ss}}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) acts by Lie bracket on ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and so one can define ![{\hbox{ad} \overline{x_{ss}} \in {\mathfrak{gl}}({\mathfrak g})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). We now consider the quantity

![\displaystyle \hbox{tr}_{\mathfrak g} (\hbox{ad} \overline{x_{ss}}) (\hbox{ad} [y,z]).](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Chbox%7Btr%7D_%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D%29+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5By%2Cz%5D%29.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

We can rearrange this as

![\displaystyle \hbox{tr}_{\mathfrak g} (\hbox{ad} [y,\overline{x_{ss}}]) (\hbox{ad} z).](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Chbox%7Btr%7D_%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5By%2C%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D%5D%29+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+z%29.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

By Lemma [5](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#so), ![{[y,\overline{x_{ss}}] \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5By%2C%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D%5D+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so this is equal to

![\displaystyle K( [y,\overline{x_{ss}}], z ) = 0,](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+K%28+%5By%2C%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D%5D%2C+z+%29+%3D+0%2C&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

and so

![\displaystyle \hbox{tr}_{\mathfrak g} (\hbox{ad} \overline{x_{ss}}) (\hbox{ad} w) = 0](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Chbox%7Btr%7D_%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D%29+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+w%29+%3D+0&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for all ![{w \in [{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw+%5Cin+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). On the other hand, ![{[{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is an ideal of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); as ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is simple, we must thus have ![{{\mathfrak g} = [{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%3D+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (i.e. ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is perfect). As ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we conclude that

![\displaystyle \hbox{tr}_{\mathfrak g} (\hbox{ad} \overline{x_{ss}}) (\hbox{ad} x) = 0.](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Chbox%7Btr%7D_%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Coverline%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D%29+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%29+%3D+0.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

From [(7)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#test) we conclude that ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent for every ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By Engel’s theorem, this implies that ![{\hbox{ad} {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and hence ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), is nilpotent; but ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is simple, giving the desired contradiction. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

> **Corollary 7** Let ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a simple ideal of a Lie algebra ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is complemented by another ideal ![{{\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (thus ![{{\mathfrak h} \cap {\mathfrak k} = \{0\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5Ccap+%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D+%3D+%5C%7B0%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{{\mathfrak h} + {\mathfrak k} = {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%2B+%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)), with ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) isomorphic to the direct sum ![{{\mathfrak h} \oplus {\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5Coplus+%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

_Proof:_ The adjoint action of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) restricts to the ideal ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and gives a restricted Killing form

![\displaystyle K_\mathfrak{h}(x,y) := \hbox{tr}_\mathfrak{h}( (\hbox{ad} x) (\hbox{ad} y) ).](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+K_%5Cmathfrak%7Bh%7D%28x%2Cy%29+%3A%3D+%5Chbox%7Btr%7D_%5Cmathfrak%7Bh%7D%28+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%29+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+y%29+%29.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

By Proposition [6](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#pr), this bilinear form is non-degenerate on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so the orthogonal complement

![\displaystyle {\mathfrak k} := {\mathfrak h}^\perp = \{ x \in {\mathfrak g}: K_\mathfrak{h}(x,y) = 0\hbox{ for all } y \in {\mathfrak h} \}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D+%3A%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%5Cperp+%3D+%5C%7B+x+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%3A+K_%5Cmathfrak%7Bh%7D%28x%2Cy%29+%3D+0%5Chbox%7B+for+all+%7D+y+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5C%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

is a complementary subspace to ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). It can be verified to also be an ideal. Since ![{[{\mathfrak h},{\mathfrak k}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) lies in both ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{{\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we see that ![{[{\mathfrak h},{\mathfrak k}]=0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%5D%3D0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and so ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is isomorphic to ![{{\mathfrak h} \oplus {\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5Coplus+%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as claimed. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

Now we can prove Theorem [1](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#semisimple). We first observe that (i) trivially implies (ii); conversely, if ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has a non-trivial solvable ideal ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then every element of the derived series of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is also an ideal of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and in particular ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) will have a non-trivial abelian ideal. Thus (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

Now we show that (i) implies (iv), which we do by induction on the dimension of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Of course we may assume ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-trivial. Let ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a non-trivial ideal of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of minimal dimension. If ![{{\mathfrak h} = {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) then ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is simple (note that it cannot be abelian as ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-trivial and semisimple) and we are done. If ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is strictly smaller than ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then it also has no non-trivial solvable ideals (because the radical of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a [characteristic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_subgroup) subalgebra of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and is thus an ideal in ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)) and so by induction is isomorphic to the direct sum of simple Lie algebras; as ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) was minimal, we conclude that ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is itself simple. By Corollary [7](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#lo), ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) then splits as the direct sum of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and a semisimple Lie algebra of strictly smaller dimension, and the claim follows from the induction hypothesis.

From Proposition [6](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#pr) we see that (iv) implies (iii), so to finish the proof of Theorem [1](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#semisimple) it suffices to show that (iii) implies (ii). Indeed, if ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has a non-trivial abelian ideal ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then for any ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{y \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{\hbox{ad} x \hbox{ad} y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+y%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) annihilates ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and also has range in ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), hence has trace zero, so ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-orthogonal to ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), giving the degeneracy of the Killing form.

> **Remark 8** Similar methods also give the _Cartan solvability criterion_: a Lie algebra ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is solvable if and only if ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is orthogonal to ![{[{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with respect to the Killing form. Indeed, the “only if” part follows easily from Lie’s theorem, while for the “if” part one can adapt the proof of Proposition [6](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#pr) to show that if ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is orthogonal to ![{[{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then every element of ![{\hbox{ad} [{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent, hence by Engel’s theorem ![{\hbox{ad} [{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent, and so from the short exact sequence [(6)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#central) we see that ![{[{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent, and hence ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is solvable.

> **Remark 9** The decomposition of a semisimple Lie algebra as the direct sum of simple Lie algebras is unique up to isomorphism and permutation. Indeed, suppose that ![{\bigoplus_{i=1}^n {\mathfrak g}_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbigoplus_%7Bi%3D1%7D%5En+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is isomorphic to ![{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n'} {\mathfrak g}'_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbigoplus_%7Bj%3D1%7D%5E%7Bn%27%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%27_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for some simple ![{{\mathfrak g}_i, {\mathfrak g}'_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_i%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%27_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). We project each ![{{\mathfrak g}'_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%27_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to ![{{\mathfrak g}_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and observe from simplicity that these projections must either be zero or isomorphisms (cf. [Schur’s lemma](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur%27s_lemma)). For fixed ![{i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), there must be at least one ![{j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bj%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for which the projection is an isomorphism (otherwise ![{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n'} {\mathfrak g}'_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbigoplus_%7Bj%3D1%7D%5E%7Bn%27%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%27_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) could not generate all of ![{\bigoplus_{i=1}^n {\mathfrak g}_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbigoplus_%7Bi%3D1%7D%5En+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)); on the other hand, as any two ![{{\mathfrak g}'_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%27_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) commute with each other in the direct sum, and ![{{\mathfrak g}_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nonabelian, there is at most one ![{j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bj%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for which the projection is an isomorphism. This gives the required identification of the ![{{\mathfrak g}_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{{\mathfrak g}'_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%27_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) up to isomorphism and permutation.

> **Remark 10** One can also establish complete reducibility by using the [Weyl unitary trick](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarian_trick), in which one first creates a real compact Lie group whose Lie algebra is a real form of the complex Lie algebra being studied, and then uses the complete reducibility of actions of compact groups. This also gives an alternate way to establish Theorem [44](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#weyl-thm) in the appendix.

Semisimple Lie algebras have a number of important non-degeneracy properties. For instance, they have no non-trivial outer automorphisms (at the infinitesimal level, at least):

> **Lemma 11** Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a semisimple Lie algebra. Then every derivation ![{D \in \hbox{Der} {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BD+%5Cin+%5Chbox%7BDer%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is inner, thus ![{D = \hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BD+%3D+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for some ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

_Proof:_ From the identity ![{[D, \hbox{ad} x] = \hbox{ad} Dx}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5BD%2C+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%5D+%3D+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+Dx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) we see that ![{\hbox{ad} g}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+g%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is an ideal in ![{\hbox{Der} {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7BDer%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The trace form ![{(D_1,D_2) \mapsto \hbox{tr}(D_1 D_2)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28D_1%2CD_2%29+%5Cmapsto+%5Chbox%7Btr%7D%28D_1+D_2%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{\hbox{Der} {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7BDer%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) restricts to the Killing form on ![{\hbox{ad}({\mathfrak g})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), which is non-degenerate.

Suppose for contradiction that ![{\hbox{ad}({\mathfrak g})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is not all of ![{\hbox{Der}({\mathfrak g})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7BDer%7D%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then there is a non-trivial derivation ![{D}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BD%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) which is trace-form orthogonal to ![{\hbox{ad}({\mathfrak g})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), thus ![{D}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BD%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is trace-orthogonal to ![{[\hbox{ad} x, \hbox{ad} y]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%2C+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+y%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all ![{x,y \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%2Cy+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so that ![{[D, \hbox{ad} x] = \hbox{ad} {Dx}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5BD%2C+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%5D+%3D+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7BDx%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is trace-orthogonal to ![{\hbox{ad} y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+y%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all ![{x,y \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%2Cy+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). As ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate, we conclude that ![{Dx=0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BDx%3D0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and so ![{D}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BD%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is trivial, a contradiction. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

This fact, combined with the complete reducibility of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-modules (a fact which we will prove in an appendix) implies that the Jordan decomposition preserves concrete semisimple Lie algebras:

> **Corollary 12** Let ![{{\mathfrak g} \subset {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a concrete semisimple Lie algebra, and let ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then ![{x_{ss}, x_n, \overline{x}_{ss}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%2C+x_n%2C+%5Coverline%7Bx%7D_%7Bss%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) also lie in ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

_Proof:_ By Theorem [1](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#semisimple), ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the direct sum of commuting simple algebras. It is easy to see that if ![{x,y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%2Cy%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) commute then the Jordan decomposition of ![{x+y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%2By%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) arises from the sum of the Jordan decompositions of ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) separately, so we may assume without loss of generality that ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is simple.

Observe that if ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) splits as the direct sum ![{V = V_1 \oplus V_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV+%3D+V_1+%5Coplus+V_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of two ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-invariant subspaces (so that ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) can be viewed as a subalgebra of ![{{\mathfrak{gl}}(V_1) \oplus {\mathfrak{gl}}(V_2)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V_1%29+%5Coplus+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V_2%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and the elements of ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) can be viewed as being block-diagonal in a suitable basis of ![{V_1,V_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV_1%2CV_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)), then the claim for ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) follows from that of ![{V_1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV_1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{V_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). So by an induction on dimension, it suffices to establish the claim under the hypothesis that ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is [indecomposable](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indecomposable_module), in that it cannot be expressed as the direct sum of two non-trivial invariant subspaces.

In the appendix we will show that every invariant subspace ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is _complemented_ in that one can write ![{V = W \oplus W'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV+%3D+W+%5Coplus+W%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for some invariant subspace ![{W'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Assuming this fact, it suffices to establish the claim in the case that ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is _irreducible_, in the sense that it contains no proper invariant subspaces.

By Lemma [7](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#lo), the operation ![{y \mapsto [x_{ss}, y]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cmapsto+%5Bx_%7Bss%7D%2C+y%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a derivation on ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), thus there exists ![{a \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Ba+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that ![{[x_{ss},y] = (\hbox{ad} a) y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Bx_%7Bss%7D%2Cy%5D+%3D+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+a%29+y%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all ![{y \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), thus ![{x_{ss}-a \in {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_%7Bss%7D-a+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) centralises ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By [Schur’s lemma](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur%27s_lemma) and the hypothesis of irreducibility, we conclude that ![{x_{ss}-a}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_%7Bss%7D-a%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a multiple of a constant ![{\lambda}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Onthe other hand, every element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has trace zero since ![{{\mathfrak g} = [{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%3D+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); in particular, ![{a}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Ba%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) have trace zero, and so ![{x_{ss}-a}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_%7Bss%7D-a%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has trace zero. But this trace is just ![{\lambda\hbox{dim} W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda%5Chbox%7Bdim%7D+W%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so we conclude that ![{x_{ss}-a = \lambda = 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_%7Bss%7D-a+%3D+%5Clambda+%3D+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and the claim follows for ![{x_{ss}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Similarly for ![{x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\overline{x}_{ss}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Coverline%7Bx%7D_%7Bss%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

This allows us to make the Jordan decomposition universal for semisimple algebras:

> **Lemma 13 (Semisimple Jordan decomposition)** Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a semisimple Lie algebra, and let ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then we have a unique decomposition ![{x = x_{ss} + x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%3D+x_%7Bss%7D+%2B+x_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that ![{\rho(x_{ss}) = (\rho(x))_{ss}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28x_%7Bss%7D%29+%3D+%28%5Crho%28x%29%29_%7Bss%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\rho(x_n) = (\rho(x))_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28x_n%29+%3D+%28%5Crho%28x%29%29_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for every representation ![{\rho}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

_Proof:_ As the adjoint representation is faithful we may assume without loss of generality that ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a concrete algebra, thus ![{{\mathfrak g} \subset {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The uniqueness is then clear by taking ![{\rho}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to be the identity. To obtain existence, we take ![{x_{ss}, x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%2C+x_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to be the concrete Jordan decomposition. We need to verify ![{\rho(x_{ss}) = (\rho(x))_{ss}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28x_%7Bss%7D%29+%3D+%28%5Crho%28x%29%29_%7Bss%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\rho(x_n) = (\rho(x))_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28x_n%29+%3D+%28%5Crho%28x%29%29_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for any representation ![{\rho: {\mathfrak g} \rightarrow {\mathfrak m}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%3A+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak+m%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The adjoint actions of ![{\rho(x_{ss})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28x_%7Bss%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\rho(x_n)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28x_n%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{\rho({\mathfrak g})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) commute and are semisimple and nilpotent respectively and so

![\displaystyle \hbox{ad} \rho(x_{ss}) = (\hbox{ad} \rho(x))_{ss}; \quad \hbox{ad} \rho(x_{n}) = (\hbox{ad} \rho(x))_{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Crho%28x_%7Bss%7D%29+%3D+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Crho%28x%29%29_%7Bss%7D%3B+%5Cquad+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Crho%28x_%7Bn%7D%29+%3D+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Crho%28x%29%29_%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

in ![{\hbox{ad} \rho(g)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Crho%28g%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (cf. the proof of Lemma [5](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#so)). A similar argument (applying Corollary [12](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#pres) to ![{\rho(g)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28g%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), which is isomorphic to a quotient of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and is thus semisimple, to keep ![{\rho(x)_{ss}, \rho(x)_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28x%29_%7Bss%7D%2C+%5Crho%28x%29_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{\rho({\mathfrak g})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)) gives

![\displaystyle \hbox{ad} (\rho(x)_{ss}) = (\hbox{ad} \rho(x))_{ss}; \quad \hbox{ad}(\rho(x)_{n}) = (\hbox{ad} \rho(x))_{n}.](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%28%5Crho%28x%29_%7Bss%7D%29+%3D+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Crho%28x%29%29_%7Bss%7D%3B+%5Cquad+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D%28%5Crho%28x%29_%7Bn%7D%29+%3D+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5Crho%28x%29%29_%7Bn%7D.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

Since the adjoint representation of the semisimple algebra ![{\rho({\mathfrak g})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is faithful, the claim follows. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

One can also show that ![{x_{ss}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_%7Bss%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) commute with each other and with the centraliser ![{C(x) := \{ y \in {\mathfrak g}: [x,y] = 0 \}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BC%28x%29+%3A%3D+%5C%7B+y+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%3A+%5Bx%2Cy%5D+%3D+0+%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by using the faithful nature of the adjoint representation for semisimple algebras, though we will not need these facts here. Using this lemma we have a well-defined notion of an element ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of a semisimple algebra ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) being semisimple (resp. nilpotent), namely that ![{x=x_{ss}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%3Dx_%7Bss%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) or ![{x=x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%3Dx_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Lemma [13](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sja) then implies that any representation of a semisimple element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is again semisimple, and any representation of a nilpotent element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is again nilpotent. This apparently innocuous statement relies heavily on the semisimple nature of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); note for instance that the representation

![\displaystyle t \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} 0 & t \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+t+%5Cmapsto+%5Cbegin%7Bpmatrix%7D+0+%26+t+%5C%5C+0+%26+0+%5Cend%7Bpmatrix%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

of the non-semisimple algebra ![{{\bf C} \equiv {\mathfrak{gl}}_1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+C%7D+%5Cequiv+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D_1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) into ![{{\mathfrak{gl}}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) takes semisimple elements to nilpotent ones.

**— 4\. Cartan subalgebras —**

While simple Lie algebras do not have any non-trivial ideals, they do have some very useful subalgebras known as [Cartan subalgebras](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan_subalgebra) which will eventually turn out to be abelian and which can be used to dramatically clarify the structure of the rest of the algebra.

We need some definitions. An element ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is said to be [regular](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_element_of_a_Lie_algebra) if its generalised null space

![\displaystyle {\mathfrak g}^x_0 := \{ y \in {\mathfrak g}: (\hbox{ad} x)^n y = 0 \hbox{ for some } n \}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5Ex_0+%3A%3D+%5C%7B+y+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%3A+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%29%5En+y+%3D+0+%5Chbox%7B+for+some+%7D+n+%5C%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

has minimal dimension. A _Cartan subalgebra_ of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a nilpotent subalgebra ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) which is its own [normaliser](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer_and_normalizer), thus ![{N({\mathfrak h}) := \{ x \in {\mathfrak g}: [x,{\mathfrak h}] \subset {\mathfrak h} \}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BN%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%29+%3A%3D+%5C%7B+x+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%3A+%5Bx%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is equal to ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). From the polynomial nature of the Lie algebra operations (and the [Noetherian nature of algebraic geometry](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noetherian_topological_space)) we see that the regular elements of ![{{\mathcal g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathcal+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are generic (i.e. they form a non-empty [Zariski-open](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_topology) subset of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)).

> **Example 14** In ![{{\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), the regular elements consist of the semisimple elements with distinct eigenvalues. Fixing a basis for ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), the space of elements of ![{{\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that are diagonalised by that basis form a Cartan subalgebra of ![{{\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Cartan algebras always exist, and can be constructed as generalised null spaces of regular elements:

> **Proposition 15 (Existence of Cartan subalgebras)** Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be an abstract Lie algebra. If ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is regular, then the generalised null space ![{{\mathfrak h} := {\mathfrak g}^x_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%3A%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5Ex_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a Cartan subalgebra.

_Proof:_ Suppose that ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is not nilpotent, then by Engel’s theorem the adjoint action of at least one element of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is not nilpotent. By the polynomial nature of the Lie algebra operations, we conclude that the adjoint action of a generic element of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is not nilpotent.
