## Deep-Reasoning

This step developed the transition-inclusive normalized cutoff-flow theorem for the 4D negative-branch program. The central mathematical advance is the proof that the coupled L² curvature-defect energy system closes independently under the 4D Sobolev-energy cancellation mechanism, without requiring pointwise curvature bounds as input.

The key algebraic finding was that the defect evolution identity is cleaner than initially thought: the cubic term tr(E³) fully cancels because the inverse-metric variation ∂_t g^{ij} = 2aE^{ij} contributes +4a tr(E³) that exactly cancels the -4a tr(E³) from the Lichnerowicz/damping interaction. The correct identity (∂_t - aΔ)|E|² = -2a|∇E|² + 4a Rm(E,E) + 2⟨E, C_a(E)⟩ has no explicit cubic defect reaction — it is precisely the Step 14 identity, providing a consistency check.

However, TTCS verification identified an important correction to the original Proposition 1: the maximum principle for |E|² still requires ||a Rm(g(t))||_∞ as input. C⁰ metric closeness does not control curvature (a second-derivative quantity), so the initial K₀ cannot simply be inserted. The maximum principle thus breaks only one direction of the continuation circle: given a weighted curvature bound (e.g. Li's C/t on the plateau or a degenerate ε-regularity result on the transition), defect preservation and metric equivalence follow unconditionally.

The most important new result is the closure of the coupled L² energy system. Define H_α = R_α + κP_α (with R_α the normalized curvature energy and P_α the normalized defect energy on the α-th cover element). The critical absorption mechanism — that C_S · ||Rm||_{L²} = O(√ε*) in 4D — absorbs the dangerous ∫|Rm||E|² reaction and the transition cross terms ∫|∇a|·|∇E|·|Rm|. The resulting closed inequality dH_α/dt ≤ Cr^{-2} Σ_{β~α} H_β gives H_*(t) ≤ e^{CNθ} H_*(0), establishing critical curvature-energy persistence with volume-independent constants. A01 independently developed the same Sobolev-energy mechanism in their Step 17 Section 5.5; the two derivations agree.

A key structural observation from the Strategist is that the volume-normalized form extends the result's reach beyond fixed topology. Under the critical near-Einstein profile, Vol(M) ~ 32π²A/9 and ∫|Rm|² ≤ 8π²χ + CVol, so K̄ = Vol⁻¹∫|Rm|² ≤ C(1 + χ/A). For critical Ishida-LeBrun families built from BMY-bounded surfaces of general type, χ/A ≥ 1/3 is automatic. Thus K̄ is uniformly bounded, r* is universal, and the entire construction has dimension-only constants. If the remaining gap is closed, the result would be a uniform negative-pinching gap across all such families, not merely a fixed-topology gap.

The precisely identified remaining gap is the degenerate ε-regularity problem: going from L² energy smallness (R_α ≤ 2ε*) to weighted pointwise curvature bounds (||aRm||_∞ ≤ C/t) under the degenerate operator ∂_t - aΔ. On the plateau (a = 1), this is exactly Li's Theorem 3.4. On the transition (0 < a < 1), a new argument is needed — either a degenerate Moser iteration or an alternative regularity mechanism that handles the χ²-weighted diffusion. The correct proof hierarchy would be: L² energy → degenerate ε-regularity → maximum principle for defect → metric equivalence → Li smoothing on plateau → Corollary 3.5 defect decay → L² calibration → exponential chart → forbidden germ.
