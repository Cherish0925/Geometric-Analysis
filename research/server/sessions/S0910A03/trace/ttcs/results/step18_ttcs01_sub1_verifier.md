# Scientific memo: weighted-defect bootstrap for normalized cutoff Ricci flow

## 1. Main assessment

For
\[
\partial_t g=-2aE,\qquad a=\chi^2,\qquad E=\Ric+g
\]
on a four-manifold, the weighted tensor
\[
U:=aE
\]
has a particularly favorable evolution: all \((\Delta a)E\) and \(\nabla a*\nabla E\) terms generated by commuting \(a\) through the Laplacian cancel exactly. The remaining Hessian-\(a\) terms can be removed from localized \(L^p\) estimates by integration by parts. Consequently, under provisional metric comparability, relative Sobolev inequalities, cutoff-gradient control, and persistent small local \(L^2\) energies for both \(\Rm\) and \(E\), one obtains a fixed exponent \(p>2\) estimate
\[
\sup_{0\le s\le T}\fint_{U_\alpha}|aE(s)|^p\le C_p\delta^p.
\]

This fixed supercritical exponent is available before any pointwise curvature estimate. It is the correct starting layer for a Li/Yang-type time-cylinder iteration.

The subsequent bound
\[
|aE(x,t)|\le C_p\delta\left(\frac{r^2}{t}\right)^{2/p}
\]
follows conditionally if one also has the transition-inclusive curvature-potential estimate
\[
\int_{\widetilde U_\alpha}a|\Rm(t)|^3
 \le \frac{C}{A_\alpha^4\,t},
\tag{1}
\]
or, more strongly,
\[
a|\Rm|\le \frac Ct
\quad\text{together with persistent local critical }L^2\text{ curvature energy}.
\tag{2}
\]
The missing analytic object is therefore not the fixed-\(p\) defect estimate. It is the derivation of (1), or of (2), from the degenerate curvature equation on the transition region while simultaneously maintaining the geometric inputs used in the derivation.

A \(C/t\) weighted curvature estimate cannot be inserted directly into the maximum principle for the unweighted \(E\): the integral \(\int_0^t ds/s\) diverges.

## 2. Exact weighted tensor equation

Write
\[
\tau=\tr_gE,\qquad a_i=\nabla_i a,\qquad a_{ij}=\nabla_i\nabla_j a.
\]
Using the contracted Bianchi identity
\[
\nabla^iE_{ij}=\frac12\nabla_j\tau,
\]
the defect equation is
\[
\partial_tE_{ij}
=a(\Delta_LE_{ij}-2E_{ij})+\mathcal C_{ij},
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_{ij}={}&(\Delta a)E_{ij}
+2a^k\nabla_kE_{ij}
+\tau a_{ij}
+\frac12(a_i\nabla_j\tau+a_j\nabla_i\tau)\\
&-a_i{}^kE_{kj}-a_j{}^kE_{ki}
-a^k(\nabla_iE_{kj}+\nabla_jE_{ki}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Set
\[
U=aE,\qquad \sigma=\tr_gU=a\tau.
\]
Because
\[
a\Delta(aE)
=a^2\Delta E+a(\Delta a)E+2a\nabla^ka\,\nabla_kE,
\]
the first two cutoff terms in (3) cancel the commutator from \(a^2\Delta E\). One obtains
\[
\partial_tU=a(\Delta_LU-2U)+\mathcal B_a(U),
\tag{4}
\]
with
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\mathcal B_a(U))_{ij}={}&
\sigma a_{ij}-a_i{}^kU_{kj}-a_j{}^kU_{ki}\\
&+\frac12(a_i\nabla_j\sigma+a_j\nabla_i\sigma)
-a^k(\nabla_iU_{kj}+\nabla_jU_{ki})\\
&-\sigma\frac{a_i a_j}{a}
+\frac{a^k}{a}(a_iU_{kj}+a_jU_{ki}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
For \(a=\chi^2\), the quotients in the last line extend smoothly:
\[
\frac{da\otimes da}{a}=4\,d\chi\otimes d\chi,\qquad
\frac{|\nabla a|^2}{a}=4|\nabla\chi|^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus no \(1/\chi\) singularity occurs.

## 3. Exact squared-norm evolution

Since \(\partial_tg=-2U\),
\[
\partial_tg^{ij}=2U^{ij}.
\]
The inverse-metric variation contributes \(4\tr(U^3)\) to \(\partial_t|U|^2\). Meanwhile,
\[
2a\langle U,\Delta_LU\rangle
=a\Delta|U|^2-2a|\nabla U|^2
+4a\Rm(U,U)-4a\Ric(U,U).
\]
Using
\[
\Ric=E-g=\frac Ua-g,
\]
the last term is
\[
-4a\Ric(U,U)=-4\tr(U^3)+4a|U|^2.
\]
This cancels both the inverse-metric cubic and the damping contribution
\(-4a|U|^2\). Hence
\[
(\partial_t-a\Delta)|U|^2
=-2a|\nabla U|^2+4a\Rm(U,U)
+2\langle U,\mathcal B_a(U)\rangle.
\tag{7}
\]
There is no intrinsic \(U^3\) reaction in (7). The only extra cubic term in an integrated \(L^p\) calculation comes from the evolving volume form:
\[
\partial_t d\mu_g=-\sigma\,d\mu_g.
\tag{8}
\]

## 4. Localized tensor \(L^p\) inequality

Let \(w=|U|\), \(p\ge2\), and let \(\eta\) be a fixed spatial localization function. Regularization by \(w_\varepsilon=(|U|^2+\varepsilon)^{1/2}\) justifies the calculation at zeros of \(U\).

Define
\[
I_p=\int\eta^2w^p\,d\mu,\qquad
D_p=\int\eta^2a\,\big|\nabla w^{p/2}\big|^2\,d\mu.
\]

### 4.1 Treatment of every cutoff commutator

The cutoff terms in (5) have three types.

1. **First derivatives of \(U\):**
   \[
   da*U*\nabla U.
   \]
   After testing by \(\eta^2w^{p-2}U\), Young’s inequality gives
   \[
   C_p\eta^2|da|w^{p-1}|\nabla U|
   \le \varepsilon\eta^2a w^{p-2}|\nabla U|^2
   +C_{p,\varepsilon}\eta^2\frac{|da|^2}{a}w^p.
   \]

2. **Hessian terms:**
   \[
   \sigma\,\Hess a,\qquad \Hess a*U.
   \]
   Integrating \(\Hess a\) once by parts moves the derivative onto
   \(\eta^2w^{p-2}U*U\). The resulting terms are
   \[
   da*U*\nabla U,\qquad da*d\eta*w^2,
   \]
   and are bounded by the same dissipation and
   \[
   \left(\eta^2\frac{|da|^2}{a}+a|d\eta|^2\right)w^p.
   \]
   Thus no pointwise \(|\Hess a|\) estimate is required for this integrated defect inequality.

3. **Quadratic gradient quotients:**
   \[
   \frac{da\otimes da}{a}*U.
   \]
   These contribute directly
   \[
   C_p\frac{|da|^2}{a}w^p
   =4C_p|\nabla\chi|^2w^p.
   \]

The derivative of the coefficient \(a\) in the principal term \(a\Delta\) produces another \(da*U*\nabla U\) term and is handled identically.

Using (7), (8), Kato’s inequality, and \(|\sigma|\le2w\) in dimension four gives
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}I_p+c_pD_p
\le{}&
C_p\int\left(a|d\eta|^2+\eta^2\frac{|da|^2}{a}\right)w^p\\
&+C_p\int\eta^2a|\Rm|\,w^p
+C_p\int\eta^2w^{p+1}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{9}
\]
Equation (9) accounts for \(a\), \(da\), and \(\Hess a\) completely at the integrated tensor level.

## 5. Fixed \(p>2\) from critical \(L^2\) energies

Use the norm-form relative Sobolev inequality on an enlarged patch:
\[
\|\varphi\|_{L^4}
\le A_\alpha\left(\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^2}
+r^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{L^2}\right),
\qquad
A_\alpha\le C r\,V_\alpha^{-1/4}.
\tag{10}
\]
Set \(z=w^{p/2}\). Then
\[
\int\eta^2a|\Rm|w^p
=\int|\Rm|\,(\chi\eta z)^2.
\]
Also, since \(w=a|E|\),
\[
\int\eta^2w^{p+1}
=\int |E|\,(\chi\eta z)^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int\eta^2a|\Rm|w^p+\int\eta^2w^{p+1}\\
&\qquad\le
\big(\|\Rm\|_{L^2}+\|E\|_{L^2}\big)\|\chi\eta z\|_{L^4}^2\\
&\qquad\le
C A_\alpha^2\big(\|\Rm\|_{L^2}+\|E\|_{L^2}\big)
\left(D_p+r^{-2}\sum_{\beta\sim\alpha}I_{p,\beta}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

The collapse factors cancel:
\[
A_\alpha^2\|\Rm\|_{L^2}
\le C\left(\frac{r^4}{V_\alpha}
\int_{\widetilde U_\alpha}|\Rm|^2\right)^{1/2},
\tag{12}
\]
and similarly for \(E\). Thus, if the provisional local energies satisfy
\[
A_\alpha^2\big(\|\Rm\|_2+\|E\|_2\big)\le\varepsilon_p,
\tag{13}
\]
the dissipation term in (11) is absorbed in (9). With
\[
\frac{|da|^2}{a}=4|\nabla\chi|^2\le Cr^{-2},
\qquad |d\eta|\le Cr^{-1},
\]
one obtains
\[
\frac{d}{dt}I_{p,\alpha}+c_pD_{p,\alpha}
\le Cr^{-2}\sum_{\beta\sim\alpha}I_{p,\beta}.
\tag{14}
\]
Finite overlap, neighboring-volume comparability, and Gronwall then give
\[
\sup_{0\le s\le\theta r^2}
\fint_{U_\alpha}|aE(s)|^p
\le C_p\delta^p,
\tag{15}
\]
because \(|aE(0)|\le|E(0)|\le\delta\).

Hence one fixed \(p>2\) is obtainable from the provisional \(L^2\) layer. A prescribed \(p\) requires the curvature energy in (13) to be small by an amount depending on \(p\); taking \(p\) close to \(2\), or simply fixing \(p=3\), is sufficient for integrable metric drift.

The same absorption cannot be iterated directly to exponents \(m\to\infty\): after testing the scalar inequality by \(w^{m-1}\), the curvature reaction carries a factor proportional to \(m\), whereas the critical absorption coefficient is \(A_\alpha^2\|\Rm\|_2\). Direct absorption would require increasingly smaller curvature energy as \(m\) grows.

## 6. Li-type iteration after the fixed exponent

For high powers, the curvature term should be treated as a time-dependent potential rather than absorbed by its \(L^2\) norm.

Let \(z=\eta w^{m/2}\) and
\[
K_{3,\alpha}(t)=\int_{\widetilde U_\alpha}a|\Rm|^3.
\]
A weighted interpolation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int a|\Rm|z^2
&\le K_{3,\alpha}^{1/3}
     \left(\int a|z|^3\right)^{2/3}\\
&\le K_{3,\alpha}^{1/3}
     \|\chi z\|_4^{4/3}\|z\|_2^{2/3}\\
&\le \varepsilon\|\nabla(\chi z)\|_2^2
 +C_\varepsilon A_\alpha^4K_{3,\alpha}\|z\|_2^2
 +Cr^{-2}\|z\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
The evolving-volume term causes no new potential, since
\[
w=a|\Ric+g|\le C\,a|\Rm|+Ca.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus \(w^{m+1}\) is bounded by the same curvature-potential term plus a zeroth-order contribution.

If
\[
a|\Rm|\le \frac Ct
\]
and the local \(L^2\) curvature energy persists, then
\[
K_{3,\alpha}(t)
=\int(a|\Rm|)|\Rm|^2
\le \frac Ct\int|\Rm|^2,
\]
so
\[
A_\alpha^4K_{3,\alpha}(t)
\le \frac{C}{t}
\frac{r^4}{V_\alpha}\int|\Rm|^2
\le \frac{C\varepsilon_*}{t}.
\tag{18}
\]
On a time cylinder \([t/2,t]\), this is bounded by \(C/t\). The usual parabolic exponent sequence \(m\mapsto 3m/2\), combined with (15), then gives
\[
|U(x,t)|
\le C_p A_\alpha^{4/p}
\left(t^{-1}+r^{-2}\right)^{2/p}
\|E(0)\|_{L^p(\widetilde U_\alpha)}.
\tag{19}
\]
Since
\[
A_\alpha\le CrV_\alpha^{-1/4},
\qquad
\|E(0)\|_{L^p}\le\delta\,V_\alpha^{1/p},
\]
the volume factors cancel:
\[
\boxed{
|aE(x,t)|
\le C_p\delta\left(\frac{r^2}{t}\right)^{2/p},
\qquad 0<t\le\theta r^2,\quad p>2.}
\tag{20}
\]

This is consistent with Wang’s Theorem 6.3. Wang assumes a global Sobolev inequality, a global full-support critical curvature norm, and a global \(L^p\) Ricci input. Li’s Corollary 3.5 likewise relies on the full-support curvature propagation from Theorem 3.4. Neither supplied source directly contains the finite-neighbor relative-Sobolev transition theorem represented by (14)–(20).

## 7. Why \(C/t\) curvature does not preserve unweighted \(E\)

The maximum-principle calculation for \(E\) gives schematically
\[
D^+\|E(t)\|_\infty^2
\le C\left(
\|a\Rm(t)\|_\infty
+\|\Hess a\|_\infty
+\frac{|\nabla a|^2}{a}
\right)\|E(t)\|_\infty^2.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(\|a\Rm(t)\|_\infty\le C/t\), integration from \(\varepsilon\) to \(t\) yields
\[
\|E(t)\|_\infty^2
\le \|E(\varepsilon)\|_\infty^2
e^{Cr^{-2}t}\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\right)^C.
\tag{22}
\]
There is no uniform passage as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Even if the initial smooth metric gives a finite curvature value \(K_0\), an estimate of the form
\[
\|a\Rm(t)\|_\infty\le \min\{K_0,C/t\}
\]
produces a factor comparable to \(\log(1+K_0t)\), which is not uniform when \(K_0\) varies through the sequence.

Thus the asserted “automatic defect preservation” from \(C/t\) curvature is mathematically unsupported. The weighted estimate (20) is the appropriate replacement because its time exponent is integrable:
\[
\int_0^{\theta r^2}
\delta\left(\frac{r^2}{t}\right)^{2/p}dt
=
\frac{p}{p-2}\,
\delta\,\theta^{1-2/p}r^2.
\tag{23}
\]
Since \(\partial_tg=-2U\), (23) gives metric comparability directly without controlling unweighted \(E\) pointwise on the transition.

## 8. Exact unresolved analytic content

The defect calculation itself leaves no unmanaged \(a\), \(da\), or \(\Hess a\) term at the localized \(L^p\) level. The unresolved components are elsewhere:

1. **Degenerate curvature regularization.**  
   A derivation of
   \[
   a|\Rm|\le C/t
   \quad\text{or}\quad
   \int a|\Rm|^3\le C A^{-4}t^{-1}
   \]
   from the local critical \(L^2\) curvature energies for
   \(\partial_t-a\Delta\) on the entire transition region is absent.

2. **Conditional \(L^2\) persistence.**  
   Equations such as the coupled \(R_\alpha,P_\alpha\) system require metric comparability, evolving relative Sobolev inequalities, stable localization functions, neighboring-volume comparability, and control of all curvature–defect cross terms. The displayed critical absorption verifies an important algebraic component, but the supplied material does not independently establish this package to a uniform time.

3. **Connection and cutoff Hessian.**  
   The defect \(L^p\) calculation can integrate \(\Hess a\) away. The curvature equation and smooth extension theory still involve
   \[
   \Hess a*E,\qquad da*\nabla E,
   \]
   and
   \[
   \partial_t(\Hess a)=(\partial_t\Gamma)*da
   \sim \nabla U*da.
   \]
   The pointwise estimate (20) controls \(U\), not \(\nabla U\). Wang’s Lemma 4.11 explicitly reflects this coupling between derivatives of \(\chi\) and derivatives of weighted curvature.

4. **Finite-neighbor Moser theorem.**  
   The passage from (14), (16), and (18) to (19) is a valid conditional parabolic calculation, but its transition-inclusive finite-cover form is not supplied by Li’s theorem as quoted. It requires a full written recurrence with evolving measures and neighboring patches.

5. **Unweighted global defect energy.**  
   The weighted estimate controls metric speed but does not by itself control
   \[
   Q(t)=\int|E(t)|^2
   \]
   near points where \(a\) is small. The later topological recalibration therefore still depends on the provisional coupled \(L^2\) propagation.

In summary, the simultaneous first-exit architecture has a sound defect component: fixed \(p>2\) propagation follows from persistent local critical energies, and a Li-type iteration yields the desired integrable weighted estimate once the weighted curvature potential is available. The transition-region curvature regularization and the joint continuation geometry remain unresolved.
