\hypertarget{actual-harmonic-forms-force-whole-filling-milnor-matching}{%
\section{Actual harmonic forms force whole-filling Milnor
matching}\label{actual-harmonic-forms-force-whole-filling-milnor-matching}}

24 September 2026. This note strengthens the realization criterion in
\href{20260924_orbifold_smoothing_bridge.md}{Orbifold smoothing bridge},
Theorem C. It replaces its assumed relative diffeomorphism of singular
fillings by a condition on the \textbf{actual harmonic representative
and the topology of the whole filling}. It does not assume that a
terminal ALE bubble is the whole filling, or require a single-scale
degeneration.

The new connection in this project is analytic-to-symplectic:
actual-form alignment gives canonical contact boundaries; a zero-free
harmonic form makes the entire filling symplectic; classical filling
theorems then force its relative smooth type. The filling
classifications are not new results of this project. Global Q-Gorenstein
smoothability remains a separate input.

\hypertarget{the-input-and-the-forced-matching-theorem}{%
\subsection{1. The input and the forced-matching
theorem}\label{the-input-and-the-forced-matching-theorem}}

Let \((M_j,g_j,a_j)\) satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem A of the
orbifold smoothing bridge. Explicitly, the \(M_j\) are closed connected
oriented smooth four-manifolds, each \(a_j\) is an actual conformally
solvable Spin\(^c\) class, and, for fixed \(D,A_0>0\), \[
 \operatorname{diam}(M_j,g_j)\le D,\qquad
 p_j=(a_j^+)^2\ge A_0,\qquad
 \delta_j+q_j\longrightarrow0,                         \tag{1}
\] where \[
 \delta_j=\|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}^{\sharp}+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}},
 \quad q_j=\frac{\mathfrak D_2(g_j,a_j)}{V_j},
 \quad V_j=\operatorname{Vol}(M_j,g_j),
\] \[
 \mathfrak D_2(g,a)
 =\frac32\int_M(s^2+12|W^+|^2)\,d\mu-72\pi^2(a^+)^2.
\] The structure determining \(a_j\) is fixed while auxiliary positive
weights vary. No hypothesis on \(\pi_1\) or \(\pi_2\) is imposed.

Pass to the subsequence supplied by that theorem. Its limit is a compact
negative Kähler--Einstein orbifold \((X,h,J,\omega_\infty)\), with
finitely many isolated quotient singularities \(x_\nu\). Choose small
disjoint uniformizing neighborhoods \(U_\nu\), with spherical links
\(Y_\nu\), and retain a collar on each side of \(Y_\nu\) in the regular
locus. The regular-locus embeddings identify the exterior \[
                  K=X\setminus\bigcup_\nu\operatorname{int}U_\nu
\] with a submanifold of \(M_j\). Denote its complementary compact
pieces by \(B_{\nu,j}\); each has the actual boundary marking by
\(Y_\nu\). These are the \textbf{whole singular fillings}. Any smaller
bubbles or necks inside them are included.

Let \(H_j\) be the harmonic self-dual representative of \(a_j^+\), not
the harmonic representative of the whole class \(a_j\), and put \[
                         \Omega_j=-2\pi H_j.           \tag{2}
\]

\textbf{Theorem 1 (harmonic/contact forced matching).} In the setting
(1), suppose that, for all sufficiently large \(j\),

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \(H_j\) has no zero on any \(B_{\nu,j}\);
\item
  for each \(\nu\), either

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    \(x_\nu\) is an \(A_1\) singularity and
    \(b_2(B_{\nu,j};\mathbb Q)=1\), or
  \item
    \(B_{\nu,j}\) is a rational homology four-ball.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

Then every \(B_{\nu,j}\), with its actual boundary marking, is oriented
diffeomorphic relative to that marking to a local Q-Gorenstein Milnor
fiber of \((X,x_\nu)\). In the second alternative the singularity is
\textbf{necessarily a Wahl singularity} \[
               \frac1{n_\nu^2}(1,n_\nu a_\nu-1),
 \qquad n_\nu\ge2,
 \quad 0<a_\nu<n_\nu,
 \quad \gcd(n_\nu,a_\nu)=1.                            \tag{3}
\] In particular, cyclicity and local Q-Gorenstein smoothability need
not be separately assumed in the rational-ball alternative.

The zero-free condition is only qualitative. There is no required
uniform lower bound for \(|H_j|\) on the shrinking pieces. It is a
condition on a specified solution of a linear global elliptic equation,
not a proposed relative diffeomorphism and not a choice of a convenient
ALE model.

The proof occupies Sections 2--5. The analytic hypotheses alone do not
presently prove condition 1. In contrast, nonvanishing and the canonical
contact structure on the \textbf{fixed regular collars} will be
consequences of (1), rather than additional assumptions.

\hypertarget{actual-harmonic-alignment-upgrades-on-every-regular-collar}{%
\subsection{2. Actual harmonic alignment upgrades on every regular
collar}\label{actual-harmonic-alignment-upgrades-on-every-regular-collar}}

\textbf{Lemma 2 (strong convergence of the actual harmonic forms).}
Using the same regular-locus embeddings as for the metrics, \[
             \Omega_j\longrightarrow\omega_\infty
                    \quad\hbox{in }C^{1,\beta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X_{\rm reg})
 \quad\text{for every }\beta<1.                       \tag{4}
\] This needs neither the core zero-free assumption nor a
negative-square budget.

\textbf{Proof.} Set \[
 r_j=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}+g_j|^2,
 \qquad \kappa_j=r_j+q_j\longrightarrow0.
\] The inherited actual-form composition, Theorem A, supplies smooth
self-dual forms \(\omega_j\), with \(\|\omega_j\|_\infty\le2\sqrt3\),
such that, for \(\eta_j\downarrow0\), \[
                \fint|\omega_j-\Omega_j|^2
                          \le49\kappa_j+\eta_j.        \tag{5}
\] It also supplies the amplitude and first-derivative estimates used in
the orbifold compactification proof. Since \(V_j\le V_0(D)\), (5) is
strong unnormalized \(L^2\) alignment. That proof constructs
\(\omega_j\to\omega_\infty\) strongly in local \(L^2\). Thus the same
local \(L^2\) convergence holds for \(\Omega_j\), with uniform local
\(L^2\) bounds.

On nested regular harmonic-coordinate charts, the metrics converge in
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) and strongly in \(W^{2,p}\) for every finite \(p\). The
forms \(\Omega_j\) solve the actual homogeneous Hodge equation \[
                  (d+d_{g_j}^*)\Omega_j=0.             \tag{6}
\] Interior first-order elliptic estimates for (6), initially with
exponent 2, give a uniform local \(W^{1,2}\) bound. Sobolev embedding
gives a local \(L^4\) bound. Iterating the same interior estimate gives
\(W^{1,4}\), hence \(L^p\) for every finite \(p\), and then \(W^{1,p}\)
for these \(p\). Constants are uniform on the chosen charts: their
leading coefficients are uniformly \(C^{1,\alpha}\), their zero-order
coefficients uniformly \(C^{0,\alpha}\), and ellipticity is uniform. All
these are properties of the fixed regular charts.

For completeness the last derivative can be obtained from the
Weitzenböck equation. In coordinates its principal coefficients are
uniformly \(C^{1,\alpha}\), its first-order coefficients are bounded,
and its zero-order coefficients consist of curvature and quadratic first
metric derivatives. They are uniformly \(L^p\) because the metrics are
uniformly \(W^{2,p}\). The already obtained \(W^{1,p}\) and \(L^\infty\)
bounds therefore put its right-hand side in \(L^p\). Interior
second-order elliptic estimates give uniform \(W^{2,p}\) bounds for
\(\Omega_j\). Choosing \(p\) large gives precompactness in
\(C^{1,\beta}\) on a smaller chart. Every limit equals \(\omega_\infty\)
by (5), so (4) follows. No derivative of the Ricci error was taken.
\(\square\)

This upgrade is specific to the harmonic representative. Applying the
same conclusion directly to the auxiliary \(\omega_j\), whose small
derivative is initially only an averaged estimate, would not be
justified.

\hypertarget{the-geometric-collar-supplies-a-strong-convex-filling}{%
\subsection{3. The geometric collar supplies a strong convex
filling}\label{the-geometric-collar-supplies-a-strong-convex-filling}}

\textbf{Lemma 3 (canonical contact boundary).} For each sufficiently
small fixed \(Y_\nu\), the restriction of \(\Omega_j\) to its
pulled-back collar is symplectic for large \(j\). It has a primitive
whose Liouville field points outward from \(B_{\nu,j}\), and the induced
contact structure on its boundary is contactomorphic to the canonical
contact structure of the complex quotient singularity \((X,x_\nu)\). If
\(H_j\) is zero-free throughout \(B_{\nu,j}\), then
\((B_{\nu,j},\Omega_j)\) is a strong symplectic filling with that
contact boundary and the quotient-link orientation.

\textbf{Proof.} A uniformizing chart is a ball in \(\mathbb C^2\) with a
finite subgroup \(\Gamma\subset U(2)\) acting freely away from the
origin. The limiting smooth invariant Kähler metric has an invariant
strictly plurisubharmonic local potential \(\varphi\). After subtracting
an invariant pluriharmonic term, \(\varphi\) has a positive quadratic
leading part. Small regular level sets provide links \(Y\) transverse to
the radial direction. The convention for \(d^c\) is chosen so that
\(\omega_\infty=dd^c\varphi\). The primitive
\(\alpha_\infty=d^c\varphi\) gives a positive contact form on \(Y\) with
an outward-pointing Liouville field. Its contact structure is the
canonical quotient contact structure: interpolation of the strictly
plurisubharmonic potentials on a sufficiently small chart and Gray
stability identify it with the link of the standard quadratic potential.

Fix a compact collar \(C=Y\times[-b,b]\) in the regular locus. Since
\(Y\) is a spherical space form,
\(H^2(C;\mathbb R)=H^2(Y;\mathbb R)=0\). On a slightly smaller collar
closed two-forms admit a continuous linear right inverse to \(d\). One
concrete construction uses a fixed Hodge primitive on a slice \(Y\) and
integration of the normal contraction along the collar coordinate.
Consequently the closed difference \(\Omega_j-\omega_\infty\) has
primitives \(\gamma_j\to0\) in \(C^1\), by Lemma 2. Put \[
                  \alpha_j=\alpha_\infty+\gamma_j,
             \qquad d\alpha_j=\Omega_j.               \tag{7}
\] The contact and outward-transversality conditions are open; (4) and
(7) preserve them for large \(j\). Gray stability identifies the
boundary contact structures. The collar is fixed first and \(j\) tends
to infinity afterwards; no estimate uniform as its radius tends to zero
is asserted.

Finally \(d\Omega_j=0\), and self-duality gives \[
             \Omega_j\wedge\Omega_j=|\Omega_j|^2\,d\mu_{g_j}.
                                                                    \tag{8}
\] If the form has no zero on \(B_{\nu,j}\), (8) makes it symplectic on
the entire filling in its existing orientation. Its strong convex
boundary is supplied by (7). In particular the orientation is the
singularity-link orientation, not its reverse. \(\square\)

\hypertarget{classical-filling-classification-determines-the-whole-manifold}{%
\subsection{4. Classical filling classification determines the whole
manifold}\label{classical-filling-classification-determines-the-whole-manifold}}

\textbf{Lemma 4 (the two filling alternatives).} Let \(B\) be a strong
symplectic filling of the canonical contact link of an isolated complex
quotient surface singularity.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\alph{enumi})}
\item
  If the singularity is \(A_1\) and \(b_2(B;\mathbb Q)=1\), then \(B\)
  is oriented diffeomorphic to the \((-2)\)-disk bundle over \(S^2\),
  hence to the \(A_1\) Milnor fiber.
\item
  If \(B\) is a rational homology ball, the singularity is Wahl and
  \(B\) is oriented diffeomorphic to its Q-Gorenstein Milnor rational
  ball.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Proof.} The link in (a) is canonical contact \(L(2,1)\).
McDuff's filling theorem, stated in the precise form used here in Lisca
{[}L08, Introduction{]}, says that every such filling is an oriented
smooth blowup of the \((-2)\)-disk bundle. Each blowup increases \(b_2\)
by one, so the assumed value excludes every blowup. The quadratic
\(A_1\) Milnor fiber is the cotangent disk bundle of \(S^2\), with this
oriented smooth type.

For (b), Etnyre--Ozbagci--Tosun {[}EOT25, Theorem 1.18{]} applies to a
spherical link \textbf{with the quotient-singularity orientation}. It
forces the oriented link to be a lens space \[
                        L(n^2,na-1),
            \qquad 0<a<n,\quad\gcd(n,a)=1.             \tag{9}
\] The trivial spherical group is not at issue here, since the point is
a singularity. The fundamental group of the link is the quotient group,
so that group is cyclic. Its faithful complex representation is diagonal
and, because it acts freely on the three-sphere, the complex singularity
is \(1/P(1,Q)\). Oriented lens-space classification gives \(P=n^2\) and
\[
 Q\equiv na-1\quad\hbox{or}\quad (na-1)^{-1}\pmod{n^2}.
\] The inverse is \(n(n-a)-1\) modulo \(n^2\); either representation is
a Wahl singularity, differing at most by interchanging its coordinates.

For the standard contact structure on (9), the symplectic rational-ball
filling is unique up to symplectic deformation, in particular up to
oriented diffeomorphism: {[}GS22, Proposition A.2{]}, derived from
Lisca's classification. Its known representative is the Wahl Milnor
rational ball. The latter is a Stein filling and the \(d=1\) case of the
Q-Gorenstein deformation model in {[}S11, Proposition 2.3{]}. This
identifies the entire \(B\), not one selected ALE subdomain. \(\square\)

\hypertarget{arbitrary-actual-boundary-markings-extend}{%
\subsection{5. Arbitrary actual boundary markings
extend}\label{arbitrary-actual-boundary-markings-extend}}

Classification up to unmarked diffeomorphism alone would leave the old
gluing issue unresolved. In the preceding two alternatives that issue
can be settled explicitly.

\textbf{Lemma 5 (extension of the boundary correction).} Every
orientation-preserving self-diffeomorphism of the boundary of an \(A_1\)
Milnor fiber or a Wahl Milnor rational ball extends to an
orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of that filling.

\textbf{Proof.} For \(A_1\), the boundary is \(\mathbb{RP}^3\). Bonahon
{[}B83, Theorem 3(e){]} implies that every orientation-preserving
self-diffeomorphism is isotopic to the identity: the other component
reverses orientation. Extend such an isotopy through a collar, taking
the identity farther inside.

For a Wahl point write \(P=n^2\), \(Q=na-1\). If \(n>2\), \[
 Q^2\equiv1-2na\pmod{n^2},\qquad Q^2\not\equiv\pm1\pmod{n^2}.
                                                                    \tag{10}
\] Indeed the plus case would imply \(n\mid2a\), hence \(n\mid2\), and
the minus case already implies \(n\mid2\) after reducing modulo \(n\).
Bonahon's Theorem 3(a) gives two mapping classes, represented by the
identity and simultaneous complex conjugation. For \(n=2\), \(a=1\),
\((P,Q)=(4,1)\), and Theorem 3(c) gives exactly the same conclusion.
Both maps preserve three-dimensional orientation.

Use the standard real-parameter Wahl Milnor model \[
 F_{n,a,t}
  =\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb C^3:xy=z^n+t\}/\mu_n,
                 \qquad t>0,                           \tag{11}
\] cut off by a sufficiently small invariant Milnor ball. The action is
\[
       \xi\cdot(x,y,z)=(\xi x,\xi^{-1}y,\xi^a z),
                  \qquad\xi^n=1.                       \tag{12}
\] It is free on the smooth fiber. Complex conjugation preserves (11),
normalizes (12) by \(\xi\mapsto\xi^{-1}\), and preserves the cutoff. It
thus extends over the quotient fiber. It preserves the real orientation
because the fiber has complex dimension two. The usual equivariant
Milnor collar identification, along real parameters, identifies its
boundary action with conjugation on the quotient link. Such a collar
identification can be made equivariant by averaging its horizontal
vector field over conjugation before integrating along the real
parameter. In particular it acts by \(-1\) on
\(H_1(L(n^2,na-1);\mathbb Z)\), and represents the nontrivial class in
Bonahon's list. Every boundary diffeomorphism is isotopic to one of
these two extendible maps; its remaining isotopy extends through a
collar. \(\square\)

\textbf{Completion of Theorem 1.} Lemmas 2 and 3 give canonical strong
symplectic fillings. Lemma 4 supplies oriented diffeomorphisms from
those whole fillings to the appropriate Milnor fibers. Compare the
boundary map of any such diffeomorphism with the actual exterior marking
and the Milnor marking. Their difference is an orientation-preserving
self-diffeomorphism of the link. Extend that correction by Lemma 5 and
compose. Collar adjustment makes the maps agree on neighborhoods of the
boundaries, so they glue smoothly to the given exterior identification.
This is exactly the relative matching required in the previous Theorem
C. \(\square\)

\hypertarget{a-realization-theorem-and-a-zero-set-obstruction}{%
\subsection{6. A realization theorem and a zero-set
obstruction}\label{a-realization-theorem-and-a-zero-set-obstruction}}

\textbf{Corollary 6 (negative KE realization with forced local
matching).} Assume (1) and the two hypotheses of Theorem 1. If the
limiting surface \(X\) has a projective Q-Gorenstein smoothing, then
every sufficiently large \(M_j\) in this subsequence admits a negative
Kähler--Einstein metric, on its original smooth structure.

A separately checkable sufficient condition for this global smoothing is
\[
                 H^2(Z,T_Z(-\log E))=0,                \tag{13}
\] where \(Z\to X\) is a resolution with reduced exceptional divisor
\(E\), as in Lee--Park's global smoothing criterion {[}LP07, Theorem
2.1{]}. The local class-T condition in that criterion holds by Theorem
1.

\textbf{Proof.} Fix a small regular parameter in a global smoothing. Its
exterior is diffeomorphic to \(K\) by Ehresmann trivialization, and the
local fibers are the \(A_1\) or Wahl fibers in Theorem 1. The boundary
extension argument makes its gluing match the actual gluing in \(M_j\).
Thus \(M_j\) is oriented diffeomorphic to the smooth algebraic fiber.
The negative KE orbifold metric makes \(K_X\) ample. A Cartier multiple
of the relative canonical class remains ample on nearby smooth fibers;
the Aubin--Yau theorem supplies their negative KE metrics. Pullback
gives the metric on \(M_j\). This is Theorem C of the preceding note
with its local matching premise now proved, rather than assumed.
\(\square\)

\textbf{Corollary 7 (topology forces a zero of the actual harmonic
form).} Under (1), if a whole filling \(B_{\nu,j}\) is a rational
homology ball but the limiting singularity is not Wahl, then, for large
\(j\), \(H_j\) has a zero in the interior of that filling. Its boundary
collar is nevertheless zero-free.

\textbf{Proof.} Zero-freeness would give a strong symplectic
rational-ball filling by Lemma 3, contradicting Lemma 4(b). Lemma 2
excludes zeros on the fixed collar. \(\square\)

This is a genuine restriction on how the actual class can pass through
singular topology. It is not an Einstein nonexistence theorem: a zero
inside a shrinking core can be consistent with all the averaged
estimates.

\hypertarget{nonempty-local-models-and-the-precise-gain}{%
\subsection{7. Nonempty local models and the precise
gain}\label{nonempty-local-models-and-the-precise-gain}}

The additional geometric condition occurs in actual smooth metrics, not
just formal cohomology. On an \(A_1\) Milnor fiber the Eguchi--Hanson
Kähler form is closed, self-dual and nowhere zero. It supplies the first
filling alternative. On each Wahl Milnor fiber, the Ricci-flat Kähler
metrics supplied by Şuvaina {[}S11, Theorem A{]} have the same
properties and supply the rational-ball alternative. Their Kähler forms
have canonical convex boundaries on sufficiently large truncated ends.
These are classical local calibration examples, not new global Yau26
constructions.

For a global negative KE input with its compatible canonical Spin\(^c\)
class, \(\Omega=-2\pi H\) is exactly the Kähler form and has no zeros;
the two defects in (1) vanish. Accordingly, whenever a classical
negative KE degeneration has \(A_1\) Milnor neighborhoods or Wahl Milnor
rational balls, this criterion passes its exact-geometry test. We do not
infer a bounded-diameter degenerating sequence merely from the existence
of a local ALE model.

The topological restrictions in Theorem 1 cannot simply be dropped from
the \textbf{symplectic classification step}. Canonical contact
\(L(4,1)\) has both the \((-4)\)-disk-bundle filling and the Wahl
rational-ball filling, as recorded by McDuff/Lisca {[}L08,
Introduction{]}. Both are symplectic but have different second Betti
numbers. Contact geometry alone therefore does not select the Milnor
rational ball. This is not being offered as a counterexample satisfying
the global near-Einstein hypotheses (1).

The present result goes beyond classifying a terminal bubble in three
specific ways: it controls the whole actual filling, derives the
relevant contact boundary from the actual gauge-theoretic form, and
removes every boundary-marking ambiguity in the two stated cases. Within
these topological alternatives, it leaves two additional questions:
whether the actual harmonic form is zero-free in the core, and whether
the local smoothings globalize. Neither is replaced by the assertion
that an ALE limit exists.
