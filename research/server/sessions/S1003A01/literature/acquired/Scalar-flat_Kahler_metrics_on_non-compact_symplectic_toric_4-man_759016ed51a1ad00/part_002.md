[Part 2/2 of: Scalar-flat Kahler metrics on non-compact symplectic toric 4-manifolds]

As in Theorem 4.1 with $\nu=0$, we write
$$\begin{aligned}
2 \xi_{1} & =\log \left(H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}\right)+(p-1) \log \left(H+a+\sqrt{(H+a)^{2}+r^{2}}\right) \\
2 \xi_{2} & =\log \left(-H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}\right)-\log \left(H+a+\sqrt{(H+a)^{2}+r^{2}}\right)
\end{aligned}$$
(where we assume that $a_{1}=0$ and set $a_{2}=a$ ). Since
$$\begin{gathered}
d x_{1}=\epsilon_{1}=r\left(\frac{\partial \xi_{2}}{\partial r} d H-\frac{\partial \xi_{2}}{\partial H} d r\right) \\
d x_{2}=\epsilon_{2}=-r\left(\frac{\partial \xi_{1}}{\partial r} d H-\frac{\partial \xi_{1}}{\partial H} d r\right)
\end{gathered}$$
a simple calculation shows that
$$\begin{aligned}
& 2 x_{1}=H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}-(H+a)+\sqrt{(H+a)^{2}+r^{2}} \\
& 2 x_{2}=-H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}+(p-1)\left(-(H+a)+\sqrt{(H+a)^{2}+r^{2}}\right) .
\end{aligned}$$
It follows from these formulas that
$$\begin{aligned}
H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}} & =\frac{2 x_{1}\left(p x_{1}-x_{2}+a\right)}{p x_{1}+a} \\
-H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}} & =\frac{2 x_{2}\left(x_{1}+a\right)}{p x_{1}+a} \\
(H+a)+\sqrt{(H+a)^{2}+r^{2}} & =\frac{2\left(x_{1}+a\right)\left(p x_{1}-x_{2}+a\right)}{p x_{1}+a} .
\end{aligned}$$
Since $d \mathbf{u}=\xi_{1} d x_{1}+\xi_{2} d x_{2}$, we find that
$$\begin{aligned}
2 \mathbf{u}_{x_{1}} & =\log \left(x_{1}\right)+(p-1) \log \left(x_{1}+a\right)+p \log \left(p x_{1}-x_{2}+a\right)-p \log \left(p x_{1}+a\right)+p \log 2 \\
2 \mathbf{u}_{x_{2}} & =\log \left(x_{2}\right)-\log \left(p x_{1}-x_{2}+a\right) .
\end{aligned}$$
Hence, $2 \mathbf{u}$ is given by the standard potential associated to the moment polygon
$$x_{1} \log \left(x_{1}\right)+x_{2} \log \left(x_{2}\right)+\left(p x_{1}-x_{2}+a\right) \log \left(p x_{1}-x_{2}+a\right)$$
plus
$$(p-1)\left(x_{1}+a\right) \log \left(x_{1}+a\right)-\left(p x_{1}+a\right) \log \left(p x_{1}+a\right)-p x_{1}+p x_{1} \log 2 .$$

Note that in order to compare with the formula in [A4] we need to use a coordinate change
$$\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{1}} & =p x_{1}-x_{2}+1 \\ \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{2}} & =x_{2}\end{cases}$$
and $a=1 / 2$.

7.2. Gravitational instantons. Consider now the case where $\Gamma=\Gamma_{p}$ is the finite subgroup of $S U(2)$, of order $p \in \mathbb{N}$, generated by
$$\left(\begin{array}{cc}
e^{\frac{2 i \pi}{p}} & 0 \\
0 & e^{\frac{2 i \pi(p-1)}{p}}
\end{array}\right) .$$
Let $X$ be the minimal toric resolution of $\mathbb{C}^{2} / \Gamma$. Since $c_{1}(X)=0$, it follows from Lemma 6.3 that its moment polygon is $S L(2, \mathbb{Z})$ equivalent to one with normals $\nu_{1}=(0,1), \nu_{2}=(1,0), \ldots, \nu_{p+1}=(p,-(p-1))$ (see Figure 3).

Assume again without loss of generality that $a_{1}=0$. By applying Theorem 4.1 with $\nu=0$ to this case we see that:
$$\begin{aligned}
2 \xi_{1} & =\log \left(H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}\right)+\sum_{i=2}^{p} \log \left(H+a_{i}+\sqrt{\left(H+a_{i}\right)^{2}+r^{2}}\right) \\
2 \xi_{2} & =\log \left(-H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}\right)-\sum_{i=2}^{p} \log \left(H+a_{i}+\sqrt{\left(H+a_{i}\right)^{2}+r^{2}}\right) .
\end{aligned}$$
Again a simple calculation shows that
$$\begin{aligned}
2 x_{1} & =H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}+\sum_{i=2}^{p} \sqrt{\left(H+a_{i}\right)^{2}+r^{2}}-\left(H+a_{i}\right) \\
2 x_{2} & =-H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}+\sum_{i=2}^{p} \sqrt{\left(H+a_{i}\right)^{2}+r^{2}}-\left(H+a_{i}\right) .
\end{aligned}$$
Set $2 u=H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}$ and $2 v=-H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}$ so that $H=u-v=x_{1}-x_{2}$ and $r^{2}=4 u v$. We have that $d \mathbf{u}=\xi_{1} d x_{1}+\xi_{2} d x_{2}$ and therefore $2 d \mathbf{u}$ is given by
$$\log (2 u) d x_{1}+\log (2 v) d x_{2}+\sum \log \left(u-v+a_{i}+\sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}\right)\left(d x_{1}-d x_{2}\right) .$$
As in [D2] we set $\mathbf{v}=x_{1} \log (2 u)+x_{2} \log (2 v)$ so that $2 d \mathbf{u}-d \mathbf{v}$ is equal to
$$\frac{-x_{1} d u}{u}+\frac{-x_{2} d v}{v}+\sum \log \left(u-v+a_{i}+\sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}\right)(d u-d v) .$$

But
$$\begin{aligned}
& \frac{x_{1}}{u}=1+\sum \frac{-\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)+\sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}}{2 u} \\
& \frac{x_{2}}{v}=1+\sum \frac{-\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)+\sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}}{2 v},
\end{aligned}$$
therefore $2 d \mathbf{u}-d \mathbf{v}+d(u+v)$ is equal to
$$-\frac{1}{2} \sum\left(\sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}-\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)\right)\left(\frac{d u}{u}+\frac{d v}{v}\right)$$
plus
$$\sum \log \left(\sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}+u-v+a_{i}\right)(d u-d v) .$$
Set
$$A_{i}=\sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}+u-v+a_{i},$$
and
$$B_{i}=\sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}-\left(u-v+a_{i}\right) .$$
To find $\mathbf{u}$ we need to find a primitive of
$$\log \left(A_{i}\right)(d u-d v)-\frac{B_{i}}{2}\left(\frac{d u}{u}+\frac{d v}{v}\right) .$$
Note that
$$d u-d v=\frac{1}{2}\left(d A_{i}-d B_{i}\right)$$
and that
$$d \log \left(A_{i} B_{i}\right)=\frac{d u}{u}+\frac{d v}{v} .$$
Hence we need to find a primitive of
$$\frac{1}{2}\left(\log \left(A_{i}\right)\left(d A_{i}-d B_{i}\right)-B_{i} d \log \left(A_{i} B_{i}\right)\right)$$
which is
$$\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(A_{i}-B_{i}\right) \log A_{i}-\left(A_{i}+B_{i}\right)\right) .$$
Hence $2 \mathbf{u}$ is simply
$$\begin{aligned}
& x_{1} \log (2 u)+x_{2} \log (2 v)-(u+v) \\
+ & \sum\left(u-v+a_{i}\right) \log \left(\sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}+u-v+a_{i}\right) \\
- & \sum \sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}
\end{aligned}$$
where $u$ and $v$ are algebraic functions of $x_{1}$ and $x_{2}$. The case $p=2$ corresponds to $X=\mathcal{O}(-2)$ in the previous subsection. The case $p=3$ can be written more explicitly by finding the roots of a degree 4 polynomial. In fact, for any $p \in \mathbb{N}, u$ and $v$ can be obtained by solving
$$\begin{aligned}
2 x_{1} & =2 u+\sum_{i=2}^{p} \sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}-\left(u-v+a_{i}\right) \\
2 x_{2} & =2 v+\sum_{i=2}^{p} \sqrt{\left(u-v+a_{i}\right)^{2}+4 u v}-\left(u-v+a_{i}\right) .
\end{aligned}$$
7.3. Generalized Taub-NUT metrics on $\mathcal{O}(-2)$. As we have seen, the total space of $\mathcal{O}(-2)$ is a non-compact toric manifold whose moment polygon has 3 edges with normals $\nu_{1}=(0,1), \nu_{2}=(1,0)$ and $\nu_{3}=(2,-1)$ (see Figure 2). As in Theorem 4.1 set
$$\begin{aligned}
2 \xi_{1} & =\log \left(H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}\right)+\log \left(H+a+\sqrt{(H+a)^{2}+r^{2}}\right)+\alpha H \\
2 \xi_{2} & =\log \left(-H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}\right)-\log \left(H+a+\sqrt{(H+a)^{2}+r^{2}}\right)+\beta H
\end{aligned}$$
where $\nu=(\alpha, \beta)$ is such that
$$\operatorname{det}\left(\nu, \nu_{1}\right)=\alpha>0 \quad \text { and } \quad \operatorname{det}\left(\nu, \nu_{d}\right)=-\alpha-2 \beta>0 .$$
We get
$$\begin{aligned}
2 x_{1} & =H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}-(H+a)+\sqrt{(H+a)^{2}+r^{2}}-\frac{\beta r^{2}}{2} \\
2 x_{2} & =-H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}-(H+a)+\sqrt{(H+a)^{2}+r^{2}}+\frac{\alpha r^{2}}{2} .
\end{aligned}$$
Again it is useful to set $2 u=H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}$ and $2 v=-H+\sqrt{H^{2}+r^{2}}, \mathbf{v}=$ $x_{1} \log (2 u)+x_{2} \log (2 v)$ and
$$A=\sqrt{(u-v+a)^{2}+4 u v}+u-v+a,$$
and
$$B=\sqrt{(u-v+a)^{2}+4 u v}-(u-v+a) .$$
The 1 -form $2 d \mathbf{u}-d \mathbf{v}$ is equal to the sum of
$$\alpha u d u-\beta v d v+d B$$
and
$$\log (A)(d u-d v)-\frac{B}{2}\left(\frac{d u}{u}+\frac{d v}{v}\right),$$
and
$$-(\alpha+\beta)\left(\log (A)(u d v+v d u)-\frac{u-v}{2} d B\right)$$
We have seen that a primitive of the second term above is
$$\frac{1}{2}((A-B) \log A-(A+B)) .$$
To find a primitive for the third term, we note that this term is equal to
$$\log (A) \frac{d(A B)}{4}-\frac{A-B}{4} d B+\frac{a}{2} d B .$$
Therefore a primitive for the third term is
$$u v(\log (A)-1)+\frac{B^{2}}{8}(1+2 a) .$$

We conclude that $2 \mathbf{u}$ is given by
$$\begin{aligned}
& x_{1} \log (2 u)+x_{2} \log (2 v)+\frac{\alpha u^{2}-\beta v^{2}}{2}+v-u-a+\sqrt{(u-v+a)^{2}+4 u v} \\
+ & (u-v+a) \log \left(\sqrt{(u-v+a)^{2}+4 u v}+u-v+a\right)-\sqrt{(u-v+a)^{2}+4 u v} \\
- & (\alpha+\beta) u v\left(\log \left(\sqrt{(u-v+a)^{2}+4 u v}+u-v+a\right)-1\right) \\
- & \frac{(1+2 a)(\alpha+\beta)}{8}\left(\sqrt{(u-v+a)^{2}+4 u v}-(u-v+a)\right)^{2},
\end{aligned}$$
where $v$ is a zero of a degree 4 polynomial with coefficients which are degree 1 polynomials in $x_{1}$ and $x_{2}$, and
$$u=\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}+v}{1-(\alpha+\beta) v} .$$
The formulas for $u$ and $v$ come from solving the equations
$$\begin{array}{r}
2 x_{1}=\sqrt{(u-v+a)^{2}+4 u v}+u+v-a-2 \beta u v \\
2 x_{2}=\sqrt{(u-v+a)^{2}+4 u v}-u+3 v-a+2 \alpha u v .
\end{array}$$

Using Lemma 6.2 with $\eta=(1,1)$, we see that when $\alpha+\beta=0$ we get "multi Taub-NUT" Ricci-flat toric Kähler metrics on $\mathcal{O}(-2)$. By the classification result of Bielawski [B], these are isometric to the ones constructed by Hawking [Ha] and studied by LeBrun [L3].

When $\alpha+\beta \neq 0$ we get new complete scalar-flat toric Kähler metrics on $\mathcal{O}(-2)$. These are not Ricci-flat, but are asymptotic to a generalized Taub-NUT metric.
