## Deep-Reasoning

Step 64 restructured the negative branch of Yau Problem 26 around a precise distinction between equality-rigidity obstructions and stable-inequality obstructions. The central finding is that Di Cerbo's exclusion of Einstein metrics on extended graph 4-manifolds is exactly saturated at every step: the Connell–Suárez–Serrato minimal-Ricci-volume computation, the Gauss–Bonnet identity, LeBrun's Lemma 7 inequality, and the Preissmann contradiction all sit at their boundaries. Under approximate pinching ‖Ric+3g‖∞ ≤ δ, the Bishop–Gromov entropy bound degrades from h ≤ 3 to h ≤ 3+δ, so the CSS inequality yields only Vol ≥ 81/(3+δ)⁴·P, which relaxes below the fixed Vol_Ric(M) = P target by O(δ). The obstruction is thus rigidity-at-equality, not a stable inequality, and no O(1) margin survives approximate pinching. This is the same structural feature that forces the Seiberg–Witten/Ishida–LeBrun route to require the full cutoff-flow extraction machinery rather than a cheap compactness argument. The consequence is that the graph-manifold route to a quantitative gap theorem must either repair the rigidity (quantitative CSS stability, a purely geometric-measure-theoretic problem) or find a new intrinsic O(1) neck obstruction. The TTCS generator then identified the slice-defect invariant μ_m = inf over unit-volume T^m metrics of ‖Ric+(m−1)g‖_C0 as the bifurcation parameter for the entire negative branch in higher dimensions. The known values μ_2 = 1 (Gauss–Bonnet) and μ_3 ≥ 2/3 (Preissmann) explain why the 4D flat-slice barrier is absolute. The unknown μ_4 controls both the 4D gap theorem (if μ_4 > 0, the traced Gauss equation couples slice defect to ambient defect) and the 5D+ counterexample route (if μ_4 = 0, warped-product necks with almost-Einstein slices bypass the flat-slice barrier). The KK rank analysis shows the first interesting KK construction is n=6, not n=5, because the stress equation Σ F^a⊙F^a = cg has a rank obstruction: in n=5 the only solvable candidate (T³-bundle over T²) lands on a nil/solvmanifold whose polynomial growth excludes negative Einstein by Milnor–Švarc. The positive branch remains an existence problem; no closed manifold is known to carry positive Ricci and no Einstein metric. The IL restricted route remains the most developed negative gap candidate; A01's gate audit correctly identifies the defect-Moser general-p inequality and nested recurrence as the blocking gap, and A03's Codex report contains the relevant weighted mixed recurrence derivation (rejecting the ordinary unweighted Moser on U,V via a degenerate-Sobolev counterexample). The Strategist's guidance to circulate the cross-zero elimination as a Step-1 restatement rather than a new foundation is recorded.
