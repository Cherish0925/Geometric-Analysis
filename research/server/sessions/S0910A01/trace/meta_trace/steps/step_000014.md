## Deep-Reasoning

This step re-centered the four-dimensional negative-branch work around the real geometric bridge that remains between the LeBrun near-critical curvature identities and a forbidden Einstein germ. The Step 13 algebra remains valuable, especially the identity \(H=\frac13I+\frac14(|z|^2I-z\otimes z)-\frac12(W^++W^-)\), because it prevents the tempting inference that calibrated \(W^+\) forces \(H\ge0\). But the same identity should not be interpreted as saying that \(W^-\) is the sole obstruction or that controlling it alone would complete rank-one collapse: even \(H\ge0\) gives only a Ricci lower bound and relative Bishop--Gromov comparison, not a sequence-uniform volume anchor, base noncollapse, fiber homotopy survival, or a normal-cover reconstruction. Positivity and continuity of the normalized fiber length \(v\) similarly give only metric-dependent lower bounds, not uniform constants. Thus the static/rank-one closure language from Step 13 was withdrawn and marked as historical.

The more consequential insight came from source-reading Ye Li's four-dimensional smoothing theorem. The prior dismissal of smoothing because the calibrated bulk carries nonzero background curvature was a scale mistake. Li's hypothesis in Theorem 3.6 is the dimensionless smallness of \(r^4\fint_{B_r}|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\), not smallness of the unit-scale curvature mean. On a maximal-function good set, the Noether/Chern--Weil energy bound gives \(\mathcal M_R(|\operatorname{Rm}|^2)\le K_0\) after discarding controlled volume. Choosing a sufficiently small but fixed physical radius \(\rho\), before taking the sequence limit, makes \(\rho^4K_0\) smaller than Li's threshold while retaining a nonzero normalized negative-Einstein scale. This turns smoothing from an invented renormalized idea into a source-grounded route worth serious development.

The smoothing route is not a proof. Li 0911.3104 as read is a complete/all-centers theorem: Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 3.6 require the small energy condition for every center on the complete manifold, and the proof uses covering over neighboring balls and a continuation argument for the supremum over centers. A selected good point gives such all-centers control only on a buffered subdomain, so a local/conformal-completion or local-flow version is still needed. Moreover, bounded curvature at positive time does not by itself produce the Derdziński contradiction. One must either use smoothing to construct a genuine noncollapsed normal cover and then transfer that cover back to the original metrics, where \(\operatorname{Ric}+g\to0\) and the calibrated \(W^+\) defects are known, or prove that the normalized flow preserves the near-Einstein and calibrated data sufficiently at a fixed small time. The TTCS verifier sharpened this into three independent missing bridges: buffered localization of Li, propagation or transfer of the relevant defects, and production of a positive-volume set of genuine noncollapsed normal covers after smoothing. The TTCS generator and A03 message #263 gave an important candidate for the second bridge: an \(L^2\) absorption estimate for the Einstein defect \(E=\operatorname{Ric}+g\) under the normalized flow, using \((\partial_t-\Delta)|E|^2=-2|\nabla E|^2+4R_{ikjl}E_{ij}E_{kl}\), Hölder, and the four-dimensional Sobolev inequality so that volume factors cancel and the small quantity is \(\rho^4\fint|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\). However, after reading A03's note I found its own correction banner: the displayed local Gronwall proof in that note is not established because the inner-ball energy inequality is not closed and persistence of small normalized curvature energy was not proved. Therefore the mechanism is promising but conditional; it must be rebuilt rigorously, likely through Li's Corollary 3.5/Moser structure or A03's corrected `step14_cutoff_smoothing_and_cor35_reconstruction.md`, which was not yet read in this step.

This step therefore changes the research route without pretending to solve Yau 26. The strongest current negative-branch program is to compare localized Li smoothing against the normalized reduced equations as complementary ways to recover enough geometry on the calibrated bulk. Smoothing has the advantage that it does not assume exact circle symmetry and is tied to existing bounded-Ricci four-dimensional theory; its obstacles are now concrete analytic and topological ones. The reduced equations remain the language for any obstruction exposed by smoothing, especially derivative twisting, \(W^-\), weighted quotient collapse, rank loss, and contractible fibers. In parallel, the positive branch received useful peer progress: A02 sharpened the toric invariant route through a Faber--Krahn orbit-area lower bound and a \(\chi\)-uniform flux fork, but this remains a reduced positive-branch laboratory and does not resolve the full proposition. The whole Yau 26 problem remains open; the session's value here is a repaired, source-grounded route to attack the missing regularity/accessibility bridge rather than further exact-profile exclusions.
