## Deep-Reasoning

This Execute step materially changed the toric positive-branch subprogram by testing the corrected Step-15 strategist warning rather than blindly following Step 14's exact-FEM handoff. The active Yau-26 target remains the full sign-unrestricted all-dimensional proposition, and nothing here proves or refutes it. The work concerns only one laboratory for the four-dimensional positive branch: whether simply connected/invariant collapse might occur through many shrinking toric fixed spheres. The key conceptual correction is that exact high-corner Einstein solves are not the right probe for the pinched problem. A delta-pinched collapsing sequence need not solve the exact reduced Einstein system on m=8 or m=10 polygons; searching exact solutions would mostly test exact classification/nonexistence rather than the Yau-26 pinching hypothesis. Source checking reinforced this: arXiv:2311.04840 gives the exact polar/eigenfunction structure and classifies T^2-invariant Einstein four-manifolds only under nonnegative sectional curvature, while it explicitly says the general invariant case is much less known; LeBrun's theorem classifies Hermitian/conformally Kähler Einstein metrics as Kähler-Einstein plus Page/Chen-LeBrun-Weber exceptions, not arbitrary T^2-invariant Einstein metrics. Thus the right enemy is not an exact high-corner Einstein metric but a delta-pinched relaxation concentrating boundary bubbles.

The first computational finding is negative for an overoptimistic BUILD shortcut. In `workspace/scripts/step15_boundary_bubble_relaxation.py` and `workspace/data/step15_boundary_bubble_relaxation.json`, the coarse edge-ODE/spectral/Gauss-Bonnet relaxation permits collapse. If an edge has constant base curvature K_B=-A, the rod equation Q''+(1+2A)Q=0 with unit endpoint conditions gives rod length pi/sqrt(1+2A) and flux Phi=2/(1+2A). Taking A=m^2 on an m-rod polygon gives total flux 2m/(1+2m^2) ~ 1/m and hence Vol_4=2 pi^2 total_flux -> 0, while the orbit-space area can be kept above the Faber-Krahn lower bound and the signed Gauss-Bonnet deficit can be localized in boundary collars. This shows that Step 14's uniform-K_B stabilization is not representative of the real nonuniform enemy: the coarse constraints lambda_1 upper bound, signed Gauss-Bonnet, and rod unit closure do not by themselves imply a chi-uniform volume gap.

The second finding is that the base spectral bound does not by itself prohibit localized negative boundary curvature. In `step15_edge_spike_spectral_fd.py` and the sharper `step15_edge_spike_spectral_signs.py`, I solved the generalized eigenproblem -Delta_E u=lambda e^{2 phi}u on conformal rectangles normalized to fixed area A0=10. Taking phi=f(d) with f'(0)=0 preserves straight geodesic edges and right angles in this relaxed 2D model. The sign-controlled run found many examples with analytic K_edge<0 and lambda_1_scaled<=2; e.g. amp=0.25,width=0.03 gives lambda_1≈1.906 and K_edge≈-916, amp=0.5,width=0.05 gives lambda_1≈1.872 and K_edge≈-1087, amp=3,width=0.05 gives lambda_1≈1.792 and K_edge≈-9.68e5. These are not 4D toric metrics, but they prove that McKean/Cheng/global negative-curvature intuition and the Step-13 spectral pinning do not rule out boundary-localized negative curvature. The compensating positive curvature lies in a nearby subcollar, as Gauss-Bonnet would require. Therefore any invariant volume-gap proof must use full four-dimensional Ricci/gluing/topological information, not only 2D spectral geometry.

The third finding calibrates but does not complete the Page/CLW target. Using the explicit Page formula from `Knowledge/objects/papers/Toric-U-2-and-LeBrun-metrics--fb99f416c130--13e07193d27a/object.md` lines 319-340, `workspace/scripts/step15_page_edge_profile.py` computes the quotient base curvature for the U(2) formula. The Page profile is mild and sign-changing, about K_B in [-0.095,0.075], so Page is not a large negative boundary-bubble model. The script also prints flux diagnostics, but these are not source-grade per-sphere Gauss-Bonnet checks because the U(2)/Euler-angle coordinates have not been converted to primitive toric rod coordinates; the theta edges nearly give the expected 2, while the r edges do not. CLW was not computed: Hall-Murphy supplies moment-polytope/integral information and says CLW is cohomogeneity two with nonconstructive existence, not enough for direct edge-K_B extraction without a separate numerical extremal-Kähler implementation.

The most important mathematical result of the step is the local edge-germ analysis. The first rigid series test, `step15_edge_einstein_series.py`, froze a(r)=cosh(sqrt(A)r), i.e. constant off-rod base curvature. It solved only the leading Step-12 coefficients R2=A/2 and p3=-A/3-1/6 and then failed at order r^2. I corrected its saved finding: this is an artificial overconstraint, not evidence of a local obstruction. The repaired flexible formal series in `step15_edge_einstein_series_flexible.py` allows a(r)=1+(A/2)r^2+a4 r^4+... and solves Ric=g recursively through the checked order for arbitrary A. Coefficients include R2=A/2, p3=-(2A+1)/6, R4=a4=-A(2A+1)/24, p5=(22A^2+10A+1)/120, R6=a6=A(22A^2+10A+1)/720, and all four residual components vanish through the checked order. The pattern R(r)=a(r) emerged.

That pattern yielded an exact separated edge germ in `step15_edge_germ_first_integral.py`: g=dr^2+p(r)^2 dtheta^2+a(r)^2 g_{S^2(kappa)}, kappa=1+2A, with a(0)=1, a'(0)=0, p(0)=0, p'(0)=1, a''(0)=A. The Einstein equations reduce to a'=A p and 2aa''+(a')^2=kappa-a^2, with first integral a(a')^2=kappa(a-1)-(a^3-1)/3. The next zero of p occurs at a_+=(-1+sqrt(9+24A))/2, and if the first rod period is normalized smooth, the second-bolt slope is mu=(2a_+ +1)/(a_+(a_+ +2))<1 for A>0. Numerically, A=100 gives rod flux 0.00995 and mu≈0.0784; A=10000 gives flux 1e-4 and mu≈0.00813. Thus arbitrarily negative edge curvature is locally Einstein-compatible, but the simplest separated completion wants a cone/orbifold cap. This places the true BUILD/BREAK wall at boundary-bubble desingularization: either smooth toric closure/ALE-Biquard-Ozuch obstruction forces an O(1) non-scalar Ricci defect, giving a path to an invariant chi-uniform gap, or a genuine delta-pinched gluing construction exists and collapses the invariant route.

The step's synthesis is saved in `Sessions/S0910A02/workspace/notes/step15_boundary_bubble_relaxation_and_edge_germ.md`, with all script/data paths listed. Peer message #268 communicated the source correction, relaxation collapse, spectral compatibility, Page calibration, flexible edge series, exact first-integral, and boundary-bubble conclusion. The negative-branch smoothing architecture from A01 (#267) is important parallel work but was not pursued here because the toric fork was close and this step produced decisive local evidence. The resulting research state is sharper: uniform-K_B stabilization and pure spectral estimates are insufficient; local edge Einstein equations are also insufficient; the next load-bearing task is to identify the boundary bubble's orbifold/ALE obstruction or construct a smooth delta-pinched gluing sequence. This remains far from the full Yau-26 proof/refutation, and even a complete invariant result would still leave the general-to-invariant collapse reduction and n>=5 open.
