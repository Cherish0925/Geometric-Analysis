# An exact signature-zero Einstein control for the gauge route

21 September 2026.

Ordinary Seiberg--Witten existence and zero signature do not force an
Einstein four-metric to be locally symmetric. A concrete established
example is useful when formulating the nonabelian curvature equations.

Let \(S\) be a general Schoen surface. Ciliberto--Mendes Lopes--Roulleau,
[*On Schoen surfaces*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.1750), Proposition 2.1
and Theorem 3.1, give
\[
p_g=5,\quad q=4,\quad\chi(\mathcal O_S)=2,\quad K_S^2=16
\]
and a finite degree-two morphism onto a \((2,4)\) complete
intersection in \(\mathbb P^4\), branched only at its nodes. Its
canonical bundle is the pullback of the image's canonical bundle
\(\mathcal O(1)\): there is no ramification divisor. In particular
\(K_S\) is ample.
The negative Kähler--Einstein existence theorem therefore applies.
Noether's formula and the signature identity give
\[
e(S)=12\chi(\mathcal O_S)-K_S^2=8,\qquad
\tau(S)=\tfrac13(K_S^2-2e(S))=0,
\qquad b^+(S)=1+2p_g=11.
\]
Its canonical ordinary Seiberg--Witten invariant is nonzero.
Proposition 2.4 of the same paper proves that its complex universal
cover is not the bidisk. Thus its negative Kähler--Einstein metric
is not locally symmetric: in real dimension four the negative
Kähler symmetric alternatives are the bidisk and the complex ball;
the ball has \(K^2=3e\), not \(2e\).

These are deductions from known examples, not new constructions.
The paper's abstract has an inconsistent \(K^2=8\) for the Schoen
surface; its introduction, Proposition 2.1 at genus two and the
degree-two canonical map all give \(K_S^2=16\). The canonical image
does have \(K^2=8\); the two surfaces must not be confused.

This example separates two possible gauge arguments. An ordinary
monopole can coexist with a nonsymmetric signature-zero Einstein
metric. Consequently a system whose solutions would force local
symmetry needs an additional existence mechanism, not merely the
ordinary invariant. The explicit central-curvature and graded-star
calculation in [the companion note](round12_uniformization.md)
identifies where that additional condition enters.

It is not a counterexample to a theorem requiring monopoles in both
orientations or to a theorem imposing a specified nonabelian
solution. Neither extra premise has been verified for this example.
For our sheared manifolds the extra topology and the two symplectic
orientations remain available; the example prevents replacing that
work by an automatic ordinary-to-nonabelian implication.
