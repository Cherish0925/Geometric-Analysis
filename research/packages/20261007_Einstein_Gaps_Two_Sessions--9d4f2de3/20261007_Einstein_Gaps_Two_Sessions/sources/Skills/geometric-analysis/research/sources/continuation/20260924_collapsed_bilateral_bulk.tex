\hypertarget{bilateral-gauge-saturation-on-genuinely-collapsed-local-covers}{%
\section{Bilateral gauge saturation on genuinely collapsed local
covers}\label{bilateral-gauge-saturation-on-genuinely-collapsed-local-covers}}

24 September 2026. This note connects the actual-form estimates to a
collapsed regularity theorem. It does not replace base noncollapse by a
renamed base-volume hypothesis. Instead, maximal local nilpotency rank
supplies harmonic charts \textbf{after local rewinding}, and a
covering-average estimate transfers the gauge information to those
charts. The output is local product geometry outside a quantitatively
small-volume set, not a global Einstein repair or a classification of
all collapsed regions.

\hypertarget{a-collapse-tolerant-structure-statement}{%
\subsection{1. A collapse-tolerant structure
statement}\label{a-collapse-tolerant-structure-statement}}

Use the bilateral actual conformal Spin\(^c\) inputs and conventions of
\href{20260924_bilateral_realization.md}{bilateral realization}, Section
1. Thus \(a_+\) is actual in the given orientation and \(a_-\) in the
opposite orientation; their positive projected energies satisfy
\(p_+,p_->0\). Define \[
 \delta=\|\operatorname{Ric}^\sharp+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}},
 \quad r=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2,
 \quad q_\pm=\frac{\frac32\int(s^2+12|W^\pm|^2)-72\pi^2p_\pm}{V},
 \quad K=2r+q_++q_-.
 \tag{1}
\] The \(q_\pm\) are nonnegative; Weyl norms are endomorphism norms.
There is \textbf{no} lower bound on \(p_\pm\), total volume, or the
volume of base balls in the result below.

Fix a scale \(0<\rho\le1\). Let \(\epsilon_N,h_N>0\) be the
four-dimensional Euclidean-model constants in Naber--Zhang's Proposition
6.4, reduced if needed so that \(h_N<1\). Define \(\mathcal G_\rho\) to
consist of points \(x\) for which, for some \(k\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), \[
 d_{GH}(B_{2\rho}(x),B_{2\rho}(0^k))<\epsilon_N\rho,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Image}
 [\pi_1(B_{\epsilon_N\rho}(x))\to\pi_1(B_{2\rho}(x))]=4-k.
 \tag{2}
\] The rank is the Hirsch rank of a finite-index nilpotent subgroup, not
a Betti number or the rank of \(\pi_1(M)\). The small group in (2) is
almost nilpotent under these hypotheses. Closedness supplies the local
completeness required by the classical theorem. A fixed measurable
subset of the points satisfying (2) can be used if desired.

Let \(\pi_x:U_x\to B_\rho(x)\) be the \textbf{local universal cover}
with its pulled-back metric, and choose a lift \(\widetilde x\). The
metric space \(U_x\) is generally incomplete at the lifted outer
boundary. All balls used below stay a definite distance from that
boundary. We do not identify \(U_x\) with a subset of the universal
cover of \(M\).

\textbf{Theorem A (rewound product structure on the bulk).} Fix
\(\rho\in(0,1]\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). There are \(a>0\),
\(C<\infty\), and a modulus \(\Psi(t)\to0\) as \(t\downarrow0\),
depending only on \(\rho,\alpha\) and the fixed classical constants,
with the following property. Suppose \(M\) is a closed connected
oriented smooth four-manifold with the actual inputs above,
\(\delta\le1/2\), and \(0<K\le1/2880\). Then there is a measurable set
\(\mathcal E\subset M\) satisfying \[
                  \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal E)}{V}
                         \le C K^{1/4},                 \tag{3}
\] such that, for each \(x\in\mathcal G_\rho\setminus\mathcal E\), a
neighbourhood of \(\widetilde x\) containing its radius-\(a\) ball is
\(\Psi(\delta+K^{1/4})\)-close in pointed geometric \(C^{1,\alpha}\) to
a neighbourhood in \[
                         \mathbb H^2_{-1}\times\mathbb H^2_{-1}.
 \tag{4}
\] One may define this closeness using a pointed embedding of a fixed
slightly larger model ball, whose image contains \(B_a(\widetilde x)\),
and the scaled \(C^{1,\alpha}\) norm of the difference of the
pulled-back metrics. Shrinking \(a\) by a fixed factor makes these
equivalent usual local definitions interchangeable.

Equivalently, along any sequence with \(\delta_j\to0\), \(K_j\to0\),
there are exceptional sets of relative volume tending to zero, and all
the indicated rewound balls converge uniformly in this local geometric
sense to product balls. For \(K=0\), the equality case already makes
\(g\) Einstein and Kähler in both orientations, so the product statement
is exact.

This theorem does not assume bounded diameter, \(\pi_2=0\), a
class-square cost, or base injectivity radius. It allows genuine
rank-one or rank-two collapse; Section 6 gives an exact rank-two
example. The classical rank/model test (2) is not proved to hold
everywhere. The exceptional-volume estimate is relative to the whole
manifold, not to each arbitrarily small thin component.

\hypertarget{the-covering-average-lemma-multiplicity-is-not-free}{%
\subsection{2. The covering-average lemma: multiplicity is not
free}\label{the-covering-average-lemma-multiplicity-is-not-free}}

\textbf{Lemma 1.} Suppose \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge-3g\),
\(\pi:U\to B_R(x)\) is a connected normal Riemannian covering, and
\(4b<R\), \(b\le1\). For any nonnegative measurable function \(F\) on
\(B_R(x)\), \[
 \fint_{B_b(\widetilde x)}\pi^*F
       \le C_1\fint_{B_b(x)}F,
 \qquad C_1\le\frac{V_{-1}(3b)}{V_{-1}(b)}\le C(4).
 \tag{5}
\] The constant does not depend on the number of sheets, a base-ball
volume, or an injectivity radius. Both sides are \textbf{averages}.

\textbf{Proof.} Choose a Dirichlet fundamental domain based at
\(\widetilde x\). Its part \(D_b\) projecting to \(B_b(x)\) has volume
\(\operatorname{Vol}B_b(x)\) and lies in \(B_b(\widetilde x)\), apart
from measure-zero choices on its boundary. Indeed a minimizing base
geodesic from \(x\) to any point of \(B_b(x)\) lifts inside that ball,
and the Dirichlet representative is no farther.

If a point of \(B_b(x)\) has \(m\) lifts in \(B_b(\widetilde x)\), each
is a translate of its representative in \(D_b\). Its deck transformation
moves \(\widetilde x\) by less than \(2b\). Denote the number of such
deck transformations by \(N\). The sets \(\gamma D_b\) for these
transformations are disjoint up to measure zero and contained in
\(B_{3b}(\widetilde x)\). Therefore \[
 m\le N\le
 \frac{\operatorname{Vol}B_{3b}(\widetilde x)}
      {\operatorname{Vol}B_b(x)}.
 \tag{6}
\] The balls have compact closure away from the boundary of the cover;
proper discontinuity makes the relevant orbit count finite. The area
formula and (6) bound the lifted integral by
\(\operatorname{Vol}B_{3b}(\widetilde x)\fint_{B_b(x)}F\). Divide by
\(\operatorname{Vol}B_b(\widetilde x)\) and apply the local
Bishop--Gromov comparison, which is valid since \(3b<R\). This proves
(5). \(\square\)

In contrast, an estimate for an unnormalized integral lifts with the
multiplicity in (6), which can diverge. A global small average does not
say that every local base average is small. The next step quantifies
exactly this second issue rather than suppressing it.

\hypertarget{global-gauge-error-gives-small-local-averages-off-a-small-set}{%
\subsection{3. Global gauge error gives small local averages off a small
set}\label{global-gauge-error-gives-small-local-averages-off-a-small-set}}

The inherited actual-form theorem gives global forms
\(\omega_\pm\in\Omega^\pm(M)\), with
\(\|\omega_\pm\|_\infty\le2\sqrt3\). Put \[
 F=\sum_{\sigma=\pm}
     \bigl[(|\omega_\sigma|^2-2)^2+|\nabla\omega_\sigma|^2\bigr].
 \tag{7}
\] Use the smoothing tolerance \(K/4\) for each form and write
\(\kappa_\sigma=r+q_\sigma\), so \(\kappa_++\kappa_-=K\). The actual
estimates give \[
 \fint F\le734K+8\sqrt K\le750\sqrt K.
 \tag{8}
\] The first-order bound and the uniform amplitude, not the second-order
Hodge estimate, are used. There is no hidden negative-square cost.

For any fixed \(b\le1\), let \(\mathcal A_bF(x)=\fint_{B_b(x)}F\). Local
volume doubling under the Ricci lower bound implies \[
 \int_M\mathcal A_bF(x)\,d\mu(x)\le C_2\int_MF.
 \tag{9}
\] To see this explicitly, if \(d(x,y)<b\), then
\(B_b(y)\subset B_{2b}(x)\), hence
\(\operatorname{Vol}B_b(y)\le C_2\operatorname{Vol}B_b(x)\). Fubini
applied to the ball-averaging kernel now proves (9). Consequently, for
every \(t>0\), \[
 \frac{\operatorname{Vol}\{x:\mathcal A_bF(x)>t\}}{V}
              \le\frac{750C_2\sqrt K}{t}.              \tag{10}
\] Take \(t=K^{1/4}\). This proves (3). At every remaining point, Lemma
1 transfers the small base average to its local universal cover: \[
               \fint_{B_b(\widetilde x)}\pi_x^*F
                         \le C_1K^{1/4}.               \tag{11}
\] The potentially enormous covering multiplicity has cancelled against
the correct local volume normalization. No inequality asserting
\(\int\pi_x^*F\le C\int_MF\) is used.

\hypertarget{maximal-rank-supplies-regularity-the-forms-identify-the-limit}{%
\subsection{4. Maximal rank supplies regularity; the forms identify the
limit}\label{maximal-rank-supplies-regularity-the-forms-identify-the-limit}}

For \(x\in\mathcal G_\rho\), apply Naber--Zhang, Proposition 6.4, to
\(\rho^{-2}g\). Since \(\delta\le1/2\), the Ricci tensor norm of \(g\)
is at most three, and rescaling preserves the required bound. The
conclusion supplies a uniform harmonic radius at the lift of \(x\) in
the universal cover of \(B_\rho(x)\), at least \(h_N\rho\). This is a
classical theorem used with its rank condition, not a new claim that
Ricci bounds alone give regular local covers.

Choose \(b\) to be a fixed sufficiently small multiple of \(h_N\rho\),
with \(4b<\rho\). The covering metric has controlled harmonic
coordinates on a neighbourhood larger than \(B_b(\widetilde x)\). The
harmonic metric equation and bounded Ricci give uniform finite
\(W^{2,p}\) bounds on smaller charts and hence \(C^{1,\beta}\)
precompactness for every \(\beta<1\). No derivative of Ricci is needed.

We give the argument for the qualitative uniform modulus in Theorem A.
If it failed, there would be good points with \(\delta_j\to0\),
\(K_j^{1/4}\to0\), and controlled cover charts that stay a fixed
distance from product geometry. The charts have a convergent
subsequence. In harmonic coordinates \[
 g^{ab}\partial_a\partial_b g_{ij}
            =-2\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}+Q_{ij}(g,\partial g).
 \tag{12}
\] Pointwise \(\operatorname{Ric}+g\to0\) makes the limit a smooth exact
negative Einstein metric. Subtracting its equation improves convergence
to strong finite \(W^{2,p}\), and therefore to the prescribed
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) on still smaller charts.

Lift the actual forms to these charts. Their uniform bounds and (11)
give fixed-chart \(H^1\) bounds. Rellich compactness and convergence of
connections yield two smooth parallel limiting forms, one self-dual and
one anti-self-dual, both of norm \(\sqrt2\). The associated parallel
complex structures commute. Their product is a symmetric trace-zero
involution, with two rank-two parallel eigenspaces. Local de Rham
splitting and \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\) make both factors hyperbolic
surfaces of curvature \(-1\). On a fixed smaller ball this is exactly
(4), contradicting the assumed failure of uniform closeness. This proves
Theorem A. \(\square\)

The limit assertion uses neither global compactness nor an ALE theorem.
It also does not identify the limiting local deck transformations across
different points or across the exceptional set. That missing global
information is precisely why Theorem A is not a global realization
result.

\hypertarget{why-this-changes-the-scope-and-where-it-stops}{%
\subsection{5. Why this changes the scope, and where it
stops}\label{why-this-changes-the-scope-and-where-it-stops}}

The earlier bounded-diameter bilateral theorem obtains one compact
smooth limit and hence an exact metric on the original manifold. The
present theorem removes diameter and \textbf{base noncollapse} at the
cost of a different conclusion: quantitative product geometry in local
covers on the maximal-rank bulk. The two conclusions are not
interchangeable.

There are two distinct failure mechanisms left visible:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  A point can fail the rank/model condition (2). Small global gauge
  deficit does not prove maximal nilpotent rank or a smooth collapsed
  base. Naber--Zhang's singular-fibre examples explain why that topology
  cannot simply be omitted from their theorem.
\item
  A point can belong to \(\mathcal E\). The global bounds control its
  volume, not its topology, its monodromy, or the tensor modes needed to
  solve the global Einstein equation.
\end{enumerate}

Even if (2) holds everywhere and the exceptional-volume bound tends to
zero, no assertion here controls a full global tensor inverse or glues
local exact product limits into a single metric on \(M\). The
\href{20260924_local_to_global_scope.md}{diameter-free repair criterion}
cannot be invoked by replacing its base-ball Moser estimate with an
unexplained cover estimate: the inverse and the global residual norm
need their own covering-compatible control. Theorem A supplies genuine
collapsed local geometry, not that missing global solver.

\hypertarget{actual-collapse-tests-and-a-sharp-failure-of-unnormalized-transfer}{%
\subsection{6. Actual collapse tests and a sharp failure of unnormalized
transfer}\label{actual-collapse-tests-and-a-sharp-failure-of-unnormalized-transfer}}

\hypertarget{exact-bilateral-saturation-allows-rank-two-thin-geometry}{%
\subsubsection{6.1 Exact bilateral saturation allows rank-two thin
geometry}\label{exact-bilateral-saturation-allows-rank-two-thin-geometry}}

Take two closed hyperbolic surfaces of fixed genera at least two, each
with a separating geodesic of length \(\ell\to0\), and their product
metric \(g_\ell\). The canonical classes of the two product complex
structures are actual in both orientations, and \[
                    \delta_\ell=q_{+,\ell}=q_{-,\ell}=0.
 \tag{13}
\] Near the two short geodesics the metric is exactly \[
 dt_1^2+\ell^2\cosh^2(t_1)d\theta_1^2
 +dt_2^2+\ell^2\cosh^2(t_2)d\theta_2^2,
 \tag{14}
\] where each \(\theta_i\) has period one. Collar widths tend to
infinity. At their common waist, fixed-radius base-ball volumes are
\(O(\ell^2)\). The balls converge to Euclidean two-dimensional balls,
and the fibre group in (2) is \(\mathbb Z^2\), of maximal rank \(4-2\).
Unwrapping the two circles gives exact hyperbolic-product geometry.
These are real negative Einstein and gauge-saturated examples excluded
by any uniform base-ball volume lower bound, but covered by Theorem A.

This is not a new Einstein construction. It is an exact test showing
that the theorem's changed input regime includes genuine collapse, not
merely a reformulated noncollapsed condition.

\hypertarget{absolute-integral-errors-need-not-stay-small-after-rewinding}{%
\subsubsection{6.2 Absolute integral errors need not stay small after
rewinding}\label{absolute-integral-errors-need-not-stay-small-after-rewinding}}

On the same actual metrics choose a fixed nonzero nonnegative smooth
function \(f(t_1,t_2)\), supported in \(|t_i|<b/10\), independent of
\(\theta_1,\theta_2\), and extend it by zero. Then \[
 \int_M f\,d\mu_{g_\ell}
 =\ell^2\int f\cosh t_1\cosh t_2\,dt_1dt_2\to0.
 \tag{15}
\] On the local universal cover use \(u_i=\ell\theta_i\). The lifted
metric is \(dt_1^2+\cosh^2(t_1)du_1^2+dt_2^2+\cosh^2(t_2)du_2^2\),
independent of \(\ell\). Its lifted function is the same nonzero
\(f(t_1,t_2)\). Its integral over a fixed suitably chosen lifted ball is
bounded below by a positive constant. Thus no universal estimate of the
form \[
            \int_{B_b(\widetilde x)}\pi^*f\le C\int_M f
 \tag{16}
\] can hold, even when the underlying metrics have exact Einstein and
bilateral gauge equality. This counterexample concerns an inference
between integral norms. The function \(f\) is not asserted to be an
actual monopole defect on the equality metrics, where those defects are
zero. It pinpoints why a proof based only on their global integral
bounds cannot freely lift such bounds without a local-normalization
step.

\hypertarget{local-and-global-universal-covers-are-different-objects}{%
\subsubsection{6.3 Local and global universal covers are different
objects}\label{local-and-global-universal-covers-are-different-objects}}

For example, a Hopf circle in \(S^3\) is contractible in \(S^3\), but
represents the infinite cyclic fibre group over a small disk in the Hopf
base. A local cover of that tube unwraps the circle; the global
universal cover does not. Taking a product with \(S^1\) gives a
four-dimensional example with \(\pi_2=0\) exhibiting this distinction.
This elementary example is not claimed to satisfy the negative
Einstein/gauge hypotheses. It shows why even \(\pi_2(M)=0\) does not
justify embedding every rewound local ball into \(\widetilde M\).
Theorem A works entirely on the local cover and never makes that
inference.

\hypertarget{source-and-status}{%
\subsection{7. Source and status}\label{source-and-status}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Naber--Zhang, \href{https://arxiv.org/html/1412.1326}{\emph{Topology
  and epsilon-regularity Theorems on Collapsed Manifolds with Ricci
  Curvature Bounds}}, Proposition 6.4, supplies the maximal-rank
  harmonic-radius theorem on the local universal cover; its Proposition
  6.5 requires the exact Einstein equation or Ricci-derivative control
  for curvature bounds. Only Proposition 6.4 is used here. We specialize
  its model to Euclidean space so its constants do not hide a varying
  limit-space dependence.
\item
  The
  \href{../mathematics/current/line1_composed_application.md}{actual-form
  theorem} supplies (7)--(8);
  \href{20260924_bilateral_realization.md}{bilateral realization} gives
  the opposite-handed form algebra. The present theorem does not require
  the second-order form estimate.
\item
  The \href{../mathematics/current/round11_closed_gluing.md}{preserved
  collar construction}, Section 2, records the classical hyperbolic
  collar formula and its primary reference. The product tests above use
  these classical metrics, not a new gluing construction.
\end{itemize}

The new project bridge is the combination of actual gauge supply,
covering averages, and maximal-rank collapsed regularity. Its bad-set
bound is proved by a direct averaging argument; its local geometric
modulus is qualitative compactness. It neither proves maximal rank from
saturation nor settles global Einstein existence in the collapsing
regime.
