This step closed the degenerate cutoff-flow platform by proving the shifted continuation theorem and assembling the first-exit closure. The construction adapts Wang's staggered induction (Pseudolocality paper, Theorem 4.1, Lemmas 4.4–4.10) to the normalized equation ∂_t g = −2χ²(Ric+g) with the shifted variable U = χ²(Rm+G).

The central mathematical insight has three layers:

1. **L^p(Rm) propagation under weighted sup control.** The reaction term a|Rm|^{p+1} = (a|Rm|)|Rm|^p ≤ (|U|+C)|Rm|^p uses only sup|U| = K, never unweighted sup|Rm|. The Hessian-a signed contraction (Step 35) and the gradient-cutoff Young inequality both produce the factor a = χ², matching the degenerate diffusion ∫a|∇Rm|²|Rm|^{p-2}. TTCS required splitting E = Ric+g: the Ricci part gives B_Ric with |B| ~ |Rm|^p (standard IBP), while the g-part gives B_g with |B| ~ |Rm|^{p-1} (handled by Young at Rm = 0 with optional Kato regularization). The metric equivalence also needed correction from direct subtraction to the logarithmic estimate d/dt log g(v,v) ≤ CK, giving sharp bilipschitz control.

2. **Unweighted derivative recovery (Lemma 1.2a).** This is the Wang Proposition 4.13 analog that TTCS identified as structurally missing. The argument splits M = {χ ≥ δ} ∪ {χ < δ}: on the interior, Leibniz inversion gives |∇^k Rm| ≤ (1/δ²)|∇^k U| + (lower terms); on the boundary where χ < δ, the metric drift is bounded by 2δ²K·T_max, so curvature stays close to initial data. This gives per-datum ||∇^k Rm||_p bounds at each induction level, without requiring uniform bounds across the sequence.

3. **First-exit closure logic.** The Moser endpoint ||aF(t)||_∞ ≤ Cγ/t gives sup|U(t)| ≤ Cγ/τ + C < ∞ for any τ > 0 (directly, without using unweighted sup|T|). The continuation theorem extends the flow past any candidate first-exit time T*. The K_2 energy grows at most exponentially with coefficient C(1+r⁻²), giving strict improvement of (F1) for small θ. The metric drift ∫₀^T ||aE||_∞ dt ≤ Cδθ^{2/3}r² gives strict improvement of (F2)–(F3). The contradiction at T* is logically complete.

The quantifier separation is now precise: Layer A (DeTurck existence) and Layer B (continuation) are per-datum, with constants depending on the individual metric, χ, and τ. Layer C (K_2, Y, J_6, Q, Moser endpoints) must be sequence-uniform for the terminal geometric contradiction. This separation means the continuation theorem does not impose any sequence-uniform higher-derivative requirements — it takes the weighted curvature endpoint from Layer C as input and produces per-datum derivative bounds as output.

TTCS also noted a residual expositional gap: the explicit Hölder exponent arrangement for m ≥ 2 top-jet terms is stated schematically rather than displayed for each product. This mirrors Wang's original treatment and is not a structural error, but future revision should make it explicit for the m = 1 and m = 2 base cases.

A03's Step 39 result (message #346) is important: the general-p homogeneous defect power inequality with three-factor Hölder (not two-factor) completes the defect Moser ladder. Combined with the continuation theorem and first-exit closure, the smoothing principle's dependency graph is now complete modulo support construction and DeTurck existence.

The last major analytic piece before the terminal geometric passage is the macro-disjoint support construction: maximal-function good centers, volume-proportional assignment, coloring, and plateau containment with Vol(P_i) ≥ αA_i. Even completing this would address only the near-critical Ishida–LeBrun negative family in dimension four; the positive branch, arbitrary four-manifold topology, dimensions n ≥ 5, dimension-only interval constants, and Lean formalization remain open.
