# Proved implications versus unresolved upgrades

## Proved without compactness

For an exact Ishida--LeBrun boundary metric with Ricci operator defect
\(\delta\), let \(R=(8\pi^2)^{-1}\int|\operatorname{Ric}^\circ|^2\) and
\(\kappa=3\delta^2/(1-\delta)^2\).  Then

\[
R\le\frac{2A}{3}\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa}.
\]

There is a Bauer--Furuta sign class whose primitive scalar--Weyl deficit is
at most \(108\pi^2R\).  For its regularized optimal rescaled monopoles:

* the harmonic-projection error satisfies
  \(\|iF_A^+-2\pi H_a\|_2^2\le\Delta/18+o(1)\);
* every Weitzenbock, amplitude, quadratic-map/Kato, Hodge, and Weyl-eigenvalue
  defect is at most \(4\Delta/27+o(1)\);
* \(\int f^2(|\varphi|-f)^2\le\Delta+o(1)\);
* the triangle deficit is at most \(108\pi^2R\) and the finite-dimensional
  Cauchy--Schwarz deficit at most \(864\pi^2R\);
* the conformal value gap is at most \(\Delta\), but this does not control a
  conformal Euler--Lagrange residual.

For all sign choices on average, the scalar--Weyl deficit is at most
\(108\pi^2R\).  Walsh polarization then proves spatial \(L^1\)
orthogonality of the harmonic coordinate forms, with error
\(O(\sqrt{AR})=O(A\delta)\).  This conclusion does not assume convergence,
noncollapse, or a curvature bound.

The normalized energy and volume limits in the Step 3 note are correct.  A
fixed-topology sequence forces every sign deficit to zero; a varying-topology
sequence only forces the average deficit to zero.

## Proved under explicit auxiliary assumptions

* A uniform scale-invariant Sobolev constant upgrades spinor-amplitude
  convergence from \(L^2\) to \(L^4\), but not to \(C^0\).
* Uniform Hölder control of harmonic representatives together with a uniform
  lower volume ratio upgrades their nearly constant \(L^2\) norm to a
  pointwise positive lower bound.
* A uniform simple-lowest-eigenvalue gap for \(W_+\), plus an upper bound for
  the optimal weight, combines with the Weyl-eigenvalue defect to force a
  common approximate eigenline.  Together with Walsh orthogonality, this
  forces phase separation.
* On a fixed boundary manifold, a uniform positive \(C^{2,\alpha}\) harmonic
  radius and a diameter bound are impossible for any sequence with
  \(\|\operatorname{Ric}+g\|_{\rm op}\to0\).  Such bounds would yield a
  negative Kähler--Einstein limit, contradicting the connected-sum topology.

## Still unresolved

No argument presently upgrades the unconditional critical \(L^2/W^{1,2}\)
control to a globally nonvanishing symplectic form.  In particular:

* a Sobolev bound alone does not rule out small-capacity transition sets;
* the scalar--Weyl deficit does not control the Blair/Chern--Weil equality
  stage before an almost-Kähler form exists;
* no uniform lowest-eigenline gap follows from the known integral bounds;
* no pure Ricci pinching theorem presently supplies harmonic-radius
  noncollapse or prevents long/cusp-like degeneration;
* when the sign cube grows, average saturation does not imply saturation of
  every vertex.

Thus a hypothetical counterexample must leave bounded geometry and can remain
compatible with all estimates only through collapse, long-end escape,
Weyl/eigenline degeneration, or phase separation across a transition region.
