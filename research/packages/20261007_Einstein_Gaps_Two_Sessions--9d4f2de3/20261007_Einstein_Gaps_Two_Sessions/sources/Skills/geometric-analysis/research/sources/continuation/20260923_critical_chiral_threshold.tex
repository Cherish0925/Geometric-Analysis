\hypertarget{critical-one-half-energy-thresholds-and-paired-curvature-concentration}{%
\section{Critical one-half energy thresholds and paired curvature
concentration}\label{critical-one-half-energy-thresholds-and-paired-curvature-concentration}}

23 September 2026. Continuation of the
\href{20260923_compact_realization.md}{compact-regime realization
theorem}. The increment is at the scale-invariant exponent:
supercritical control or critical equiintegrability is replaced by a
fixed small local critical energy threshold. The small chirality may
vary from ball to ball.

\hypertarget{conventions-and-statements}{%
\subsection{1. Conventions and
statements}\label{conventions-and-statements}}

Use the Hilbert--Schmidt curvature-operator convention \[
 |W^\pm|^2=\tfrac14 W^\pm_{ijkl}(W^\pm)^{ijkl}.             \tag{1}
\] This is not the largest-eigenvalue norm. In the full tensor
convention, all energy thresholds below must be multiplied by four. Let
\(\omega_4=\pi^2/2\), and let \(V_{-K/3}(R)\) be the volume of a
radius-\(R\) ball in the simply connected four-dimensional space form of
sectional curvature \(-K/3\). For fixed \(K,D,v>0\), set \[
 \theta_0=\frac{v}{V_{-K/3}(D+1)},\qquad
 S_0=\frac9{16\sqrt{\omega_4\theta_0}},\qquad
 \kappa_0=\frac1{6S_0^2}=\frac{128\omega_4\theta_0}{243},
 \qquad \epsilon_0=\frac{\kappa_0}{4}.                    \tag{2}
\] If the class below is nonempty then \(0<\theta_0\le1\). These are
sufficient constants, not claimed to be sharp.

The topological assumption is \[
 \pi_1(M)\text{ torsion-free},\qquad \pi_2(M)=0.            \tag{H}
\] Every closed aspherical manifold has (H). The
\href{20260923_topological_core_scope.md}{topological core-scope
extension} shows that half-flat ALE core exclusion needs only (H): the
universal cover has \(H_1=H_2=0\), and a finite-fundamental-group core
lifts to that cover. In particular, everything below may also be read in
the older aspherical scope.

\textbf{Theorem A (critical local compactness).} Fix \(K,D,v,r_0>0\) and
\(0<\alpha<1\). There is a uniform positive lower bound for the
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) harmonic radius on closed connected smooth oriented
four-manifolds satisfying (H), \[
 \|\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}\le K,
 \qquad \operatorname{diam}(M,g)\le D,\qquad
 \operatorname{Vol}(M,g)\ge v,                            \tag{3}
\] and \[
 \sup_{x\in M}\min\left\{
   \int_{B_{r_0}(x)}|W^+|^2\,d\mu,\
   \int_{B_{r_0}(x)}|W^-|^2\,d\mu
                   \right\}\le\epsilon_0.               \tag{4}
\] Consequently the class has geometric \(C^{1,\beta}\) and weak
\(W^{2,p}\) precompactness, and only finitely many smooth types. Here
\(\beta<1\) and \(p<\infty\). No uniform \(L^q\), \(q>2\), bound or
vanishing equiintegrability modulus is required.

\textbf{Theorem B (critical-regime realization).} Fix \(D,v,r_0>0\). Use
(2) with \(K=2\). There is \(\delta_0(D,v,r_0)>0\) such that a closed
connected smooth oriented four-manifold satisfying (H) and carrying a
metric with these diameter/volume bounds, (4), and \[
 \delta_-(g)=\|\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}_g+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}
                       <\delta_0                       \tag{5}
\] admits a smooth metric \(h\) with \(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\).

The simpler condition
\(\min\{\int_M|W^+|^2,\int_M|W^-|^2\}\le\epsilon_0\) implies (4), but is
usually unnecessarily restrictive. Condition (4) allows substantial
total Weyl energy and different choices of the small half in different
regions.

\hypertarget{a-sobolevweitzenbuxf6ck-gap-on-the-exact-blowup}{%
\subsection{2. A Sobolev--Weitzenböck gap on the exact
blowup}\label{a-sobolevweitzenbuxf6ck-gap-on-the-exact-blowup}}

\textbf{Lemma C.} Let \(X\) be a complete noncompact smooth Ricci-flat
oriented four-manifold, ALE of some positive order, with asymptotic
volume ratio \(\theta\ge\theta_0>0\). Each Weyl half separately
satisfies \[
 W^\pm\equiv0\quad\hbox{or}\quad
                       \int_X|W^\pm|^2\,d\mu\ge\kappa_0. \tag{6}
\] This is a classical analytic mechanism, not a classification of
general Ricci-flat ALE spaces.

\textbf{Proof.} Brendle's nonnegative-Ricci Sobolev inequality applied
to compactly supported \(f=|u|^3\), by approximation at zeros, gives \[
 4(\omega_4\theta)^{1/4}
       \left(\int|u|^4\right)^{3/4}
 \le3\int|u|^2|\nabla u|
 \le3\left(\int|u|^4\right)^{1/2}
           \left(\int|\nabla u|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\] Consequently \[
 \|u\|_4^2\le S(\theta)\|\nabla u\|_2^2,\qquad
 S(\theta)=\frac9{16\sqrt{\omega_4\theta}}\le S_0.          \tag{7}
\] For either half let \(w=|W^\pm|\). On the Ricci-flat limit the
Einstein Weitzenböck identity reads \[
 \tfrac12\Delta w^2=|\nabla W^\pm|^2-18\det W^\pm,
 \qquad\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla.                   \tag{8}
\] For a real symmetric trace-free \(3\times3\) matrix, \[
 |\det W^\pm|\le\frac{w^3}{3\sqrt6}.                      \tag{9}
\] Indeed, with eigenvalues summing to zero, \[
 (\sum\lambda_i^2)^3-54(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3)^2
       =2\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^2\ge0.
\]

ALE asymptotics of a positive order \(\tau\) suffice for exhaustion:
\(W=O(r^{-2-\tau})\), \(\nabla W=O(r^{-3-\tau})\). Einstein elliptic
regularity gives the derivative asymptotics. Thus \(w\in L^2\cap L^4\),
\(\nabla W\in L^2\), and boundary errors in (8) vanish. Cutoffs and Kato
extend (7) to \(w\). No assertion about the optimal ALE coordinate order
is used. Integration followed by Hölder, Kato and (7) yields \[
 \begin{aligned}
 J:=\int|\nabla W^\pm|^2
 &=18\int\det W^\pm
 \le\sqrt6\int w^3\\
 &\le\sqrt6\,\|w\|_2\|w\|_4^2
 \le\sqrt6\,S_0\|w\|_2J.
 \end{aligned}                                          \tag{10}
\] If \(\|w\|_2^2<\kappa_0\), the last coefficient is less than one, so
\(J=0\). A parallel \(L^2\) tensor on this infinite-volume connected
manifold vanishes. This proves (6). \(\square\)

The Sobolev estimate is on an \textbf{exact Ricci-flat blowup} with a
controlled volume ratio. It does not add a hidden uniform Sobolev
assumption to the earlier unrestricted monopole integral-gap theorem.

\hypertarget{topology-forces-both-halves-to-concentrate-together}{%
\subsection{3. Topology forces both halves to concentrate
together}\label{topology-forces-both-halves-to-concentrate-together}}

Suppose a sequence satisfying (H) and (3) has minimum harmonic radius
\(\rho_j\to0\), attained at \(x_j\). The harmonic-radius argument of the
preceding continuation gives a complete smooth nonflat Ricci-flat limit
\[
 (M_j,\rho_j^{-2}g_j,x_j)\longrightarrow
                  (X,g_\infty,x_\infty).                 \tag{11}
\] Here are the necessary inputs, rather than an implicit invocation of
smooth almost-Einstein compactness.

Relative Bishop comparison gives, at every centre and for \(r\le D+1\),
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}B_r(x)
 \ge \frac{vV_{-K/3}(r)}{V_{-K/3}(D+1)}
 \ge\theta_0\omega_4r^4.                                 \tag{12}
\] Every fixed-radius ball in the rescaled limit obeys this Euclidean
lower bound, so its asymptotic volume ratio is at least \(\theta_0\).
Ricci tends uniformly to zero after rescaling. The normalized
harmonic-radius lower bound gives \(C^1\) charts; their elliptic metric
equation gives strong \(W^{2,p}\) convergence for every finite \(p\),
then a smooth Ricci-flat limit. Strong \(C^{1,\alpha}\) recovery makes
the limiting harmonic-radius normalization nontrivial, excluding a flat
limit. No derivative of the original Ricci error is estimated.

Jiang--Naber's local full \(L^2\) curvature estimate, summed over the
bounded global covering provided by (3), bounds the scale-invariant full
energy. The bound passes to (11). Finite energy and Euclidean growth
give ALE structure by the Ricci-flat asymptotic theorem. Only a positive
ALE order is needed here.

The geometric convergence embeds every large smooth compact core of
\(X\) in \(M_j\). If \(W^+_\infty=0\), the half-flat compact-core
exclusion contradicts that embedding. If \(W^-_\infty=0\), the same
argument applies with reversed orientation. Therefore both halves of
this \textbf{same} blowup are nonzero, and Lemma C gives \[
              \int_X|W^+|^2\ge\kappa_0,\qquad
              \int_X|W^-|^2\ge\kappa_0.                  \tag{13}
\] For every fixed \(r>0\), strong local curvature convergence and
exhaustion in the rescaled radius imply \[
 \liminf_j\int_{B_r(x_j)}|W^+_{g_j}|^2\ge\kappa_0,\qquad
 \liminf_j\int_{B_r(x_j)}|W^-_{g_j}|^2\ge\kappa_0.          \tag{14}
\] To see this, a fixed radius-\(R\) ball in (11) is eventually
contained in the unscaled radius-\(r\) ball. First let \(j\to\infty\),
then \(R\to\infty\).

\textbf{Paired-concentration alternative.} In the bounded Ricci,
diameter and noncollapse regime (3), harmonic-radius degeneration on the
topological class (H) forces a definite critical amount of \textbf{both}
Weyl energies at a common shrinking location, in the precise sense (14),
after a subsequence.

This is stronger than saying that one half must concentrate. It does not
assert the existence of non-half-flat Ricci-flat ALE spaces or assume
their classification. It identifies the curvature required of any
surviving bubble. If such bubbles are excluded by additional arguments,
compactness strengthens further.

\hypertarget{proofs-of-compactness-and-realization}{%
\subsection{4. Proofs of compactness and
realization}\label{proofs-of-compactness-and-realization}}

\textbf{Proof of Theorem A.} If the harmonic radii were not uniformly
positive, apply (14) with \(r=r_0\). Both energies at \(x_j\) eventually
exceed \(\kappa_0/2\), contradicting (4) and \(\epsilon_0=\kappa_0/4\).
No globally consistent choice of chirality in (4) is needed. The
controlled harmonic atlases give the stated compactness and smooth-type
finiteness exactly as in the preceding continuation. \(\square\)

\textbf{Proof of Theorem B.} Failure would give input metrics with
\(\delta_-(g_j)\to0\), the fixed bounds and (4), but no normalized
negative Einstein metric on their smooth manifolds. For large \(j\) the
Ricci bound (3) holds with \(K=2\). Theorem A gives a subsequence
identified with a fixed smooth manifold, and harmonic-coordinate
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) convergence. Pass to \[
 g^{ab}\partial_a\partial_bg_{ij}
    =-2\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}+Q_{ij}(g,\partial g).
\] The limiting metric weakly solves \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\). Elliptic
bootstrapping makes it smooth. Transporting this metric through the
smooth identifications supplies the forbidden Einstein metric on each
late original manifold. \(\square\)

An actual monopole projection budget \((a_g^+)^2\ge A_0>0\) can replace
the lower volume hypothesis: if \(\delta_-\le1/2\), the scalar estimate
gives \(V\ge8\pi^2A_0/9\). Substitute this \(v\) into (2). This
observation does not delete actual solvability or square costs from
other theorems.

\hypertarget{increment-and-the-remaining-original-problem-gap}{%
\subsection{5. Increment and the remaining original-problem
gap}\label{increment-and-the-remaining-original-problem-gap}}

The preceding \(L^q\), \(q>2\), hypothesis and uniform critical
equiintegrability each imply (4) at a common sufficiently small radius.
Conversely, (4) does not itself impose uniform vanishing of small-ball
energy: it permits a fixed nonzero subthreshold amount. No actual new
geometric family is claimed merely from this measure-theoretic
weakening.

The variable-chirality form is useful when global control of one fixed
half is unavailable but the two energies have different spatial
distributions. It provides a mathematically precise potential role for
chiral information in existence, rather than just another global
curvature coefficient.

For a hypothetical negative-side Yau26 counterexample sequence in (H),
with bounded diameter and a uniform positive actual projection budget,
harmonic radii must degenerate along a subsequence; otherwise the
smooth-type realization argument produces Einstein metrics. Consequently
(14) holds at suitable centres: the remaining compact-regime escape must
spend critical energy in \textbf{both} halves at the same location.

Diameter escape, collapse without a positive volume budget, and paired
concentration above threshold remain possible. No dimension-only Yau26
threshold is proved. Sobolev and Weyl gap estimates are classical
ingredients; the increment is their explicit composition with the
topological core exclusion to obtain critical, locally
variable-chirality compactness and same-type realization. No exhaustive
publication-priority claim is made.
