\section{Scope and corrected quantifier}

Fix a critical Ishida--LeBrun four-manifold and suppose, for contradiction,
that
\[
 \delta_i=\|g_i^{-1}(\Ric_{g_i}+g_i)\|_{L^\infty,\mathrm{op}}\to0.
\]
The global near-equality analysis supplies a collection of nonnegative
curvature/monopole deficits.  Let $F_i$ denote a scalar package containing at
least the Ricci, scalar--Weyl completion, and algebraic lowest-eigenvalue
terms needed for the forbidden-germ passage; its precise monopole components
are those in the Step--8 and Codex notes.  Its integral tends to zero, and
maximal-function selection gives a set of fixed positive volume on which,
for every $0<r\le1$,
\[
 \fint_{B_r(p)}|\Rm_i|^2\le K,
 \qquad \fint_{B_r(p)}F_i\le\varepsilon_i\to0.       \tag{1}
\]
Here and below the curvature norm convention must be fixed; changing between
the curvature-operator and four-index tensor conventions only changes $K$.

The covering domain is part of the geometric choice.  The universal cover of
a large ball, restricted to a smaller sub-ball, is generally not the
universal cover of that sub-ball.  If a short fiber contracts only after a
larger core is included, a smaller buffered domain can still unwrap it.  The
correct fixed-scale question is therefore whether, for some radius bounded
below in terms of $K$, one can first choose a buffered domain and then take a
normal cover which is noncollapsed and has positive harmonic radius.

\section{Taub--NUT--AdS: exact correction}

For
\[
 g_\nu={dr^2\over F_\nu}+(r^2-\nu^2)d\Omega_2^2
 +4\nu^2F_\nu(d\psi+\cos\theta\,d\phi)^2,
\]
\[
 F_\nu={r-\nu\over r+\nu}
 \left(1+{(r-\nu)(r+3\nu)\over3}\right),
\]
the symbolic computation in
\texttt{workspace/scripts/compute\_taub\_nut\_ads\_curvature.py} gives
\[
 \Ric=-g,
 \qquad
 |W|_{(0,4)}^2={32\nu^2(4\nu^2-3)^2\over3(r+\nu)^6}.             \tag{2}
\]
In the coframe orientation used by that script,
\[
 W^+=0,
\]
while
\[
 \Spec(W^-)=\left(
 {2\nu(4\nu^2-3)\over3(r+\nu)^3},
 {2\nu(4\nu^2-3)\over3(r+\nu)^3},
 {-4\nu(4\nu^2-3)\over3(r+\nu)^3}
 \right).                                                       \tag{3}
\]
Thus the family is half-conformally flat, as in the primary Taub--NUT--AdS
source \cite{akbar2002classical}.  Reversing orientation swaps the two
halves.  The four-index norm is four times the Hilbert--Schmidt norm of the
nonzero Weyl block.

At the NUT,
\[
 |W|_{(0,4)}^2={ (4\nu^2-3)^2\over6\nu^4}
 \sim{3\over2\nu^4}.                                          \tag{4}
\]
The NUT is smooth for each $\nu$, but its curvature is not uniform as
$\nu\to0$.  The complete-orbit radial volume density is
\[
 {dV\over dr}=32\pi^2\nu(r^2-\nu^2),                            \tag{5}
\]
and the cumulative full-Weyl energy is
\[
 E_\nu^W(R)={32\pi^2(4\nu^2-3)^2\over9}
 { (R-\nu)^2(R^2+6R\nu+17\nu^2)\over(R+\nu)^4}.                 \tag{6}
\]
Consequently $E_\nu^W(\infty)\to32\pi^2$.  On a fixed physical
neighborhood reaching the NUT, volume is $O(\nu)$ but Weyl energy tends to a
positive constant, so its mean curvature energy diverges like $\nu^{-1}$.
On the NUT scale it diverges like $\nu^{-4}$.  This corrects the Step--9
statement that the all-radius curvature maximal function stayed bounded.

Let $p_\nu$ have distance $1/2$ from the NUT.  For radii at most $1/3$, its
balls lie in a fixed annulus; there $|W|^2=O(\nu^2)$ and their volumes are
$\Theta(\nu)$.  If instead a ball radius exceeds $1/2$, it sees the NUT core
and its curvature mean diverges.  Moreover:
\begin{itemize}
\item the universal cover of a large domain containing the NUT does not
unwrap the short Hopf circle, because that circle contracts through the NUT;
\item if one first chooses a fixed buffered radius $R_0<1/2$ around $p_\nu$,
the domain misses the NUT, lies over a contractible annular base patch, and
its universal cover unwraps the circle at a fixed scale.
\end{itemize}
Hence Taub--NUT--AdS refutes neither the adjustable-buffer covering question
nor the simultaneous input (1).

On the unwrapped annulus, with $z=2\nu\psi$,
\[
 g_\nu\to {dr^2\over1+r^2/3}+r^2d\Omega_2^2+(1+r^2/3)dz^2,
                                                                    \tag{7}
\]
which is real hyperbolic four-space of sectional curvature $-1/3$.  Thus
$s=-4$, $W^+=0$, and
\[
 (s+8\sqrt6|W^+|)^2=16.                                       \tag{8}
\]
The standard NUT profile cannot occur on the calibrated good set.  Near its
core curvature is too large for (1); on fixed annuli its scalar--Weyl deficit
is macroscopically positive; the calibrated Weyl magnitude occurs only in a
transition shell of radius comparable to $\nu^{1/3}$ and not on all centered
scales.

\section{Homothetic scaling of the forbidden germ}

If $\widehat g=a^2g$, then the $(0,2)$ Ricci tensor and the $(1,3)$ Weyl
tensor are unchanged, but the corresponding endomorphisms scale by $a^{-2}$:
\[
 \widehat g^{-1}\Ric_{\widehat g}=a^{-2}g^{-1}\Ric_g,
 \qquad W^\pm_{\widehat g}=a^{-2}W^\pm_g.                       \tag{9}
\]
Therefore the calibrated germ at negative Einstein scale $\kappa>0$ is
\[
 \Ric=-\kappa g,
 \qquad
 \Spec(W^+)=\left(-{\kappa\over6},{\kappa\over12},{\kappa\over12}\right).
                                                                    \tag{10}
\]
The constant-spectrum projector identity gives $\nabla W^+=0$, while the
Einstein--Weyl identity requires
\[
 |\nabla W^+|^2={\kappa^3\over16}>0.                            \tag{11}
\]
Thus the contradiction survives every fixed rescaling.  It degenerates only
when the available covering radius tends to zero and hence $\kappa\to0$.
The Step--9 phrase that the numerical spectrum was invariant under homothety
is withdrawn.

\section{The corrected fixed-scale accessibility target}

For a buffered scale $\rho$ and constants $A>1$, $0<\eta<1$, define
\[
 \Gamma(p;\rho,A,\eta)=\Image\left[
 \pi_1(B_{\eta\rho}(p))\longrightarrow\pi_1(B_{A\rho}(p))
 \right].                                                       \tag{12}
\]
Changing $A\rho$ changes the group: enlarging the domain can kill a short
fiber by admitting a contraction core.  Naber--Zhang's result
\cite{naber2016topology}, and the related buffered normal-cover statement in
\cite{huang2020ricci}, show that a low-dimensional GH model, nondegenerate
model scale, and maximal rank of (12) yield cover noncollapse and harmonic
radius at a definite smaller scale.  Combined with normal-cover mean
transport, (1), and (10)--(11), any positive-volume set with this package is
excluded.

A theorem of the following strength would settle this fixed-topology negative
branch:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Calibrated fixed-scale cover theorem.}
For each finite $K$, there are fixed buffer and regularity constants such
that every sequence satisfying (1) contains, at a good point, a scale
$\rho\ge r_*(K)>0$ and a buffered normal cover chosen after fixing
$B_{A\rho}(p)$, whose lifted smaller ball has a uniform four-dimensional
volume ratio and harmonic radius.  The mean-transfer radius must fit inside
the chosen buffer (for example a $3a$ core as in Step 8).

\medskip
Equivalently, one may prove a multiscale rank-recovery statement: persistent
rank loss for every $\rho\in[r_*(K),1]$ forces either a positive centered
mean of $F$, model-scale degeneration, or a quantified curvature/topology
cost.  Taub--NUT supplies only a single threshold at the distance to its core;
below that threshold rank recovers.  A genuine countermodel must arrange a
nested or diffuse family of contraction/twist mechanisms at every fixed
scale while maintaining the calibrated Weyl profile and (1).

\section{Diffuse Pontryagin density}

Let $E=\Lambda^2_+M$ and let $A_+^{LC}$ be its induced $SO(3)$ connection.
The Chern--Weil representative in our convention is
\[
 \rho_+={1\over4\pi^2}\left(
 {s^2\over24}+2|W^+|^2-{1\over2}|\Ric^\circ|^2
 \right),
 \qquad \int_M\rho_+\,d\mu=2\chi+3\tau={2A\over3}.             \tag{13}
\]
The calibrated profile gives the stronger localizable fact
\[
 \left\|\rho_+-{3\over16\pi^2}\right\|_{L^1}\to0.             \tag{14}
\]
Consequently, for every measurable $U_i$,
\[
 \int_{U_i}\rho_{+,i}\,d\mu_i
 ={3\over16\pi^2}\Vol(U_i)+o(1),                               \tag{15}
\]
where the same global $o(1)$ bounds the error for every $U_i$.  Thus
characteristic mass follows volume.  It cannot be assigned only to
vanishing-volume NUT cores, and counting integer local charges is not the
correct first move.

On a regular collapse region choose a basic or split comparison connection
$B$ and put $a=A_+^{LC}-B$, $A_t=B+ta$.  For the normalized quadratic
invariant polynomial $P$,
\[
 P(F_A)-P(F_B)=dT_P(A,B),                                      \tag{16}
\]
\[
 T_P(A,B)=2P(a,F_B)+P(a,d_Ba)+{1\over3}P(a,[a,a]).              \tag{17}
\]
If $B$ is pulled back from a base of dimension at most three,
$P(F_B)=0$ as a four-form.  Hence on $U$,
\[
 \int_U\rho_+\,d\mu=\int_{\partial U}T_P(A,B).                \tag{18}
\]
This is a continuous Chern--Simons flux, not a local integer without extra
boundary data.  Equations (15) and (18) show that a positive-volume collapsed
bulk must support persistent non-basic connection data or persistent flux
through its interfaces.

For an invariant rank-one circle collapse write, using a unit vertical
one-form $\eta$,
\[
 F_A=F_H+\eta\wedge\Xi,
 \qquad \Xi=\iota_VF_A.                                       \tag{19}
\]
Horizontal dimension is three, so
\[
 P(F_A)=2\eta\wedge P(\Xi,F_H).                                \tag{20}
\]
In a standard inner-product normalization this gives the robust inequality
\[
 \left|\int_U\rho_+\,d\mu\right|^2
 \le C_P\left(\int_U|F_H|^2\right)
          \left(\int_U|\Xi|^2\right).                          \tag{21}
\]
If the horizontal curvature mean is bounded by $K$, (15) yields
\[
 \fint_U|\Xi|^2\ge c(K)-o(1).                                 \tag{22}
\]
Thus the calibrated bulk cannot be basic: mixed vertical--horizontal
curvature survives at order one in mean.

This still does not imply that ordinary fiber holonomy is far from the
identity.  If the fiber length is $\ell$ and
$A=A_H+\Phi\vartheta$ with $\eta=\ell\vartheta$, then schematically
\[
 \Xi=\ell^{-1}D_H\Phi+\mathcal E_{\rm O'Neill}.                 \tag{23}
\]
The ordinary holonomy may vary only by $O(\ell)$ while its scaled logarithm
has order-one derivative.  Therefore diffuse Pontryagin mass can in principle
be carried throughout a collapsed bulk by scaled holonomy, O'Neill tensors,
nil commutators, or vertical frame twisting.  It does not by itself prove
fiber survival in the buffered fundamental group.

The next analytic object is an estimate decomposing the mixed curvature into
fixed-scale accessibility and contraction/twist costs,
\[
 \int_U|\Xi|^2
 \le C\big(E_{\rm accessible}+E_{\rm fill}
                 +E_{\rm O'Neill/nil}\big)+o(1),               \tag{24}
\]
with $E_{\rm fill}$ measuring curvature needed to contract short fibers in a
buffered domain.  Combined with (22), such an estimate would force a positive
volume of fixed-scale accessible points unless the filling/twisting terms
remain diffuse.  The alternative is equally informative: a counterexample
must solve a reduced mixed-curvature equation with diffuse Chern--Simons flux,
not merely attach finitely many NUT caps.

\section{Implications beyond the negative test family}

This step does not settle either normalized branch.  It repairs a false
route exclusion and identifies a sharper common collapse question.  The
positive branch also cannot derive pointwise curvature control from a
$C^0$ Ricci defect: the opposite-warping Calder\'on--Zygmund spike constructed
by S0910A02 keeps the Ricci defect small while its mixed Riemann curvature
spikes.  Thus both signs require harmonic-coordinate/cover methods,
$L^p$ control, smoothing, or weak test-field identities rather than a direct
$L^\infty$ Ricci-to-curvature implication.

Finally, the logical reduction is
\[
 Q_n\Longleftrightarrow P_n^+\lor P_n^-.
\]
To refute $Q_n$ one must refute both normalized branches in the same
dimension, but the two witnessing manifolds or sequences need not be the
same.  Earlier notes requiring a single manifold to realize both failures
imposed an unnecessary strengthening and are superseded on this point.
