The first result is a precise closure of the proposed harmonic-form alignment mechanism. The Strategist suggested that on a positive Einstein 4-manifold with b_+ = 3, three independent harmonic self-dual forms should squeeze W^+ through a summed Weitzenböck inequality. I source-pinned the relevant statements (Wu/LeBrun Weitzenböck formula line 141, Proposition 2.2 line 181, Gursky positive-Yamabe bound lines 88--102, Gursky--LeBrun positive-Einstein bound lines 206--224) and proved the mechanism fails for three independent structural reasons: (i) all classical estimates (Gursky, Gursky--LeBrun, Bishop) give LOWER bounds on ∫|W^+|^2, while CGB gives an upper bound of 32π^2; the resulting range [24π^2, 32π^2] is nonempty and unconstrained; (ii) three-lump concentration IS realizable (each harmonic form concentrates on one connect-sum piece with exponential decay through necks, consistent with Aronszajn unique continuation); (iii) b_+ ≥ 2 pushes det W^+ < 0, precisely the complement of Wu's det W^+ > 0 hypothesis that forces conformally Kähler structure. The known-example pattern min(b_+,b_-) ≤ 1 is fully explained by conformally Kähler classification, not by a general theorem. The frontier statement is symmetric: P_4^+ requires positive Einstein metrics with min(b_+,b_-) ≥ 2 that are non-conformally-Kähler in both orientations; no known construction produces them and no known obstruction excludes them.

The second result is the transition-scale theorem, which opens the collapse front by providing the controlled-annulus ingredient Step 75 identified as missing. The theorem uses Bishop--Gromov monotonicity: for collapsing near-Einstein sequences with Ric ≥ (n-1)κg and Vol → 0, the transition scale ρ_ε(x) = sup{r : Θ(x,r) ≥ ε} satisfies ρ_i → 0, and the rescaled sequence at the maximal point has unit-ball volume ratio ≈ ε (noncollapsed) and all-larger-scale ratio ≤ ε (collapsed). TTCS verification found and corrected a material error: part (c) (Ric → 0) requires TWO-SIDED Ricci control (available in the near-Einstein case but not from the one-sided hypothesis alone); the counterexample is shrinking round spheres. The part (b) bound was also strengthened: V_{n,κ}(t) ≤ ω_n t^n for κ > 0, so the ratio is ≤ ε exactly, not ≤ ε(1+O(ρ^2)).

Combining the transition theorem with the Hitchin--Nakajima CGB-ALE formula ∫|W|^2 = 8π^2(χ - 1/|Γ|) yields the ALE exclusion: for χ = 8, the energy budget 64π^2 forces rank ≤ 7 for any outermost hyper-Kähler ALE, giving max |Γ| = 48 (E_7, energy 63.83π^2). E_8 doesn't fit (71.93π^2). For ε < 1/48, no Kronheimer ADE ALE can serve as the outermost model. TTCS Branch 2 found a powerful chiral refinement: hyper-Kähler ALE has W^- = 0, so all energy is in one chirality; for the balanced τ=0 rows where each chirality budget is 32π^2, the bound becomes rank ≤ 3, |Γ| ≤ 4 (A_3), a 12x improvement. For ε < 1/4, no hyper-Kähler ALE outermost model on balanced rows.

The TTCS verification also correctly identified that non-hyper-Kähler Ricci-flat ALE 4-manifolds (Γ ⊂ SO(4) \ SU(2)) are not excluded by the ADE table; the CGB formula applies to all ALE, but the χ-|Γ| relation is type-dependent. Closing this gap requires either a holonomy reduction theorem (ALE Ricci-flat 4-manifolds with suitable decay have hol ⊂ SU(2)) or a direct topological bound. The conclusion 'outermost must be ALF/ALG/ALH' was corrected to 'outermost cannot be Kronheimer hyper-Kähler ALE' with the non-ADE gap explicitly preserved.

These are complementary to the collapse front but not directly load-bearing for this step's results.
