This Explore/Preceptor step critically consolidated Step 26’s positive-collapse Heisenberg algebra and found that the two relative-rate inequalities are stronger than the prior handoff emphasized. The exact Yau-26 target remains unchanged and unresolved: no proof or strict refutation of the full all-dimensional Ricci-pinching-to-Einstein-existence proposition has been obtained, and the Lean work here verifies only a real-algebra kernel, not Ricci geometry or the final theorem. The justified contribution is a model-level positive-branch theorem for the diagonal cohomogeneity-one Heisenberg nilmanifold ansatz.

I read the Step-26 note, data, TTCS integration, and both branch records. The branch records already confirmed the moving-frame Ricci formulas, the Hamiltonian constraint, the exact relative-rate inequalities, and the correct source scope for Kapovitch--Wilking/Naber--Zhang/Rong. I then restored the Einstein constant \(\Lambda\) in the diagonal \(H_3\) model. For
\[g=dt^2+a(t)^2(\theta_1^2+\theta_2^2)+c(t)^2\theta_3^2,\qquad b=c/a^2,\quad k_1=a'/a,\quad k_3=c'/c,\]
with \([E_1,E_2]=E_3\), the vertical equations for \(\operatorname{Ric}=\Lambda g\) give
\[k_1'=-\Lambda-\frac{b^2}{2}-(2k_1+k_3)k_1,\qquad k_3'=-\Lambda+\frac{b^2}{2}-(2k_1+k_3)k_3,\qquad (\log b)'=k_3-2k_1.\]
The remaining \(00\)-equation is
\[C_\Lambda=b^2+4k_1^2+8k_1k_3+4\Lambda=0,\]
with \(C_\Lambda'=-2(2k_1+k_3)C_\Lambda\). The two decisive identities are
\[2|T|^2-(b^2+4\Lambda)=2(2k_1+k_3)^2-C_\Lambda,\]
and
\[4((\log b)')^2-5(b^2+4\Lambda)=4(3k_1+k_3)^2-5C_\Lambda.\]
Thus on the exact constraint surface,
\[|T|^2\ge\frac{b^2+4\Lambda}{2},\qquad |(\log b)'|^2\ge\frac54(b^2+4\Lambda).\]
For componentwise pinching \(|\rho_i-\Lambda|\le\delta\), the eliminated relation \(S=b^2+4k_1^2+8k_1k_3=2\rho_0-4\rho_1-2\rho_3\) gives the robust loss
\[|T|^2\ge\frac{b^2+4\Lambda-8\delta}{2},\qquad |(\log b)'|^2\ge\frac54(b^2+4\Lambda-8\delta).\]
In particular, for \(\Lambda>0\) and \(\delta<\Lambda/2\), the normalized bracket has no critical point anywhere in the regular model. This is the actual quantitative abelian-forcing statement in the codimension-one diagonal \(H_3\) model: persistent ungraded nonabelian bracket plateaux are impossible in the positive branch. Large bracket, if it appears locally, is a thin high-curvature shell because \(|d(1/b)/dt|\ge\sqrt5/2\) in the exact nonnegative case and \(K_{12}=-3b^2/4-k_1^2\).

Restoring \(\Lambda\) also explains the sign asymmetry. The complex-hyperbolic horosphere data \(\Lambda=-3/2, b=1, k_1=-1/2, k_3=-1\) satisfy \(C_\Lambda=0\), \((\log b)'=0\), and \(k_1'=k_3'=0\), with sectional curvatures \(-1\) and \(-1/4\) in the expected pattern. Thus stable nonabelian nilpotent horosphere geometry is compatible with negative Einstein geometry precisely because \(b^2+4\Lambda<0\) makes the positive lower bounds vacuous. This is a useful conceptual link to the session’s repeated negative-branch obstructions: CH-type cusp/tube geometry naturally permits bracket plateaux, whereas positive Einstein constant forbids them in this model.

I then checked how much closed-model nonexistence is truly justified. Periodic regular orbit space is excluded immediately because a positive periodic \(C^1\) function \(b\) must have a critical point, contradicting \((\log b)'\ne0\). For compact interval regular orbit space, I first wrote a cautious review note saying ordinary central-bolt closures are excluded but a full endpoint classification was still missing. I then added the endpoint classification argument under ordinary smooth tubular-end hypotheses: a boundary hypersurface equal to a Heisenberg nilmanifold cannot be the normal sphere bundle of a point or circle stratum; for a two-dimensional stratum it is a circle bundle over a closed surface, and the circle fiber subgroup must be central in the Heisenberg group. The quotient is \(\mathbb Z^2\), so the bolt is a torus and the collapsing circle is the central one. Hence \(c\to0\), \(a\to a_0>0\), and \(b=c/a^2\to0\) at both finite ordinary endpoints, forcing an interior maximum of \(b\), again impossible. This upgrades the model result to: under diagonal \(H_3\), one-dimensional base, no twist, and ordinary smooth tubular endpoints, no compact closed positive-pinched model exists for \(\delta<\Lambda/2\). I deliberately did not claim coverage of orbifold/edge/nonordinary singular ends, twist-active higher-base models, or arbitrary infranil collapse.

Because this session has already suffered several algebraic/sign overclaims, I machine-checked the surviving algebra. I wrote `Sessions/S0910A02/workspace/proofs/Step27H3Algebra.lean`, formalizing the two exact identities, exact lower bounds, pinched \(8\delta\)-loss bounds, and nonvanishing of the relative bracket rate in the positive pinched regime. `lean_check` accepted the file with no source axioms or placeholders. The stricter `qiushi-yau26 lean` dependency checks succeeded for `Yau26Step27.both_lower_pinched_from_rho_bounds` and `Yau26Step27.rate_ne_zero_pinched_from_rho_bounds`, with only permitted Mathlib axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`; certificates are `workspace/proofs/step27_h3_algebra_both_check_v2.json` and `workspace/proofs/step27_h3_algebra_rate_ne_zero_pinched_check.json`. This is genuine Lean verification of the real-algebra kernel, while the moving-frame Ricci derivation, endpoint topology, and smooth polar behavior remain ordinary mathematical arguments supported by Python symbolic computation and Step-26 TTCS verification, not Lean-formalized geometry.

The research picture after this step is sharper. Step 6 obstructs a special four-dimensional constant-length free-circle positive collapse; Step 24 shows higher-dimensional constant-length circle collapse exists and is Einstein-compatible; Steps 26--27 now exclude the simplest closed diagonal nonabelian \(H_3\) positive-collapse model and identify the negative CH witness.
