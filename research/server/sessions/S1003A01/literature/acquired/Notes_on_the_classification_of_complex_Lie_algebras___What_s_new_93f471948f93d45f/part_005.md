[Part 5/6 of: Notes on the classification of complex Lie algebras | What's new]

As ![{{\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is closed under the adjoint action of both the ![{X'_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX%27_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and the ![{Y'_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BY%27_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we have ![{[{\mathfrak k},{\mathfrak l}] \subset {\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%5D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Projecting onto ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we see that the projection of ![{{\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is an ideal of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and is hence ![{0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) or ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is simple. As ![{{\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is not contained in ![{0 \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B0+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we see that ![{{\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) surjects onto ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); similarly it surjects onto ![{\tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). An analogous argument shows that the intersection of ![{{\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is either ![{0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) or ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); the latter would force ![{{\mathfrak l} = {\mathfrak g} \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by the surjective projection onto ![{\tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), which was already ruled out. Thus ![{{\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has trivial intersection with ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and similarly with ![{0 \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B0+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and is thus a graph. Such a graph cannot be an ideal of ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so that ![{{\mathfrak k} \neq {\mathfrak g} \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D+%5Cneq+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). As ![{{\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) was a subalgebra that surjected onto both ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we conclude by arguing as before that ![{{\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is also a graph; as ![{{\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a Lie algebra, the graph is that of a Lie algebra isomorphism by the Lie algebra closed graph theorem (see [this previous blog post](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2012/11/20/the-closed-graph-theorem-in-various-categories/)). Since ![{[X'_\alpha,Y'_\alpha] = H_\alpha \oplus H_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5BX%27_%5Calpha%2CY%27_%5Calpha%5D+%3D+H_%5Calpha+%5Coplus+H_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we see that ![{{\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) restricts to the graph of the identity on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and the claim follows. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

> **Remark 40** The above arguments show that every root can be obtained from the maximal root by iteratively subtracting off simple roots (while staying in ![{\Phi \cup \{0\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi+%5Ccup+%5C%7B0%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)), which among other things implies that the maximal root is unique. These facts can also be established directly from the axioms of a root system (or from the classification of root systems), but we will not do so here. By using Theorem [39](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#slae), one can convert graph automorphisms of the Dynkin diagram (e.g. the automorphism sending the ![{A_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) Dynkin diagram to its inverse, or the [triality](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triality) automorphism that rotates the ![{D_4}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BD_4%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) diagram) to automorphisms of the Lie algebra; these are important in the theory of twisted [groups of Lie type](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groups_of_lie_type), and more specifically the [Steinberg groups](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groups_of_lie_type#Steinberg_groups) and [Suzuki-Ree groups](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groups_of_lie_type#Suzuki.E2.80.93Ree_groups), but will not be discussed further here.

> **Remark 41** In a converse direction, once one establishes that in an irreducible root system ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that every root can be obtained from the maximal root by subtracting off simple roots (while staying in ![{\Phi \cup \{0\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi+%5Ccup+%5C%7B0%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)), this shows that any Lie algebra ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) associated to this system is necessarily simple. Indeed, given any non-trivial ideal ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and a non-trivial element ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), one locates a minimal element of ![{\Phi \cup \{0\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi+%5Ccup+%5C%7B0%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in which ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has a non-trivial component, then iteratively applies raising operators to then locate a non-trivial element of the root space of the maximal root in ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); if one then applies lowering operators one recovers all the other root spaces, so that ![{{\mathfrak h}={\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%3D%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Theorem [39](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#slae), when combined with the results from previous sections, already gives Theorem [2](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#class), but without a fully explicit way to determine the Lie algebras ![{A_n,B_n,C_n,D_n,E_6,E_7,E_8,F_4,G_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA_n%2CB_n%2CC_n%2CD_n%2CE_6%2CE_7%2CE_8%2CF_4%2CG_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) listed in that theorem (or even to establish whether these systems exist at all). In the case of the [classical Lie algebras](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Lie_algebra)![{A_n,B_n,C_n,D_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA_n%2CB_n%2CC_n%2CD_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), one can explicitly describe these algebras in terms of the [special linear algebras](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_linear_algebra)![{\mathfrak{sl}_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), [special orthogonal algebras](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group)![{\mathfrak{so}_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bso%7D_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and [symplectic algebras](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_group)![{\mathfrak{sp}_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsp%7D_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), but this does not give too much guidance as to how to explicitly describe the _exceptional Lie algebras_![{E_6,E_7,E_8,F_4,G_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_6%2CE_7%2CE_8%2CF_4%2CG_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). We now turn to the question of how to explicitly describe all the simple Lie algebras in a unified fashion.

Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a simple Lie algebra, with Cartan algebra ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). We view ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as a Hilbert space with the Killing form, and then identify this space with its dual ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Thus for instance the coroot ![{H_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of a root ![{\alpha \in {\mathfrak h}^* \equiv {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A+%5Cequiv+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is now given by the simpler formula

![\displaystyle H_\alpha = \frac{2}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle} \alpha. \ \ \ \ \ (20)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+H_%5Calpha+%3D+%5Cfrac%7B2%7D%7B%5Clangle%5Calpha%2C%5Calpha%5Crangle%7D+%5Calpha.+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2820%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

Let ![{\Phi \subset {\mathfrak h}^* \equiv {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A+%5Cequiv+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the root system, which is irreducible. As described in Section [6](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#root-sec), we have the vector space decomposition

![\displaystyle {\mathfrak g} \equiv {\mathfrak h} \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi} {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Cequiv+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5Coplus+%5Cbigoplus_%7B%5Calpha+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

where the spaces ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are one-dimensional, thus we can choose a generator ![{E_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for each ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), though we have the freedom to multiply each ![{E_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by a complex constant, which we will take advantage of to perform various normalisations. A basis for algebra ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) together with the ![{E_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) then form a basis for ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), known as a _Cartan-Weyl basis_ for this Lie algebra. From [(11)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#gha), [(20)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#half) we have

![\displaystyle [H_\alpha, E_\beta] = A_{\alpha,\beta} E_\beta](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5BH_%5Calpha%2C+E_%5Cbeta%5D+%3D+A_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+E_%5Cbeta&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

where ![{A_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the quantity

![\displaystyle A_{\alpha,\beta} := \frac{2\langle \alpha,\beta\rangle}{\langle \alpha,\alpha\rangle}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+A_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B2%5Clangle+%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%5Crangle%7D%7B%5Clangle+%5Calpha%2C%5Calpha%5Crangle%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

which is always an integer because ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a root system (indeed ![{A_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) takes values in ![{\{ 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3 \}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5C%7B+0%2C+%5Cpm+1%2C+%5Cpm+2%2C+%5Cpm+3+%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and form an interesting matrix known as the [Cartan matrix](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan_matrix)).

As discussed in Section [6](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#root-sec), ![{[E_\alpha, E_{-\alpha}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5BE_%5Calpha%2C+E_%7B-%5Calpha%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a multiple of the coroot ![{H_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); by adjusting ![{E_\alpha,E_{-\alpha}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%5Calpha%2CE_%7B-%5Calpha%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for each pair ![{\{\alpha,-\alpha\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5C%7B%5Calpha%2C-%5Calpha%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) we may normalise things so that

![\displaystyle [E_\alpha, E_{-\alpha}] = H_\alpha \ \ \ \ \ (21)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5BE_%5Calpha%2C+E_%7B-%5Calpha%7D%5D+%3D+H_%5Calpha+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2821%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for all ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (here we use the fact that ![{H_{-\alpha} = -H_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH_%7B-%5Calpha%7D+%3D+-H_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to avoid inconsistency). Next, we see from [(19)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#add) that

![\displaystyle [E_\alpha, E_\beta] = 0](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5BE_%5Calpha%2C+E_%5Cbeta%5D+%3D+0&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

if ![{\alpha+\beta \not \in \Phi \cup \{0\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta+%5Cnot+%5Cin+%5CPhi+%5Ccup+%5C%7B0%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and

![\displaystyle [E_\alpha, E_\beta] = N_{\alpha,\beta} E_{\alpha+\beta} \ \ \ \ \ (22)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5BE_%5Calpha%2C+E_%5Cbeta%5D+%3D+N_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+E_%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta%7D+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2822%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for some complex number ![{N_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BN_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) if ![{\alpha+\beta \in \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By considering the action of ![{E_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on [(16)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#lambada) using Theorem [20](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sl2) one can verify that ![{N_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BN_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-zero; however, its value is not yet fully determined because there is still residual freedom to normalise the ![{E_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Indeed, one has the freedom to multiply ![{E_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by any non-zero complex scalar ![{c_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bc_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as long as ![{c_{-\alpha} = c_\alpha^{-1}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bc_%7B-%5Calpha%7D+%3D+c_%5Calpha%5E%7B-1%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (to preserve the normalisation [(21)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#eaa)), in which case the structure constant ![{N_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BN_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) gets transformed according to the law

![\displaystyle N_{\alpha,\beta} \mapsto \frac{c_\alpha c_\beta}{c_{\alpha+\beta}} N_{\alpha,\beta}. \ \ \ \ \ (23)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+N_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+%5Cmapsto+%5Cfrac%7Bc_%5Calpha+c_%5Cbeta%7D%7Bc_%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta%7D%7D+N_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D.+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2823%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

However, observe that the combined structure constant ![{N_{\alpha,\beta} N_{-\alpha,-\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BN_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+N_%7B-%5Calpha%2C-%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is unchanged by this rescaling. And indeed there is an explicit formula for this quantity:

> **Lemma 42** For any roots ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{\alpha+\beta \in \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), one has
>
> ![\displaystyle N_{\alpha,\beta} N_{-\alpha,-\beta} = (r+1)^2](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+N_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+N_%7B-%5Calpha%2C-%5Cbeta%7D+%3D+%28r%2B1%29%5E2&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)
>
> where ![{\beta-r\alpha,\ldots,\beta,\ldots,\beta+q\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta-r%5Calpha%2C%5Cldots%2C%5Cbeta%2C%5Cldots%2C%5Cbeta%2Bq%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are the string of roots of the form ![{\beta + m\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta+%2B+m%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for integer ![{m}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bm%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

This formula can be confirmed by an explicit computation using Theorem [20](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sl2) (using, say, the standard basis for ![{P_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BP_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to select ![{E_{\beta+m\alpha}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%7B%5Cbeta%2Bm%5Calpha%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), which then fixes ![{E_{-\beta-m\alpha}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%7B-%5Cbeta-m%5Calpha%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by [(21)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#eaa)); we omit the details.

On the other hand, we have the following clever renormalisation trick of Chevalley, exploiting the abstract isomorphism from Theorem [39](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#slae):

> **Lemma 43 (Chevalley normalisation)** There exist choices of ![{E_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that
>
> ![\displaystyle N_{\alpha,\beta} = N_{-\alpha,-\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+N_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+%3D+N_%7B-%5Calpha%2C-%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)
>
> for all roots ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{\alpha+\beta \in \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

_Proof:_ We first select ![{E_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) arbitrarily, then we will have

![\displaystyle N_{\alpha,\beta} = a_{\alpha,\beta} N_{-\alpha,-\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+N_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+%3D+a_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+N_%7B-%5Calpha%2C-%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for some non-zero ![{a_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Ba_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all roots ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The plan is then to locate coefficients ![{c_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bc_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) so that the transformation [(23)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#law) eliminates all of the ![{a_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Ba_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) factors.

To do this, observe that we may identify ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with itself and ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with itself via the negation map ![{x \mapsto -x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cmapsto+-x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\alpha \mapsto -\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha+%5Cmapsto+-%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for ![{\alpha \in \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). From this and Theorem [39](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#slae), we may find a Lie algebra isomorphism ![{\phi: {\mathfrak g} \rightarrow {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cphi%3A+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that maps ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to ![{-x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B-x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and thus maps ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{-\alpha}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B-%5Calpha%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for any root ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). In particular, we have

![\displaystyle \phi( E_\alpha ) = b_\alpha E_{-\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Cphi%28+E_%5Calpha+%29+%3D+b_%5Calpha+E_%7B-%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for some non-zero coefficients ![{b_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bb_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); from [(21)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#eaa) we see in particular that

![\displaystyle b_\alpha b_{-\alpha} = 1. \ \ \ \ \ (24)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+b_%5Calpha+b_%7B-%5Calpha%7D+%3D+1.+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2824%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

If we then apply ![{\phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cphi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to [(22)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#noey), we conclude that

![\displaystyle b_\alpha b_\beta N_{-\alpha,-\beta} = b_{\alpha+\beta} N_{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+b_%5Calpha+b_%5Cbeta+N_%7B-%5Calpha%2C-%5Cbeta%7D+%3D+b_%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta%7D+N_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

when ![{\alpha+\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a root, so that ![{a_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Ba_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) takes the special form

![\displaystyle a_{\alpha,\beta} = \frac{b_\alpha b_\beta}{b_{\alpha+\beta}}.](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+a_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+%3D+%5Cfrac%7Bb_%5Calpha+b_%5Cbeta%7D%7Bb_%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta%7D%7D.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

If we then select ![{c_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bc_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) so that

![\displaystyle c_\alpha = b_\alpha c_{-\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+c_%5Calpha+%3D+b_%5Calpha+c_%7B-%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for all roots ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (this is possible thanks to [(24)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#boi)), then the transformation [(23)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#law) eliminates ![{a_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Ba_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as desired. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

From the above two lemmas, we see that we can select a special Cartan-Weyl basis, known as a [Chevalley basis](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalley_basis), such that

![\displaystyle [E_\alpha, E_\beta] = \pm (r+1) E_{\alpha+\beta} \ \ \ \ \ (25)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5BE_%5Calpha%2C+E_%5Cbeta%5D+%3D+%5Cpm+%28r%2B1%29+E_%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta%7D+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2825%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

whenever ![{\alpha+\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a root; in particular, the structure constants ![{N_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BN_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are all integers, which is a crucial fact when one wishes to construct Lie algebras and [Chevalley groups](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalley_group#Chevalley_groups) over fields of arbitrary characteristic. This comes very close to fully describing the Lie algebra structure associated to a given Dynkin diagram, except that one still has to select the signs ![{\pm}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cpm%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in [(25)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#load) so that one actually gets a Lie algebra (i.e. that the Jacobi identity [(1)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#jacobi) is obeyed). This turns out to be non-trivial; see [this paper of Tits](http://www.ams.org/mathscinet-getitem?mr=214638) for details. (There are other approaches to demonstrate existence of a Lie algebra associated to a given root system; one popular one proceeds using the Chevalley-Serre relations, see e.g. this [text of Serre](http://www.ams.org/mathscinet-getitem?mr=215886). There is still a certain amount of freedom to select the signs, but this ambiguity can be described precisely; see [the book of Carter](http://www.ams.org/mathscinet-getitem?mr=1266626) for details.) Among other things, this construction shows that every root system actually creates a Lie algebra (thus far we have only established uniqueness, not existence), though once one has the classification one could also build a Lie algebra explicitly for each Dynkin diagram by hand (in particular, one can build the simply laced classical Lie algebras ![{A_n, D_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA_n%2C+D_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and the maximal simply laced exceptional algebra ![{E_8}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BE_8%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and construct the remaining Lie algebras by taking fixed points of suitable involutions; see e.g. [these notes of Borcherds](http://math.berkeley.edu/~theojf/LieQuantumGroups.pdf) et al. for this approach).

**— 9\. Appendix: Casimirs and complete reducibility —**

In this appendix we supply a proof of the following fact, used in the proof of Corollary [12](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#pres):

> **Theorem 44 (Weyl’s complete reducibility theorem)** Let ![{{\mathfrak g} \subset {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a simple Lie algebra, and let ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-invariant subspace of ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then there exists a complementary ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-invariant subspace ![{W'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that ![{V = W \oplus W'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV+%3D+W+%5Coplus+W%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Among other things, [Weyl’s complete reducibility theorem](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl's_theorem_on_complete_reducibility) shows that every finite-dimensional linear representation of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) splits into the direct sum of irreducible representations, which explains the terminology. The claim is also true for semisimple Lie algebras ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), but we will only need the simple case here, which allows for some minor simplifications to the argument.

The proof of this theorem requires a variant ![{B: {\mathfrak g} \times {\mathfrak g} \rightarrow {\bf C}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BB%3A+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Ctimes+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of the Killing form associated to ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), defined by the formula

![\displaystyle B(x,y) := \hbox{tr}( xy ), \ \ \ \ \ (26)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+B%28x%2Cy%29+%3A%3D+%5Chbox%7Btr%7D%28+xy+%29%2C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2826%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

and a certain element of ![{{\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) associated to this form known as the [Casimir operator](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_element). We first need to establish a variant of Theorem [1](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#semisimple):

> **Proposition 45** With the hypotheses of Theorem [44](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#weyl-thm), ![{B}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BB%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate.

_Proof:_ This is a routine modification of Proposition [6](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#pr) (one simply omits the use of the adjoint representation). ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

Once one establishes non-degeneracy, one can then define the _Casimir operator_![{C \in {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BC+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by setting

![\displaystyle C:= \sum_{i=1}^n e_i f_i](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+C%3A%3D+%5Csum_%7Bi%3D1%7D%5En+e_i+f_i&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

whenever ![{e_1,\ldots,e_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Be_1%2C%5Cldots%2Ce_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a basis of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{f_1,\ldots,f_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bf_1%2C%5Cldots%2Cf_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is its [dual basis](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_basis), thus ![{B(e_i,f_j) = \delta_{ij}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BB%28e_i%2Cf_j%29+%3D+%5Cdelta_%7Bij%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). It is easy to see that this definition does not depend on the choice of basis, which in turn (by infinitesimally conjugating both bases by an element ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of the algebra ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)) implies that ![{C}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BC%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) commutes with every element ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

On the other hand, ![{C}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BC%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) does not vanish entirely. Indeed, taking traces and using [(26)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#bdef) we see that

![\displaystyle \hbox{tr}(C) = \hbox{dim}({\mathfrak g}). \ \ \ \ \ (27)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Chbox%7Btr%7D%28C%29+%3D+%5Chbox%7Bdim%7D%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29.+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2827%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

This already gives an important special case of Theorem [44](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#weyl-thm):

> **Proposition 46** Theorem [44](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#weyl-thm) is true when ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has codimension one and is irreducible.

_Proof:_ The Lie algebra ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) acts on the one-dimensional space ![{V/W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%2FW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); since ![{{\mathfrak g} = [{\mathfrak g},{\mathfrak g}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%3D+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (from the simplicity hypothesis), we conclude that this action is trivial. In other words, each element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) maps ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so the Casimir operator ![{C}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BC%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) does as well. In particular, the trace of ![{C}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BC%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the same as the trace of ![{C}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BC%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). On the other hand, by Schur’s lemma, ![{C}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BC%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a constant on ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); applying [(27)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#c-trace), we conclude that this constant is non-zero. Thus ![{C}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BC%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate on ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), but is not full rank on ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as it maps ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Thus it must have a one-dimensional null-space ![{W'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) which is complementary to ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). As ![{C}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BC%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) commutes with ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{W'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-invariant, and the claim follows. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

We can then remove the irreducibility hypothesis:

> **Proposition 47** Theorem [44](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#weyl-thm) is true when ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has codimension one.

We remark that this statement is essentially a reformulation of [Whitehead’s lemma](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehead%27s_lemma_(Lie_algebras)).

_Proof:_ We induct on the dimension of ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (or ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)). If ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is irreducible then we are already done, so suppose that ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has a proper invariant subspace ![{U}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then ![{W/U}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%2FU%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has codimension one in ![{V/U}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%2FU%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so by the induction hypothesis ![{W/U}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%2FU%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is complemented by a one-dimensional invariant subspace ![{Y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BY%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{V/U}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%2FU%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), which lifts to an invariant subspace ![{Z}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BZ%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in which ![{U}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has codimension one. By the induction hypothesis again, ![{U}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is complemented by a one-dimensional invariant subspace ![{W'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{Z}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BZ%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and it is then easy to see that ![{W'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) also complements ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and the claim follows. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

Next, we remove the codimension one hypothesis instead:

> **Proposition 48** Theorem [44](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#weyl-thm) is true when ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is irreducible.

_Proof:_ Let ![{A}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the space of linear maps ![{T: V \rightarrow W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BT%3A+V+%5Crightarrow+W%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) whose restriction to ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a constant multiple of the identity, and let ![{B}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BB%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the subalgebra of ![{A}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) whose restriction to ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) vanishes. Then ![{A, B}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA%2C+B%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-invariant (using the Lie bracket action), and ![{B}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BB%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has codimension one in ![{A}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Applying Proposition [47](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#cod) (pushing ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) forward to ![{\mathfrak{gl}(A)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%28A%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and treating the degenerate case when ![{\mathfrak{gl}(A)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%28A%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) vanishes separately) we see that ![{B}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BB%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is complemented by a one-dimensional invariant subspace ![{B'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BB%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{A}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Thus there exist ![{T \in A}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BT+%5Cin+A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that does not lie in ![{B}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BB%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and which commutes with every element of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The kernel ![{W'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{T}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BT%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is then an invariant complement of ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and the claim follows. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

Applying the induction argument used to prove Proposition [47](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#cod), we now obtain Theorem [44](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#weyl-thm) in full generality.
