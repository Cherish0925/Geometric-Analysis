## Deep-Reasoning

This step was an Agent4 verification of Step 12's toric determinant route, and it changed the priority of the positive-branch Execute program. The core mathematical correction is that the determinant identity is stronger than Step 12's Section 8 assumed. For a local T^2-invariant four-metric g = g_B + h_IJ(dphi^I + A^I)(dphi^J + A^J), w = sqrt(det h), Step 12's vertical-trace formula tr_h Ric^V = -Delta_B w / w + |F|_h^2/2 implies, for exact Ric = lambda g, the Schrödinger ground-state equation (-Delta_B + |F|_h^2/2) w = 2 lambda w. Thus if the orbit space has a degeneration boundary, w is a positive Dirichlet ground state, and lambda_1^Dir(Omega,g_B) <= 2 lambda because the potential is nonnegative. Twist therefore does not defeat scale pinning; it enters as a nonnegative potential. For positive C0 pinching |g^{-1}Ric - I|_op <= delta, the same pointwise identity and Rayleigh testing with w give lambda_1^Dir(Omega) <= 2(1+delta) without approximate orthogonal transitivity, derivatives of the Ricci defect, or pointwise curvature regularity. This is the cleanest realization so far of the Step-8 projection-bridge idea: the Killing fields are the smooth test data, and the Ricci error is only paired in operator norm. The sign/factor survived two consistency anchors. First, if lambda <= 0 and a degeneration boundary exists, the positive Dirichlet eigenvalue contradiction matches Bochner: compact Ricci-flat Killing fields are parallel and zero-free, while compact negative Ricci manifolds have no nonzero Killing fields. Second, the normalized round S^4 anchor gives radius sqrt(3), orbit space a quarter S^2(sqrt(3)), w = r1 r2 a degree-2 spherical harmonic with Delta w = -2w, int_Omega w = 6, Vol = 24 pi^2, and the correct boundary flux/fixed-sphere area identity. These checks are saved in workspace/data/step13_toric_spectral_anchors.json. I also made a limited symbolic higher-dimensional check for diagonal twist-free T^k metrics, verifying for k=1,2,3 that tr_h Ric^V = -Delta(prod p_i)/(prod p_i), saved in workspace/data/step13_tk_trace_diagonal_symbolic.json; the arbitrary twisted non-diagonal T^k formula remains only a plausible extension until proved cleanly.

The source situation improved substantially. The Knowledge object for arXiv:2311.04840, Toric Einstein 4-manifolds with non-negative sectional curvature, contains source-scoped exact toric Einstein inputs stronger than memory alone: lines 84-87 state the Orlik-Raymond polygonal quotient picture with principal stratum, edges, vertices, right angles and geodesic edges for invariant metrics; lines 181-210 prove that a T^2 action on a simply connected closed Einstein four-manifold is geometrically polar by showing the twist scalars are constant from the mixed Ricci equations and vanish along singular strata; lines 233-247 state the polar orbit-volume eigenfunction theorem -Delta phi = 2 Lambda phi. Thus exact orthogonal transitivity is a sourced lemma in the simply connected closed Einstein case, not an urgent first construction target. However this source does not prove the C0-pinched approximate case and does not reduce arbitrary simply connected positive-pinched collapse to toric invariant geometry. I also read source excerpts around Page and Chen-LeBrun-Weber: known positive toric/Hermitian Einstein examples include S^4, CP^2, S^2 x S^2, Page, Chen-LeBrun-Weber, and the CP^2#3CPbar Kähler-Einstein metric. Any invariant volume-gap proof must accommodate their non-rectangular rod behavior and must not assume K_B >= 0, constant edge curvature, a rectangular orbit space, or product rods.

The global invariant gap is now much sharper. For a disk-like orbit space with geodesic edges and right-angle corners, two-dimensional Gauss-Bonnet gives int_Omega K_B dA = 2 pi - (pi/2)m, with m the number of vertices/fixed points, usually chi(M) in the toric four-manifold setting. This matches S^4, CP^2, S^2 x S^2, and predicts negative total base curvature for CLW topology. Step 12's edge coefficient a2 is therefore not just vague Weyl freedom: since K_B at the edge equals -a2 and the fixed-sphere edge equation gives Q'' + (1+2a2)Q = 0, the fixed two-sphere has intrinsic curvature K_fixed = 1 + 2a2 = 1 - 2K_B|edge. Invariant volume collapse would force fixed-sphere areas to collapse; Gauss-Bonnet on each fixed sphere then forces large positive intrinsic curvature somewhere along rods, equivalently large positive a2 and large negative base curvature near edges. The remaining proof problem is to combine the spectral bound lambda_1 <= 2(1+delta), primitive unit closure, polygonal corner Gauss-Bonnet, and the weighted four-dimensional Chern-Gauss-Bonnet/Ricci budget into a lower bound for int w, or else to construct a large-a2 reduced solution sequence showing the invariant gap fails. The real weakness is explicit: signed Gauss-Bonnet controls int K_B, not int K_B^-; a proof needs weighted energy or an isoperimetric input controlling how much high negative base curvature can hide where w is small. This invariant route is still only a laboratory, not Proposition A and not Yau26: the general-to-invariant reduction after Step 11's C0 regularity counterexample is a separate major open obligation, likely involving Naber-Zhang accessible covers and group-action limits rather than Cheeger-Fukaya-Gromov pointwise curvature regularity. Peer messages #256-#257 fit this picture on the negative branch: NUT residue counting is being replaced by diffuse twist/anisotropy and fiber survival/death mechanisms, which may later inform the general collapse reduction but do not settle the positive toric gap.
