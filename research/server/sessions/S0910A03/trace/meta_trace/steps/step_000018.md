## Deep-Reasoning

Step 18 repaired the logical order of the four-dimensional negative-branch cutoff-flow program and isolated a sharper analytic mechanism. The Step 17 picture of a completed integral layer followed by one transition regularity problem was too strong. Coupled curvature–defect L² estimates use evolving metric comparability, relative Sobolev inequalities, fixed ball and overlap geometry, cutoff control, and small neighboring energies. They can improve energy bounds inside a simultaneous first-exit argument, but cannot independently produce all those inputs.

The proposed maximum-principle route also fails at the initial slice. Even if one proves |aRm(t)|≤C/t for a=χ², the coefficient in the defect maximum principle has divergent integral ∫₀ᵀdt/t. Beginning at t₀>0 yields a factor (T/t₀)^C, with no sequence-uniform passage to t₀=0 because the initial metrics have only curvature-mean control. Moreover, the covariant Hessian of a evolves with the connection; fixing χ as a scalar function does not keep Hess a bounded. Metric motion must instead be controlled directly through an integrable estimate for the velocity aE.

The main new structural result comes from weighting the Einstein defect before performing higher-power estimates. With E=Ric+g and U=aE, commuting a through the Laplacian cancels the terms a(Δa)E and 2a∇a·∇E exactly. The resulting tensor equation has only Hessian-a, first-gradient, curvature, and lower-order homogeneous terms. Apparent quotients by a are regular because da⊗da/a=4dχ⊗dχ. In a localized tensor Lᵖ calculation, Hessian-a terms can be integrated by parts and absorbed using the full tensor dissipation; scalar Kato reduction alone would lose rotational components of ∇U. The resulting inequality has the form

d/dt ∫η²|U|ᵖ + cₚ∫η²a|∇|U|^(p/2)|² ≤ cutoff terms + Cₚ∫η²a|Rm||U|ᵖ + Cₚ∫η²|U|^(p+1).

On a relative-Sobolev ball, both reactions are estimated through χη|U|^(p/2). In dimension four the product of the relative Sobolev coefficient with the local L² curvature norm is the square root of the normalized critical energy, so collapsed volume cancels. Consequently, for any one fixed p>2, sufficiently small local curvature and defect energies yield conditional propagation of the spatial supercritical norm: the local average of |aE|ᵖ remains bounded by Cₚδᵖ throughout the provisional first-exit interval. This cannot be repeated unchanged as p tends to infinity because the reaction-to-dissipation coefficient grows with p; a fixed energy threshold cannot absorb every stage.

The correct second stage follows Li and Yang: treat curvature as a time-dependent potential. A sufficient transition estimate is S_α⁴∫a|Rm|³≤C/t, where S_α is the norm-form relative Sobolev coefficient; the stronger |aRm|≤C/t plus persistent local L² curvature energy implies it. Starting from the fixed supercritical norm, a time-cylinder iteration would then give |aE(t)|≤Cₚδ(r²/t)^(2/p). Its integral is Cₚ[p/(p−2)]δθ^(1−2/p)r², finite exactly for p>2, and therefore improves metric comparability. The endpoint p=2 reproduces the logarithmic divergence. What remains unresolved is deriving this weighted curvature potential for the degenerate transition operator while simultaneously controlling the connection, Hessian a, finite-neighbor geometry, and the unweighted global defect Q(t)=∫|E|². Weighted pointwise control near a=0 does not imply the latter.

The topology-uniformity argument was also corrected. BMY gives c₁²≤3c₂ and therefore a lower bound in the wrong direction. For each minimal general-type summand, Noether’s formula and inequality instead give c₂≤5c₁²+36. For M=(#X_j)#N with m=2 or 3, the exact critical relation D=A/3 implies χ(N)=A/3+6−4m+6(b₊(N)−b₁(N)). Thus D alone does not bound χ(N), but the standard Ishida–LeBrun hypothesis b₊(N)=0 gives χ(N)≤A/3 and A≥12(m−1). Combining this with connected-sum arithmetic yields χ(M)/A≤65/6 for minimal general-type summands. This supports topology-uniform analytic constants for that subclass if the simultaneous flow theorem is eventually established, but not for arbitrary symplectic summands.

The negative critical family remains only one laboratory for Yau’s full problem. Completing its flow theorem would materially strengthen the four-dimensional negative obstruction, especially because the downstream 7/24 calibration inequality, exponential-pullback averaging, quantitative monopole amplitude/eigenline density, and forbidden calibrated Einstein germ are already available. It would still leave the unrestricted negative branch, the positive branch, dimensions at least five, dimension-only quantifiers across all topologies, and Lean formalization open.
