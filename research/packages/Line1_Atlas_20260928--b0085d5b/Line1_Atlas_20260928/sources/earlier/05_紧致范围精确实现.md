# A compact-regime Einstein realization theorem on aspherical four-manifolds

23 September 2026. Research continuation; standard geometric compactness
inputs are separated from the new application of the compact-core exclusion.
The result concerns the original pointwise Ricci defect, not a replacement
by a volume-normalized integral defect.

## 1. Statement and mathematical increment

For a closed Riemannian four-manifold define
\[
 \delta_-(g)=\|\operatorname{Ric}_g^{\sharp}+I\|_{L^\infty,\mathrm{op}}.
\]
All Weyl norms below are tensor norms; a fixed equivalent curvature-operator
norm changes only constants. An oriented manifold is **aspherical** when
its universal cover is contractible.

**Theorem A (compact-regime realization).** Fix
\(D,v,\Lambda>0\) and \(2<q<\infty\). There exists
\(\varepsilon(D,v,\Lambda,q)>0\) with the following property. If a
closed connected smooth oriented aspherical four-manifold \(M\) carries
a smooth metric \(g\) satisfying
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(M,g)\le D,\qquad
 \operatorname{Vol}(M,g)\ge v,\qquad
 \|W_g^+\|_{L^q(M,g)}\le\Lambda,\qquad
 \delta_-(g)<\varepsilon,
                                                        \tag{1}
\]
then the **same smooth manifold** carries a smooth metric \(h\) with
\[
                         \operatorname{Ric}_h=-h.        \tag{2}
\]
The threshold is uniform across the manifolds satisfying (1). One may
replace \(W^+\) by \(W^-\), by reversing orientation. A pointwise bound
on one Weyl half is a sufficient special case, since volume has a
diameter-dependent upper bound under the Ricci bounds in (1).

No bound on \(\nabla\operatorname{Ric}\), the full pointwise curvature,
Euler characteristic, injectivity radius, or a separately supplied full
curvature energy occurs in the theorem. The volume, diameter, and
supercritical one-sided Weyl assumptions are genuine restrictions. In
particular, the threshold is not dimension-only.

The preceding [aspherical compact-core exclusion and exact no-bubbling
theorem](../mathematics/current/line1_aspherical_no_bubbles.md) used
Einstein regularity after rescaling a maximum of the full curvature.
That proof does **not** apply verbatim to almost-Einstein metrics. The
new argument normalizes a harmonic radius instead. Ricci convergence
in the harmonic-coordinate elliptic equation supplies precisely the
regularity needed for a smooth Einstein limit and for preservation of
the smooth type; derivatives of the Ricci error are never estimated.

## 2. Inputs, with the regularity actually used

We use the following results.

1. The inherited compact-core lemma states that no large compact core
   of a nonflat smooth Ricci-flat anti-self-dual ALE four-manifold
   embeds smoothly into a closed aspherical four-manifold. Its proof
   treats both the negative-definite \(b_2>0\) case and the rational-ball
   \(b_2=0\) case. For the latter, lifting the core to the contractible
   universal cover and applying integral Mayer--Vietoris would give
   \(\mathbb Z_{n^2}\cong\mathbb Z_n\oplus G\), \(|G|=n\), which is
   impossible. The classification inputs are Wright and Suvaina.
2. Uniform harmonic-coordinate radii give pointed geometric
   \(C^{1,\alpha}\) precompactness, with a loss of Hölder exponent
   before using the metric equation. On harmonic charts,
   \[
     g^{ab}\partial_a\partial_b g_{ij}
        =-2\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}+Q_{ij}(g,\partial g), \tag{3}
   \]
   where \(Q\) is quadratic in first derivatives. Bounded Ricci and
   controlled charts give local \(W^{2,p}\) bounds for every finite
   \(p\). Strong convergence of the right side gives strong
   \(W^{2,p}\) convergence on smaller charts. These are the harmonic
   coordinate compactness tools of Anderson; the strong convergence
   step is also discussed explicitly by Zhang--Zhu.
3. Jiang--Naber's local \(L^2\) curvature estimate applies to
   noncollapsed balls with two-sided bounded Ricci curvature, without
   any derivative bound on Ricci. With fixed diameter and positive
   total volume it gives a uniform global \(L^2\) curvature bound in
   dimension four. Section 3 records the covering argument.
4. A complete Ricci-flat four-manifold with Euclidean lower volume
   growth and finite \(L^2\) curvature is ALE, by the
   Bando--Kasue--Nakajima asymptotic theorem, Theorem 1.5; in dimension
   four the resulting ALE order is four. This result is applied
   only to a smooth, complete **exact Ricci-flat limit**, not to the
   original approximate metrics.

These inputs do not include an assertion that an almost-Einstein
sequence converges smoothly. The convergence established below is
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) and strong \(W^{2,p}\) for finite \(p\), after
diffeomorphisms. The limiting metric is smooth by (3).

## 3. Noncollapse and curvature energy from the bounded global regime

Fix a two-sided Ricci bound \(|\operatorname{Ric}|\le K\), diameter
bound \(D\), and volume lower bound \(v\). Set \(D_*=D+1\). Relative
Bishop--Gromov comparison gives, for all \(x\in M\) and
\(0<r\le D_*\),
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}B_r(x)
   \ge v\,\frac{V_{-K/3}(r)}{V_{-K/3}(D_*)}
   \ge c(K,D,v)r^4.                                    \tag{4}
\]
Here \(V_{-K/3}\) denotes the four-dimensional model-ball volume;
using a larger negative comparison constant causes no problem.
The same comparison gives
\(\operatorname{Vol}(M)\le V_{-K/3}(D_*)\).

Choose a fixed small radius \(r_*=r_*(K,D)>0\). A maximal disjoint
collection of \(r_*/4\)-balls has uniformly bounded cardinality by
(4) and the total-volume upper bound. The concentric \(r_*/2\)-balls
cover \(M\). On these balls the rescaled Ricci bound and volume lower
bound meet Jiang--Naber's hypotheses, with constants depending only
on \(K,D,v\). Summing their estimates gives
\[
                 \int_M|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\le E(K,D,v).
                                                               \tag{5}
\]
Thus neither a bound on \(\chi(M)\) nor an additional curvature-energy
assumption is needed. If one elects not to use the Jiang--Naber
theorem, all subsequent statements remain valid with (5) supplied as
an extra hypothesis and constants also depending on that energy bound.

## 4. Half-Weyl control prevents harmonic-radius collapse

**Theorem B (harmonic compactness).** Given
\(K,D,v,\Lambda>0\), \(q>2\), and \(0<\alpha<1\), there is a
positive uniform lower bound for the \(C^{1,\alpha}\) harmonic radius
on every closed connected smooth oriented aspherical four-manifold
with
\[
 |\operatorname{Ric}|\le K,\quad \operatorname{diam}\le D,
 \quad\operatorname{Vol}\ge v,
 \quad\|W^+\|_{L^q}\le\Lambda.                         \tag{6}
\]
Consequently this class is geometrically precompact in
\(C^{1,\beta}\), \(\beta<1\), and weak \(W^{2,p}\), \(p<\infty\),
and has only finitely many diffeomorphism types.

**Proof.** Fix a standard harmonic-radius definition with metric
comparability and scaled \(C^{1,\alpha}\) norm bounded by fixed
constants. Suppose its global minimum \(\rho_j\), attained at
\(x_j\), tends to zero. Write
\[
 \widehat g_j=\rho_j^{-2}g_j,
 \quad r_h(\widehat g_j,x_j)=1,
 \quad r_h(\widehat g_j,x)\ge1\quad\hbox{for all }x.      \tag{7}
\]
The rescaled Ricci tensors satisfy
\[
                |\operatorname{Ric}_{\widehat g_j}|
                     \le K\rho_j^2\longrightarrow0.   \tag{8}
\]
For any fixed \(R\), (4) gives, for large \(j\),
\[
        \operatorname{Vol}_{\widehat g_j}B_R(x_j)
                          \ge c(K,D,v)R^4.              \tag{9}
\]
The lower volume and Ricci bounds ensure that the rescaled diameters
tend to infinity. Indeed, the unscaled diameters have a positive
lower bound from \(v\) and Bishop comparison.

The uniform harmonic charts in (7), bounded numbers of charts on each
fixed-radius ball, and completeness produce pointed
\(C^{1,\beta}\) convergence on compact sets, \(\beta<\alpha\), to a
complete smoothable manifold \((X,g_\infty,x_\infty)\). Initially the
limiting metric has only the indicated low regularity. Equation (3)
and (8) imply distributionally \(\operatorname{Ric}(g_\infty)=0\).
Local elliptic bootstrapping in harmonic coordinates makes
\(g_\infty\) smooth and Ricci-flat.

It is important that convergence can now be improved without
differentiating (8). In compatible harmonic charts, subtract the
limiting version of (3). For \(u_j=g_j-g_\infty\), the right side of
the resulting uniformly elliptic equation is a sum of
\[
 -2\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j},\qquad
 Q(g_j,\partial g_j)-Q(g_\infty,\partial g_\infty),\qquad
 (g_\infty^{ab}-g_j^{ab})\partial_a\partial_b g_\infty.
                                                               \tag{10}
\]
Each tends to zero in \(L^p\) on compact smaller charts for every
finite \(p\), by (8), the initial \(C^1\) convergence, and smoothness
of the limit. Interior elliptic estimates imply
\[
                    g_j\longrightarrow g_\infty
                    \quad\hbox{strongly in }W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}
                    \quad(p<\infty).                   \tag{11}
\]
Choose \(p>4/(1-\alpha)\) to recover strong \(C^{1,\alpha}\)
convergence. Harmonic-coordinate transition maps are controlled by
their own harmonic equations; the usual geometric compactness
construction yields embeddings of compact subsets. More explicitly,
take an open neighbourhood of any chosen smooth compact core of the
limit and a finite harmonic atlas there. Controlled transition maps
give a common \(C^1\) differentiable structure and \(C^1\) embeddings
into the approximating smooth manifolds. A compatible smoothing of
this atlas is unique up to diffeomorphism; \(C^1\) approximation of
each embedding by a smooth map remains an embedding on the compact
core. This preserves its smooth type. No convergence of holomorphic
curves or \(C^2\) convergence of the approximating metrics is used.

The limit is not flat. If it were flat, (9) forces it to be Euclidean
space: a complete flat quotient with an infinite deck group has
sub-Euclidean volume growth, and a finite Euclidean isometry group
acting freely is trivial. Equivalently, the ALE conclusion below also
excludes every nontrivial smooth flat limit with (9). On a fixed
Euclidean ball of radius greater than two there are harmonic charts
with strict margins in all defining inequalities. Strong convergence
in (11), followed by solving the harmonic coordinate Dirichlet problem
on a slightly smaller ball, gives such a chart of radius greater than
one on \((M_j,\widehat g_j,x_j)\). This contradicts (7). This is the
harmonic-radius normalization argument, not an assertion that a
pointwise maximum of curvature survives weak convergence.

Under the rescaling in (7), the half-Weyl norm has the decisive
transformation rule
\[
 \int_{M_j}|W^+_{\widehat g_j}|^q\,d\mu_{\widehat g_j}
       =\rho_j^{2q-4}\int_{M_j}|W^+_{g_j}|^q\,d\mu_{g_j}
       \le\rho_j^{2q-4}\Lambda^q\longrightarrow0.        \tag{12}
\]
By (11), curvature converges strongly in local finite \(L^p\) spaces.
The Hodge star also converges, so (12) implies \(W^+_{g_\infty}=0\).
The full \(L^2\) curvature is scale invariant in dimension four;
(5), (11), and exhaustion imply
\[
                       \int_X|\operatorname{Rm}_{g_\infty}|^2
                              \le E(K,D,v).              \tag{13}
\]
Equations (9), (13), and Ricci-flatness place the complete nonflat
limit within the Bando--Kasue--Nakajima ALE theorem. It is therefore
a smooth nonflat ASD Ricci-flat ALE four-manifold. Its end is unique:
a complete Ricci-flat manifold with two ends splits as a line times
a compact manifold, which is incompatible with (9). The finite
fundamental group, negative-definite intersection form, and the
rational-ball alternatives used by the compact-core lemma are supplied
by Wright's classification; simple connectivity is not assumed.

Choose a sufficiently large compact core \(B\subset X\). The compact
geometric convergence embeds \(B\) into \(M_j\) for large \(j\),
contradicting the inherited aspherical compact-core exclusion. Thus
the assumed convergence \(\rho_j\to0\) is impossible, proving the
uniform harmonic-radius bound.
Standard finite-atlas geometric compactness, using the fixed diameter,
now proves the final assertions of the theorem. \(\square\)

**Endpoint variation.** The same argument permits the \(L^q\) bound
in (6) to be replaced by uniform local equiintegrability of one Weyl
half:
\[
 \sup_{(M,g)}\sup_{x\in M}
       \int_{B_r(x)}|W^+|^2\,d\mu\le\eta(r),
              \qquad \eta(r)\longrightarrow0\ (r\downarrow0).
                                                               \tag{14}
\]
Indeed the integral on every fixed rescaled ball is bounded by
\(\eta(R\rho_j)\to0\). Constants then depend on the common modulus
\(\eta\). A bare bounded critical \(L^2\) norm does **not** supply
(14); the proof makes no assertion at that unrestricted endpoint.

## 5. Proof of exact realization on the same smooth manifold

Suppose Theorem A fails. There are closed aspherical \(M_j\) and
metrics satisfying its fixed \(D,v,\Lambda,q\) bounds such that
\(\delta_-(g_j)\to0\), but no \(M_j\) admits a normalized negative
Einstein metric. Discard finitely many terms so that
\(\delta_-(g_j)\le1/2\). This gives a fixed two-sided Ricci bound.

Theorem B and compact geometric convergence give, after a subsequence,
a fixed closed smooth \(M_\infty\), diffeomorphisms
\(\phi_j:M_\infty\to M_j\), and
\[
                       \phi_j^*g_j\longrightarrow g_\infty
                              \quad\hbox{in }C^{1,\alpha}
                                                               \tag{15}
\]
for every fixed \(\alpha<1\), with weak local \(W^{2,p}\) control.
In these charts,
\[
     \operatorname{Ric}_{\phi_j^*g_j}=-\phi_j^*g_j+e_j,
                         \qquad\|e_j\|_\infty\to0.       \tag{16}
\]
Pass to the harmonic-coordinate equation (3). The strong convergence
of the lower-order terms and (16) imply
\(\operatorname{Ric}_{g_\infty}=-g_\infty\) weakly. Elliptic
bootstrapping makes the limit a smooth negative Einstein metric.
The difference-equation argument (10) gives strong \(W^{2,p}\)
convergence for every finite \(p\), if desired. Transporting
\(g_\infty\) by \(\phi_j\) gives a smooth metric with Ricci equal to
minus itself on **each sufficiently late \(M_j\)**, a contradiction.
This proves Theorem A. \(\square\)

This argument establishes existence, not an effective Newton radius,
uniqueness, or a canonical choice of the output. It also does not
claim \(C^2\), let alone smooth, convergence of the input metrics.
Small, rapidly oscillating Ricci errors cannot be differentiated for
free. Finite-\(p\) harmonic elliptic control is exactly what the
topology-preserving limit argument needs.

## 6. The gauge-theoretic input can supply noncollapse

**Corollary C.** Fix \(D,\Lambda,A_0>0\) and \(q>2\). In Theorem A,
the explicit volume lower bound may be replaced by the following
actual monopole supply: for every allowed input metric, there is an
actual class \(a_g\) for which the scalar-curvature estimate holds and
\[
                           (a_g^+)^2\ge A_0.             \tag{17}
\]
The resulting threshold depends on \(D,\Lambda,q,A_0\).

**Proof.** The actual monopole scalar estimate and \(\delta_-\le1/2\)
give
\[
 32\pi^2 A_0\le\int_M s_-^2\,d\mu
       \le16(1+\delta_-)^2\operatorname{Vol}(M,g).
\]
Consequently
\[
             \operatorname{Vol}(M,g)
                 \ge\frac{2\pi^2A_0}{(1+\delta_-)^2}
                 \ge\frac{8\pi^2A_0}{9}.               \tag{18}
\]
Use this \(v\) in Theorem A. The class is allowed to depend on the
metric. No assertion about an arbitrary formal cohomology vector is
substituted for the actual scalar estimate. \(\square\)

This supplies a genuine bridge between the established gauge budget
and exact Einstein existence: the gauge input is used to prevent
collapse, the one-sided curvature budget determines the possible
blowup orientation, and asphericity excludes the resulting smooth ALE
cores. It is not the false implication “no numerical obstruction,
therefore existence.” The independent geometric hypotheses remain.

## 7. Consequences for Yau26 and the precise remaining escape

Let \(M_j\) be closed oriented aspherical four-manifolds with no
Einstein metric, and suppose \(\delta_-(g_j)\to0\). For every fixed
\(q>2\), the following three properties cannot all hold uniformly:
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(g_j)\le D,
 \qquad\operatorname{Vol}(g_j)\ge v>0,
 \qquad\|W^+_{g_j}\|_{L^q}\le\Lambda.                  \tag{19}
\]
If the actual positive projection budget (17) is uniform, the volume
condition is automatic. In that setting a hypothetical counterexample
sequence must escape every joint diameter/half-Weyl bound. This is a
statement about the approximate inputs themselves; unlike the earlier
spectral escape theorem, it does not assume that the inputs are exact
Einstein metrics or satisfy a rank-one Weyl spectral ansatz.

The theorem works equally for changing smooth types, because its
compactness conclusion recovers the smooth type of each late term.
It does not complete the negative Yau26 branch: arbitrary diameter,
collapse without a gauge volume budget, and concentration of both
Weyl halves remain outside (1). No assumption is silently made that
the first-line scalar or spectral inequalities control those freedoms.

The meaningful increment is a theorem-level realization mechanism:
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{pointwise almost-Einstein input}
 +\text{bounded global regime}
 +\text{one-sided supercritical Weyl control}\\
 +\text{aspherical topology}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{Einstein metric on the same smooth manifold}.
 \end{gathered}}
\]
The compactness, curvature-energy, and ALE classification theorems are
not newly proved here. The continuation is their explicit composition
with the inherited compact-core obstruction, including the missing
almost-Einstein regularity argument. No worldwide-priority claim is
made.

### Comparison with an existing no-bubbling mechanism

Fine's 2017 twistor argument also excludes locally hyperkähler bubbles,
but uses a different input: a curvature inequality making the twistor
space tamed. His Corollary 4 gives compactness for normalized ASD
Einstein metrics under diameter and volume bounds, without our
asphericity assumption. His Remark 5 explicitly identifies the lack
of \(C^2\) convergence for bounded-Ricci sequences as an obstacle to
using his holomorphic-curve persistence proof. We do not remove that
obstacle for his theorem. Here no twistor taming or persistence is
assumed; the compact-core contradiction is purely topological once
the smooth ALE limit and core embedding have been obtained. Neither
result subsumes the other with its hypotheses unchanged.

# Half-flat bubble exclusion beyond aspherical topology

23 September 2026. This continuation weakens the topology in the compact-core
argument, and therefore in the compact-regime realization theorem. It does not
alter the archived proofs. The classification of half-flat ALE spaces is a
classical input; the extension below identifies the homotopical information
actually needed for their exclusion.

## 1. The topological hypothesis

Let \(M\) be a closed connected smooth oriented four-manifold satisfying
\[
 \pi_1(M)\text{ is torsion-free},\qquad \pi_2(M)=0.       \tag{H}
\]
Equivalently, the second condition says
\(H_2(\widetilde M;\mathbb Z)=0\): the universal cover is simply
connected, so this is the degree-two Hurewicz theorem. No vanishing of
\(\pi_3(M)\), no contractibility, and no assumption on spin are required.

**Theorem 1 (compact-core exclusion under (H)).** No sufficiently large
compact core of a nonflat smooth Ricci-flat ALE four-manifold with one
identically vanishing Weyl half embeds smoothly in a manifold satisfying (H).
The assertion is independent of the orientation of the proposed embedding.

**Proof.** Reverse orientation on the ALE space if necessary, so that
\(W^+=0\). Let \(B\) be a large core with one connected spherical
space-form boundary \(Y\), and with the homotopy type of the ALE space.
The classification of Wright, together with Suvaina's description of the
Kähler ALE quotients, gives the following alternatives:

1. \(\pi_1(B)\) is finite and the rational intersection form of \(B\)
   is negative definite, with \(b_2(B)>0\); or
2. \(B\) is a rational homology ball with
   \[
   \pi_1(B)=H_1(B;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z_n,
   \qquad Y=L(n^2,nr-1),\quad n\ge2,\ (n,r)=1.
   \tag{1}
   \]

These are the same classification inputs used in the
[inherited compact-core proof](../mathematics/current/line1_aspherical_no_bubbles.md).
The nonflat assumption excludes the Euclidean case.

Suppose \(i:B\hookrightarrow M\) is an embedding. The image of the
finite group \(\pi_1(B)\) in the torsion-free group \(\pi_1(M)\) is
trivial. Thus \(i\) lifts to an embedding into \(\widetilde M\).
The lift is injective since its composition with the covering projection
is injective. This does not assert that a limiting fundamental group
injects into the fundamental group of \(M\).

In the first case a compact rational two-cycle in the interior of \(B\)
has nonzero self-intersection. Its image is zero in
\(H_2(\widetilde M;\mathbb Q)\), which vanishes by (H).
Intersection of compact cycles is unchanged by an embedding, up to an
overall orientation sign, and is zero for a null-homologous compact cycle.
This is a contradiction.

In the second case put
\(C=\widetilde M\setminus\operatorname{int}B\) and apply integral
Mayer--Vietoris using collar enlargements. Since
\(H_1(\widetilde M;\mathbb Z)=H_2(\widetilde M;\mathbb Z)=0\),
\[
 \mathbb Z_{n^2}\cong H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)
 \cong H_1(B;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_1(C;\mathbb Z)
 \cong\mathbb Z_n\oplus H_1(C;\mathbb Z).               \tag{2}
\]
The last summand has order \(n\). The right side has exponent at most
\(n\), whereas the left side has exponent \(n^2\). This contradiction
also works for composite \(n\). Only the first two homology groups of
the universal cover were used. \(\square\)

### 1.1 What an arbitrary remaining ALE core would have to be

There is also a classification-free part of this argument. If a connected
compact oriented four-manifold \(B\) has finite fundamental group and
connected rational-homology-three-sphere boundary and embeds in a manifold
satisfying (H), then
\[
                         H_2(B;\mathbb Q)=0.             \tag{2a}
\]
Indeed the map \(H_2(B;\mathbb Q)\to H_2(B,\partial B;\mathbb Q)\)
is an isomorphism by the boundary homology sequence. Poincaré--Lefschetz
duality therefore makes the rational intersection pairing nondegenerate.
The lifted embedding into \(\widetilde M\), whose second homology
vanishes, makes every such pairing zero. Nondegeneracy implies (2a).
Also \(H_1(B;\mathbb Q)=0\) by finiteness of its fundamental group.
Connected boundary and duality give \(H_3(B;\mathbb Q)=0\), so \(B\)
is a rational homology ball.

In particular, a remaining smooth Ricci-flat ALE bubble (these have finite
fundamental group and spherical-space-form ends) could not carry nonzero
rational second homology. Combining this with Theorem 1 and the
[paired-concentration theorem](20260923_critical_chiral_threshold.md), a
surviving bubble would have to be a **rational homology ball with both
Weyl halves nonzero and carrying positive critical mass**. This does not
claim that such Ricci-flat ALE spaces exist, or exclude them by importing
the classification restricted to half-flat spaces. It narrows precisely
the unresolved compact-regime degeneration.

## 2. The enlarged class contains genuinely nonaspherical manifolds

**Proposition 2.** Suppose \(X_1,\ldots,X_N\) are closed oriented
four-manifolds satisfying (H). Then
\[
 M=X_1\#\cdots\#X_N\#m(S^1\times S^3)
 \tag{3}
\]
also satisfies (H).

**Proof.** Van Kampen identifies \(\pi_1(M)\) with the free product
of the \(\pi_1(X_i)\) and a free group of rank \(m\). A finite-order
element of a free product is conjugate into one of its factors; this
group is therefore torsion-free.

Cut (3) along its connected-sum three-spheres. Its universal cover is a
tree of universal covers of punctured factors, glued along three-spheres.
Removing a locally finite collection of open four-balls does not change
\(H_1\) or \(H_2\): excision gives relative groups only in degree four.
Consequently each lifted vertex piece has vanishing \(H_1,H_2\).
The same holds for the punctured universal cover of \(S^1\times S^3\),
whose unpunctured cover is \(\mathbb R\times S^3\).
Mayer--Vietoris over finite subtrees, followed by the direct limit,
gives \(H_2(\widetilde M;\mathbb Z)=0\); edge three-spheres have no
first or second homology. Compact singular cycles involve only finitely
many pieces. Hurewicz now gives \(\pi_2(M)=0\). \(\square\)

For a concrete distinction, take
\[
 M=(\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h)\#m(S^1\times S^3),
 \qquad g,h\ge2,\quad m>0.                            \tag{4}
\]
It satisfies (H), but is not aspherical. If it were aspherical it
would be a \(K(\pi,1)\) for
\(\pi=\pi_1(\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h)*F_m\).
Another finite \(K(\pi,1)\) is
\((\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h)\vee\bigvee_{i=1}^m S^1\), of Euler
characteristic \(\chi(\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h)-m\). In contrast the
connected sum (4) has Euler characteristic
\(\chi(\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h)-2m\). Homotopy invariance contradicts
\(m>0\). Thus Theorem 1 is a genuine enlargement of the topological
scope, not another definition of asphericity.

## 3. Consequences for compactness and exact realization

**Corollary 3.** Every assertion in
[compact-regime realization](20260923_compact_realization.md), including
its critical equiintegrability version, remains valid with “aspherical”
replaced by (H), with the same stated geometric dependencies.

**Proof.** In that proof the geometric inputs are Ricci bounds, diameter,
volume, and one-sided Weyl control. Harmonic-radius blow-up produces a
smooth nonflat half-flat Ricci-flat ALE space and embeds its compact
core in the original manifold. Asphericity enters only in forbidding
that embedding. Apply Theorem 1 instead. The later diffeomorphism
compactness and Einstein-limit argument are unchanged. \(\square\)

In particular, for \(q>2\), fixed \(D,v,\Lambda>0\), and topology
(H),
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(g)\le D,\quad V_g\ge v,\quad
 \|W_g^+\|_{L^q}\le\Lambda,\quad
 \|\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}_g+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}
       <\varepsilon(D,v,\Lambda,q)
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \exists h:\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h.
 \tag{5}
\]
The conclusion concerns the same smooth manifold. It does not assume
that the input is close to a previously known Einstein metric.

The theorem also supplies the topological step in the new
[critical chiral threshold](20260923_critical_chiral_threshold.md) and
[gauge-saturation realization](20260923_gauge_saturation_ke.md).
Those notes prove their analytical inputs independently of Proposition 2.

## 4. An application to handle additions with actual gauge supply

For (4), let \(A=8(g-1)(h-1)>0\). The canonical SW class on the
product, followed by the handle surgery formula retaining the degree-one
insertions, supplies an actual conformally solvable class with
\[
 a^2=A,\qquad (a_g^+)^2\ge A.
 \tag{6}
\]
The supply statement, not merely this intersection calculation, is the
classical input detailed in the
[polarized closure](../mathematics/current/lebrun_stability_closure.md),

If \(m\) is odd, then \(b_1(M)=2g+2h+m\) is odd. No complex
structure on this smooth manifold can be Kähler, in either orientation,
since a compact Kähler manifold has even first Betti number.
Apply the gauge-saturation realization theorem with its extension (H).
For every fixed diameter bound \(D\), it follows that
\[
 \delta_-(g)+\frac{\mathfrak D_2(g,a)}{V_g}
       \ge\varepsilon(D,A_0)>0,
 \qquad (a_g^+)^2\ge A_0>0,\quad \operatorname{diam}(g)\le D.
 \tag{7}
\]
One may choose \(A_0=8\) simultaneously for all products in (4).
When the sum on the left is small, the scalar monopole bound supplies
the volume lower bound used by the realization theorem; it is not an
additional hypothesis on these examples.

\((2\chi+3\tau)(M)=76\), and handle ratio \(4m/A=1/20\).

\(1/3-1/20=17/60>1/4\). Statement (7) is a different conclusion:
it forbids joint gauge saturation and pointwise Einstein approximation
under a diameter bound. It does not replace that old unrestricted
integral gap or prove a gap for \(\delta_-\) alone.

No assertion is made that every topology in this infinite family admits
metrics satisfying a common diameter bound and very small Ricci defect.
Indeed (6) and volume comparison restrict the available topologies when

such a metric could have positive \(\mathfrak D_2/V\). Its precise

# Critical one-half energy thresholds and paired curvature concentration

23 September 2026. Continuation of the
[compact-regime realization theorem](20260923_compact_realization.md).
The increment is at the scale-invariant exponent: supercritical control
or critical equiintegrability is replaced by a fixed small local critical
energy threshold. The small chirality may vary from ball to ball.

## 1. Conventions and statements

Use the Hilbert--Schmidt curvature-operator convention
\[
 |W^\pm|^2=\tfrac14 W^\pm_{ijkl}(W^\pm)^{ijkl}.             \tag{1}
\]
This is not the largest-eigenvalue norm. In the full tensor convention,
all energy thresholds below must be multiplied by four.
Let \(\omega_4=\pi^2/2\), and let \(V_{-K/3}(R)\) be the volume of
a radius-\(R\) ball in the simply connected four-dimensional space
form of sectional curvature \(-K/3\). For fixed \(K,D,v>0\), set
\[
 \theta_0=\frac{v}{V_{-K/3}(D+1)},\qquad
 S_0=\frac9{16\sqrt{\omega_4\theta_0}},\qquad
 \kappa_0=\frac1{6S_0^2}=\frac{128\omega_4\theta_0}{243},
 \qquad \epsilon_0=\frac{\kappa_0}{4}.                    \tag{2}
\]
If the class below is nonempty then \(0<\theta_0\le1\).
These are sufficient constants, not claimed to be sharp.

The topological assumption is
\[
 \pi_1(M)\text{ torsion-free},\qquad \pi_2(M)=0.            \tag{H}
\]
Every closed aspherical manifold has (H). The
[topological core-scope extension](20260923_topological_core_scope.md)
shows that half-flat ALE core exclusion needs only (H): the universal
cover has \(H_1=H_2=0\), and a finite-fundamental-group core lifts to
that cover. In particular, everything below may also be read in the
older aspherical scope.

**Theorem A (critical local compactness).** Fix \(K,D,v,r_0>0\) and
\(0<\alpha<1\). There is a uniform positive lower bound for the
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) harmonic radius on closed connected smooth oriented
four-manifolds satisfying (H),
\[
 \|\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}\le K,
 \qquad \operatorname{diam}(M,g)\le D,\qquad
 \operatorname{Vol}(M,g)\ge v,                            \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{x\in M}\min\left\{
   \int_{B_{r_0}(x)}|W^+|^2\,d\mu,\
   \int_{B_{r_0}(x)}|W^-|^2\,d\mu
                   \right\}\le\epsilon_0.               \tag{4}
\]
Consequently the class has geometric \(C^{1,\beta}\) and weak
\(W^{2,p}\) precompactness, and only finitely many smooth types.
Here \(\beta<1\) and \(p<\infty\). No uniform \(L^q\), \(q>2\),
bound or vanishing equiintegrability modulus is required.

**Theorem B (critical-regime realization).** Fix \(D,v,r_0>0\).
Use (2) with \(K=2\). There is \(\delta_0(D,v,r_0)>0\) such that
a closed connected smooth oriented four-manifold satisfying (H)
and carrying a metric with these diameter/volume bounds, (4), and
\[
 \delta_-(g)=\|\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}_g+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}
                       <\delta_0                       \tag{5}
\]
admits a smooth metric \(h\) with \(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\).

The simpler condition
\(\min\{\int_M|W^+|^2,\int_M|W^-|^2\}\le\epsilon_0\) implies
(4), but is usually unnecessarily restrictive. Condition (4) allows
substantial total Weyl energy and different choices of the small half
in different regions.

## 2. A Sobolev--Weitzenböck gap on the exact blowup

**Lemma C.** Let \(X\) be a complete noncompact smooth Ricci-flat
oriented four-manifold, ALE of some positive order, with asymptotic
volume ratio \(\theta\ge\theta_0>0\). Each Weyl half separately satisfies
\[
 W^\pm\equiv0\quad\hbox{or}\quad
                       \int_X|W^\pm|^2\,d\mu\ge\kappa_0. \tag{6}
\]
This is a classical analytic mechanism, not a classification of
general Ricci-flat ALE spaces.

**Proof.** Brendle's nonnegative-Ricci Sobolev inequality applied to
compactly supported \(f=|u|^3\), by approximation at zeros, gives
\[
 4(\omega_4\theta)^{1/4}
       \left(\int|u|^4\right)^{3/4}
 \le3\int|u|^2|\nabla u|
 \le3\left(\int|u|^4\right)^{1/2}
           \left(\int|\nabla u|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \|u\|_4^2\le S(\theta)\|\nabla u\|_2^2,\qquad
 S(\theta)=\frac9{16\sqrt{\omega_4\theta}}\le S_0.          \tag{7}
\]
For either half let \(w=|W^\pm|\). On the Ricci-flat limit the
Einstein Weitzenböck identity reads
\[
 \tfrac12\Delta w^2=|\nabla W^\pm|^2-18\det W^\pm,
 \qquad\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla.                   \tag{8}
\]
For a real symmetric trace-free \(3\times3\) matrix,
\[
 |\det W^\pm|\le\frac{w^3}{3\sqrt6}.                      \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, with eigenvalues summing to zero,
\[
 (\sum\lambda_i^2)^3-54(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3)^2
       =2\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^2\ge0.
\]

ALE asymptotics of a positive order \(\tau\) suffice for exhaustion:
\(W=O(r^{-2-\tau})\), \(\nabla W=O(r^{-3-\tau})\).
Einstein elliptic regularity gives the derivative asymptotics.
Thus \(w\in L^2\cap L^4\), \(\nabla W\in L^2\), and boundary errors
in (8) vanish. Cutoffs and Kato extend (7) to \(w\).
No assertion about the optimal ALE coordinate order is used.
Integration followed by Hölder, Kato and (7) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J:=\int|\nabla W^\pm|^2
 &=18\int\det W^\pm
 \le\sqrt6\int w^3\\
 &\le\sqrt6\,\|w\|_2\|w\|_4^2
 \le\sqrt6\,S_0\|w\|_2J.
 \end{aligned}                                          \tag{10}
\]
If \(\|w\|_2^2<\kappa_0\), the last coefficient is less than one,
so \(J=0\). A parallel \(L^2\) tensor on this infinite-volume
connected manifold vanishes. This proves (6). \(\square\)

The Sobolev estimate is on an **exact Ricci-flat blowup** with a
controlled volume ratio. It does not add a hidden uniform Sobolev
assumption to the earlier unrestricted monopole integral-gap theorem.

## 3. Topology forces both halves to concentrate together

Suppose a sequence satisfying (H) and (3) has minimum harmonic radius
\(\rho_j\to0\), attained at \(x_j\). The harmonic-radius argument of
the preceding continuation gives a complete smooth nonflat
Ricci-flat limit
\[
 (M_j,\rho_j^{-2}g_j,x_j)\longrightarrow
                  (X,g_\infty,x_\infty).                 \tag{11}
\]
Here are the necessary inputs, rather than an implicit invocation of
smooth almost-Einstein compactness.

Relative Bishop comparison gives, at every centre and for \(r\le D+1\),
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}B_r(x)
 \ge \frac{vV_{-K/3}(r)}{V_{-K/3}(D+1)}
 \ge\theta_0\omega_4r^4.                                 \tag{12}
\]
Every fixed-radius ball in the rescaled limit obeys this Euclidean
lower bound, so its asymptotic volume ratio is at least \(\theta_0\).
Ricci tends uniformly to zero after rescaling. The normalized
harmonic-radius lower bound gives \(C^1\) charts; their elliptic
metric equation gives strong \(W^{2,p}\) convergence for every finite
\(p\), then a smooth Ricci-flat limit. Strong
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) recovery makes the limiting harmonic-radius
normalization nontrivial, excluding a flat limit. No derivative of
the original Ricci error is estimated.

Jiang--Naber's local full \(L^2\) curvature estimate, summed over the
bounded global covering provided by (3), bounds the scale-invariant
full energy. The bound passes to (11). Finite energy and Euclidean
growth give ALE structure by the Ricci-flat asymptotic theorem.
Only a positive ALE order is needed here.

The geometric convergence embeds every large smooth compact core of
\(X\) in \(M_j\). If \(W^+_\infty=0\), the half-flat compact-core
exclusion contradicts that embedding. If \(W^-_\infty=0\), the same
argument applies with reversed orientation. Therefore both halves of
this **same** blowup are nonzero, and Lemma C gives
\[
              \int_X|W^+|^2\ge\kappa_0,\qquad
              \int_X|W^-|^2\ge\kappa_0.                  \tag{13}
\]
For every fixed \(r>0\), strong local curvature convergence and
exhaustion in the rescaled radius imply
\[
 \liminf_j\int_{B_r(x_j)}|W^+_{g_j}|^2\ge\kappa_0,\qquad
 \liminf_j\int_{B_r(x_j)}|W^-_{g_j}|^2\ge\kappa_0.          \tag{14}
\]
To see this, a fixed radius-\(R\) ball in (11) is eventually contained
in the unscaled radius-\(r\) ball. First let \(j\to\infty\),
then \(R\to\infty\).

**Paired-concentration alternative.** In the bounded Ricci, diameter
and noncollapse regime (3), harmonic-radius degeneration on the
topological class (H) forces a definite critical amount of **both**
Weyl energies at a common shrinking location, in the precise sense
(14), after a subsequence.

This is stronger than saying that one half must concentrate. It does
not assert the existence of non-half-flat Ricci-flat ALE spaces or
assume their classification. It identifies the curvature required of
any surviving bubble. If such bubbles are excluded by additional
arguments, compactness strengthens further.

## 4. Proofs of compactness and realization

**Proof of Theorem A.** If the harmonic radii were not uniformly
positive, apply (14) with \(r=r_0\). Both energies at \(x_j\)
eventually exceed \(\kappa_0/2\), contradicting (4) and
\(\epsilon_0=\kappa_0/4\). No globally consistent choice of chirality
in (4) is needed. The controlled harmonic atlases give the stated
compactness and smooth-type finiteness exactly as in the preceding
continuation. \(\square\)

**Proof of Theorem B.** Failure would give input metrics with
\(\delta_-(g_j)\to0\), the fixed bounds and (4), but no normalized
negative Einstein metric on their smooth manifolds. For large \(j\)
the Ricci bound (3) holds with \(K=2\). Theorem A gives a subsequence
identified with a fixed smooth manifold, and harmonic-coordinate
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) convergence. Pass to
\[
 g^{ab}\partial_a\partial_bg_{ij}
    =-2\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}+Q_{ij}(g,\partial g).
\]
The limiting metric weakly solves \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\).
Elliptic bootstrapping makes it smooth. Transporting this metric
through the smooth identifications supplies the forbidden Einstein
metric on each late original manifold. \(\square\)

An actual monopole projection budget \((a_g^+)^2\ge A_0>0\) can
replace the lower volume hypothesis: if \(\delta_-\le1/2\), the
scalar estimate gives \(V\ge8\pi^2A_0/9\).
Substitute this \(v\) into (2). This observation does not delete
actual solvability or square costs from other theorems.

## 5. Increment and the remaining original-problem gap

The preceding \(L^q\), \(q>2\), hypothesis and uniform critical
equiintegrability each imply (4) at a common sufficiently small
radius. Conversely, (4) does not itself impose uniform vanishing
of small-ball energy: it permits a fixed nonzero subthreshold
amount. No actual new geometric family is claimed merely from this
measure-theoretic weakening.

The variable-chirality form is useful when global control of one
fixed half is unavailable but the two energies have different spatial
distributions. It provides a mathematically precise potential role
for chiral information in existence, rather than just another global
curvature coefficient.

For a hypothetical negative-side Yau26 counterexample sequence in
(H), with bounded diameter and a uniform positive actual projection
budget, harmonic radii must degenerate along a subsequence; otherwise
the smooth-type realization argument produces Einstein metrics.
Consequently (14) holds at suitable centres: the remaining
compact-regime escape must spend critical energy in **both** halves
at the same location.

Diameter escape, collapse without a positive volume budget, and
paired concentration above threshold remain possible. No
dimension-only Yau26 threshold is proved. Sobolev and Weyl gap
estimates are classical ingredients; the increment is their explicit
composition with the topological core exclusion to obtain critical,
locally variable-chirality compactness and same-type realization.
No exhaustive publication-priority claim is made.

# From actual monopole saturation to a Kähler–Einstein realization

23 September 2026. New composition theorem. The inherited bounded-form
construction and the compact-regime realization proof remain unchanged.
This note proves that their combination eliminates the separately assumed
half-Weyl bound and yields a global Kähler structure, not merely an Einstein
metric or an eigenline on a double cover.

## 1. Statement from the original geometric and gauge input

Use exterior-algebra norms on two-forms, Hilbert–Schmidt norms on the Weyl
endomorphism of \(\Lambda^+\), and tensor norms on Ricci. Define
\[
 \delta_-(g)=\|\operatorname{Ric}_g^\sharp+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}},
 \qquad r(g)=\fint_M|\operatorname{Ric}_g+g|^2.
 \tag{1}
\]
Fix a Spin\(^c\) structure whose unperturbed monopole equations are solvable
for every metric in the conformal class \([g]\). It is an **actual** gauge
input, not an arbitrary cohomology vector. The structure may depend on the
input metric but is fixed through its auxiliary positive conformal weights.
Write \(a\) for its real first Chern class, \(p=(a_g^+)^2\), and
\[
 \mathfrak D_2(g,a)
  =\frac32\int_M\bigl(s_g^2+12|W_g^+|^2\bigr)\,d\mu_g
           -72\pi^2p\ge0,
 \qquad q(g,a)=\frac{\mathfrak D_2(g,a)}{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The nonnegativity is the classical monopole curvature inequality. This is
the parameter-two deficit, not an Einstein defect. Its exact equality set
contains compatible constant-scalar-curvature Kähler metrics, including
non-Einstein ones.

**Theorem A (saturation implies exact realization).** For every
\(D,A_0>0\) there is \(\varepsilon(D,A_0)>0\) such that the following
holds. Let \(M\) be a closed connected smooth oriented aspherical
four-manifold. If \(g\) and an actual conformally solvable Spin\(^c\)
structure satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(g)\le D,\qquad p\ge A_0,
 \qquad \delta_-(g)+q(g,a)<\varepsilon(D,A_0),
 \tag{3}
\]
then the **same oriented smooth manifold** admits a smooth complex structure
\(J\) and a smooth Kähler metric \(h\) such that
\[
                          \operatorname{Ric}_h=-h.
 \tag{4}
\]
The complex orientation agrees with the specified orientation. No finite
cover is needed. The threshold is uniform across changing manifolds and
metric-dependent actual classes. No separate half-Weyl bound, curvature
energy bound, lower volume bound, square-cost bound, Sobolev constant,
injectivity-radius bound, or Ricci-derivative bound is an assumption.

The diameter bound, asphericity and actual positive projection budget are
retained. The simultaneous smallness in (3) is essential to the argument:
pointwise Ricci pinching alone does not automatically make \(q\) small.
No claim is made that the original \(g\) itself is Kähler, that its original
Spin\(^c\) structure must be canonical for the produced complex structure,
or that a numerical threshold has been computed.

**Positive-square specialization.** If the supplied Spin\(^c\) class has
\(a^2>0\), integrality gives \(a^2\ge1\), and Hodge decomposition gives
\(p=a^2+\|a_g^-\|^2\ge1\). One can therefore take \(A_0=1\): the
threshold in this specialization depends only on \(D\), not on a further
chosen numerical projection budget. This uses an actual integral Chern
class, not an arbitrary rescaled real cohomology vector; it does not remove
the diameter or simultaneous-saturation conditions.

This is a first-line realization theorem rather than another forbidden-mode
estimate. Its proof does not try to contradict the allowed Kähler–Einstein
mode. It shows that, under the stated global conditions, this mode is
realized on the original smooth type.

## 2. The two inherited inputs used in the proof

The [actual-form theorem](../mathematics/current/line1_composed_application.md)
supplies, for
\[
                  \kappa=r+q\le1/2880,
\]
and each \(0<\eta\le1/4\), a single smooth self-dual two-form \(\omega\)
such that
\[
 \|\omega\|_\infty\le2\sqrt3,
 \qquad \fint(|\omega|^2-2)^2\le720\kappa+\eta,
 \tag{5}
\]
\[
 \fint|\nabla\omega|^2
        \le13\kappa+4\sqrt{2\kappa}+\eta.
 \tag{6}
\]
All estimates in (5)–(6) are independent of \((-a^2)_+/\operatorname{Vol}\).
The square cost in the inherited theorem is needed for its additional
Hodge-Laplacian estimate, which is **not used here**. The bound (5) is
proved in Section 2 of that source before any second-order estimate.

The second input is the endpoint form of the
[compactness/realization theorem](20260923_compact_realization.md), Sections
3–5. Closed oriented aspherical four-manifolds with two-sided bounded Ricci,
uniformly bounded diameter, volume bounded below, and uniformly locally
equiintegrable \(|W^+|^2\) have a uniform harmonic-radius lower bound.
An almost-Einstein sequence then converges, after diffeomorphisms and a
subsequence, in \(C^{1,\alpha}\) and strongly in finite \(W^{2,p}\)
to a smooth exact Einstein metric on the common smooth type.

The geometric reason is important. At a vanishing harmonic radius the
rescaled Ricci tensor tends to zero and the equiintegrable Weyl half
disappears. Jiang–Naber gives finite total curvature energy. The resulting
complete nonflat Ricci-flat ASD limit is ALE by Bando–Kasue–Nakajima.
Its compact core would embed in the original manifold, contradicting the
[aspherical compact-core lemma](../mathematics/current/line1_aspherical_no_bubbles.md).
This includes rational-ball ALE quotients, not just bubbles containing
exceptional spheres. Harmonic-coordinate elliptic convergence uses the
small Ricci tensor itself, never derivatives of that tensor.

The substantive new connection is to use a classical amplitude consequence
of saturation for endpoint compactness, then pass the **global** supplied
forms to an actual parallel form on the limiting manifold. A separate
polynomial Weyl-surrogate or second-order form bound is not necessary here.

## 3. Positive projection and bounded diameter fix the volume regime

Consider any sequence satisfying the fixed \(D,A_0\) hypotheses with
\[
                    \delta_j+q_j\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{7}
\]
Discard finitely many terms so that \(\delta_j\le1/2\). Each Ricci
eigenvalue lies in \([-3/2,-1/2]\), so
\[
 |\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}|\le3,
 \qquad -6\le s_{g_j}\le-2,
 \qquad r_j\le4\delta_j^2.
 \tag{8}
\]
The actual monopole scalar estimate gives
\[
 32\pi^2 A_0\le\int s_-^2
       \le16(1+\delta_j)^2V_j,
 \qquad V_j\ge\frac{8\pi^2A_0}{9}=:v_0.
 \tag{9}
\]
Bishop comparison, with \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge-3g\), gives
\[
                  V_j\le V_{-1}(D+1)=:V_0(D).
 \tag{10}
\]
It also gives uniform bounds
\[
 c(D,A_0)\rho^4\le\operatorname{Vol}_{g_j}B_\rho(x)
                         \le C\rho^4\quad(0<\rho\le1).
 \tag{11}
\]
For the lower bound compare with a ball of radius \(D+1\) and use (9).
For the upper bound use the small-radius model-ball estimate. These are
geometric ball bounds at every point, not merely a positive total volume.
They justify the noncollapsed compactness and curvature-energy input.

## 4. The gauge deficit prevents one-sided critical concentration

We have \(\kappa_j=r_j+q_j\to0\). Put
\(w_j=\sqrt6|W^+_{g_j}|\), \(e_j=s_j+4\), and \(z_j=s_j+2w_j\).
The completed-square decomposition of the signed monopole inequality gives
\(\int z_j^2\le2\mathfrak D_2\), while
\(\fint e_j^2\le4r_j\). Hence
\[
 \fint(w_j-2)^2
    =\frac14\fint(z_j-e_j)^2
    \le q_j+2r_j\le2\kappa_j.
 \tag{12}
\]
This is equation (7) of the inherited actual-form paper, and precedes its
form construction. The elementary inequality
\[
             |W^+|^2=w^2/6\le\frac13(w-2)^2+\frac43
 \tag{13}
\]
therefore implies, for every measurable subset \(E\subset M_j\),
\[
 \int_E|W^+_{g_j}|^2
          \le\frac43\operatorname{Vol}_{g_j}(E)
                            +\frac23\kappa_jV_j.
 \tag{14}
\]
In particular, (11) yields
\[
 \sup_x\int_{B_\rho(x)}|W^+_{g_j}|^2
       \le\frac43C\rho^4+\frac23V_0\kappa_j.
 \tag{15}
\]
This is stronger than bounded total \(L^2\) energy: it rules out a
nonzero critical half-Weyl mass at arbitrarily small scales in the sequence.

For completeness, a common equiintegrability modulus does follow from
(15). Given \(\epsilon>0\), make the second term less than
\(\epsilon/2\) on the tail, and then take \(\rho\) small so that
the first term is less than \(\epsilon/2\). The finitely many preceding
smooth compact metrics have uniformly vanishing small-ball integrals too.
Taking the minimum of these radius choices proves
\[
 \lim_{\rho\downarrow0}\sup_j\sup_x
           \int_{B_\rho(x)}|W^+_{g_j}|^2=0.
 \tag{16}
\]
The modulus may depend on the sequence. That is sufficient for the
contradiction proof of the uniform threshold below. We do not claim that
a bare fixed upper bound on \(\kappa\) supplies (16) without a smallness
or limiting argument.

An equivalent direct check avoids introducing a modulus. If harmonic
radii \(\rho_j\to0\), then on each fixed-radius ball for
\(\widehat g_j=\rho_j^{-2}g_j\), scale invariance gives
\[
 \int_{B_R(\widehat g_j)}|W^+_{\widehat g_j}|^2
 \le\frac43C(R\rho_j)^4+\frac23V_0\kappa_j\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{17}
\]
This is exactly the vanishing needed in the no-ALE-core blowup argument.
It requires no supercritical \(L^q\) estimate and no full pointwise
curvature estimate on the original inputs.

## 5. From convergence of metrics to a global parallel form

Apply the inherited endpoint compactness to (8)–(11) and (16). After
a subsequence there are a fixed closed oriented smooth \(M_\infty\),
orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms \(\phi_j:M_\infty\to M_j\),
and a smooth metric \(h\) such that, with \(h_j=\phi_j^*g_j\),
\[
 h_j\longrightarrow h\quad\text{in }C^{1,\alpha}
         \text{ and strongly in }W^{2,p}\ (p<\infty),
 \qquad\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h.
 \tag{18}
\]
For any one fixed \(\alpha<1\), these convergences hold along a
suitable subsequence. The limiting smooth structure is the original
smooth type of every sufficiently late term, not merely its topology.

Choose \(\eta_j\downarrow0\) and actual forms \(\omega_j\) satisfying
(5)–(6). Pull them back by the same diffeomorphisms and call the forms
\(\widetilde\omega_j\). They remain uniformly bounded in \(L^\infty\),
and (6), (10) imply
\[
                  \|\nabla^{h_j}\widetilde\omega_j\|_{L^2(h_j)}
                                   \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{19}
\]
Because \(h_j\to h\) in \(C^1\), the difference of their connections
tends uniformly to zero. Thus
\[
 \|\nabla^h\widetilde\omega_j\|_{L^2(h)}
 \le C\|\nabla^{h_j}\widetilde\omega_j\|_{L^2(h_j)}
       +o(1)\|\widetilde\omega_j\|_{L^2(h)}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{20}
\]
The forms are bounded in \(H^1(M_\infty,h)\). Rellich compactness gives
strong \(L^2\) convergence to a globally defined form \(\omega_\infty\).
Passing (20) weakly proves \(\nabla^h\omega_\infty=0\).
The distributional parallel equation with smooth coefficients implies
smoothness, for example by taking its rough-Laplacian equation and using
elliptic regularity.

Self-duality passes to the limit because the Hodge stars converge uniformly.
The uniform bound and strong \(L^2\) convergence also allow products to
converge in \(L^2\). The amplitude part of (5) therefore gives
\[
                      *_h\omega_\infty=\omega_\infty,
                 \qquad |\omega_\infty|_h^2=2.
 \tag{21}
\]
The amplitude statement first holds almost everywhere and then everywhere
by smoothness. In particular the limit form does not vanish.

Define \(J\) by \(h(JX,Y)=\omega_\infty(X,Y)\). The elementary
four-dimensional algebra of a self-dual form of norm \(\sqrt2\) gives
\(J^2=-I\). Parallelness gives \(\nabla^hJ=0\), so \(J\) is integrable
and \(d\omega_\infty=0\). Hence \((h,J)\) is Kähler, and (18) makes
it negative Kähler–Einstein.

The distinction from a local Weyl eigenline argument is substantial.
Each \(\omega_j\) was a **global actual form**, despite possibly having
zeros. Its limiting form is globally nonvanishing. No choice of a local
unit eigenvector, no orientability assertion about an eigenline bundle,
and no passage to a two-fold cover occurs.

## 6. Sequential compactness gives a fixed small-defect threshold

Suppose Theorem A is false for fixed \(D,A_0\). Choose manifolds,
metrics and actual classes meeting the topological and gauge hypotheses
with \(\delta_j+q_j<1/j\), but for which no compatible normalized
negative Kähler–Einstein metric exists on \(M_j\).

Sections 3–5 produce a smooth Kähler–Einstein pair \((h,J)\) on
\(M_\infty\) and diffeomorphisms \(\phi_j:M_\infty\to M_j\).
Transporting \((h,J)\) to every sufficiently late \(M_j\) contradicts
the choice of the sequence. This proves the existence of
\(\varepsilon(D,A_0)>0\). The argument allows the topology and actual
class to vary; neither is fixed in the contradiction sequence. \(\square\)

This last step is necessary. The theorem is not merely the assertion that
one already given sequence has a Kähler–Einstein limit. Nor does the
proof invent a sequence of improving metrics on the same manifold from
one small-error input. Uniformity is obtained by excluding a sequence of
counterexamples to the fixed-threshold statement.

## 7. Global consequences and a weaker topological input

### 7.1 A joint gap and its topological range

**Corollary B (joint gap on non-Kähler smooth types).** Fix \(D,A_0>0\).
On every closed connected oriented aspherical four-manifold admitting no
compatible Kähler structure, every input with
\(\operatorname{diam}(g)\le D\) and an actual conformally solvable
class satisfying \(p\ge A_0\) obeys
\[
                         \delta_-(g)+q(g,a)
                              \ge\varepsilon(D,A_0).
 \tag{22}
\]
The same conclusion holds on the larger class of such manifolds admitting
no compatible negative Kähler–Einstein metric. It follows directly by
contraposition of Theorem A.

For example, the inherited nonzero-shear self-gluings are aspherical and
have no Kähler finite cover; their specified actual classes have
\(a^2=2\chi>0\), hence \(p\ge a^2\). The theorem therefore applies to
that family whenever the bounded-diameter and simultaneous-deficit
conditions are considered. This is a constraint on arbitrary input
metrics, not only on the chosen shear construction. It does not rule out
Einstein metrics whose associated \(q\) stays away from zero.

In particular, a sequence on non-Kähler aspherical types with a fixed
positive projection budget and \(\delta_j+q_j\to0\) must have
\[
                           \operatorname{diam}(g_j)\to\infty.
 \tag{23}
\]
If diameters did not tend to infinity, a bounded subsequence would
contradict (22). Equation (23) is a joint-saturation escape theorem,
not a proof that every pointwise near-Einstein sequence saturates (2).

**Topological extension.** The same proof works on any class of closed
oriented smooth four-manifolds for which the compact-core non-embedding
lemma holds uniformly as a qualitative topological statement. In particular,
the [torsion-free, \(\pi_2=0\) core-exclusion extension](20260923_topological_core_scope.md)
allows asphericity to be replaced by
\[
                      \pi_1(M)\text{ torsion-free},
                            \qquad \pi_2(M)=0.
 \tag{24}
\]
Only the compact-core step uses this topology; all analytic estimates and
the threshold dependence are unchanged. The extension depends on the
separately proved core lemma, not on any assertion that (24) implies
asphericity. Connected sums with \(S^1\times S^3\) are a useful reason
to keep that distinction.

### 7.2 An actual fixed-manifold sharpness example for the diameter bound

The diameter hypothesis cannot be omitted from Theorem A, even if one adds
uniform bounds on full curvature and every fixed curvature derivative.
This is demonstrated by actual inherited metrics, not merely by a formal
escape alternative.

Fix one nonzero-shear self-gluing
\[
 M=M(h,g;u,f),\qquad h\ge1,\quad g\ge2,\quad [u]\ne0,
 \quad f\in\operatorname{Diff}^+(\Sigma_g).
 \tag{25}
\]
The [closed construction](../mathematics/current/round11_closed_gluing.md),
Sections 1 and 5, supplies \(G_\ell\) with
\(\operatorname{Ric}_{G_\ell}\) tending pointwise to \(-3G_\ell\)
and the **exact** volume \(4\pi^2\chi(M)/9\). Set \(g_\ell=3G_\ell\)
and
\[
 A=2\chi(M)>0,\qquad T=2\chi(M)+3\tau(M)=A.
\]
Then on this fixed closed smooth manifold
\[
 \delta_\ell\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad V_\ell=4\pi^2\chi(M)=2\pi^2A,
 \qquad\|\nabla^k\operatorname{Rm}_{g_\ell}\|_\infty\le C_k
                           \quad(k\ge0).
 \tag{26}
\]
The [topology proof](../mathematics/current/round11_graph_topology.md)
gives asphericity and signature zero. The
[virtual Kähler obstruction](../mathematics/current/round11_kahler.md)
excludes every Kähler structure even on a finite cover. The two symplectic
forms of the construction provide a fixed actual canonical class \(a\)
in the chosen orientation with
\[
                  a^2=A,\qquad p_\ell=(a_{g_\ell}^+)^2\ge A.
 \tag{27}
\]
Here \(b_+>1\), so the Taubes nonzero invariant supplies unperturbed
solutions for every metric and, in particular, every metric in each
\([g_\ell]\). The class-square calculation and this actual-supply
argument are in [Round 12](../mathematics/current/round12_global.md),
Sections 1–3, and [Round 11 variational theory](../mathematics/current/round11_variational.md),
Section 2. No metric-dependent formal replacement for this class is used.

It remains to check the extra saturation hypothesis of the new theorem.
Put \(S_2=\int s^2\), \(W_2=\int|W^+|^2\), and
\(P=\int|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2\). Chern–Weil gives
\[
              4\pi^2T=S_2/24+2W_2-P/2,
\]
and eliminating \(W_2\) from (2) yields the exact identity
\[
 \mathfrak D_2
   =\frac98S_2+36\pi^2T+\frac92P-72\pi^2p.
 \tag{28}
\]
If \(\delta_\ell\le1/2\), then
\[
 S_2/V_\ell\le16(1+\delta_\ell)^2,
 \qquad P/V_\ell\le4\delta_\ell^2.
\]
The latter follows by orthogonal projection of \(\operatorname{Ric}+g\)
onto the traceless tensors. Using (26)–(28),
\[
 \begin{split}
 0\le q_\ell
 &\le18(1+\delta_\ell)^2+18\delta_\ell^2
                                 -\frac{36\pi^2A}{V_\ell}\\
 &=36\delta_\ell(1+\delta_\ell)\longrightarrow0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{29}
\]
Therefore this actual fixed manifold has simultaneous
\(\delta_\ell\to0\), \(q_\ell\to0\), a fixed positive actual
projection budget, zero negative-square cost, exact positive volume, and
the curvature bounds in (26), but it has no Kähler–Einstein metric because
it has no Kähler structure. Theorem A consequently forces
\[
                         \operatorname{diam}(g_\ell)\to\infty.
 \tag{30}
\]
More importantly, (25)–(29) refute the version of Theorem A with its
diameter hypothesis deleted. The preserved long-neck construction is thus
a genuine sharpness test for the new realization theorem. We have not
constructed new shear metrics or newly proved their virtual Kähler
obstruction; the new deduction is their simultaneous parameter-two
saturation and their exact role as counterexamples to diameter-free
Kähler–Einstein realization. This says nothing new about whether they
admit **non-Kähler** negative Einstein metrics, which remains open here.

## 8. What has changed, and what has not

The new implication is
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{actual gauge supply + positive projection + bounded diameter}\\
 +\ \text{pointwise negative Ricci approximation}
 +\ \text{parameter-two saturation}\\
 +\ \text{no admissible ALE core in the smooth type}\\
 \Longrightarrow\ \text{negative Kähler–Einstein realization on that type}.
 \end{gathered}}
\]
Three earlier conditions have not merely been renamed: the separate
volume lower bound is supplied by the actual gauge class; the independent
half-Weyl bound is replaced by derived critical equiintegrability; and
the limiting local Kähler mode is realized by a global parallel form.
The negative-square cost is absent because no second-order form estimate
is needed after geometric compactness has been established.

This is not a universal obstruction/existence dichotomy. The deficit
\(q\) and the diameter restriction still select a genuine regime, and
the relevant topological core exclusion does not hold on all four-manifolds.
In particular, aspherical negative Kähler–Einstein surfaces themselves
have \(\delta_-=q=0\) for their compatible canonical input, so the
allowed regime is nonempty. Unequal-curvature hyperbolic products have
\(q=0\) but nonzero Ricci error: they do not turn cscK saturation alone
into an Einstein implication. The theorem's output also need not preserve
a preassigned complex structure.

The proof is a new composition of the inherited first-line actual-field
estimate and no-bubble compactness mechanism. The monopole curvature
inequality, bounded-Ricci compactness, finite-energy ALE asymptotics and
ALE classification remain classical inputs. No exhaustive priority search
or field-wide novelty certification is claimed.

# An averaged Weyl test for exact allowed-mode rigidity

23 September 2026. Status: manuscript proofs, not an independent certification
or priority claim. All manifolds in the statements are connected and oriented.
The curvature normalization is \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\); the scalar Laplacian
is nonnegative. Weyl tensors are regarded as self-adjoint endomorphisms of
\(\Lambda^+\).

## 1. The mathematical advance and its scope

The main result below gives an exact conclusion on a genuine allowed family:
for each diameter bound, a sufficiently small **averaged upper-eigenvalue
splitting** forces an Einstein metric on a product of closed hyperbolic
surfaces to be locally a hyperbolic product. It does not assume proximity to
a prescribed product metric, pointwise Weyl pinching, a lower Weyl amplitude,
or a preselected complex structure. It is uniform over the genera.

The new compactness step is that small splitting itself prevents curvature
bubbles. A putative Ricci-flat bubble has zero splitting. The Weyl equation
then forces that bubble to be anti-self-dual, and its compact core is
topologically forbidden. This differs from the earlier argument, which first
used pointwise relative pinching to bound \(W^+\).

Classical deformation theory is still essential: compactness identifies the
possible limits, and an unobstructed local moduli space rules out a nearby
non-Kähler Einstein branch. An integral estimate alone does not make its
right-hand side vanish.

**Dependencies and extensions.** The argument uses the compact-core proof in
[the inherited aspherical note](../mathematics/current/line1_aspherical_no_bubbles.md),
the Weyl eigenvalue calculation in
[the exact spectral application](../mathematics/current/line1_einstein_spectral_application.md),
and the classical inputs identified below. Section 8 removes the relative
pinching assumption from the exact-output shear theorem in that aspherical
note. The local product deformation result was already present in
[Round 18](../mathematics/current/round18_twisted_operator.md); it is not
claimed as new here. The compact realization theorem in
[the preceding continuation](20260923_compact_realization.md) has different
inputs: it treats approximate Einstein metrics, whereas the present exact
equation provides smooth curvature blow-ups.

Write
\[
 b_g=\lambda_3(W_g^+)-\lambda_2(W_g^+)\ge0,
 \qquad \mathscr S(g)=\frac1{V_g}\int_M b_g^2\,d\mu_g.       \tag{1}
\]
Ordered eigenvalues, including collisions and zeros, are allowed in (1).

## 2. The topological input does not require full asphericity

Use the following condition:
\[
 \pi_1(M)\text{ is torsion-free},\qquad \pi_2(M)=0.           \tag{TF2}
\]
Every closed aspherical manifold satisfies (TF2).

**Lemma 2.1 (compact-core exclusion).** No sufficiently large compact core
of a smooth nonflat Ricci-flat anti-self-dual ALE four-manifold embeds in
a closed oriented manifold satisfying (TF2).

**Proof.** The inherited core proof needs only the following facts about the
ambient universal cover: \(H_1=H_2=0\). These follow here from simple
connectivity, \(\pi_2=0\), and Hurewicz. A core \(B\) has finite fundamental
group, so an embedding into \(M\) lifts to an embedding into its universal
cover by torsion-freeness. If \(b_2(B)>0\), its negative-definite rational
intersection form is incompatible with \(H_2\) of the ambient cover being
zero. If \(b_2(B)=0\), the smooth ASD Ricci-flat ALE classification gives
\[
 H_1(B;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z_n,\quad
 H_1(\partial B;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z_{n^2},\quad n\ge2.
\]
Mayer--Vietoris in the ambient cover would identify
\(\mathbb Z_{n^2}\) with \(\mathbb Z_n\oplus H_1(C;\mathbb Z)\), where
\(C\) is the exterior and the second factor has order \(n\). Its exponent
is at most \(n\), a contradiction. The classification and rational-ball
case are precisely those detailed in the inherited proof; no assumption
that every bubble is simply connected or non-spin is used. \(\square\)

## 3. Why zero splitting kills the self-dual curvature of a bubble

**Lemma 3.1.** A complete Ricci-flat ALE four-manifold satisfying
\(\lambda_3(W^+)=\lambda_2(W^+)\) has \(W^+\equiv0\).

**Proof.** Put \(t=-3\lambda_1(W^+)/4\ge0\). Where \(t>0\), the lowest
eigenvalue is simple and
\[
 \operatorname{spec}(W^+)
   =\left(-\frac{4t}{3},\frac{2t}{3},\frac{2t}{3}\right),
 \qquad |W^+|^2=\frac83t^2.                                \tag{2}
\]
There is no need to orient its eigenline globally. At such a point the
Ricci-flat Weyl equation and the lowest-eigenvalue second derivative formula
give
\[
 \nabla^*\nabla W^+=6(W^+)^2-2|W^+|^2I,
\]
\[
 \Delta\lambda_1
 =6\lambda_1^2-2|W^+|^2
 -2\sum_{a=2}^3
 \frac{|\langle\nabla W^+ e_1,e_a\rangle|^2}
      {\lambda_1-\lambda_a}
 \ \ge\ \frac{16}{3}t^2.
\]
Multiplication by \(-3/4\) therefore yields
\[
                         \Delta t\le-4t^2.                 \tag{3}
\]
ALE curvature decay implies \(t\to0\) at infinity. If \(t\) were positive
somewhere, it would attain a positive global maximum. It is smooth there,
and the nonnegative Laplacian has \(\Delta t\ge0\) at a maximum, contrary
to (3). Thus \(t=0\), and (2) gives the assertion. \(\square\)

This is the decisive distinction between the negative Einstein scale and
the bubble scale. The negative Einstein equation allows the nonzero
Kähler root; at the Ricci-flat scale the restoring linear term disappears
and (3) eliminates that root on an ALE space.

## 4. Spectral no-bubbling, without pointwise spectral hypotheses

**Theorem 4.1.** Given \(D,v>0\), there are \(\varepsilon_0(D,v)>0\) and
\(K(D,v)<\infty\) such that every closed oriented four-manifold satisfying
(TF2), equipped with
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_g=-g,\qquad
 \operatorname{diam}_gM\le D,\qquad V_g\ge v,
 \qquad \mathscr S(g)\le\varepsilon_0(D,v),                 \tag{4}
\]
satisfies \(\|\mathrm{Rm}_g\|_\infty\le K(D,v)\).
Consequently any such sequence with \(\mathscr S(g_j)\to0\) has a smooth
subsequence, modulo diffeomorphisms, on a fixed closed smooth manifold.

There is no Euler-characteristic bound, pointwise \(W^\pm\) bound,
eigenvalue simplicity, relative pinching or lower eigenvalue amplitude in
the hypotheses.

**Proof.** Bishop comparison gives
\[
 V_g\le V_D:=\operatorname{Vol}_{\mathbb H^4_{-1/3}}B_{D+1},
 \qquad \operatorname{Vol}_g B_r(x)\ge c(D,v)r^4
 \quad(0<r\le1).                                          \tag{5}
\]
The local bounded-Ricci, noncollapse curvature theorem of Jiang--Naber,
applied to a uniformly finite covering, gives
\[
                       \int_M|\mathrm{Rm}_g|^2\le E(D,v).  \tag{6}
\]
The number of covering balls is bounded using (5) and \(V_D\). Thus (6)
does not silently require a topological bound. One can instead assume a
full curvature-energy bound and omit this classical input, obtaining the
corresponding version with an additional constant dependency.

Suppose the theorem fails. Select metrics satisfying the first three
conditions of (4), with \(\mathscr S(g_j)\le1/j\), and with
\(Q_j=\max|\mathrm{Rm}_{g_j}|\to\infty\). At a maximum point rescale by
\(h_j=Q_jg_j\). Then
\[
 |\mathrm{Rm}_{h_j}|\le1,\quad
 |\mathrm{Rm}_{h_j}|(p_j)=1,\quad
 \operatorname{Ric}_{h_j}=-Q_j^{-1}h_j.                     \tag{7}
\]
Equation (5), rescaled at radii \(R/\sqrt{Q_j}\), gives all-center
noncollapse on every fixed radius of the rescaled sequence. Bounded
curvature, injectivity control and the exact Einstein equation give a
smooth pointed limit \((X,h,p)\). It is complete, Ricci-flat and nonflat,
has Euclidean lower volume growth, and has finite curvature energy by
(6) and four-dimensional scale invariance. The
Bando--Kasue--Nakajima asymptotic theorem therefore makes it ALE.

The splitting energy has the same scale invariance:
\[
 b_{Q_jg_j}=Q_j^{-1}b_{g_j},\qquad
 \int_M b_{h_j}^2\,d\mu_{h_j}
   =\int_M b_{g_j}^2\,d\mu_{g_j}
   \le V_D\mathscr S(g_j)\longrightarrow0.                 \tag{8}
\]
Smooth convergence on compact subsets and continuity of ordered
eigenvalues show \(b_h=0\). Lemma 3.1 forces \(W_h^+=0\). A sufficiently
large ALE core embeds in \(M_j\) by the actual smooth convergence maps,
contradicting Lemma 2.1. This proves the curvature bound.

Finally (5), the curvature bound and the diameter bound yield a uniform
injectivity radius and global smooth Einstein compactness. The limiting
manifold is diffeomorphic to the late members, not merely homeomorphic to
an orbifold limit. \(\square\)

## 5. Passing from compactness to an exact equality mode

The following formulation separates the global coverage statement from
the classical local deformation input.

**Proposition 5.1 (unobstructed allowed-mode criterion).** Let \(\mathcal C\)
be a collection of closed oriented smooth four-manifolds satisfying (TF2).
Fix \(D,v>0\). Assume that, whenever \(M\in\mathcal C\) admits a normalized
negative Einstein metric with \(b=0\) and \(W^+\not\equiv0\), its
lowest-eigenline orientation cover carries a negative Kähler--Einstein
structure whose infinitesimal complex deformations are all integrable.
Then there is \(\varepsilon(\mathcal C,D,v)>0\) such that every normalized
negative Einstein metric on a member of \(\mathcal C\), of diameter at
most \(D\) and volume at least \(v\), satisfies
\[
 \mathscr S(g)<\varepsilon(\mathcal C,D,v)
                         \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_g=0. \tag{9}
\]
Such a metric is ASD or Kähler--Einstein on a cover of degree at most two.

It suffices more generally that each non-ASD zero-splitting limit have a
fixed finite cover on which it is KE with unobstructed complex
deformations. No uniform degree bound on these auxiliary covers is needed
for this qualitative contradiction argument.

**Proof.** Otherwise take nonzero-splitting metrics with \(\mathscr S\to0\).
Theorem 4.1 supplies smooth convergence on a fixed \(M\in\mathcal C\) to
\(g_\infty\) with \(b=0\). Derdziński's Proposition 5(iv) and Theorem 2
give the following alternatives for this compact negative Einstein limit:
\(W^+\equiv0\), or KE on the orientation cover of the lowest eigenline.
The nonparallel conformal-Kähler alternative produces a nonzero Killing
field on that compact cover, which negative Ricci curvature excludes by
Bochner.

In the ASD case, the chiral curvature operator is
\(R_+=W^++sI/12=-I/3\). Fine--Krasnov--Singer Theorem 1.2 gives local
Einstein rigidity. Late metrics, already known to converge smoothly, are
isometric to the limit and hence have \(b=0\).

In the KE case, fix the limit's eigenline cover (or the finite cover in the
generalized hypothesis) once and pull back every metric in the smoothly
identified sequence to it. There is no need to assert that the eigenlines
of the approximating metrics are orientable, or even simple. The lifted
metrics converge smoothly to the KE metric. By Koiso's local deformation
theory, in the form of Dai--Wang--Wei Theorem 3.3, every sufficiently close
Einstein metric is KE when all infinitesimal complex deformations of the
limit integrate. Its complex orientation agrees with that of the limit.
Thus the lifted late metrics have \(b=0\), and this local tensor identity
descends to \(M\), a contradiction. \(\square\)

The conclusion does not assume closeness to a chosen moduli neighborhood:
Theorem 4.1 and zero-splitting classification prove that every violating
sequence enters one. The integrability hypothesis is genuine. No theorem
here says that every negative KE surface is unobstructed or that a
second-order deformation calculation controls the full Einstein germ.

## 6. A concrete exact classification on products of curves

**Theorem 6.1.** For every \(D>0\) there is \(\varepsilon(D)>0\) such that,
for all \(g,h\ge2\), every Einstein metric \(k\) on the oriented smooth
product \(M=\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h\) satisfying
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_k=-k,\quad \operatorname{diam}_kM\le D,
 \quad\fint_M(\lambda_3(W_k^+)-\lambda_2(W_k^+))^2
       <\varepsilon(D)                                    \tag{10}
\]
is locally isometric to
\(\mathbb H^2_{-1}\times\mathbb H^2_{-1}\).
In particular, the spectral splitting is exactly zero. No claim that the
given marking is an isometric product marking is needed.

**Proof.** The canonical class of the product is an actual monopole class
with square
\(a^2=2\chi(M)=8(g-1)(h-1)\). Its self-dual projection has square at
least \(a^2\). The classical scalar monopole estimate, with scalar
curvature \(-4\), gives
\[
 16V_k\ge32\pi^2a^2,\qquad
                  V_k\ge4\pi^2\chi(M)\ge16\pi^2.          \tag{11}
\]
All these products are aspherical. If (10) did not imply the conclusion,
Theorem 4.1 and (11) would give smooth convergence of a violating sequence
with \(\mathscr S\to0\), modulo diffeomorphisms, on a fixed smooth product
\(M\). Its limiting Einstein metric \(k_\infty\) has \(b=0\).

An ASD limit is impossible. Since \(\tau(M)=0\), the signature formula
would then also force \(W^-=0\). The Einstein limit would have constant
sectional curvature \(-1/3\), contrary to Preissmann's theorem because
\(\pi_1(M)\) contains \(\mathbb Z^2\). Hence the limit is KE on its
lowest-eigenline orientation cover.

Here is the cover argument with the topology and marking retained. Identify
\(\pi_1(M)=\Pi_g\times\Pi_h\) using the smooth product. The orientation
character of the limit eigenline is
\[
 \xi:\Pi_g\times\Pi_h\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_2,
 \qquad \xi(a,b)=\xi_1(a)+\xi_2(b).
\]
Let \(K_i=\ker\xi_i\), allowing \(K_i=\Pi_i\). The subgroup
\(K_1\times K_2\subset\ker\xi\) defines a product cover
\[
 P=\Sigma_{g'}\times\Sigma_{h'}\longrightarrow M
                      \quad\text{of degree at most four}. \tag{12}
\]
It dominates the eigenline double cover; when \(\xi=0\), take \(P=M\).
Use precisely this one cover of the smoothly identified limiting manifold
for every member of the sequence. Thus the pulled-back metrics converge
smoothly on one fixed product \(P\) to a global negative KE metric.

The limit complex surface on \(P\) has ample canonical bundle, and is
projective. Catanese's Theorem 3.4, specifically its degree-one case proved
in Steps I--II on pages 16--18, applies because
\[
 \pi_1(P)=\Pi_{g'}\times\Pi_{h'},\qquad
 \chi(P)=4(g'-1)(h'-1).
\]
It identifies this complex surface biholomorphically with a product of
curves. Both the group and Euler conditions are used; a group-only
characterization is not being asserted. Uniqueness of the negative KE
metric then makes the lifted limit the normalized hyperbolic product.

All infinitesimal complex deformations of this product integrate. Indeed,
\(H^0(C_i,TC_i)=0\), and Künneth gives
\[
 H^1(C_1\times C_2,T(C_1\times C_2))
       =H^1(C_1,TC_1)\oplus H^1(C_2,TC_2).                 \tag{13}
\]
The product of the two smooth Teichmüller deformation families has
Kodaira--Spencer map equal to (13), and supplies all infinitesimal
directions. This proves unobstructedness; it does not use the generally
false assertion that \(H^2(TP)=0\).

Dai--Wang--Wei Theorem 3.3 now makes every sufficiently late lifted Einstein
metric KE. Applying Catanese's same degree-one statement to its complex
structure, and negative KE uniqueness, makes each such metric a hyperbolic
product as well. Local product geometry descends through (12), contradicting
the choice of sequence. Constants depend only on \(D\): the volume lower
bound in (11) is uniform, and the compactness contradiction selects its
own limit and local deformation neighborhood. \(\square\)

This proves an exact conclusion for the metric under test, not merely the
existence of some Einstein metric on its underlying manifold. The latter
was already known for products. It does not classify all Einstein metrics
on products without a small-splitting or bounded-diameter hypothesis, and
does not assert a globally oriented complex eigenline on the quotient.

## 7. The other allowed endpoint: a uniform ASD isolation statement

**Corollary 7.1.** For every \(D,v>0\) there is \(\eta(D,v)>0\) such that
a closed oriented four-manifold satisfying (TF2), with
\(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\), \(\operatorname{diam}\le D\), \(V\ge v\), obeys
\[
                \fint|W^+|^2<\eta(D,v)
                         \quad\Longrightarrow\quad W^+=0. \tag{14}
\]

**Proof.** Since \(b^2\le2|W^+|^2\), a counterexample sequence with the
left side tending to zero is smoothly precompact by Theorem 4.1. The
limit is ASD. Fine--Krasnov--Singer local rigidity at
\(R_+=-I/3\) makes all late normalized Einstein metrics isometric to the
limit, contradicting \(W^+\not\equiv0\). \(\square\)

This is a uniform integral criterion in a global bounded regime. The local
chiral rigidity theorem used to prove it is classical, not a new
infinitesimal rigidity theorem.

## 8. The same compactness removes pinching from the shear output gap

For the actual self-gluings \(M(q,g;u,f)\) of
\(F_{q,2}\times\Sigma_g\), take \(q\ge1\), \(g\ge2\), nonzero
\([u]\in H^1(\Sigma_g;\mathbb Z)\), and the precise marked gluing
\((\phi,x)\mapsto(-\phi+u(x),f(x))\) of the inherited theorem. Its
topological and monopole conclusions are inputs: asphericity,
\(\tau=0\), a subgroup \(\mathbb Z^2\subset\pi_1\), no finite Kähler
cover, and an actual class of square \(2\chi>0\).

**Corollary 8.1.** For each \(D>0\), all exact \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\)
metrics on these actual self-gluings with diameter at most \(D\) satisfy
\[
                              \mathscr S(g)\ge\eta_D>0.    \tag{15}
\]
In particular the previous hypothesis \(b\le4\theta t\),
\(\theta<1/\sqrt3\), is no longer required.

**Proof.** The inherited monopole estimate supplies
\(V\ge4\pi^2\chi\ge16\pi^2\). A sequence violating (15) converges
smoothly by Theorem 4.1 to a zero-splitting Einstein metric on the same
smooth manifold as each sufficiently late term. Its KE-cover alternative
contradicts the virtual Kähler obstruction. Its ASD alternative gives
constant negative sectional curvature by \(\tau=0\), contradicting
\(\mathbb Z^2\subset\pi_1\). \(\square\)

This covers every exact negative Einstein output in the bounded-diameter
regime, not only metrics near the explicit gluing construction. It still
allows hypothetical outputs with definite spectral splitting or unbounded
diameter. It is not global Einstein nonexistence or a Yau26 counterexample.

## 9. What has and has not been connected

The actual implication proved here is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{exact negative Einstein + (TF2) + diameter/volume bounds}\\
 +\ \text{small averaged upper Weyl splitting}\\
 \Longrightarrow\ \text{smooth compactness}\\
 \Longrightarrow\ \text{ASD or KE-covered limit}\\
 +\ \text{an exhausted local Einstein germ}\\
 \Longrightarrow\ \text{exact allowed geometry}.
 \end{gathered}                                             \tag{16}
\]
The product application verifies the local-germ condition on an actual
family; the ASD application uses classical chiral rigidity. Neither
replaces a missing integrability assumption by a small-error estimate.
For a general obstructed negative KE surface, whether a nearby non-Kähler
Einstein branch exists is not decided here. Likewise, the diameter and
volume regime is not supplied for arbitrary Yau26 inputs. These are the
remaining mathematical arrows, not conclusions to be inserted into (16).

The contribution relative to the inherited project is the spectral
no-bubbling bridge and its uniform exact-output consequences. The Weyl
identity, ALE classification, local moduli exhaustion, and complex product
characterization are external or inherited ingredients. The uniform
constants are qualitative; no sharpness or global novelty is claimed.

# Removing the Weyl hypothesis by the topology of ALE embeddings

24 September 2026. This note supplies the previously missing input in the
[compact-regime realization proof](20260923_compact_realization.md).
The stronger ALE nonembedding theorem is **an existing result**, not a new
classification discovered here: it is Zhang's theorem with the noncompact
extension stated explicitly by Bamler--Chen. Its application changes the
scope of the project's realization theorem substantially.

In particular, the residual possibility recorded in
[the preceding topology note](20260923_topological_core_scope.md), namely a
mixed-chirality rational-ball bubble in the universal cover, can now be
excluded. No classification of arbitrary Ricci-flat ALE metrics is assumed.

## 1. The classical input that closes the remaining bubble alternative

**ALE nonembedding lemma (Zhang; Bamler--Chen).** Let \(N\) be a smooth
four-manifold, possibly noncompact, with
\[
 H_2(N;\mathbb Z)=0,\qquad H_1(N;\mathbb Z)\text{ torsion-free}.
 \tag{1}
\]
No compact smooth domain \(B\subset N\) has interior diffeomorphic to a
nonflat complete Ricci-flat ALE four-manifold.

The precise noncompact formulation is
[Bamler--Chen, Lemma 4.35, p. 41](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.03154),
which cites [Zhang, Theorem 6.1 and Lemmas 6.2--6.5,
pp. 378--382](https://msp.org/pjm/2019/303-1/pjm-v303-n1-p14-s.pdf).
In the published version of Bamler--Chen, the statement is still Lemma 4.35:
[DOI 10.1002/cpa.70024](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.70024).
The isolated \(H_1(M)\) in its printed statement denotes the ambient
\(H_1(N)\), as its following proof makes explicit. We use the lemma only
when this group is zero.

Here is the mechanism, to distinguish this input from the unavailable
classification of all Ricci-flat ALE spaces. In an ambient with
\(H_1=H_2=0\), write \(Y=\partial B=S^3/\Gamma\) and
\(C=N\setminus\operatorname{int}B\). Integral Mayer--Vietoris gives
\[
 H_2(B;\mathbb Z)=0,\qquad
 H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\simeq H_1(B;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_1(C;\mathbb Z).
 \tag{2}
\]
The ALE fundamental group is finite, and the end group surjects onto it.
Thus \(H_1(B,Y;\mathbb Z)=0\). Duality and the universal coefficient
theorem identify the kernel of \(H_1(Y)\to H_1(B)\) with a group
isomorphic to \(H_1(B)\). The splitting in (2) respects this projection,
so
\[
                  H_1(Y)\simeq G\oplus G.               \tag{3}
\]
This is stronger than merely requiring that \(|H_1(Y)|\) be a square.

Zhang's spherical-space-form calculation reduces the nontrivial possibilities
in (3) to binary dihedral groups of order \(8n\), \(n\ge1\), and the
binary icosahedral group. His representation lemma and eta calculation give,
up to orientation,
\[
 |\eta(Y)|=\frac{8n^2+1}{12n}\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{361}{180},
 \qquad\text{respectively}.                            \tag{4}
\]
Both exceed \(2/3\). On the other hand, \(b_2(B)=0\), finite first
homology, connected boundary and duality give \(\chi(B)=1\), \(\tau(B)=0\).
Ricci-flat Chern--Gauss--Bonnet and APS signature, in curvature-operator
norms, imply
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_++E_-&=8\pi^2\left(1-|\Gamma|^{-1}\right),\\
 E_+-E_-&=12\pi^2\eta(Y),\qquad
 E_\pm=\int|W^\pm|^2\ge0.
 \end{split}                                           \tag{5}
\]
Consequently
\[
 |\eta(Y)|\le\frac23\left(1-|\Gamma|^{-1}\right)<\frac23,
 \tag{6}
\]
contradicting (4). The trivial end group gives zero total curvature in
(5), hence flatness. This explains why a mixed-chirality rational ball
does not evade the argument: nonnegativity of **both** energies is already
incompatible with its boundary data. The argument does not set either
Weyl half to zero.

For the quaternion test \(\Gamma=Q_8\), (3) is indeed possible: the
boundary has \(H_1=(\mathbb Z/2)^2\). It is (6), not this group
calculation alone, that excludes a Ricci-flat rational-ball filling:
\(3/4>7/12\). This separates the topological and geometric restrictions.

## 2. Vanishing second homotopy suffices; torsion in the first is allowed

**Theorem 1 (all-ALE-core exclusion).** Let \(M\) be a connected smooth
oriented four-manifold with \(\pi_2(M)=0\). No large compact core of a
nonflat complete smooth Ricci-flat ALE four-manifold embeds smoothly in
\(M\). No torsion-free assumption on \(\pi_1(M)\) is needed.

**Proof.** Let \(i:B\hookrightarrow M\) be such an embedding and let
\(p:\widetilde M\to M\) be the universal cover. Hurewicz gives
\[
 H_1(\widetilde M;\mathbb Z)=H_2(\widetilde M;\mathbb Z)=0.
 \tag{7}
\]
The fundamental group of a Ricci-flat ALE space is finite. A component
\(\widehat B\) of \(p^{-1}(i(B))\) is therefore a **finite** cover of
\(B\): its degree is the order of the finite image of
\(i_*:\pi_1(B)\to\pi_1(M)\). In particular, it is a compact smooth
domain in \(\widetilde M\). This step neither assumes that the core
fundamental group injects nor that its image is trivial.

The interior of \(\widehat B\) carries the lifted complete Ricci-flat
metric. A finite cover of an ALE space has finitely many lifted ALE ends
and Euclidean volume growth. It has only one end: if it had two, the
nonnegative-Ricci splitting theorem would split it as a line times a
compact manifold, contradicting Euclidean four-dimensional volume growth.
Thus the lifted space is itself nonflat Ricci-flat ALE. Apply the classical
lemma to its compact domain \(\widehat B\subset\widetilde M\), using
(7), to obtain a contradiction. \(\square\)

For completeness, finiteness of the ALE fundamental group can also be
seen directly from the end: take the cover corresponding to the image
of \(\pi_1(Y)\to\pi_1(B)\). This subgroup is finite. If its index
were greater than one, one lifted end has degree one and is separated
from the rest by a compact cross-section. The complete lifted metric
would have at least two ends and Euclidean lower volume growth, again
contradicting splitting. Therefore the end group surjects onto the full
fundamental group. This reasoning uses Ricci-flatness and the ALE end,
not any holonomy classification. The same end-cover/splitting argument
appears in [Wright, Section 3 and Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1102.2442);
only that argument is used, not the paper's half-flat classification.

The conclusion is strictly broader in topology than the previous condition
\(\pi_1\) torsion-free, \(\pi_2=0\). For example,
\(L(p,q)\times S^1\) has vanishing \(\pi_2\) and fundamental group
\((\mathbb Z/p)\times\mathbb Z\); homotopy four-spheres also have
vanishing \(\pi_2\). These illustrate the topological scope, not a claim
that every example has the near-Einstein inputs of the next theorem.

## 3. Bounded-geometry realization without gauge saturation or Weyl bounds

**Theorem 2 (harmonic compactness).** Fix \(K,D,v>0\) and
\(0<\alpha<1\). Closed connected smooth oriented four-manifolds with
\[
 \pi_2(M)=0,\qquad
 \|\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}\le K,
 \qquad\operatorname{diam}(g)\le D,\qquad V_g\ge v
 \tag{8}
\]
have a uniformly positive \(C^{1,\alpha}\) harmonic radius. They are
precompact modulo diffeomorphisms in \(C^{1,\beta}\), \(\beta<1\), and
weak \(W^{2,p}\), \(p<\infty\), and have finitely many smooth types.

**Proof.** The analytic argument of
[compact realization, Sections 3--4](20260923_compact_realization.md)
is used up to, but not including, its one-half Weyl step. Bishop--Gromov
gives uniform noncollapse on fixed scales and a total-volume upper bound.
Jiang--Naber supplies the full curvature-energy bound. If the minimum
harmonic radius \(\rho_j\) tends to zero, rescale by \(\rho_j^{-2}\).
The normalized harmonic atlases converge, Ricci tends uniformly to zero,
and the harmonic-coordinate metric equation upgrades convergence locally
to strong \(W^{2,p}\) for finite \(p\). The limit is smooth Ricci-flat.
Recovery of harmonic charts with strict margins excludes a flat limit.

Euclidean volume growth and finite curvature energy make this limit ALE
by Bando--Kasue--Nakajima. Only positive-order ALE asymptotics are needed;
no optimal decay-order assertion is used. Its smooth compact core embeds
in each sufficiently late \(M_j\), contradicting Theorem 1. There is
**no need to force \(W^+\) or \(W^-\) to vanish**. The remaining
finite-atlas compactness argument is unchanged. \(\square\)

**Theorem 3 (same-smooth-type realization).** Fix \(D,v>0\). There is
\(\varepsilon(D,v)>0\), uniform over smooth types and
\(\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}\), such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \pi_2(M)=0,\qquad \operatorname{diam}(g)\le D,\qquad V_g\ge v,\\
 \delta_\sigma(g):=
 \|\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}_g-\sigma I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}
          <\varepsilon(D,v)
 \end{gathered}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists h\text{ on }M:\ \operatorname{Ric}_h=\sigma h.
 \tag{9}
\]

**Proof.** Otherwise take counterexamples with the fixed data and
\(\delta_{\sigma_j}(g_j)\to0\). Pass to a subsequence with constant
sign. Theorem 2 applies with \(K=2\) to late terms and identifies them
with a fixed smooth manifold. In harmonic coordinates the limiting
metric satisfies \(\operatorname{Ric}=\sigma g\) weakly. Elliptic
regularity makes it smooth. Pull it back to the original smooth manifolds,
contradicting their defining absence of such an Einstein metric.
\(\square\)

In the positive case, \(\delta_+<1/2\) gives
\(\operatorname{Ric}\ge g/2\); Bonnet--Myers supplies
\(\operatorname{diam}\le\pi\sqrt6\). Thus for each \(v>0\),
\(\pi_2=0\), \(V_g\ge v\), and sufficiently small \(\delta_+\)
already imply a positive Einstein metric, with threshold depending only
on \(v\). This still retains the noncollapse input.

On the negative side an actual conformally solvable monopole class with
\((a_g^+)^2\ge A_0>0\) gives
\[
 V_g\ge\frac{2\pi^2 A_0}{(1+\delta_-(g))^2}
       \ge\frac{8\pi^2 A_0}{9}\quad(\delta_-\le1/2).
 \tag{10}
\]
Hence (9) yields a threshold \(\varepsilon(D,A_0)\) from this actual
class input and diameter alone. Unlike the preceding KE realization
theorem, **no smallness of \(\mathfrak D_2/V\) is required**. The
conclusion is negative Einstein, not necessarily Kähler--Einstein.
That already satisfies the existence output of the adopted Yau26 problem.

## 4. What is resolved, and what is not

This closes the entire compact, noncollapsed bubble alternative on
\(\pi_2=0\) topology. A hypothetical negative-side Yau26 counterexample
sequence in that class, with uniform positive actual projection budget,
must have diameters tending to infinity: any bounded-diameter subsequence
would contradict (9)--(10). No near-saturation is necessary for this
escape conclusion.

On the positive side, if \(\pi_2(M_j)=0\), no \(M_j\) admits any
Einstein metric, and \(\delta_+(g_j)\to0\), then necessarily
\(V_{g_j}\to0\). Otherwise a subsequence has \(V_{g_j}\ge v>0\),
and Myers plus Theorem 3 produces a positive Einstein metric on every
sufficiently late member of that subsequence. Thus, within the
\(\pi_2=0\) topology, the two signs have different remaining escape
mechanisms: positive candidates must collapse in total volume; negative
candidates with a positive actual projection budget must escape in
diameter.

The original dimension-only problem still allows diameter escape,
collapse without a supplied volume budget, and topology with nonzero
\(\pi_2\). Those are not excluded here. The theorem does not decide
whether the fixed non-Kähler shear examples admit general Einstein metrics:
their known approximating sequence has diverging diameter.

The old near-saturation theorem retains its independent stronger geometric
output: its actual forms recover a global parallel form and hence KE.
For **general Einstein existence** in the compact regime, however,
saturation and the one-sided Weyl assumptions are now unnecessary.

The mathematical increment in this note is the explicit composition of
the full ALE nonembedding input, the finite-cover lift, and the preserved
almost-Einstein compactness argument. Zhang's boundary/eta obstruction and
Bamler--Chen's noncompact formulation are credited as existing results.
No assertion of world-first priority for the resulting compact-regime
corollary, or classification of all Ricci-flat ALE spaces, is made.

For the historical comparison, [Anderson's survey, Corollary 5.8](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~anderson/einsurv.pdf)
already gives exclusion of the orbifold boundary of the exact Einstein
moduli space under vanishing \(H_2(M;\mathbf F)\) for every field.
Bamler--Chen explicitly credit Anderson's earlier work for the
nonembedding idea. The composition here uses the later explicit
noncompact lemma, the finite-cover lift under \(\pi_2=0\), and
bounded-Ricci harmonic compactness to handle varying smooth types and
pointwise approximate Einstein inputs. This distinguishes the statements
being used; it does not establish that this composed corollary was absent
from the literature.

# Bilateral monopole saturation and exact bidisk realization

24 September 2026. A continuation of the actual-form and compact-realization
arguments. The new feature is the removal of **every topological no-bubble
hypothesis**: saturation in both orientations eliminates the complete Weyl
curvature on a harmonic-radius blowup. The resulting two global parallel
forms give an exact locally symmetric metric on the original smooth manifold.
This is a composition theorem, not a new construction of a bidisk quotient
or a claim of unrestricted Yau26 realization.

## 1. Conventions and theorem

Let \(M\) be a closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold. Write
\(M_+=M\) and \(M_-=-M\). A Spin\(^c\) structure on \(M_\sigma\)
is called an **actual conformal input for \(g\)** here if its unperturbed
Seiberg–Witten equations are solvable for every metric in \([g]\). The
structure is fixed while the positive conformal weight varies. It may depend
on the input metric. The two structures below are independent; no common
spinor, connection, or equation between the orientations is postulated.

Let \(a_\sigma\) be their real first Chern classes, and set
\[
 p_+=\|\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal H^+_g}a_+\|_{L^2}^2,
 \qquad
 p_-=\|\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal H^-_g}a_-\|_{L^2}^2.
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(p_-\) is positive energy, not its negative square in the original
orientation. All norms on \(W^\pm:\Lambda^\pm\to\Lambda^\pm\) are
Hilbert–Schmidt operator norms, norms on two-forms are exterior-algebra
norms, and Ricci uses the tensor norm. Define
\[
 \delta=\|\operatorname{Ric}_g^\sharp+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}},
 \qquad r=\fint_M|\operatorname{Ric}_g+g|^2\le4\delta^2,
 \qquad V=\operatorname{Vol}(g),
 \tag{2}
\]
\[
 \mathfrak D_\sigma
 =\frac32\int_M(s^2+12|W^\sigma|^2)-72\pi^2p_\sigma,
 \qquad q_\sigma=\mathfrak D_\sigma/V\ge0.
 \tag{3}
\]
Nonnegativity is the classical monopole curvature inequality in the
respective orientation.

**Theorem A (bilateral realization).** For every \(D,A_0>0\) there exists
\(\varepsilon(D,A_0)>0\) with the following property. Suppose \(M,g\)
has actual conformal inputs in both orientations, with
\[
 p_+>0,\quad p_->0,\quad \max\{p_+,p_-\}\ge A_0,
 \qquad \operatorname{diam}(g)\le D,
 \tag{4}
\]
and
\[
                    \delta+q_++q_-<\varepsilon(D,A_0).
 \tag{5}
\]
Then the **same smooth manifold** admits a smooth metric \(h\) with
\(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\) whose universal Riemannian cover is
\[
                    \mathbb H^2_{-1}\times\mathbb H^2_{-1}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Moreover, \(h\) admits global parallel compatible complex structures
\(J_+,J_-\) inducing the two opposite orientations. In particular it is
negative Kähler–Einstein in both orientations.

There is no hypothesis on \(\pi_1(M)\), \(\pi_2(M)\), an ALE core,
the intersection form, or an a priori complex or symplectic structure.
No separate volume lower bound, Weyl bound, curvature-energy bound,
Sobolev constant, class-square cost, or Ricci-derivative bound is required.
The threshold is uniform over varying manifolds and actual class choices.

The conclusion is not that \(g\) is already Einstein, nor that \(M\)
is a globally marked direct product of two surfaces. A compact quotient of
the bidisk need not be a direct product. The actual inputs and smallness of
both deficits remain real restrictions. In particular, this theorem is
not the earlier one-sided theorem with all conditions simply removed.

If either actual integral Chern class has positive square in its own
orientation, its projection has \(p_\sigma\ge1\). Then one may set
\(A_0=1\). No negative-square bound on the other class is introduced.

## 2. The inherited gauge estimates and the volume regime

For each orientation put \(\kappa_\sigma=r+q_\sigma\). The
[actual-form theorem](../mathematics/current/line1_composed_application.md),
Sections 2–3, gives, when \(\kappa_\sigma\le1/2880\), a smooth global
form \(\omega_\sigma\in\Omega^\sigma(M)\) for every \(\eta>0\)
sufficiently small, such that
\[
 \|\omega_\sigma\|_\infty\le2\sqrt3,
 \qquad
 \fint(|\omega_\sigma|^2-2)^2\le720\kappa_\sigma+\eta,
 \tag{7}
\]
\[
 \fint|\nabla\omega_\sigma|^2
 \le13\kappa_\sigma+4\sqrt{2\kappa_\sigma}+\eta.
 \tag{8}
\]
These are its zeroth- and first-order conclusions. They have no
negative-square cost. The second-order estimate from that theorem is not
used. In particular, the proof below does not implicitly add the budget
that is needed for a different weak-Bianchi application.

The same classical completed-square estimate, before constructing forms,
gives, with \(w_\sigma=\sqrt6|W^\sigma|\),
\[
 \fint(w_\sigma-2)^2\le q_\sigma+2r\le2\kappa_\sigma.
 \tag{9}
\]
Indeed \(\int(s+2w_\sigma)^2\le2\mathfrak D_\sigma\), whereas
\(\fint(s+4)^2\le4r\), and
\(w_\sigma-2=[(s+2w_\sigma)-(s+4)]/2\).

Consider a sequence satisfying (4) and
\(\delta_j+q_{+,j}+q_{-,j}\to0\). For late terms,
\(\delta_j\le1/2\), so \(-3g_j\le\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}\le3g_j\).
Use the scalar monopole inequality in whichever orientation has
\(p_\sigma\ge A_0\):
\[
 32\pi^2 A_0\le\int s_-^2
 \le16(1+\delta_j)^2V_j,
 \qquad V_j\ge v_0:=8\pi^2A_0/9.
 \tag{10}
\]
The two choices of orientation give the same scalar curvature and volume.
Bishop–Gromov comparison and the diameter bound consequently give
\[
 V_j\le V_0(D),\qquad
 c(D,A_0)\rho^4\le\operatorname{Vol}_{g_j}B_\rho(x)
 \le C\rho^4\quad(0<\rho\le1).
 \tag{11}
\]
For example \(V_0(D)=V_{-1}(D+1)\) suffices; the lower estimate follows
by comparing with the entire manifold inside a \((D+1)\)-ball.

The elementary inequality
\(w^2/6\le (w-2)^2/3+4/3\) turns (9) into, for every measurable set
\(E\subset M_j\),
\[
 \int_E|W^\sigma_{g_j}|^2
 \le\frac43\operatorname{Vol}_{g_j}(E)
       +\frac23\kappa_{\sigma,j}V_j.
 \tag{12}
\]
This holds in **both** orientations. That is the information absent from
the one-sided realization theorem.

## 3. Topology-free exclusion of harmonic-radius collapse

**Lemma 1 (topology-free critical compactness).** Fix \(K,D,v>0\) and a
modulus \(\eta(\rho)\to0\) as \(\rho\downarrow0\). A family of closed
connected smooth four-manifolds satisfying
\[
 |\operatorname{Ric}|\le K,\qquad\operatorname{diam}\le D,
 \qquad V\ge v,\qquad
 \sup_x\int_{B_\rho(x)}(|W^+|^2+|W^-|^2)\le\eta(\rho)
 \tag{13a}
\]
has a uniform positive \(C^{1,\alpha}\) harmonic radius for each fixed
\(\alpha<1\). It is precompact modulo diffeomorphisms in
\(C^{1,\beta}\), \(\beta<1\), and weak finite \(W^{2,p}\), and has
finitely many smooth types. A sequence in this family with
\(\operatorname{Ric}+g\to0\) uniformly has a smooth negative Einstein
limit on its eventual smooth type and strong finite \(W^{2,p}\)
convergence. No topological, ALE, or separately supplied curvature-energy
hypothesis enters this lemma.

For the sequence in Section 2, (11)–(12) give a common modulus in (13a).
Given a target error, choose a tail for which the \(\kappa_{\sigma,j}\)
terms are small, then choose \(\rho\) small for its volume terms; the
remaining finitely many smooth metrics also have vanishing small-ball
energies. The proof below gives both the lemma and a direct proof for
the sequence, without naming that modulus.

Fix \(0<\alpha<1\) and a standard \(C^{1,\alpha}\) harmonic-radius
definition with fixed metric-comparability and scaled norm bounds. Suppose
the global minimum \(\rho_j\) tends to zero, attained at \(x_j\).
Rescale
\[
 \widehat g_j=\rho_j^{-2}g_j,
 \qquad r_h(\widehat g_j,x_j)=1,
 \qquad r_h(\widehat g_j,x)\ge1.
 \tag{13}
\]
The rescaled Ricci norm is \(O(\rho_j^2)\), and (11) gives a uniform
Euclidean lower growth estimate on every fixed rescaled ball. The
rescaled manifolds are complete, their diameters tend to infinity, and
the normalized harmonic charts give a pointed complete limit, initially
in \(C^{1,\beta}\), \(\beta<\alpha\).

For clarity, the regularity improvement uses the actual Ricci tensors,
not their derivatives. In a harmonic chart the metric equation is
\[
 g^{ab}\partial_a\partial_b g_{ij}
       =-2\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}+Q_{ij}(g,\partial g).
 \tag{14}
\]
Its limit is weakly Ricci-flat and hence smooth by elliptic bootstrapping.
Subtract the equation of this smooth limit from (14). The coefficients
converge in \(C^1\), the quadratic lower-order terms converge, and the
Ricci tensors converge uniformly to zero. Interior elliptic estimates
therefore give strong \(W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\) convergence for every
finite \(p\). Taking \(p>4/(1-\alpha)\) recovers strong
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) convergence on smaller charts. Curvature consequently
converges strongly in local finite \(L^p\) spaces. Compatible harmonic
charts give the usual embeddings of compact subsets into late terms.
These are the same harmonic-coordinate steps spelled out in
[the compact-realization proof](20260923_compact_realization.md), Section 4.

For any fixed \(R\), four-dimensional scale invariance and (12) give
\[
 \int_{B_R(x_j,\widehat g_j)}|W^\sigma_{\widehat g_j}|^2
 \le \frac43C(R\rho_j)^4
       +\frac23V_0(D)\kappa_{\sigma,j}\longrightarrow0
       \quad(\sigma=+,-).
 \tag{15}
\]
Thus **both** Weyl halves of the Ricci-flat limit vanish. The limit is
flat; for the general lemma, (15) is replaced by the bound
\(\eta(R\rho_j)\to0\), with exactly the same conclusion. A complete
smooth flat four-manifold with positive Euclidean
four-dimensional volume growth is \(\mathbb R^4\): a nontrivial infinite
flat deck group reduces the volume-growth degree, whereas a finite group
of Euclidean isometries fixes a point and cannot act freely.

On a Euclidean ball of radius larger than two the harmonic-coordinate
defining bounds have strict margins. Strong \(W^{2,p}\) convergence,
followed by solving the harmonic-coordinate Dirichlet problem on a
slightly smaller ball, transfers a chart of radius greater than one to
\(x_j\). This contradicts (13). Hence the original harmonic radii are
bounded below along the sequence.

This argument does not produce a nonflat ALE bubble and then try to
classify or forbid its core. It makes every putative blowup **flat before
any ALE theorem is invoked**. Neither a global full-curvature energy
estimate nor Jiang–Naber, Bando–Kasue–Nakajima, an ALE classification,
or a hypothesis on the smooth topology is needed here.

The diameter and harmonic-radius bounds now give a finite-atlas compact
limit on a closed smooth manifold. After a subsequence and
orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms \(\phi_j:N\to M_j\),
\[
 h_j:=\phi_j^*g_j\longrightarrow h
 \quad\text{in }C^{1,\alpha}\text{ and strong finite }W^{2,p},
 \qquad\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h.
 \tag{16}
\]
The same elliptic argument proves smoothness of \(h\); it does not claim
smooth convergence of the original approximate metrics. Smooth-type
preservation is the ordinary harmonic-atlas compactness conclusion, not
a homeomorphism of a possibly singular limit.

## 4. Two global forms force exact product geometry

Choose \(\eta_j\downarrow0\) and forms from (7)–(8) in both orientations.
Pull them back by the **same** diffeomorphisms in (16). Uniform boundedness,
the vanishing covariant derivatives in (8), and \(h_j\to h\) in \(C^1\)
make the two sequences bounded in \(H^1(N,h)\). After a common subsequence
they converge strongly in \(L^2\) to global forms \(\omega_+,\omega_-\).
The connection differences tend uniformly to zero; hence their
distributional covariant derivatives for \(h\) vanish. Elliptic
regularity and the amplitude estimates give
\[
 \nabla^h\omega_\pm=0,
 \qquad *_h\omega_\pm=\pm\omega_\pm,
 \qquad|\omega_\pm|_h^2=2.
 \tag{17}
\]
In particular, neither form vanishes. There is no eigenline sign choice
and no two-fold cover: the approximating forms were global.

Define \(J_\pm\) by \(h(J_\pm X,Y)=\omega_\pm(X,Y)\). In four
dimensions (17) gives \(J_\pm^2=-I\). Self-dual and anti-self-dual
skew endomorphisms commute, so
\[
 K=-J_+J_-,\qquad K^*=K,\qquad K^2=I,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}K=0,\qquad\nabla K=0.
 \tag{18}
\]
The trace follows equally from the orthogonality of the two forms.
Consequently \(TN=E_1\oplus E_2\), the two orthogonal rank-two parallel
eigenbundles of \(K\). Parallel distributions are integrable and their
leaves are totally geodesic. On the complete simply connected universal
cover the de Rham splitting is a global Riemannian product of two complete
simply connected surfaces. Since \(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\), the Ricci
tensor of each factor is minus its metric. In dimension two this means
constant Gaussian curvature \(-1\). Both factors are \(\mathbb H^2_{-1}\),
proving (6). Parallelness of \(J_\pm\) also proves their integrability
and the global Kähler assertions directly.

If Theorem A failed for fixed \(D,A_0\), choose counterexamples with
\(\delta_j+q_{+,j}+q_{-,j}<1/j\). Sections 2–4 give (16)–(18).
Pulling \(h,J_+,J_-\) forward to each late \(M_j\) contradicts its being
a counterexample. This proves a fixed threshold uniform across changing
smooth types. It does not assume that an individual small-error metric
can be improved into a whole sequence on its own manifold. \(\square\)

## 5. Raw Ricci-energy formulation

Let
\[
 T_\pm=2\chi(M)\pm3\tau(M),\quad
 R_2=\int_M|\operatorname{Ric}|^2,\quad
 S_2=\int_Ms^2,\quad P=\int_M|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2.
\]
Since \(R_2=P+S_2/4\) and
\(4\pi^2T_\sigma=S_2/24+2\int|W^\sigma|^2-P/2\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak D_\sigma
   =\frac92\bigl[R_2-8\pi^2(2p_\sigma-T_\sigma)\bigr].}
 \tag{19}
\]
This is a classical Chern–Weil identity, the exact deficit form of
LeBrun's Ricci-energy estimate. It is not a new inequality.

Suppose independent actual projection budgets are available,
\(p_\sigma\ge A_\sigma>0\), and
\(\max(A_+,A_-)\ge A_0\). Define the nonnegative averaged two-sided
energy excess
\[
 e_{\rm bi}
 =\frac{R_2-8\pi^2(A_++A_--2\chi)}{V}.
 \tag{20}
\]
Adding (19) and using \(T_++T_-=4\chi\) gives the exact decomposition
\[
 \boxed{
 e_{\rm bi}
 =\frac{q_++q_-}{9}
   +\frac{8\pi^2}{V}
      \bigl[(p_+-A_+)+(p_--A_-)\bigr].}
 \tag{21}
\]
Every term on the right is nonnegative. Thus no cancellation between the
orientations is hidden by the single energy condition.

**Corollary B (energy-to-geometry realization).** Under the actual-input,
budget and diameter assumptions just stated, without any topological
restriction, the condition
\[
                  \delta+9e_{\rm bi}<\varepsilon(D,A_0)
 \tag{22}
\]
implies the full conclusion of Theorem A.

Indeed (21) gives \(q_++q_-\le9e_{\rm bi}\), so (5) follows. This
replaces the two separately displayed functional deficits by a raw
Ricci-energy excess above the average of two genuine gauge bounds. It
does not replace the existence of the two actual inputs by numerical
cohomology data, and it does not claim that (20) is the attained global
Ricci-energy infimum on every supplied topology.

The exact counterpart using the actual projections is
\[
 q_++q_-=9\,\frac{R_2-8\pi^2(p_++p_--2\chi)}{V}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Neither (22) nor (23) follows from \(\delta\ll1\) alone. Their purpose
is a checkable interface for independent topological or variational inputs.

## 6. Consequences, tests, and scope

### 6.1 Topology is now a conclusion, not an assumption

Let \(\mathcal B\) denote the smooth manifolds admitting a metric with
universal Riemannian cover (6) and the two global parallel structures of
Theorem A. If \(M\notin\mathcal B\), then every input meeting (4) obeys
\[
                  \delta+q_++q_-\ge\varepsilon(D,A_0).
 \tag{24}
\]
Examples of independently checkable exclusions from \(\mathcal B\) are
\(\pi_2(M)\ne0\), torsion in \(\pi_1(M)\), or \(\tau(M)\ne0\).
Indeed (6) is contractible; a finite-order deck isometry of this Hadamard
space has a fixed point and cannot act freely; and for the product metric
both Weyl halves have the same squared norm \(2/3\), so the signature is
zero. The output also has \(\chi(M)>0\).

Thus the theorem does not merely improve a threshold within the old
no-ALE-core class. It tests inputs on **arbitrary** smooth four-manifolds
and, when they pass, deduces the topology and geometry that the earlier
realization theorem had partly assumed. The new condition is bilateral
saturation; neither theorem subsumes the other on all its inputs.

Equation (24) is a joint deficit gap, not a pointwise-only gap on every
non-bidisk manifold. No claim follows that such a manifold has no other
Einstein metric.

### 6.2 Actual equality models

For \(M=\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h\), \(g,h\ge2\), take the product of
curvature \(-1\) metrics and complex structures
\(J_+=(J_1,J_2)\), \(J_-=(J_1,-J_2)\). The canonical Spin\(^c\)
structures in the two orientations supply actual classes: both are
general-type Kähler surfaces with \(b_\pm>1\), and their canonical
Seiberg–Witten invariants are nonzero. Set
\(A=8(g-1)(h-1)=2\chi(M)\). Then
\[
 V=2\pi^2A,\quad p_+=p_-=A,
 \quad |W^+|^2=|W^-|^2=2/3,
 \quad \delta=q_+=q_-=e_{\rm bi}=0
 \tag{25}
\]
with \(A_+=A_-=A\). This verifies simultaneous saturation in a real
nonempty family. It is a classical equality model, not a newly constructed
family. The theorem's increment is the uniform implication for arbitrary
smooth inputs and changing topologies, without a presumed product structure.

Unequally scaled hyperbolic products remain cscK in both orientations and
have \(q_+=q_-=0\), while generally \(\delta\ne0\). They test why
simultaneous functional equality must not be identified with the input
metric itself being Einstein. Their underlying manifold already admits
the normalized product metric, consistently with the existence conclusion.

### 6.3 Diameter cannot simply be dropped, even with both inputs

The fixed nonzero-shear self-gluings of
[Round 11](../mathematics/current/round11_closed_gluing.md), Section 6,
carry symplectic structures in **both** orientations. Their canonical
classes are actual monopole inputs with
\(p_\pm\ge A=2\chi>0\); this follows from \(b_\pm>1\) and Taubes
as recorded in [the variational proof](../mathematics/current/round11_variational.md),
Sections 1–2. The actual normalized metrics \(g_\ell\) satisfy
\[
 \tau=0,\quad V_\ell=2\pi^2A,\quad
 \delta_\ell\to0,
 \tag{26}
\]
and [the global topology proof](../mathematics/current/round11_kahler.md)
excludes a Kähler structure on every finite cover.

Using (19), \(R_2/V\le4(1+\delta_\ell)^2\),
\(T_\pm=A\), and \(p_\pm\ge A\), one obtains the simultaneous bounds
\[
 0\le q_{\pm,\ell}
 \le18\bigl[(1+\delta_\ell)^2-1\bigr]
 =36\delta_\ell+18\delta_\ell^2\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{27}
\]
Thus bilateral actual saturation and pointwise almost-Einstein input
do occur on a fixed manifold with no bidisk metric, when diameter is
unrestricted. Theorem A forces \(\operatorname{diam}(g_\ell)\to\infty\).
Equation (27) uses the direct Ricci-energy estimate and is slightly tighter
than the separate scalar/traceless estimates in the one-sided note;
that numerical improvement is not a separate mathematical achievement.

These are inherited constructions and a new consequence, not a new
counterexample construction. They disprove diameter-free **bidisk or KE**
realization with these inputs. They do not decide whether those manifolds
carry a general non-Kähler Einstein metric, and therefore do not furnish
Yau26 counterexamples.

### 6.4 What is and is not new in the proof

The scalar and curvature-functional bounds, Chern–Weil algebra, harmonic
compactness and de Rham splitting are classical. The actual global form
estimates are inherited first-line results. The new composition uses the
**second actual orientation** twice: to rule out all harmonic-radius
blowups without topology, and to upgrade the limiting KE structure to an
exact bidisk realization. It thereby connects two-sided near-optimal
Ricci energy to exact geometry on the same smooth manifold.

The result establishes the Yau26 existence output on its stated inputs;
locally symmetric negative KE is more than the required output. The
remaining limitations are the diameter, actual bilateral class supply,
and simultaneous-saturation inputs. The proof supplies neither a
dimension-only threshold nor an effective numerical constant. No exhaustive
priority claim is made.

# Pointwise Einstein approximation on the Miyaoka–Yau numerical boundary

24 September 2026. This continuation removes externally prescribed saturation
and topological bubble-exclusion hypotheses at a realizable numerical boundary.
The Chern–Weil identities are classical; the new project conclusion is their
composition with compactness and actual-form supply. Global priority is not asserted.

## 1. The realization statement

Put \(T_\pm=2\chi\pm3\tau\) and
\[
 \delta(g)=\|\operatorname{Ric}_g^\sharp+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Use tensor Hilbert–Schmidt norms for Ricci, exterior norms for two-forms,
and endomorphism Hilbert–Schmidt norms for \(W^\pm\).

**Theorem A.** For every \(D>0\) there exists \(\varepsilon(D)>0\) with
the following property. Let \(M\) be a closed connected oriented smooth
four-manifold with
\[
 A:=T_+=3T_->0.                                           \tag{1}
\]
Let \(g\) have diameter at most \(D\). Suppose a Spin\(^c\) structure is
actually monopole-solvable for every metric conformally related to \(g\),
and its first Chern class \(a\) satisfies
\[
                          p=(a_g^+)^2\ge A.               \tag{2}
\]
If \(\delta(g)<\varepsilon(D)\), the **same oriented smooth manifold**
admits a complex-hyperbolic metric \(h\) with \(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\).
Its universal Riemannian cover is the complex hyperbolic plane of holomorphic
sectional curvature \(-2/3\).

No input Kähler structure, \(\pi_1\) or \(\pi_2\) hypothesis, Weyl bound,
class-square cost, Ricci-derivative bound, or functional-saturation assumption
is imposed. The class may depend on \(g\), but is fixed throughout its
conformal class. The threshold is uniform over the stated manifolds and classes.

Condition (1) is exactly \(\chi=3\tau>0\), not a general condition on
four-manifolds. Complex-hyperbolic existence fully supplies the Einstein
output required by the adopted Yau26 question on these inputs. The remaining
limitations are the diameter and numerical/gauge **input** conditions.

## 2. Saturation follows from the numerical boundary

Write
\[
 S=\int s^2,\quad P=\int|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2,\quad
 W_\pm=\int|W^\pm|^2,\quad V=\operatorname{Vol}(g),
\]
\[
 \mathfrak D_2=\frac32(S+12W_+)-72\pi^2p,\qquad q=\mathfrak D_2/V.
\]
The actual-monopole inequalities give
\[
 S\ge32\pi^2p\ge32\pi^2A,\qquad \mathfrak D_2\ge0.          \tag{3}
\]
The opposite-orientation Chern–Weil formula is exactly
\[
                    S-32\pi^2A+48W_-=12P.                \tag{4}
\]
Consequently
\[
 0\le W_-\le P/4,\qquad 0\le S-32\pi^2A\le12P.
\]
Eliminating \(W_+\) with the other Chern–Weil formula first gives
\[
 \mathfrak D_2=\frac98S+\frac92P+36\pi^2A-72\pi^2p.
\]
Substitution of (4) gives a second exact balance:
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak D_2+72\pi^2(p-A)+54W_-=18P.}             \tag{5}
\]
All terms on its left are nonnegative. Orthogonal trace decomposition and
\(|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2\le4\delta^2\) now imply
\[
 \boxed{q\le72\delta^2,\qquad W_-/V\le\delta^2.}           \tag{6}
\]
Thus the previously independent gauge deficit and opposite Weyl energy
are forced small by the **original pointwise input**. This implication is
specific to (1)–(2), not a general property of almost-Einstein metrics.

Volume is also controlled:
\[
 V\ge\frac{2\pi^2A}{(1+\delta)^2},\qquad
 |V-2\pi^2A|\le(2\delta+4\delta^2)V.                       \tag{7}
\]
The second bound follows from
\(|S/V-16|\le32\delta+16\delta^2\) and
\(0\le S-32\pi^2A\le48\delta^2V\). No volume normalization is assumed.

## 3. Both Weyl halves exclude concentration without topology

Suppose Theorem A were false for a fixed \(D\). Choose counterexamples
\((M_j,g_j,a_j)\) with \(\delta_j\to0\) and no complex-hyperbolic metric
on their oriented smooth types. For large \(j\), \(\delta_j\le1/2\).
The positive integer \(A_j=T_+(M_j)\) is at least one, so (7) gives
\(V_j\ge8\pi^2/9\). Ricci is bounded on both sides. Bishop comparison
and the diameter bound give \(V_j\le V_0(D)\) and
\[
 c(D)r^4\le \operatorname{Vol}_{g_j}B_r(x)\le Cr^4
 \quad(0<r\le1).                                        \tag{8}
\]

Set \(r_j=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}+g_j|^2\) and
\(\kappa_j=r_j+q_j\le76\delta_j^2\).
The elementary amplitude consequence of the classical completed-square
estimate, recorded in Section 4 of the
[gauge-realization proof](20260923_gauge_saturation_ke.md), gives
\[
 \int_E|W^+|^2\le\frac43\operatorname{Vol}(E)
                          +\frac23\kappa_jV_j            \tag{9}
\]
for every measurable set \(E\). Meanwhile (6) gives
\[
                  \int_{M_j}|W^-|^2\to0.                \tag{10}
\]
Both halves therefore vanish on every shrinking ball in a harmonic-radius
blowup. There is no half-flat ALE core left to exclude by topology.

For clarity, the compactness argument is the following. If the minimum
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) harmonic radius \(\rho_j\to0\), rescale by \(\rho_j^{-2}\).
All harmonic radii are at least one, the base radius is one, and Ricci
tends uniformly to zero. Harmonic-coordinate compactness, (8), and the
metric equation give a complete smooth Ricci-flat limit with Euclidean
lower volume growth. Subtracting the limiting metric equation improves
convergence to strong local \(W^{2,p}\), all finite \(p\), recovering the
Hölder exponent used to normalize the harmonic radius. This uses no
derivative of Ricci. Section 4 of
[compact realization](20260923_compact_realization.md) supplies this
analytic argument before its ALE step.

Scale invariance of Weyl energy, (8)–(10), and strong curvature convergence
give \(W^+=W^-=0\) on the limit. Thus it is flat. A complete smooth flat
manifold with Euclidean lower volume growth at all scales is Euclidean:
an infinite deck group lowers the growth order, and a finite Euclidean
isometry group cannot act freely unless trivial. Harmonic charts on a
larger Euclidean ball transfer back and contradict the normalized base
harmonic radius. Hence harmonic radii are uniformly positive.

This particular proof needs neither a Jiang–Naber total-energy bound, nor
ALE asymptotics or classification, nor any homotopy restriction.
Global diameter and (8) give a finite atlas. After diffeomorphisms and a
subsequence the metrics converge on a common smooth oriented manifold
in \(C^{1,\alpha}\) and strongly in finite \(W^{2,p}\) to a smooth metric
\(h\) with \(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\) and \(W_h^-=0\).
No convergence of every derivative of the approximate metrics is asserted.

## 4. Global complex structure and the exact output

The retained
[actual-form theorem](../mathematics/current/line1_composed_application.md)
supplies global self-dual forms \(\omega_j\), of uniformly bounded amplitude,
with
\[
 \fint(|\omega_j|^2-2)^2\to0,\qquad
 \fint|\nabla\omega_j|^2\to0.                             \tag{11}
\]
These first-order estimates require no negative-square budget.
Transfer the forms to the common smooth manifold. The volume bounds and
\(C^1\) metric convergence give a fixed-metric \(H^1\) bound. Rellich
compactness gives strong \(L^2\) convergence to \(\omega\), and (11) gives
\(\nabla_h\omega=0\), \(|\omega|^2=2\). Thus \(\omega\) is smooth and
defines a global parallel orthogonal complex structure \(J\).
It is integrable and has the prescribed orientation; no double cover occurs.

A Kähler–Einstein surface with \(W^-=0\) has vanishing Bochner curvature,
hence constant holomorphic sectional curvature. With \(\operatorname{Ric}=-h\),
that curvature is \(-2/3\). Completeness identifies the universal cover
with the complex hyperbolic plane. Pull this exact metric back to each
sufficiently late \(M_j\), contradicting the counterexample choice.
This proves the uniform \(\varepsilon(D)\).

## 5. Suppliers, examples, and the actual increment

An actual conformally solvable class with \(a^2\ge T_+>0\) automatically
satisfies (2), because \(p=a^2+\|a_g^-\|^2\). A nonzero ordinary SW
invariant in nonnegative formal dimension, with applicable chamber
conditions, is a classical supplier. A formal cohomology vector is not.
For \(b_+=1\), retain the metricwise polarized/chamber argument.

Compact complex-hyperbolic surfaces and their canonical classes are real
equality models:
\[
 S=32\pi^2A,\quad P=W_-=\mathfrak D_2=0,\quad p=A,\quad V=2\pi^2A.
\]
The theorem does not assume the input smooth type already supports such a
structure. Conversely, on a smooth type satisfying the stated gauge supply
and (1), but not supporting complex-hyperbolic geometry, it gives a
diameter-dependent **pointwise defect gap**, not merely a joint deficit gap.

The zero-defect classification is classical Seiberg–Witten/Miyaoka–Yau
rigidity; (4)–(5) are classical identities. The project increment is the
uniform approximate-to-exact conclusion, with saturation and earlier
topological bubble-exclusion assumptions removed. The older unrestricted
integral obstacles remain distinct: they have no diameter hypothesis and
cover incompatible numerical regions. Here a compatible numerical boundary
produces an actual exact geometry.

No optimal threshold, new non-complex-hyperbolic example, or unrestricted
Yau26 solution is claimed.
