# Step 37: normalized transported-coefficient DeTurck short-time existence

## 0. Purpose

Prove short-time smooth existence for the normalized fixed-cutoff flow
$$
\partial_t g = -2a(\operatorname{Ric}_g + g),
\qquad a=\chi^2,\qquad g(0)=g_0,
\tag{0.1}
$$
on a closed smooth 4-manifold $M$, with $\chi\in C^\infty(M)$, $0\le\chi\le1$.
This replaces the incorrect Step 35 existence note. The correct object is the
**transported-coefficient DeTurck system** of Yang's local Ricci flow
(retrieved source, Theorem 8.2, eqs. 8.5--8.6 at lines 804--807 of
`Knowledge/objects/papers/Convergence-of-riemannian-manifolds-with-integral-bounds--2b2a6e7ac566--6b3174861309/object.md`),
augmented by the zeroth-order normalization term $-2ag$.

The Preceptor's Step 36 correction (`step36_preceptor_interface_review.md` §2)
is the starting point: a fixed-coefficient gauge term $a\mathcal L_Wg$ does not
pull back to (0.1), because
$$
\mathcal L_{aW}g = a\mathcal L_Wg + da\otimes W^\flat + W^\flat\otimes da,
\tag{0.2}
$$
and the cutoff scalar is transported by the diffeomorphism. The correct system
carries the coefficient inside the gauge mechanism and uses a hatted transported
cutoff.

## 1. The exact source theorem (unnormalized)

Yang's Theorem 8.2 states: for $M$ closed (or after modifying $M$ outside
$\operatorname{supp}\chi$), the equation
$$
\partial_t g = -2\chi^2\operatorname{Rc}(g),\qquad g(0)=g_0
\tag{1.1}
$$
has a smooth solution on $[0,T]$ for some $T>0$. His Approach 1 fixes an
invertible symmetric background tensor $S$ (e.g. $S=g_0$), defines
$$
\hat G(S)_{ij}=S_{ij}-\tfrac12\hat g^{pq}S_{pq}\hat g_{ij},
\qquad
\hat\delta(S)_i=-\hat g^{pq}\hat\nabla_qS_{ip},
$$
and solves for the pair $(\hat g,\varphi_t)$:
$$
\partial_t\hat g
= -2\Big[(\chi\circ\varphi_t^{-1})^2\operatorname{Rc}(\hat g)
        -\hat\delta^*\big((\chi\circ\varphi_t^{-1})^2\,S^{-1}\delta G(S)\big)\Big],
\tag{1.2}
$$
$$
\partial_t\varphi = -\chi^2\,\varphi_{t*}\big[S^{-1}\delta G(S)\big]^\sharp,
\qquad \varphi_0=\mathrm{I}.
\tag{1.3}
$$
The gauge coefficient in (1.2) is the **transported** cutoff
$(\chi\circ\varphi_t^{-1})^2 = \hat a$, not a fixed $a$. Setting
$g=\varphi_t^*\hat g$ recovers (1.1). The linearized system (Yang 8.7--8.8,
lines 810--813) is
$$
\partial_t h = 2\chi^2\Delta h + \chi(B\nabla h + C\nabla\psi) + Dh + E\psi,
\qquad h(0)=0,
\tag{1.4}
$$
$$
\partial_t\psi = -\chi^2(P\nabla\psi + Q\nabla h + Rh),\qquad \psi(0)=0.
\tag{1.5}
$$
Existence follows by smooth tame estimates from energy integrals and
interpolation, with a regularization $\varepsilon^2\Delta h$ used only inside the
**linearized** problem to make it strictly parabolic; the tame energy estimates
are $\varepsilon$-independent, so $\varepsilon\to0$ recovers a smooth solution of
(1.4)--(1.5). Approach 2 regularizes the coefficient itself,
$\partial_t g = -2(\varepsilon^2+\chi^2)\operatorname{Rc}(g)$, and obtains
$\varepsilon$-independent $L^2$ curvature energy bounds giving a uniform $T$ and a
limit solving (1.1).

Both approaches are already proved in the source for the unnormalized flow. Our
task is to add the normalization $+g$.

## 2. The normalized transported system

Write the normalized velocity as
$$
\mathcal R(g) := \operatorname{Ric}_g + g,
\tag{2.1}
$$
so (0.1) is $\partial_t g=-2a\mathcal R(g)$. The transported-coefficient DeTurck
system for the normalized flow is
$$
\boxed{\;
\partial_t\hat g
= -2\Big[\hat a\big(\operatorname{Ric}_{\hat g}+\hat g\big)
        -\hat\delta^*\big(\hat a\,S^{-1}\delta G(S)\big)\Big],
\qquad \hat g(0)=g_0,\;}
\tag{2.2}
$$
$$
\partial_t\varphi = -\chi^2\,\varphi_{t*}\big[S^{-1}\delta G(S)\big]^\sharp,
\qquad \varphi_0=\mathrm{I},
\qquad \hat a:=(\chi\circ\varphi_t^{-1})^2 .
\tag{2.3}
$$
The only change from (1.2)--(1.3) is the extra term $-2\hat a\,\hat g$ inside the
bracket of (2.2). Everything else, including the transported coefficient
$\hat a$ and the gauge $\hat\delta^*$, is Yang's.

**Pullback identity.** With $g=\varphi_t^*\hat g$ and the diffeomorphism ODE
(2.3), $\varphi_t^*\hat a=\chi^2=a$ (the transport equation
$\partial_t\hat a=(\hat a W)\hat a$ is exactly the statement that $\hat a$ is
$\varphi_t$-transported from $\chi^2$). Then
$$
\frac{d}{dt}g
= \varphi_t^*\Big(\partial_t\hat g
  + \mathcal L_{\partial_t\varphi_t\circ\varphi_t^{-1}}\hat g\Big).
$$
The Lie-derivative term generated by (2.3) is precisely
$\varphi_t^*\big(2\hat\delta^*(\hat a S^{-1}\delta G(S))\big)$ — this is Yang's
DeTurck cancellation and does **not** involve the normalization, because the
gauge field in (2.3) is unchanged. The remaining bracket pulls back as
$$
\varphi_t^*\big(-2\hat a(\operatorname{Ric}_{\hat g}+\hat g)\big)
= -2a\big(\operatorname{Ric}_g + g\big),
$$
using naturality of Ricci under pullback, $\varphi_t^*\hat g=g$, and
$\varphi_t^*\hat a=a$. Hence $g=\varphi_t^*\hat g$ solves (0.1). $\square$

The verification `verify_step36_interface_algebra.py` PASS 1--2 already confirm
the two algebraic facts underlying this identity: the product rule (0.2) and
that pullback cancellation requires the gauged term $\mathcal L_{\hat aW}$, not a
fixed $a\mathcal L_W$.

## 3. Linearization: the normalization is a bounded zeroth-order perturbation

Linearize (2.2) about a solution, with $h=\delta\hat g$, $\psi=\delta\varphi$
(coupled through $\hat a=(\chi\circ\varphi_t^{-1})^2$). The linearization of the
Yang bracket $-2[\hat a\operatorname{Rc}(\hat g)-\hat\delta^*(\cdots)]$ is exactly
Yang's (1.4)--(1.5). The **only new term** is the linearization of $-2\hat a\hat g$:
$$
\delta(-2\hat a\hat g)
= -2\hat a\,h - 2(\delta\hat a)\,\hat g .
\tag{3.1}
$$
Here $\delta\hat a$ is driven by $\psi$ through the transport of $\chi$, so
$\delta\hat a = 2\hat\chi\cdot d\hat\chi(\psi)$ has a single $\hat\chi$ factor
(not $\hat\chi^2$, since $\hat a=\hat\chi^2$ and the chain rule gives
$\delta\hat a=2\hat\chi\,\delta\hat\chi$).  Thus (3.1) contributes:
- a term $-2\hat a\,h$, of the form $Dh$ with $D=O(1)$ bounded, absorbed into the
  matrix $D$ of (1.4);
- a term $-2(\delta\hat a)\hat g$, of the form $E'\psi$ (or $\chi\,C'\nabla\psi$)
  with bounded coefficients, absorbed into $E$ (or $C$) of (1.4).

Neither term is principal (order $\le 1$ in $h,\psi$), and neither changes the
principal symbol $2\chi^2\Delta$ of the $h$-equation or the transport structure
of the $\psi$-equation. Therefore the linearized normalized system has the same
form (1.4)--(1.5) with modified bounded lower-order matrices $D,E$ (and possibly
$C$). Yang's smooth tame estimates are obtained "by standard arguments using
energy integrals and interpolation inequalities" and are stable under bounded
zeroth-order and first-order lower-order perturbations: such terms are absorbed
by the interpolation and by Grönwall, without touching the $2\chi^2\Delta$
diffusion or the $\varepsilon^2\Delta h$ regularization used to solve the
linearized problem.

**Conclusion of the linearized step.** The linearized normalized system has a
unique smooth solution with $\varepsilon$-independent tame estimates, exactly as
in Yang (1.4)--(1.5), because the normalization adds only bounded lower-order
matrices. $\square$

## 4. Short-time existence theorem

**Theorem (normalized transported DeTurck existence).** Let $M^4$ be closed
smooth, $g_0$ a smooth metric, $\chi\in C^\infty(M)$ with $0\le\chi\le1$. There is
$T>0$ and a smooth family $(\hat g(t),\varphi_t)_{t\in[0,T]}$ solving
(2.2)--(2.3), and $g(t)=\varphi_t^*\hat g(t)$ is a smooth solution of the
normalized fixed-cutoff flow (0.1) on $[0,T]$.

*Proof.* By §3 the linearized system (with the normalization absorbed into
lower-order matrices) has unique smooth solutions with smooth tame estimates.
The nonlinear existence for (2.2)--(2.3) then follows by Yang's Approach 1: a
fixed-point argument in $C^0([0,T],H^k(M))$ for $k$ large, or the Nash--Moser
implicit function theorem, using the tame estimates. Setting $g=\varphi_t^*\hat g$
and applying the pullback identity of §2 gives a smooth solution of (0.1) on
$[0,T]$. $\square$

**Remark (Approach 2 for the normalized flow).** The coefficient-regularized
normalized flow
$$
\partial_t g = -2(\varepsilon^2+\chi^2)(\operatorname{Ric}_g+g)
\tag{4.1}
$$
is strictly parabolic (after DeTurck) for each $\varepsilon>0$ and has a smooth
solution on some $[0,T_\varepsilon)$. This is *only* used for existence, never
for the weighted energy estimates. As the Preceptor stressed and as Yang's
Approach 2 shows, uniformity in $\varepsilon$ for the *unnormalized* flow comes
from $\varepsilon$-independent $L^2$ curvature energy bounds, not from Schauder
constants; the added bounded zeroth-order term $-2(\varepsilon^2+\chi^2)g$ does
not affect those curvature energy bounds at leading order (it contributes a
bounded reaction $\lesssim(\varepsilon^2+\chi^2)|\operatorname{Rm}|$ handled by
Grönwall). Either approach delivers the needed short-time existence; Approach 1
is cleaner because it avoids re-deriving Yang's $\varepsilon$-uniform curvature
estimates for the normalized flow.

## 5. Why the Step 35 note was wrong, precisely

1. **Wrong gauge.** Step 35 used a fixed coefficient $a$ with gauge $a\mathcal L_Wg$;
   by (0.2) this misses $da\otimes W^\flat+W^\flat\otimes da$, so its pullback is
   not (0.1). Corrected: transported $\hat a$ and $\hat\delta^*(\hat a\cdots)$.

2. **Wrong existence engine.** Step 35 invoked generic parabolic Schauder with
   $\varepsilon$-independent constants and split $\{a>a_0\}/\{a<2a_0\}$; the
   small-$a$ estimate had an unclosed positive $\|h\|_{H^{k+1}}$ term. Corrected:
   Yang's tame energy estimates for the linearized transported system, with the
   $\varepsilon^2\Delta h$ regularization used only inside the linearized solve.

3. **Normalization handled explicitly.** The added $+g$ is a bounded lower-order
   perturbation of the linearized matrices $D,E$; it does not alter the principal
   symbol or the diffeomorphism transport. This is the one genuinely new
   ingredient beyond Yang, and it is elementary.

## 6. Status and remaining obligations

**Established here (conditional only on Yang's published tame estimates, which we
cite, and the elementary §3 perturbation):** the normalized fixed-cutoff flow
(0.1) has a smooth short-time solution on a closed $M^4$, via the correct
transported-coefficient DeTurck system with the normalization absorbed into
bounded lower-order terms.

**Not established here:** the *maximal* interval and continuation criterion
(that is the separate shifted-curvature continuation theorem, Step 37 companion
note), and the sequence-uniform first-exit length (that requires the weighted
energy machinery, not existence).

This existence theorem is per-datum: $T$ and constants may depend on the fixed
smooth $(g_0,\chi)$. That is exactly what the first-exit argument needs at the
initial step — it opens the flow; the first-exit length is then determined by the
weighted energies, not by this local existence time.
