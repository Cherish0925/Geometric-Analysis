This step resolved the two analytic gaps that Step 38 and its TTCS branches had precisely identified as the blocking objects for the four-dimensional negative Ishida–LeBrun cutoff-flow program.

The first and more consequential result is the general-p homogeneous defect power inequality (Theorem 7.1). Working directly with s=|E|² and the smooth power s^{p/2} for p≥6, the inequality H_p' + c||∇z_p||² ≤ Cp⁴(1+r⁻²+S²Y)H_p was derived term-by-term. The key structural finding — §6 of the note — is the two-factor versus three-factor dichotomy. The Step 35 two-factor absorption P_p ≤ γ·W_p is exact and elegant at fixed p (giving J₆ with a time-independent Grönwall coefficient), but it requires γ ≲ 1/p for absorption at exponent p, which fails along the Moser ladder p_k → ∞. The three-factor Hölder identity R_p ≤ Y^{1/3}H_p^{1/3}||z_p||_4^{4/3} followed by Young(3,3/2) converts the reaction to a Grönwall coefficient Cp⁴S²Y·H_p with only polynomial p-growth and no p-dependent smallness condition. TTCS confirmed this is the simplest efficient pre-endpoint implementation and identified an alternative (the direct curvature L^∞ bound once available). This means the defect ladder is necessarily coupled to the curvature quantity Y=∫aF³, even though the J₆ seed is not — a genuine structural discovery about the energy architecture.

The gradient comparison §5 revealed a related technical necessity: at the Sobolev variable level, ||∇z_p||² has a q²/2 coefficient in front of X_p', while the available chain-rule dissipation is q(q-1)X_p'. The ratio q/(2(q-1)) reaches 3/4 exactly at q=3 (p=6) and decreases to 1/2 for large q, so the chain-rule term -aq(q-1)s^{q-2}|∇s|² cannot be discarded at any p — it must be retained and used. This is why the Step 35 J₆ calculation, which discards the chain-rule term as '≤0', works at fixed p but is not a model for the general case.

The second result is the finite-exponent continuation induction (Theorem 8.1). The signed Hessian-cutoff lemma of Step 38 was shown to handle the current-order single-Hessian term χ∇²χ*∇ᵐU at every order m, not just m=2. For the top-jet terms, one integration by parts on χ∇^{m+2}χ*U always brings ∇^{m+1}χ to the current level — the same level as ∇ᵐU — and never produces a higher cutoff derivative. This is the structural reason that (∇ᵐU, ∇^{m+1}χ) forms a closed pair for each m, with the Grönwall inhomogeneity controlled by previous levels. The subsequent ∇ᵐT estimate follows from the identity a∇ᵐT = ∇ᵐU - Σ (∇ʲa)*(∇^{m-j}T), where every ∇ʲa carries at least one χ factor. Per-datum constants (depending on the initial metric, cutoff, and the maximal time T) suffice; sequence-uniformity is not needed for continuation.

With these two results, the full dependency graph for the restricted 4D negative route is now complete at the level of proved conditional lemmas:

- Layer A (existence/continuation): transported DeTurck + Thm 8.1
- Layer B (zero-order estimates): K₂ persistence, J₆ persistence, global Q, Y = Cγ K₂/t
- Layer C (Moser endpoints): curvature ||aF||∞ ≤ Cγ/t, defect ||aE||∞ ≤ Cδ(r²/t)^{1/3}
- Layer D (self-improvement): continuity argument in exit time, drift proportional to δ

The self-improving first-exit theorem was written as a complete proof structure showing the circularity check: drift ∝ δ → metric comparison → Sobolev → curvature/defect → drift. Making δ small makes all variations small while the curvature constants (K₀, γ₀) are fixed initial data.

What is genuinely missing now is not an analytic lemma but rather the terminal geometric passage (Layer E): from the controlled terminal metric to the calibrated Einstein germ, the projector contradiction, and the conclusion. The pieces exist in Steps 3-6 and 29-30 but have not been assembled into one continuous argument. This is the natural next construction task.
