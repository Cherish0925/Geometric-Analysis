## Deep-Reasoning

This step developed the core-entry problem for the graph-manifold negative branch of Yau 26, producing three structural results and one important correction.

The z-independent impossibility is the cleanest new result. For g = dt² + e^{2a(t)}dz² + e^{2b(t)}dx² + e^{2c(t)}dy², the fiber slices are flat T³ with zero intrinsic curvature. The Gauss equations give H' + H² = 9 (tangential sum) and H' + |S|² = 3 (normal), so |S|² = H² − 6. At the reflection center H = 0, |S|² = 0, this yields −6 = 0, an algebraic contradiction. The approximate version gives δ ≥ 3/2, matching the Step 63 collar barrier. The related isotropic impossibility shows that S = sI on T³ forces Ric(q₀) = −2g₀, which in three dimensions means constant sectional curvature −2/3, excluded by Preissmann since π₁(T³) is abelian. Together these prove that z-dependence and anisotropy are both structurally necessary: no shortcut through ODEs or isotropic models exists.

The more important finding is the general constraint |S|² = H² − 6 − R₃(q_t), which gives R₃(q₀) = −6 at the center. This is the Koiso condition. The intrinsic curvature of the z-dependent fiber resolves the impossibility: a scalar-curvature −6 metric on T³ exists in the negative-Yamabe class through the conformal equation −8u″ − 2(f′)²u = −6u⁵, verified numerically for f = sin z. The traceless Ricci Ric(q₀)° is nonzero and drives the initial anisotropy P′(0) = e^{−2a(0)}Ric(q₀)°.

The linearized mode analysis around the flat cusp g₀ = dt² + e^{2t}(dz² + dx² + dy²) decomposes the T²-invariant Einstein system into: (i) the trace h = α + β + γ satisfying h″ + 2h′ = 0, decaying at rate 2; (ii) the sum w = β + γ constrained to be constant in t for k ≠ 0 by E₀z = 0; (iii) the anisotropy ψ = β − γ satisfying ψ″ + 3ψ′ − k²e^{−2t}ψ = 0; and (iv) a coupling relation 4D + k²W = 0 from E_zz. The anisotropy equation reduces to a modified Bessel equation with ν = 3/2 under x = ke^{−t} and ψ = x^{3/2}z. TTCS caught a chain-rule sign error in my initial derivation: the correct variable-change equation is x²ψ_xx − 2xψ_x − x²ψ = 0, not x²ψ_xx + 4xψ_x − x²ψ = 0, and the correct substitution is ψ = x^{3/2}z, not x^{−3/2}z. This changes the asymptotics completely: both branches ψ = x^{3/2}I_{3/2}(x) and ψ = x^{3/2}K_{3/2}(x) are bounded as t → ∞, with the I-branch decaying as e^{−3t} and the K-branch approaching √(π/2) ≈ 1.253. There are no exponentially growing modes. The corrected Bessel solutions match direct ODE integration to 10^{−12} precision.

The BVP structure is: since both branches are bounded, Neumann + boundedness alone does not determine a unique solution (only one constraint for a second-order ODE). The correct formulation is Neumann at the center plus Dirichlet at the far end, which is unique by the energy identity (e^{3t}ψ′)′ − k²e^tψ = 0. The k = 0 mode has a free constant (cusp cross-section shape modulus) that must be fixed by the global geometry. The TTCS verification also confirmed that full constraint propagation requires all five Einstein equations, not just the anisotropy difference.

The CMC gauge ODE model (a simplified version with scalar coupling) shows the core naturally reaches near-cusp data (|S − I|² < 0.01) by t ≈ 3, making the nonlinear core a compact BVP on [0, R] × S¹ with R ≈ 5. This is a standard domain for numerical elliptic PDE solvers.

The overall picture for the negative branch is now sharply decomposed: the tail is solved, the linearized modes are favorable (no amplification), and the nonlinear core BVP on a compact domain is the critical bottleneck. The positive branch (Ozuch desingularization) has been substantially corrected by A01 and A02: the scalar kernel modes must be properly sliced, the inter-node Green response uses the full product P-resolvent with additive spectral denominators, and the Bianchi-corrected local scalar projection vanishes. Both branches have well-defined mathematical next steps, but the graph-manifold negative branch is closer to producing a concrete result.
