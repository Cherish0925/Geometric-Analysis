## 5 Obstruction theory and proof of the main theorem\

### 5.2 Killing the obstruction by varying the Ricci potential

Here we carry out the last step of the argument. Recall that for a fixed Ricci potential _f_ _σ_ we have solved the Monge-Ampère equation modulo obstructions in Proposition [5.2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#FPar108). Our final goal is to kill the obstruction by using the freedom of adding a constant to the Ricci potential. At first this seems contradictory because if we add a constant to _f_ _σ_, then the solution _u_ _σ_ to the Monge-Ampère equation changes by the same constant. However, \\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma}, the solution “modulo obstructions,” does not change in such an obvious way.

To be slightly more precise, we change _f_ _σ_ by a constant _s_ with \| _s_ \| not much bigger than the C^{0,\\bar{\\alpha}}\_{\\tilde{w}} bound of f\_{\\sigma}^{\\perp} in ( [5.20](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ435)). By running the same iteration as in the proof of Proposition [5.2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#FPar108), we obtain \\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s} \\in \\langle \\hat{u}\_{\\sigma }\\rangle ^{\\perp} solving the Monge-Ampère equation with right-hand side _f_ _σ_+ _s_ modulo obstructions. Moreover, \\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s} satisfies a C^{2,\\bar{\\alpha}}\_{w} bound like ( [5.20](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ435)) independently of _s_. The obstruction coefficient _λ_ _σ_, _s_ depends continuously on _s_. It turns out that we can calculate _λ_ _σ_, _s_ up to errors that are negligible at the boundary of the allowed range for _s_, and this “leading term” of _λ_ _σ_, _s_ is proportional to _s_. So by the intermediate value theorem, \\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s} is the true solution _u_ _σ_ for some _s_ in the allowed range.

The precise statement is as follows. After the proof we will deduce our Main Theorem from this.

\\begin{aligned} M\_{\\sigma }\\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s}=f\_{\\sigma}+s+\\lambda \_{\\sigma ,s} \\hat{v}\_{\\sigma } \\end{aligned}

This is a key improvement over the case _s_ =0, where we almost lost a factor of \| _b_ \|−1/6 (compare ( [5.6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ421)) to ( [5.8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ423))), which is close to the worst possible loss ( [5.30](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ445)).

By taking the L^{2}(\\mathcal{X}\_{\\sigma}, \\omega \_{glue,\\sigma})-inner product with \\hat{v}\_{\\sigma},

\\begin{aligned} \\lambda \_{\\sigma ,s} = \\frac{\\langle L\_{\\sigma}\\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s} + Q\_{\\sigma}\\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s} - f\_{\\sigma}-s, \\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}}{\\\|\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\\|\_{L^{2}}^{2}} = \\frac{\\langle Q\_{\\sigma}\\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s} - f\_{\\sigma}-s, \\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}}{\\\|\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\\|\_{L^{2}}^{2}}, \\end{aligned}

(5.40)


where we have used \\langle L\_{\\sigma}\\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s},\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}= \\langle \\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s}, L\_{\\sigma}\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}= \\langle \\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s},\\hat{u}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}=0. Our goal is to show that as _s_ goes from − _A_ to _A_, the numerator of _λ_ _σ_, _s_ will have a sign change. One can prove directly from ( [5.5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ420)) that, for a fixed _σ_, the solution \\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s} depends continuously on _s_ in the C^{2,\\bar{\\alpha}} topology on \\mathcal{X}\_{\\sigma}. Thus, if there is indeed a sign change, then _λ_ _σ_, _s_ must vanish for some _s_ ∈\[− _A_, _A_\], as desired.

To prove that the numerator of _λ_ _σ_, _s_ changes sign, we bound \\langle s,\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}, \\langle f\_{\\sigma},\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}} and \\langle Q\_{\\sigma }\\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s}, \\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}} separately. This is done in the following three steps.

(1) From Lemmas [2.31](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#FPar48) and [4.8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#FPar96),

\|\\langle 1, \\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}\|\\geqslant C^{-1}\|T\|^{-2}\\geqslant C^{-1}\|b\|^{ \\frac{2}{3}},

(5.41)


i.e., we have a lower bound matching the upper bound ( [5.39](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ454)) up to a constant. This is the good term that will enforce a sign change. Here we crucially use the fact that \\hat{v} has a sign, so that it suffices to integrate over a small neighborhood of the left endpoint _s_ =0, and that \\hat{v}(0) = C\_{0} \\neq 0.

(2) We have already proved in ( [5.13](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ428))–( [5.14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ429)) that

\\begin{aligned} \|\\langle f\_{\\sigma}, \\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}\| \\leqslant C\|b\|^{ \\frac{3}{2}-\\frac{5}{6}\\alpha}. \\end{aligned}

(5.42)


(3) Lastly, we claim that

\\begin{aligned} \|\\langle Q\_{\\sigma}\\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s},\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}} \| \\leqslant C\|b\|^{\\frac{5}{3}(1-\\mathbf{a})-\\frac{2}{3}\\delta}. \\end{aligned}

(5.43)


To prove this, we first make a pointwise estimate using ( [5.36](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ451)):

\\begin{aligned} \\begin{aligned} &\\left \|\\nabla ^{2}\_{\\omega \_{glue,\\sigma}}\\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s} \\right \|\_{\\omega \_{glue,\\sigma}} \\\ &\\quad \\leqslant \\\|\\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s}\\\|\_{C^{2, \\bar{\\alpha}}\_{w}} \\cdot \\mathbf{r}\_{\\sigma}^{-2} w\_{\\sigma} \\\ &\\quad \\leqslant C\|b\|^{\\frac{5}{6}(1-\\mathbf{a})-\\frac{\\delta}{3}} \\cdot \\textstyle\\begin{cases} \|T\|^{-\\delta} &\\text{on}\\;\\,\\mathfrak{R}\_{1}, \\\ \|b\|^{-\\frac{1}{2}}\\Psi \_{\\sigma}^{\*}(\\Phi \_{\\sigma}^{-1})^{\*}((t-T)^{- \\frac{3}{2}-\\delta})&\\text{on}\\;\\,\\mathfrak{R}\_{2} \\cup \\cdots \\cup \\mathfrak{R}\_{6}, \\\ \|b\|^{-\\frac{1}{2}} &\\text{on}\\;\\,\\mathfrak{R}\_{7}. \\end{cases}\\displaystyle \\end{aligned} \\end{aligned}

(5.44)


Thus, using again Lemmas [2.31](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#FPar48) and [4.8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#FPar96),

\\begin{aligned} \\begin{aligned} \|\\langle Q\_{\\sigma}\\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s},\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}} \| &\\leqslant C\|b\|^{\\frac{5}{3}(1-\\mathbf{a})-\\frac{2}{3}\\delta} \\cdot \\biggl( \|b\| \\cdot \|b\|^{-1} + \|b\| \\cdot \\int \_{1}^{2\|T\|^{\\alpha}} \|b\|^{-1} y^{-3-2\\delta} \\,dy \\\ &+ \|T\|^{-2} \\int \_{2\|T\|^{\\alpha -1}}^{1-\\frac{\\tau}{T}} \|b\|^{-1}(\|T\|s)^{-3-2 \\delta}\\,ds\\biggr). \\end{aligned} \\end{aligned}

(5.45)


All terms in the big parenthesis are uniformly bounded as _σ_ →0, which proves ( [5.43](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ458)).

Finally, let us prove the theorem using these estimates. Choosing _α_ < **a** we get

\\begin{aligned} \\frac{5}{6}(1-\\mathbf{a}) +\\frac{2}{3} < \\frac{3}{2}-\\frac{5}{6} \\alpha . \\end{aligned}

(5.46)


Thus, from ( [5.41](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ456)) and ( [5.42](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ457)) we have that

\|\\langle A,\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}\| \\gg \|\\langle f\_{\\sigma}, \\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}\|.

(5.47)


Similarly, if \\mathbf{a}<\\frac{1}{5} and _δ_ ≪1, then

\\begin{aligned} \\frac{5}{6}(1-\\mathbf{a}) +\\frac{2}{3} < \\frac{5}{3}(1-\\mathbf{a}) - \\frac{2}{3}\\delta , \\end{aligned}

(5.48)


so from ( [5.41](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ456)) and ( [5.43](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ458)),

\|\\langle A,\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}\| \\gg \|\\langle Q\_{\\sigma} \\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s},\\hat{v}\_{\\sigma}\\rangle \_{L^{2}}\|.

(5.49)


It is now clear from ( [5.40](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ455)) that _λ_ _σ_, _s_ changes sign at the boundary of the interval _s_ ∈\[− _A_, _A_\]. □

We now deduce the statements of the Main Theorem.

We begin by applying Theorem [5.4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#FPar111) with _α_ < **a** both very small and with \\bar{\\alpha}\\in (0,1) arbitrary. This yields that for all 0<\| _σ_ \|≪1 there exists an _s_ _σ_∈\[− _A_, _A_\] such that \\lambda \_{\\sigma ,s\_{\\sigma}} = 0, so \\tilde{u}\_{\\sigma ,s\_{\\sigma}} coincides with the (unique) solution _u_ _σ_ to our Monge-Ampère equation. Thus, _u_ _σ_ satisfies estimate ( [5.36](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ451)), i.e.,

\\begin{aligned} \\\|u\_{\\sigma}\\\|\_{C^{2,\\bar{\\alpha}}\_{w}} = O\_{\\varepsilon}(\|b\|^{ \\frac{5}{6}-\\varepsilon}) \\end{aligned}

(5.50)


for any _ε_ >0\. The first part of the Main Theorem concerns the behavior of \\omega \_{KE,\\sigma} = \\omega \_{glue,\\sigma}+i\\partial \\overline {\\partial }u\_{\\sigma} on the Tian-Yau cap, region \\mathfrak{R}\_{7}. The definition of \\mathfrak{R}\_{7} depends on a parameter _R_ via the definition of Φ_σ_, ( [2.17](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ20)). Up to replacing the _R_ of the Main Theorem by _R_/ _C_ and assuming that this is > _C_ for some universal constant _C_ ≫1, the _R_ of the Main Theorem can be identified with the _R_ of ( [2.17](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ20)). Fixing _R_, the weight function _w_ _σ_ equals 1 and the regularity scale **r** _σ_ equals \| _b_ \|1/4 on \\mathfrak{R}\_{7}. So it follows from ( [5.50](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ465)) and from the definition of the weighted norm, ( [3.112](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ320)), that

\\begin{aligned} \\sup \\nolimits \_{\\mathfrak{R}\_{7}} \|i\\partial \\overline {\\partial }u\_{\\sigma}\|\_{\\omega \_{glue, \\sigma}} = O(\|b\|^{-\\frac{1}{2}}) \\cdot O\_{\\varepsilon}(\|b\|^{ \\frac{5}{6}-\\varepsilon}) = O\_{\\varepsilon}(\|{\\log \|\\sigma \|}\|^{-1+ \\varepsilon}). \\end{aligned}

(5.51)


The left-hand side of ( [5.51](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ466)) is invariant under diffeomorphisms and rescalings applied simultaneously to _ω_ _KE_, _σ_ and _ω_ _glue_, _σ_. This implies the first statement of the Main Theorem.

### Remark 5.5

Given ( [5.51](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ466)) and the estimate \\sup \_{\\mathfrak{R}\_{7}} \|u\_{\\sigma}\| = O\_{\\varepsilon}(\|b\|^{(5/6)- \\varepsilon}) that also follows from ( [5.50](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ465)), the standard theory of the Monge-Ampère equation tells us that in fact, the C^{1,\\beta}(\\mathfrak{R}\_{7},\\omega \_{glue,\\sigma}) norm of _ω_ _KE_, _σ_− _ω_ _glue_, _σ_ goes to zero for \\beta < \\frac{1}{3}. Clearly, the optimal result would be to have this for all _β_ <1 because _ω_ _glue_, _σ_ is Ricci-flat on \\mathfrak{R}\_{7} whereas _ω_ _KE_, _σ_ has Ricci =−1. This suggests that the best possible exponent in ( [5.50](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ465)) is 1 rather than \\frac{5}{6} (and -\\frac{3}{2} rather than −1 in ( [5.51](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ466)) and in the Main Theorem).

### Remark 5.6

Note the following subtlety related to the asymptotic expansion of _ω_ _KE_,0 in ( [2.54](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ57)). As explained in Remark [2.15](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#FPar24), we need to replace Ψ_σ_ by scale\_{\\lambda ^{-1}}\\circ \\Psi \_{\\sigma} to be able to carry out our gluing construction, where \\lambda = e^{\\mathfrak{s}} for the constant \\mathfrak{s} from ( [2.54](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#Equ57)), which is uniquely determined by the global geometry of (\\mathcal{X}\_{0}^{reg}, \\omega \_{KE,0}) but is not known explicitly. We have assumed that \\mathfrak{s} = 0 for simplicity, but if \\mathfrak{s} \\neq 0 then \\mathfrak{s} or _λ_ could in principle appear in the statement of the Main Theorem. However, in the Main Theorem we consider the pullback ( _m_ _σ_∘Ψ_σ_)−∗( _ω_ _KE_, _σ_), and _ω_ _KE_, _σ_ is approximated by

(scale\_{\\lambda ^{-1}} \\circ \\Psi \_{\\sigma})^{\*}\[\\sqrt{2\|b\|}\\, (m\_{ \\sigma \\lambda ^{-3}})^{\*}(\\omega \_{TY\_{1}})\]

(5.52)


in the bubbling region. One easily checks that the unknown constant _λ_ cancels out.

As for the rest of the Main Theorem, the Euler numbers of \\mathcal{X}\_{0}^{reg} and of \\mathcal{X}\_{\\sigma} (0<\| _σ_ \|≪1) obviously differ by the Euler number of the Tian-Yau space. The Euler number of a smooth sextic in \\mathbb{CP}^{3} is 108 by a standard computation. As a smoothing of an isolated cubic cone singularity, the Tian-Yau space is homotopy equivalent to \\bigvee \_{i=1}^{8} S^{2} and so has Euler number 9 \[ [MO70](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-025-00708-y#ref-CR27 " 					Milnor, J., Orlik, P.: Isolated singularities defined by weighted homogeneous polynomials. Topology 9, 385–393 (1970) 				"), Thm 1\]. The _L_ 2-curvature identity follows from this by using the Chern-Gauß-Bonnet theorem for Einstein 4-manifolds and the standard fact that the Chern-Gauß-Bonnet formula holds without boundary terms both on (TY\_{1}, \\omega \_{TY\_{1}}) and on (\\mathcal{X}\_{0}^{reg},\\omega \_{KE,0}). Because there is no loss of _L_ 2-curvature, there cannot be any other bubbles.
