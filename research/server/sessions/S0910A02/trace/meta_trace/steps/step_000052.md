The first decisive finding is the flat T⁴ falsification, independently verified: on the flat four-torus with χ=(1+cos x₁)/2, the normalized cutoff flow ∂_t g=-2χ²(Ric+g) creates curvature from zero initial data. The leading-order L² energy is 48π⁴t² + O(t³), giving E'/E = 2/t → ∞ as t→0. This rules out any homogeneous energy estimate E'≤CE for the unshifted critical curvature energy. The second finding confirms A03's DeTurck pullback issue: φ*(aĝ) = (a∘φ)φ*ĝ, NOT aφ*ĝ, so the DeTurck trick gives a transported-cutoff flow rather than the fixed-cutoff geometric flow. A03 correctly identified the coupled system (5.2) — metric evolution with coefficient â plus the scalar transport ∂_t â = â W(â) — as the correct repair target. This system is structurally plausible (the coupling is through coefficients, not principal parts; the scalar equation is first-order transport; and Xu's fixed-point argument can in principle extend with staggered Sobolev grading) but requires a genuinely new coupled contraction estimate that nobody has written.

The third finding is new to A02: the critical-product scaling analysis. The Maheux–Saloff-Coste Sobolev constant S on a support ball of radius r_* = c(A/K₀)^{1/4} grows with the IL topological parameter A, giving bar_beta = S·Ĥ₂^{1/2} ∼ O(A^{1/2}). Yang's Theorem 9.2 at the critical exponent requires bar_beta below a universal threshold, which fails for large A. The metric comparison integral gives δ₀ ≤ c/A^{1/2}. This means the restricted IL theorem — even if all technical issues were resolved — can only produce per-manifold gaps that shrink as the manifold becomes topologically complex: δ₀(M) ∼ C'/√A(M). There is no uniform gap across the IL family, and certainly no dimension-only constant. This is a structural limitation of the maximal-function/Sobolev/Moser approach, not a fixable bug.

The overall negative-chain status is now: A1 (start) NOT proved — coupled system is the correct target; A2 (first-exit) cannot be checked without A1, and the flat T⁴ example falsifies the homogeneous closure; A3 (metric comparison) and terminal density are downstream; A4 (continuation) via shifted T/U/C is separate and may survive independently. Even if fully repaired, the outcome is per-manifold gaps δ₀(M) > 0 for each fixed critical IL manifold, with δ₀ shrinking as topology grows.

This audit changes the global allocation picture. The positive product-quotient hardness reduction (Steps 49-50) remains the session's strongest bankable result. The negative chain is now understood to be broken at the start module, with structural limitations even after repair. The positive collapse branch has no new mechanism. The n≥5 front is completely untouched. No branch of Yau-26 is proved or refuted in any dimension ≥ 4.

The most important mathematical insight from this step is the connection between the critical product, the topology-dependent Sobolev constant, and the gap scaling. The IL metrics are near-hyperbolic with |Rm| ∼ O(1) everywhere, so the L² curvature energy on any ball of definite radius is O(1) — bounded but not small. Yang's iteration at the critical exponent tolerates this only when the Sobolev constant is correspondingly small, which it is for fixed topology but not uniformly. This is the same phenomenon Step 22 identified in the collapse regime (the relative critical product stays O(1)) now seen from the complementary topology-growth perspective.
