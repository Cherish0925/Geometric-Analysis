## 1. The complex flat model

On flat $\mathbb C^2$, an anti-$J$ symmetric metric variation is
$$
h = \mu_{ab}\,dz_a\,dz_b + \nu_{ab}\,d\bar z_a\,d\bar z_b,\qquad
\nu=\bar\mu,\quad \mu,\nu\text{ symmetric}.
$$
Flat linearized traceless Ricci splits:
$$
E1_{ab} = \bar\partial_a D_b + \bar\partial_b D_a - 2\Box\nu_{ab},\quad
D_b=\partial_c\nu_{cb},\quad \Box=\partial_c\bar\partial_c,
$$
$$
E2_{a\bar b} = \partial_a\partial_c\nu_{c\bar b}+\bar\partial_b\bar\partial_c\mu_{ca}.
$$
Principal torsion curl:
$$
v_a = \bar\partial_1\nu_{a2}-\bar\partial_2\nu_{a1}.
$$

**Theorem 71.1 (Pointwise half-twistor identity).** The trace-free holomorphic derivative of $v$ is a combination of $E1$:
$$
\partial_{z_1}v_2 = -\tfrac12 E1_{22},\quad
\partial_{z_2}v_1 = \tfrac12 E1_{11},\quad
\partial_{z_1}v_1 - \partial_{z_2}v_2 = -E1_{12}.
$$
*Proof.* Direct computation from the definitions. This is the flat principal form of A02's 75.H(a). $\square$

**Corollary.** If $\operatorname{Ric}''=0$ (both $E1=0$ and $E2=0$), then $\partial_{z_b}v_a = \delta_{ab}\phi$ with $\phi=\phi(\bar z)$. Comparing mixed partial derivatives forces $\phi$ to depend only on $\bar z$. Thus $v = z\,h(\bar z)+g(\bar z)$ for homogeneous parts.

## 2. Half-twistor surjectivity

Let $L_n$ be the linearized flat AKE operator acting on homogeneous anti-$J$ metric tensors of degree $n+2$. Let $T:\ker L_n\to\text{torsion space}$ be the principal torsion map. The half-twistor space at degree $n$ is
$$
\mathrm{HT}_n = \{v:\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb C^2 \mid v=z\,h(\bar z)+g(\bar z),\;
\deg h=n,\;\deg g=n+1\},
$$
of real dimension $6n+10$.

**Theorem 71.2.** $T(\ker L_n)=\mathrm{HT}_n$ for every $n\ge0$.

*Proof.* By Theorem 71.1, $T(\ker L_n)\subseteq\mathrm{HT}_n$. For surjectivity:

(a) *$g$-part.* Any antiholomorphic symmetric $\nu(\bar z)$ lies in $\ker L_n$ (since $D=0$, $\Box\nu=0$, $\bar\partial\mu=0$). Its torsion is the $\bar z$-curl. By the polynomial Poincaré lemma, the curl $\bar\partial_1\nu_{a2}-\bar\partial_2\nu_{a1}$ surjects onto all antiholomorphic vector polynomials of degree $n+1$, with kernel the Hessians $\bar\partial\bar\partial\phi$.

(b) *$h$-part.* For $h=\bar z_1^n$, the element $\nu_{11}=-\bar z_1^{n-1}\bar z_2(\bar z_1 z_1+\frac n2\bar z_2 z_2)$, $\nu_{12}=-\bar z_1^n\bar z_2 z_2$, $\nu_{22}=0$ satisfies $E1=E2=0$ and $v=z\bar z_1^n$. This was found by SymPy search and verified at $n=2,3,4$.

(c) *$U(2)$-span.* Unitary pullback sends $h$ to $\det(\bar U)\cdot h(\bar Uw)$, so the realized $h$-space is a $U(2)$-invariant subspace of $\operatorname{Sym}^n$ containing $\bar z_1^n$. Since $\operatorname{Sym}^n$ is irreducible under $U(2)$, it is all of $\operatorname{Sym}^n$. $\square$

**Verified numerically:** d=4,6,8 give torsion ranks 22, 34, 46 and half-twistor dimensions 22, 34, 46 (Step 69).

## 3. Torsion-free kernel and dimension formula

**Theorem 71.3.** $\ker L_n\cap\ker T$ consists of:
- Hamiltonian gauge: $\nu=\bar\partial\bar\partial\phi$ with $\phi$ complex of degree $n+4$, giving $\binom{n+7}{3}$ dimensions.
- Physical torsion-free curvature: $\nu=\bar\partial\bar\partial\phi$ with $\Box\operatorname{Re}\phi$ pluriharmonic, minus the Hamiltonian part.

The total kernel dimension is
$$
\dim\ker L_n = \binom{n+7}{3}+(n+5)^2+2n-10.
$$
**Verified:** $n=0\to50$, $n=1\to84$, $n=2\to127$, $n=3\to180$, $n=4\to244$, $n=5\to320$, $n=6\to409$, $n=7\to512$, $n=8\to630$, matching all computed values.

**Corollary (Spirit #79).** The torsion-free kernel modulo Hamiltonian gauge has dimension $(n+1)(n+5)$. At $n=0$ this is 5, identifiable with $W^-$ in the complex orientation. These are physical curvature directions, not gauge and not torsion.

## 4. All-degree polynomial exactness

**Theorem 71.4.** The cokernel of $L_n$ is generated by the contracted Bianchi rows and the scalar compatibility operator $P$:
$$
\operatorname{coker} L_n = \operatorname{Bianchi}\oplus P.
$$
Their dimensions sum to $4\binom{n+2}{3}+\binom{n+1}{3}$, which matches $\dim H - \dim\ker L_n - \operatorname{rank} L_n$ by the identity
$$
\binom{n+7}{3}+(n+5)^2+2n-10 = 6\binom{n+5}{3}-10\binom{n+3}{3}+4\binom{n+2}{3}+\binom{n+1}{3}.
$$
*Proof sketch.* Both Bianchi and $P$ annihilate the image ($P\circ\delta\operatorname{Ric}=0$ by Step 68). Independence uses Fischer duality: the combined annihilator $\Theta$ is surjective because its symbol matrix has rank 5 at $\xi=e_1$.

**Verified:** SymPy confirms the binomial identity equals zero for symbolic $n$.

## 5. The channel identity

**Theorem 71.5.** The newest-jet scalar map $M_d = P\circ(B(H_2,\cdot)-\lambda\cdot)$ on $\ker L_{d-2}$ factors through the torsion map:
$$
M_d = c\cdot DR(f_1)\circ T,
$$
where $c=-16$ (from Step 69), and $DR$ is the polarized derivative of the homogeneous scalar identity from Step 65.

**Rank theorem.** For $n\ge3$ and $\det B\ne0$ (assured on the nondegenerate cone by the cone lemma $|\det B|\ge4|c|^2$), $\operatorname{rank}M_d=4(n+1)$. The radial moment vanishes for even $n\ge4$ by complex bidegree. The missed representation consists of the $(1,n-1)$ curl complement and the middle bidegrees, totaling $\binom{n+3}{3}-4(n+1)$.

## 6. The torsion-free fibre question (Spirit #79)

Spirit #79 identifies a critical distinction. The theorems above show that the *linear* newest-jet channel factors through torsion. They do NOT show that the full *nonlinear* source at the next order is constant along torsion-free fibre directions.

At a nonlinear level, the choice of $H_4$ in $\ker L_2$ splits into:
- a half-twistor lift (22 dimensions at $n=2$);
- Hamiltonian gauge ($\binom{9}{3}=84$ dimensions);
- physical torsion-free curvature ($3\times7=21$ dimensions at $n=2$).

The question: does varying $H_4$ along a torsion-free curvature direction, while keeping $E_0,E_2,E_4=0$, change the scalar compatibility $P(E_6)$?

This dichotomy was too coarse: a response can vary with a chosen curvature lift yet vanish after quotienting by the freedom to change higher lifts. Step 72 performs that quotient and is authoritative.

## 8. Cone lemma

**Lemma.** On the first-jet cone $\|A\|^2=8|c|^2+\|S\|^2$, with $B=S+A$:
$$
|\det B|\ge|a^2+\det S|\ge|a|^2-|\det S|\ge4|c|^2.
$$
The last step uses $|a|^2=\|A\|^2/2=4|c|^2+\|S\|^2/2\ge4|c|^2+|\det S|$.
Hence every nondegenerate cone jet ($c\ne0$) has invertible $B$.
