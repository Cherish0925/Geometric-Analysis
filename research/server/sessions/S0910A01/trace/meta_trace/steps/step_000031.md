## Deep-Reasoning

This step produced three substantive mathematical results for the four-dimensional negative-branch Yau Problem 26 program, together with a critical correction that strengthens rather than weakens the conditional argument.

The first result is the explicit general-p tensor power inequality for the normalized cutoff flow. The derivation tracks all five term types (Bochner principal, cutoff gradient, Hessian-of-a, algebraic coupling, metric/volume variation) through the chain rule with weight a^{p-2}F^{p-2}, showing that the dissipation coefficient is p(p-1)D_p^F (from the Bochner pair) while the reaction R_p = int a^{p-1}F^{p+1} carries a factor of p from the chain rule. The three-factor Holder identity a^{p-1}F^{p+1} = (aF^3)^{1/3}(a^{p-2}F^p)^{1/3}Z_p^{4/3} connects R_p to the cubic potential Y = K_3 and the Sobolev output ||Z_p||_4, and Young absorption with lambda chosen to match the dissipation scale produces the absorbed form K_p' + (p^2/16)D_p^F <= C(p^3 S^4 Y + p^2 r^{-2} + p)K_p + C_0 p. The polynomial growth O(p^3) in the reaction coefficient is compatible with Moser convergence because sum(log p_k / p_k) < infinity for the geometric sequence p_k = (3/2)^k p_0. The verification script confirms all exponent matchings, Holder identities, volume cancellations, and Moser product convergence for both seeds p_0=3 (curvature) and p_0=6 (defect).

The second result is a critical correction to the Moser output form. Steps 27 and 29 claimed ||aF(t)||_inf <= C beta/t and ||aE(t)||_inf <= C delta(r^2/t)^{1/3}. These are withdrawn. The standard parabolic Moser iteration from an L^{p_0} seed on [0,T] produces ||u||_inf <= C(VT)^{-1/p_0}(int_0^T K_{p_0})^{1/p_0}, where V is the spatial volume. The singular coefficient S^4 Y(t) ~ 1/t contributes through an integrated Gronwall/Moser constant (bounded for small gamma), not as a pointwise 1/t decay factor in the output. TTCS independently confirmed this and computed the four-dimensional volume cancellation: ||aF||_inf <= C S^{1/3} theta^{-1/3}(1+K_0)^{1/2} on [T/4, T], a uniform bound. The defect Moser from J_6 gives ||aE||_inf <= C delta (uniform), and the velocity V(T) = O(delta theta r^2) -> 0 as delta -> 0 for any fixed theta. This correction does not weaken the downstream argument: uniform bounds suffice for metric comparison, continuation, and terminal recalibration.

The third result answers A03's critical question about the m=2 derivative estimate in the shifted-curvature continuation. The single-chi Hessian term int chi |nabla^2 chi| |nabla^2 U|^p does NOT need to be absorbed by the weighted dissipation D_{2,p} = int chi^2 |nabla^3 U|^2 |nabla^2 U|^{p-2}. Using chi <= 1 and |nabla^2 chi| <= C/r^2, it is bounded by Cr^{-2} A_2(p)^p where A_2(p) = ||nabla^2 U||_{L^p} is the unweighted L^p norm. This enters the Gronwall RHS with a bounded coefficient: A_2' + cD_{2,p} <= C(1+A_2^p), giving finite-time control from smooth initial data. The mechanism is Wang's Proposition 4.14: IBP reduces the cutoff derivative order of the dangerous Gamma_1-type terms, and the remaining lower-order terms are bounded by the current unweighted norm. No exact tensor cancellation is needed. For the a -> a+epsilon regularization, A03 is correct that it activates outside-support curvature; the estimates use a = chi^2 directly, with existence handled by a separate DeTurck/mollification construction.

The deeper mathematical structure revealed by this step concerns the relationship between weighted and unweighted norms in the degenerate flow. The weighted moments K_p = int a^{p-2}F^p and their Moser iteration give control of ||aF||_inf (the weighted curvature), while the continuation theorem requires unweighted norms A_m(p) = ||nabla^m U||_{L^p}. The bridge is that U = a(Rm+G) encodes the weight in the variable itself: the unweighted L^p norm of U is equivalent to the weighted L^p norm of Rm+G. The Wang triangle A_{m-1} -> C_m -> B_{m-1} -> A_m propagates both types of control simultaneously, with the cutoff derivative evolution (4.3) providing the missing C_m step.

A key open question from the TTCS verification is the Sobolev constant dependence under collapse. If the Dirichlet Sobolev inequality applies (Z_p vanishes at the boundary of supp chi because a=0 there), then S is replaced by a volume-independent constant C_{Dir}(n, kappa r^2), and the entire Moser iteration becomes collapse-independent. This would be the cleanest resolution. The alternative (Neumann Sobolev with S^2 = Cr^4/V) leaves an S^{1/3} factor that diverges under collapse. This distinction is important for the terminal recalibration, where the bound must be sequence-uniform.

Relating to the full Yau Problem 26: even if the restricted four-dimensional negative Ishida-LeBrun conditional theorem is completed with all corrections, it covers only one family of non-Einstein four-manifolds under negative Ricci pinching. The positive branch (where A02's noncollapsed dichotomy and spectral obstruction det R_+ = 0 are relevant), dimensions n >= 5 (where SW and W^+ mechanisms are absent), and the full all-dimensional proposition remain open. The Lean formalization cannot begin without a complete mathematical theorem. The research is at a point where the analytic machinery for one restricted negative-branch family is largely assembled but not yet proved as a complete closed theorem, and the harder part of the original problem (positive branch, general topology, higher dimensions) has not been seriously attempted.
