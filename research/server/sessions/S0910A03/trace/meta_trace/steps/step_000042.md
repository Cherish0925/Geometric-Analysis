On [T/2,T], T=θr², the Moser endpoint supplies a sequence-uniform curvature bound. This repairs the invalid passage from C^{1,α} plus weak W^{2,p} convergence to curvature eigenvalues.

The terminal argument also simplifies materially. The explicit nonnegative LeBrun density G=f²−(b_-)² already determines the spectrum, so the positive-part rescaled-monopole harmonic-form construction is unnecessary and should not carry the proof. For a trace-free symmetric endomorphism of Λ⁺, the least eigenvalue w satisfies w≥−√(2/3)|W⁺|, hence b≥f and G≥0. At s=−4 and |W⁺|²=1/24, G=0 forces w=−1/6 and the remaining eigenvalues to equal 1/12. Smooth curvature convergence passes G uniformly, and Derdziński’s conformal factor is then one; local Kähler–Einstein geometry would instead have |W⁺|²=s²/24=2/3, contradicting 1/24. The normalized transported-coefficient DeTurck construction still lacks a fully written tame-estimate, regularization-limit, uniqueness, and restart argument; the arbitrary-order shifted U/T/cutoff structural equations and zero-order unweighted T estimate remain partly schematic. The theorem’s topology must also state the assumptions that uniformly bound χ(M_i)/A_i and provide the Ishida–LeBrun monopole classes. It does not resolve arbitrary topology, the positive forcing branch, dimensions n≥5, dimension-only constants, or Lean formalization. The full Yau 26 proposition needs one forcing sign branch in every dimension; a refutation in one dimension must defeat both signs.
