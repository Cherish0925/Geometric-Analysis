## 2. Lemma 53.0 (telescoping) and the energy equality

### 2.1 Euler bookkeeping, corrected for reducible points

The formula χ_orb(V) = χ(V) − Σ_j(1 − |Γ_j|⁻¹) is correct only at irreducible points. At a point q where c cones C(S³/Γ_{q,1}), …, C(S³/Γ_{q,c}) meet,

  χ_orb(V) = χ(V) − Σ_q ( 1 − Σ_{c at q} |Γ_{q,c}|⁻¹ ),

where χ(V) is the Euler characteristic of the underlying space, with q counted once.

*Check:* two round S⁴ meeting at one point give χ(V) = 3, so χ_orb = 3 − (1 − 2) = 4 = 2 + 2.

For an ALE (orbifold) bubble with ends e and interior orbifold points j,

  ∫(|R|² − |z|²) = 8π² ( χ(N) − Σ_e |Γ_e|⁻¹ − Σ_j (1 − |Γ_j|⁻¹) ).

Two-ended necks are covered. For example, Schwarzschild has χ = 0, two ends, W = s = 0, hence P = 32π², matching the handle tariff of Step 20.

Removing a contractible neighbourhood of q (whose link has χ = 0) and gluing gives

  χ(M) = χ(V) − #{points} + Σ_k χ(N_k).

This equals χ_orb(V) − Σ_{cones} |Γ_c|⁻¹ + Σ_k χ(N_k). Each cone is matched with one bubble end, so the −|Γ|⁻¹ terms cancel level by level. This includes intermediate flat-cone levels (R⁴/Γ with marked points, P = 0) and ε-level Kronheimer bubbles (P = 0).

**Conclusion.** p_*(M) = P(V, g₀) + Σ_k P(N_k) holds whenever the R² equality holds. The topology (4.65) and the convergence of the end transgressions to the flat-cone values are needed. That convergence requires only C² cone convergence on dyadic annuli, not a decay rate.

### 2.2 The neck regions do not enter the Euler count

Neck regions are topologically S³/Γ × I with χ = 0. So the R² and P versions of the energy statement are equivalent as inequalities, independently of neck energy.

### 2.3 Equality from reassembly

- **"≤":** the bulk and the bubbles occupy disjoint regions at separated scales, and convergence is smooth on compacta of each scale. Hence P(g₀) + Σ P(N_k) ≤ liminf P(g_ε) ≤ p_*.
- **"≥":** Lemma 53.R produces metrics on M (via (4.65)) with P → P(g₀) + Σ P(N_k), so p_*(M) ≤ P(g₀) + Σ P(N_k).

## 3. Theorem 53.F′

### 3.1 Topological reduction (verified)

From the session conventions,

  P = 4∫|W⁺|² + (1/12)∫s² − 8π²(2χ + 3τ).

Connected sum gives (2χ+3τ)(V#N̂) = (2χ+3τ)(V) + (2χ+3τ)(N̂) − 4. On an AE manifold (end Euler term 1, η(S³) = 0, end transgressions vanishing),

  P(N) = 4∫|W⁺_N|² + (1/12)∫s_N² − 8π² [ (2χ+3τ)(N̂) − 4 ].

Subtracting:

  P(ĝ) − P(g) − P(N) = 4ΔW⁺ + ΔS/12 − 4∫|W⁺_N|² − (1/12)∫s_N².

This is an identity for every scalar-flat AE N, ASD or not.

### 3.2 Flux, cutoff independence, and remainder

- **Cutoff independence.** P₊R^lin(h₂)P₊ is the degree −4 part of R⁺(g_N). The remainder O(r^{−2−δ}) contributes O(r^{−4−δ}), and the quadratic terms O(r⁻⁶). So W⁺ ≡ s ≡ 0 forces P₊R^lin(h₂)P₊ = 0 pointwise. R^lin is a constant-coefficient second-order operator, so the annulus integral equals the inner-sphere flux. That flux is radius-independent by homogeneity. ✓

- **The remainder is harmless, though the note does not say so.** Since R⁺(g_N) = 0, the + part of R^lin(rest) is O(r⁻⁶). Its flux at radius ρ is therefore F_∞ + O(ρ⁻²), with F_∞ = 0 because rest = O(ρ^{−2−δ}). At ρ = r₀/λ this contributes λ²·O(λ²/r₀²) = O(λ^{16/5}).

- **Error budget.** The remaining errors are λ²r₀, λ²r₀², λ⁴r₀⁻⁴ and λ⁴ log(1/λ). With r₀ = λ^{2/5} these are O(λ^{12/5}) ✓.

- **Assembly.** Since tr R⁺ = s/4, we have F_s = 4 tr Φ^{++}. Then

  4(−2λ²⟨W⁺_p, UΦU^T⟩) − (2/3)λ² s_p tr Φ = −8λ² ⟨R⁺_p, UΦU^T⟩ ✓.

### 3.3 Frame maximization and the sign of tr Φ^{++}

**Frame maximization (verified).** SO(4) → SO(3)₊ is onto. Conjugation by O(3) equals conjugation by SO(3), because ±O induce the same conjugation. So von Neumann gives

  max_U ⟨A, UBU^T⟩ = Σ λ_i(A) μ_i(B).

Only orientation-preserving frames are allowed, and that is exactly the SO(4) condition.

**Positivity of tr Φ^{++} (new).**
- Since s^lin(h) = ∂_i(∂_j h_ij − ∂_i tr h), the scalar flux F_s is the ADM integral ∮(∂_j h_ij − ∂_i h_jj) n_i. For Burns this integrand is 2/r³, giving 4π².
- The positive mass theorem applies: dimension 4, AE, s = 0, decay τ = 2 > 1, Schoen–Yau without spin. So tr Φ^{++} = F_s/4 > 0 for every non-flat N in the class.

**Consequences.**
- The SO(3)-average of ⟨R⁺_p, UΦU^T⟩ is (s_p/12) tr Φ. Hence 𝔯_N(p) ≥ s_p F_s/48.
- For rank-one Φ = μ w w^T we have μ > 0, so 𝔯_N(p) = μ λ_max(R⁺_p).

### 3.4 The log term does not occur; general scalar-flat AE bubbles

For f homogeneous of degree −2,

  ∫_{1<|x|<2} ∂_i∂_j f dx = (flux at 2) − (flux at 1) = 0,

and the left side equals log 2 · (mean of ∂_i∂_j f over S³). So the r⁻⁴ part of W⁺_N always has zero spherical mean when the leading metric term is homogeneous of degree −2. The predicted λ² log(1/λ) cross term is absent.

For non-ASD scalar-flat N, the cross term over the core and intermediate region is

  2λ² ⟨W⁺_p, U I_N U^T⟩, with I_N = lim_{R→∞} ∫_{B_R} W⁺_N dV_N.

This limit converges: the mean-zero r⁻⁴ part cancels, and the remainder is O(r^{−4−δ}). The mixed-difference bounds are unchanged. The expected formula is

  P(ĝ_λ) = P(g) + P(N) − 8λ² ⟨R⁺_p, U Ψ(N) U^T⟩ + o(λ²),  Ψ(N) = Φ^{++}(N) − I_N,

with I_N traceless, so tr Ψ = tr Φ^{++} > 0. This is a derivation sketch whose remainder needs to be written out. It requires the homogeneous r⁻² expansion; ends with r⁻² log r terms would invalidate it.

## 6. Lemma 53.Y and Theorem 53.E

### 6.1 Lemma 53.Y holds for every τ > 0

The conformal factor should be taken exact: u = |y|⁻² on the end, extended positively to N, with ĝ = u⁻²g. Then L_ĝ u = s_g u³ = 0 away from the point, and −6Δ|y|⁻² = 24π²δ₀.

With decay τ:
- ĝ − δ = O(|y|^τ) and ∂²ĝ = O(|y|^{τ−2}), so ĝ ∈ C^{0,τ} ∩ W^{2,q} for some q > 2 = n/2 as soon as τ > 0.
- The products ∂ĝ·∂u are integrable, and the flux corrections vanish on small spheres.
- φ ∈ W^{2,q} is continuous and positive.
- The pairing 24π²φ(0) = λ₁∫uφ and continuity of λ₁ under W^{2,q} mollification go through.

So τ > 1 is not needed. For multi-ended N, compactify every end; L u is then a sum of Dirac masses.

### 6.2 Theorem 53.E

**Item 2** ✓. Y(V_a) < 0 excludes scalar-flat metrics, and s₀ is a constant < 0.

**Items 3–4 can be proved directly**, independently of Prop. nonbach. Since ∇P = 2∇W² + ∇S²/12, a P-critical, Bach-flat metric with s constant has ∇S² = −2s Ric + ½s²g = 0, hence it is Einstein. Therefore:
