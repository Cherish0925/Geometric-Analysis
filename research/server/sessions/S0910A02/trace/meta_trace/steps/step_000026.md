## Deep-Reasoning

This Execute step tested the all-dimensional positive-collapse idea that Step 25 called "abelian forcing." The exact Yau-26 target remains unchanged: prove or strictly refute the all-dimensional Ricci-pinching-to-Einstein-existence proposition, with full mathematical proof and eventual Lean formalization required for completion. The step did not solve any branch and did not begin Lean; it changed the positive-branch architecture by replacing an overstrong pointwise Milnor argument with a sharper graded/relative-rate statement.

The conceptual issue was whether lower-Ricci collapse structure plus two-sided positive pinching can force nilpotent collapse fibers to be abelian. The strategist correctly warned that the relevant object is not just the intrinsic Milnor Ricci of a nilpotent fiber, but the full vertical Ricci equation with twist \(A\), second fundamental form \(T\), and mean-curvature terms. I therefore separated three levels. First, source checking from Naber--Zhang and Rong confirms that positive pinching supplies the lower-Ricci hypothesis needed for Kapovitch--Wilking type almost-nilpotent short-loop groups, but it does not by itself give a smooth Riemannian submersion with controlled \(A,T,N\). Naber--Zhang quote KW: under \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge -(n-1)\), \(\Gamma_{\epsilon_1}(p)\) is \((w(n),n)\)-nilpotent, and near a \(k\)-dimensional Ricci limit ball the refined fibered group has rank \(\le n-k\); maximal rank gives noncollapse on local covers. Rong's local Ricci-bounded covering geometry results give smooth infra-nil fiber bundles only under additional local rewinding-volume and GH-to-noncollapsed-base hypotheses. Thus the bridge is narrower than before: nilpotent group information is genuinely available from lower Ricci, but quantitative submersion/O'Neill control is still an added regularity problem.

Second, in the totally geodesic fiber case \(T=0\), the Heisenberg obstruction is real but narrow. For \([e_1,e_2]=b e_3\), Milnor gives \(\operatorname{Ric}^F=(-b^2/2,-b^2/2,+b^2/2)\), scalar \(-b^2/2\), and vertical anisotropy \(b^2\). In a twist-only Riemannian submersion, \(\operatorname{Ric}_M^V=\operatorname{Ric}^F+Q\), where \(Q\ge0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}Q\le {d\choose2}\). Exact \(\operatorname{Ric}=g\) would require \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(1+b^2/2,1+b^2/2,1-b^2/2)\). Hence upper pinching bounds the center contribution \(b^2/2\le1+\delta\), and an \(H_3\) fiber over a 2-dimensional base is impossible by rank when \(T=0\). This confirms a useful algebraic obstruction but also shows why the easy test case is not the desired all-dimensional theorem.

Third and most important, I built and independently verified a \(T\)-active local model showing that pointwise Milnor-only forcing is false. For the cohomogeneity-one diagonal Heisenberg metric
\[g=dt^2+a(t)^2(\theta_1^2+\theta_2^2)+c(t)^2\theta_3^2,\quad [E_1,E_2]=E_3,\]
with \(b=c/a^2\), \(k_1=a'/a\), \(k_3=c'/c\), \(H=2k_1+k_3\), a moving-frame calculation gives
\[\operatorname{Ric}_{00}=-2(k_1'+k_1^2)-(k_3'+k_3^2),\]
\[\operatorname{Ric}_{11}=\operatorname{Ric}_{22}=-b^2/2-k_1'-Hk_1,\quad \operatorname{Ric}_{33}=b^2/2-k_3'-Hk_3.\]
The script `Sessions/S0910A02/workspace/scripts/step26_heisenberg_moving_frame_ricci.py` derives these from Koszul/anholonomic brackets and writes `Sessions/S0910A02/workspace/data/step26_heisenberg_moving_frame_ricci.json`. Imposing the three vertical equations \(\operatorname{Ric}_{ii}=1\) gives ODEs for \(k_1,k_3,b\), while \(\operatorname{Ric}_{00}=1\) is the Hamiltonian constraint \(4k_1^2+8k_1k_3+b^2+4=0\), preserved by the ODE. The sample \(b_0=20,k_1=10,k_3=-10.05\) integrates with sampled Ricci/constraint errors below \(10^{-12}\), showing that arbitrarily large orthonormal Heisenberg bracket is locally compatible with exact positive Einstein equations, but only with \(|T|\sim b\) and rapid relative bracket variation.

TTCS verifier `Sessions/S0910A02/trace/ttcs/results/step26_ttcs01_verifier1_integration.md` and both branches confirmed the computations and sharpened the theorem-shaping consequence. I then encoded the sharpened algebra in `Sessions/S0910A02/workspace/scripts/step26_heisenberg_relative_rate_bounds.py`, producing `Sessions/S0910A02/workspace/data/step26_heisenberg_relative_rate_bounds.json`. The exact model has the identities \(C'= -2HC\) for \(C=4k_1^2+8k_1k_3+b^2+4\),
\[|T|^2=2k_1^2+k_3^2\ge (b^2+4)/2,\]
and
\[|\partial_t\log b|^2=(k_3-2k_1)^2\ge \frac54(b^2+4)\]
on the constraint surface. With Ricci components only pinched in \([1-\delta,1+\delta]\), the same diagonal model gives conservative losses \((b^2+4-8\delta)\). Thus large bracket is not pointwise impossible under positive pinching; it forces large \(T\) and fast relative scaling. This is a better result than a simple obstruction because it identifies exactly where the proof pressure must move: either obtain bounded \(T/N\) on regular maximal-rank regions and get first-stage virtual abelianization, or account globally for large \(T\) through scalar/divergence/boundary/fixed-stratum flux.

The toric positive-branch correction from the strategist was also assimilated. Step 25's phrase "no interior curvature constraint" should mean only that the false integral identity \(\int K_Bw=\int w\) is gone. The corrected individual equation \((-\Delta_B+K_B)p=p\) with mixed rod boundary conditions remains genuine variational interior content. The formal \(\alpha\)-family evades it through a precise boundary-layer cancellation: short-rod endpoint vanishing creates a tangential mode of order \(\pi^2/L^2\sim2A\), offsetting the well \(K_B=-A\). Thus future toric BUILD work must target the corner-to-interior junction and next-order cancellation, not another coarse integral identity.

Peer messages #309 and #310 were read for context. They sharpen the four-dimensional negative cutoff-flow side branch by correcting conformal curvature normalization and retaining a conditional buffered-support tensor-energy architecture. I did not verify that branch because the current positive nilpotent-collapse target was higher priority and no direct peer request required it. Its status remains a side branch that could at most address a special four-dimensional negative laboratory, not the all-dimensional proposition.

The resulting research judgment is that the positive-collapse route remains valuable, but its first possible theorem must be conditional and region-specific. On regular maximal-rank collapsed regions where Naber--Zhang/Rong-type hypotheses give controlled fibration geometry, the Step-26 estimates support first-visible-stage abelianization: if \(T\), mean curvature, and entering horizontal derivatives are bounded at the collapse scale, nonabelian Milnor bracket cannot dominate. Nonabelian collapse, if it survives, must be graded so that commutator directions fall to smaller scales, producing an iterated circle/torus architecture linked to the Step-24 Kaluza--Klein equations. Singular/fixed strata, non-maximal rank, \(\ell=4\) phenomena, and full O'Neill formulas with nonzero \(T,N\) remain load-bearing open issues. This is real progress toward understanding the left-hand class of positive Ricci pinching, not a proof of Yau-26.
