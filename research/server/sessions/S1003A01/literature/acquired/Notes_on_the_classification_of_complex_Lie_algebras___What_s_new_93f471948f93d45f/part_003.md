[Part 3/6 of: Notes on the classification of complex Lie algebras | What's new]

The action of ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{{\mathfrak g}/{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2F%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-singular, so the action of generic elements of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{{\mathfrak g}/{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2F%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is also non-singular. Thus we can find ![{y \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that ![{\hbox{ad} y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+y%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is not nilpotent on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and not singular on ![{{\mathfrak g}/{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2F%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). From this we see that ![{{\mathfrak g}^y_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5Ey_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a proper subspace of ![{{\mathfrak g}^x_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5Ex_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), contradicting the regularity of ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Thus ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent.

Finally, we show that ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is its own normaliser. Suppose that ![{y \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) normalises ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then ![{(\hbox{ad} x) y \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%29+y+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). But ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the generalised null space of ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and so ![{y \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as required.

Furthermore, all Cartan algebras arise as generalised null spaces:

> **Proposition 16 (Cartans are null spaces)** Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be an abstract Lie algebra, and let ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a Cartan subalgebra. Let
>
> ![\displaystyle {\mathfrak g}_0^{\mathfrak h} = \{ x \in {\mathfrak g}: (\hbox{ad} {\mathfrak h})^n x = 0 \hbox{ for some } n \}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%3D+%5C%7B+x+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%3A+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%29%5En+x+%3D+0+%5Chbox%7B+for+some+%7D+n+%5C%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)
>
> be the generalised null space of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then ![{{\mathfrak g}_0^{\mathfrak h} = {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Furthermore, for generic ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), one has
>
> ![\displaystyle {\mathfrak h} = {\mathfrak g}_0^x.](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5Ex.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

_Proof:_ As ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent, we certainly have ![{{\mathfrak h} \subset {\mathfrak g}_0^{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Now, for any ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) acts nilpotently on both ![{{\mathfrak g}_0^{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and hence on ![{{\mathfrak g}_0^{\mathfrak h}/{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%2F%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By Engel’s theorem, we can thus find ![{y \in {\mathfrak g}_0^{\mathfrak h}/{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%2F%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that is annihilated by the adjoint action of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); pulling back to ![{{\mathfrak g}_0^{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we conclude that the normaliser of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is strictly larger than ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), contradicting the hypothesis that ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a Cartan subalgebra. This shows that ![{{\mathfrak g}_0^{\mathfrak h} = {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Now let ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be generic, then ![{{\mathfrak g}_0^x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5Ex%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has minimal dimension amongst ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Let ![{y \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be arbitrary. Then for any scalar ![{t}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bt%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{\hbox{ad} (x+ty)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%28x%2Bty%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) acts on ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and hence on ![{{\mathfrak g}/{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2F%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). This action is invertible when ![{t=0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bt%3D0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and hence is also invertible for generic ![{t}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bt%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); thus for generic ![{t}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bt%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{{\mathfrak g}^{x+ty}_0 \subset {\mathfrak g}^x_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7Bx%2Bty%7D_0+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5Ex_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By minimality we conclude that ![{{\mathfrak g}^{x+ty}_0 = {\mathfrak g}^x_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7Bx%2Bty%7D_0+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5Ex_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so ![{\hbox{ad} (x+ty)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%28x%2Bty%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent on ![{{\mathfrak g}^x_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5Ex_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for generic ![{t}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bt%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and thus for all ![{t}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bt%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). In particular ![{\hbox{ad} (x+y)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%28x%2By%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent on ![{{\mathfrak g}^x_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5Ex_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for any ![{y \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), thus ![{{\mathfrak g}^x_0 \subset {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_0 = {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5Ex_0+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_0+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Since ![{{\mathfrak h} \subset {\mathfrak g}^x_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5Ex_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we obtain ![{{\mathfrak h} = {\mathfrak g}_0^x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5Ex%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as required. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

> **Corollary 17 (Cartans are conjugate)** Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a Lie algebra, and let ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a Cartan algebra. Then for generic ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is conjugate to ![{{\mathfrak g}_0^x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5Ex%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by an inner automorphism of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (i.e. an element of the algebraic group generated by ![{\exp(\hbox{ad} y)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cexp%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+y%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for ![{y \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)). In particular, any two Cartan subalgebras are conjugate to each other by an inner automorphism.

_Proof:_ Let ![{S}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BS%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the set of ![{x' \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%27+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{{\mathfrak h} = {\mathfrak g}_0^{x'}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5E%7Bx%27%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then ![{x'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a Zariski open dense subset of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by Proposition [16](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#hp). Then let ![{T}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BT%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the collection of ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that are conjugate to an ![{x' \in S}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%27+%5Cin+S%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then ![{T}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BT%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a algebraically constructible subset of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). For ![{x' \in S}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%27+%5Cin+S%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), observe that ![{(\hbox{ad} {x'})({\mathfrak g})}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7Bx%27%7D%29%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) span ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), since ![{{\mathfrak h} = {\mathfrak g}_0^{x'}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D_0%5E%7Bx%27%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and so by the [inverse function theorem](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_function_theorem), a (topological) neighbourhood of ![{x'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is contained in ![{T}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BT%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). This implies that ![{T}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BT%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is Zariski dense, and the claim follows. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

In the case of semisimple algebras, the Cartan structure is particularly clean:

> **Proposition 18** Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a semisimple Lie algebra. Then every Cartan subalgebra ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is abelian, and ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

The dimension of the Cartan algebra of a semisimple Lie algebra is known as the _rank_ of the algebra.

_Proof:_ The nilpotent algebra ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) acts via the adjoint action on ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and by Lie’s theorem this action can be made upper triangular. From this it is not difficult to obtain a decomposition

![\displaystyle {\mathfrak g} = \bigoplus_{\lambda \in \sigma({\mathfrak h})} {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\lambda](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%3D+%5Cbigoplus_%7B%5Clambda+%5Cin+%5Csigma%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%29%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Clambda&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for some finite set ![{\sigma({\mathfrak h}) \subset {\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csigma%28%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%29+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), where ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\lambda}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Clambda%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are the generalised eigenspaces

![\displaystyle {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\lambda = \{ x \in {\mathfrak g}: (\hbox{ad} {\mathfrak h}-\lambda)^k x = 0 \hbox{ for some } k \}.](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Clambda+%3D+%5C%7B+x+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%3A+%28%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D-%5Clambda%29%5Ek+x+%3D+0+%5Chbox%7B+for+some+%7D+k+%5C%7D.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

From the Jacobi identity [(2)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#jacobi-deriv) we see that ![{[{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{\lambda}, {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{\mu}] \subset {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{\lambda+\mu}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B%5Clambda%7D%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B%5Cmu%7D%5D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B%5Clambda%2B%5Cmu%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Among other things, this shows that ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{\lambda}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B%5Clambda%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has ad-trace zero for any non-zero ![{\lambda}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and hence ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{\lambda}, {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{\mu}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B%5Clambda%7D%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B%5Cmu%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-orthogonal if ![{\lambda+\mu \neq 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda%2B%5Cmu+%5Cneq+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). In particular, ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{0}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B0%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-orthogonal to ![{\bigoplus_{\lambda \neq 0} {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\lambda}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbigoplus_%7B%5Clambda+%5Cneq+0%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Clambda%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By Theorem [1](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#semisimple), ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate on ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and thus also non-degenerate on ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); by Proposition [16](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#hp), ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is thus non-degenerate on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). But by Lie’s theorem, we can find a basis for which ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) consists of upper-triangular matrices in the adjoint representation of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so that ![{[{\mathfrak h},{\mathfrak h}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is strictly upper-triangular and thus ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-orthogonal to ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). As ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), this forces ![{[{\mathfrak h},{\mathfrak h}]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%2C%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to be trivial, as required. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

We now use the semisimple Jordan decomposition (Lemma [13](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sja)) to obtain a further non-degeneracy property of the Cartan subalgebras of semisimple algebras:

> **Proposition 19** Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a semisimple Lie algebra. Then every Cartan subalgebra ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) consists entirely of semisimple elements.

_Proof:_ Let ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then (by the abelian nature of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)) ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) annihilates ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); as ![{\hbox{ad} {x_{n}}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7Bx_%7Bn%7D%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a polynomial in ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with zero constant coefficient, ![{\hbox{ad} {x_n}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7Bx_n%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) annihilates ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as well; thus ![{x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) normalises ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and thus also lies in ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is Cartan. If ![{y \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then ![{y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) commutes with ![{x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and so ![{\hbox{ad} y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+y%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) commutes with ![{\hbox{ad} {x_n}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7Bx_n%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). As the latter is nilpotent, we conclude that ![{\hbox{ad} {x_n} \hbox{ad} y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%7Bx_n%7D+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+y%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent and thus has trace zero. Thus ![{x_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-orthogonal to ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and thus vanishes since the Killing form is non-degenerate on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Thus every element of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is semisimple as required. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

**— 5\. ![{{\mathfrak{sl}}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) representations —**

To proceed further, we now need to perform some computations on a very specific Lie algebra, the special linear algebra ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{2 \times 2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B2+%5Ctimes+2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) complex matrices with zero trace. This is a three-dimensional concrete Lie algebra, spanned by the three generators

![\displaystyle H := \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}; X := \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}; Y := \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+H+%3A%3D+%5Cbegin%7Bpmatrix%7D+1+%26+0+%5C%5C+0+%26+-1+%5Cend%7Bpmatrix%7D%3B+X+%3A%3D+%5Cbegin%7Bpmatrix%7D+0+%26+1+%5C%5C+0+%26+0+%5Cend%7Bpmatrix%7D%3B+Y+%3A%3D+%5Cbegin%7Bpmatrix%7D+0+%26+0+%5C%5C+1+%26+0+%5Cend%7Bpmatrix%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

which obey the commutation relations

![\displaystyle [H,X] = 2X; [H,Y] = -2Y; [X,Y] = H. \ \ \ \ \ (8)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5BH%2CX%5D+%3D+2X%3B+%5BH%2CY%5D+%3D+-2Y%3B+%5BX%2CY%5D+%3D+H.+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%288%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

Conversely, any abstract three-dimensional Lie algebra generated by ![{H,X,Y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH%2CX%2CY%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with relations [(8)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#concrete) is clearly isomorphic to ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). One can check that this is a simple Lie algebra, with the one-dimensional space generated by ![{H}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) being a Cartan subalgebra.

Now we classify by hand the representations ![{\rho: \mathfrak{sl}_2 \rightarrow {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%3A+%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Observe that ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) acts infinitesimally on ![{{\bf R}^2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5E2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by the differential operators (or vector fields)

![\displaystyle H \rightarrow x \partial_x - y \partial_y; \quad X \rightarrow x \partial_y; \quad Y \rightarrow y \partial_x.](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+H+%5Crightarrow+x+%5Cpartial_x+-+y+%5Cpartial_y%3B+%5Cquad+X+%5Crightarrow+x+%5Cpartial_y%3B+%5Cquad+Y+%5Crightarrow+y+%5Cpartial_x.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

In particular, we see that for each natural number ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), the space ![{P_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BP_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of homogeneous polynomials in two variables ![{x,y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%2Cy%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of degree ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has a representation ![{\sigma_n: \mathfrak{sl}_2 \rightarrow {\mathfrak{gl}}(P_n)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csigma_n%3A+%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28P_n%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); if we give this space the basis ![{e_{2i-n} := x^i y^{n-i}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Be_%7B2i-n%7D+%3A%3D+x%5Ei+y%5E%7Bn-i%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for ![{i=0,\ldots,n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bi%3D0%2C%5Cldots%2Cn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), the action is then described by the formulae

![\displaystyle \sigma_n(H) e_j = j e_j; \quad \sigma_n(X) e_j = \frac{n-j}{2} e_{j+2}; \quad \sigma_n(Y) e_j = \frac{n+j}{2} e_{j-2} \ \ \ \ \ (9)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Csigma_n%28H%29+e_j+%3D+j+e_j%3B+%5Cquad+%5Csigma_n%28X%29+e_j+%3D+%5Cfrac%7Bn-j%7D%7B2%7D+e_%7Bj%2B2%7D%3B+%5Cquad+%5Csigma_n%28Y%29+e_j+%3D+%5Cfrac%7Bn%2Bj%7D%7B2%7D+e_%7Bj-2%7D+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%289%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for ![{j = n, n-2, \ldots, -n+2, n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bj+%3D+n%2C+n-2%2C+%5Cldots%2C+-n%2B2%2C+n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). From these formulae it is also easy to see that these representations are irreducible in the sense that the ![{P_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BP_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) have no non-trivial ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-invariant subspaces.

Conversely, these representations (and their direct sums) describe (up to isomorphism) all of the representations of ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002):

> **Theorem 20 (Representations of ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002))** Any representation ![{\rho: \mathfrak{sl}_2 \rightarrow {\mathfrak{gl}}(V)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%3A+%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is isomorphic to the direct sum of finitely many of the representations ![{\sigma_n: \mathfrak{sl}_2 \rightarrow {\mathfrak{gl}}(P_n)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csigma_n%3A+%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28P_n%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Here of course the direct sum ![{\rho_1 \oplus \rho_2: {\mathfrak g} \rightarrow {\mathfrak{gl}}(V_1 \oplus V_2)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho_1+%5Coplus+%5Crho_2%3A+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V_1+%5Coplus+V_2%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of two representations ![{\rho_1: {\mathfrak g} \rightarrow {\mathfrak{gl}}(V_1)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho_1%3A+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V_1%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{\rho_2: {\mathfrak g} \rightarrow {\mathfrak{gl}}(V_2)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho_2%3A+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V_2%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is defined as ![{\rho_1 \oplus \rho_2(x) := (\rho_1(x), \rho_2(x))}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho_1+%5Coplus+%5Crho_2%28x%29+%3A%3D+%28%5Crho_1%28x%29%2C+%5Crho_2%28x%29%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and two representations ![{\rho_1: {\mathfrak g} \rightarrow {\mathfrak{gl}}(V_1)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho_1%3A+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V_1%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{\rho_2: {\mathfrak g} \rightarrow {\mathfrak{gl}}(V_2)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho_2%3A+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bgl%7D%7D%28V_2%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are isomorphic if there is an invertible linear map ![{\phi: V_1 \rightarrow V_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cphi%3A+V_1+%5Crightarrow+V_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that ![{\phi \circ \rho_1(x) = \rho_2(x) \circ \phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cphi+%5Ccirc+%5Crho_1%28x%29+%3D+%5Crho_2%28x%29+%5Ccirc+%5Cphi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

_Proof:_ By induction we may assume that ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-trivial, the claim has already been proven for any smaller dimensional spaces than ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

As ![{H}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is semisimple, ![{\rho(H)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28H%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is semisimple by Lemma [13](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sja), and so we can split ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) into the direct sum

![\displaystyle V = \oplus_{\lambda \in \sigma(H)} V^H_\lambda](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+V+%3D+%5Coplus_%7B%5Clambda+%5Cin+%5Csigma%28H%29%7D+V%5EH_%5Clambda&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

of eigenspaces of ![{H}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for some finite ![{\sigma(H) \subset {\bf C}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csigma%28H%29+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

From [(8)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#concrete) we have the raising law

![\displaystyle \rho(X) V^H_\lambda \subset V^H_{\lambda+2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Crho%28X%29+V%5EH_%5Clambda+%5Csubset+V%5EH_%7B%5Clambda%2B2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

and the lowering law

![\displaystyle \rho(Y) V^H_\lambda \subset V^H_{\lambda-2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Crho%28Y%29+V%5EH_%5Clambda+%5Csubset+V%5EH_%7B%5Clambda-2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

As ![{\sigma(H)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csigma%28H%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is finite, we may find a “highest weight” ![{\lambda \in \sigma(H)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda+%5Cin+%5Csigma%28H%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with the property that ![{\lambda+2 \not \in \sigma(H)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda%2B2+%5Cnot+%5Cin+%5Csigma%28H%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), thus ![{\rho(X)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28X%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) annihilates ![{V^H_\lambda}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%5EH_%5Clambda%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by the raising law. We will use the basic strategy of starting from the highest weight space and applying lowering operators to discover one of the irreducible components of the representation.

From [(8)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#concrete) one has

![\displaystyle \rho(X) \rho(Y) = \rho(Y) \rho(X) + \rho(H)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Crho%28X%29+%5Crho%28Y%29+%3D+%5Crho%28Y%29+%5Crho%28X%29+%2B+%5Crho%28H%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

and so from induction and the lowering law we see that

![\displaystyle \rho(X) \rho(Y)^{k+1} v = (\lambda + (\lambda-2) + \ldots + (\lambda-2k)) \rho(Y)^k v \ \ \ \ \ (10)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Crho%28X%29+%5Crho%28Y%29%5E%7Bk%2B1%7D+v+%3D+%28%5Clambda+%2B+%28%5Clambda-2%29+%2B+%5Cldots+%2B+%28%5Clambda-2k%29%29+%5Crho%28Y%29%5Ek+v+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2810%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for all natural numbers ![{k}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bk%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and all ![{v \in V^H_\lambda}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv+%5Cin+V%5EH_%5Clambda%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). If ![{\lambda + (\lambda-2) + \ldots + (\lambda-2k)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda+%2B+%28%5Clambda-2%29+%2B+%5Cldots+%2B+%28%5Clambda-2k%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is never zero, this creates an infinite sequence ![{V^H_\lambda, V^H_{\lambda-2}, V^H_{\lambda-4}, \ldots}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%5EH_%5Clambda%2C+V%5EH_%7B%5Clambda-2%7D%2C+V%5EH_%7B%5Clambda-4%7D%2C+%5Cldots%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of non-trivial eigenspaces, which is absurd, so we have ![{\lambda + (\lambda-2) + \ldots + (\lambda-2n) = 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda+%2B+%28%5Clambda-2%29+%2B+%5Cldots+%2B+%28%5Clambda-2n%29+%3D+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for some natural number ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), thus ![{\lambda = n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda+%3D+n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). If we then let

![\displaystyle W := \bigoplus_{k=0}^n \rho(Y)^k V^H_n](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+W+%3A%3D+%5Cbigoplus_%7Bk%3D0%7D%5En+%5Crho%28Y%29%5Ek+V%5EH_n&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

then we see that ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is invariant under ![{H}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{X}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and ![{Y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BY%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and thus ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-invariant; also if for each ![{\lambda \in \sigma(H)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clambda+%5Cin+%5Csigma%28H%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) we let ![{\tilde V^H_\lambda}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+V%5EH_%5Clambda%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the set of all ![{v \in V^H_\lambda}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv+%5Cin+V%5EH_%5Clambda%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that ![{\rho(X)^k v}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%28X%29%5Ek+v%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is never a non-zero element of ![{V^H_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%5EH_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) then we see that

![\displaystyle \tilde W := \bigoplus_{\lambda \in \sigma(H)} \tilde V^H_\lambda](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Ctilde+W+%3A%3D+%5Cbigoplus_%7B%5Clambda+%5Cin+%5Csigma%28H%29%7D+%5Ctilde+V%5EH_%5Clambda&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

is also ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-invariant, and furthermore that ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\tilde W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+W%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are complementary subspaces in ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Applying the induction hypothesis, we are done unless ![{W=V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%3DV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), but then by splitting ![{V^H_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%5EH_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) into one-dimensional spaces and applying the lowering operators, we see that we reduce to the case that ![{V^H_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%5EH_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is one-dimensional. But if one then lets ![{e_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Be_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a generator of ![{V^H_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%5EH_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and recursively defines ![{e_{n-2}, e_{n-4},\ldots,e_{-n}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Be_%7Bn-2%7D%2C+e_%7Bn-4%7D%2C%5Cldots%2Ce_%7B-n%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by

![\displaystyle \rho(Y) e_j = \frac{n+j}{2} e_{j-2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Crho%28Y%29+e_j+%3D+%5Cfrac%7Bn%2Bj%7D%7B2%7D+e_%7Bj-2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

one then checks using [(10)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#form) that ![{\rho}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is isomorphic to ![{\sigma_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csigma_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and the claim follows. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

> **Remark 21** Theorem [20](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sl2) shows that all representations of ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are _completely reducible_ in that they can be decomposed as the direct sum of irreducible representations. In fact, all representations of semisimple Lie algebras are completely reducible; this can be proven by a variant of the above arguments (in combination with the analysis of weights given below), and can also be proven by the unitary trick, or by analysing the action of [Casimir elements](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_invariant) of the universal enveloping algebra of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), as done in the Appendix.

**— 6\. Root spaces —**

Now we use the ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) theory to analyse more general semisimple algebras.

Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a semisimple Lie algebra, and let ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a Cartan algebra, then by Proposition [18](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#lock)![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is abelian and acts in a semisimple fashion on ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and by Proposition [16](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#hp)![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is its own null space ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in the weight decomposition of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), thus we have the [Cartan decomposition](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan_decomposition)

![\displaystyle {\mathfrak g} = {\mathfrak h} \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi} {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5Coplus+%5Cbigoplus_%7B%5Calpha+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

as vector spaces (not as Lie algebras) where ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a finite subset of ![{{\mathfrak h}^* \backslash \{0\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A+%5Cbackslash+%5C%7B0%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (known as the set of _roots_) and ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the non-trivial eigenspace

![\displaystyle {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha = \{ x \in {\mathfrak g}: [y,x] = \alpha(y) x \hbox{ for all } y \in {\mathfrak h} \}. \ \ \ \ \ (11)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha+%3D+%5C%7B+x+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%3A+%5By%2Cx%5D+%3D+%5Calpha%28y%29+x+%5Chbox%7B+for+all+%7D+y+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%5C%7D.+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2811%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

> **Example 22** A key example to keep in mind is when ![{{\mathfrak g} = \mathfrak{sl}_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%3D+%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the Lie algebra of ![{n \times n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn+%5Ctimes+n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) matrices of trace zero. An explicit computation using the Killing form and Theorem [1](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#semisimple) shows that this algebra is semisimple; in fact it is simple, but we will not show this yet. The space ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of diagonal matrices of trace zero can then be verified to be a Cartan algebra; it can be identified with the space ![{{\bf C}^n_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%5En_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of complex ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-tuples summing to zero, and using the usual Hermitian inner product on ![{{\bf C}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) we can also identify ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{{\bf C}^n_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%5En_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The roots are then of the form ![{e_i - e_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Be_i+-+e_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for distinct ![{1 \leq i,j \leq n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B1+%5Cleq+i%2Cj+%5Cleq+n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), where ![{e_1,\ldots,e_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Be_1%2C%5Cldots%2Ce_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the standard basis for ![{{\bf C}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), with ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{e_i-e_j}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7Be_i-e_j%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) being the one-dimensional space of matrices that are vanishing except possibly at the ![{(i,j)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28i%2Cj%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) coefficient.

From the Jacobi identity [(2)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#jacobi-deriv) we see that the Lie bracket acts additively on the weights, thus

![\displaystyle [{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha, {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\beta] \subset {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{\alpha+\beta} \ \ \ \ \ (12)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Cbeta%5D+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta%7D+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2812%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for all ![{\alpha,\beta \in {\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Taking traces, we conclude that

![\displaystyle K( {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha, {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\beta ) = 0](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+K%28+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Cbeta+%29+%3D+0&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

whenever ![{\alpha+\beta \neq 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2B%5Cbeta+%5Cneq+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). As ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate, we conclude that if ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-trivial, then ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{-\alpha}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B-%5Calpha%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) must also be non-trivial, thus ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is symmetric around the origin.

We also claim that ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) spans ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as a vector space. For if this were not the case, then there would be a non-trivial ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that is annihilated by ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), which by [(11)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#gha) implies that ![{\hbox{ad} x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+x%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) annihilates all of the ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and is thus central, contradicting the semisimplicity of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

From Proposition [18](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#lock), ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Thus, for each root ![{\alpha \in \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), there is a corresponding non-zero element ![{t_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bt_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that ![{K( t_\alpha, x ) = \alpha(x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%28+t_%5Calpha%2C+x+%29+%3D+%5Calpha%28x%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all ![{x \in {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). If we let ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha, y \in {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{-\alpha}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%2C+y+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B-%5Calpha%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and ![{z \in {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_0= {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bz+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_0%3D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we have

![\displaystyle K( [x,y], z ) = K( y, [z,x] ) ](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+K%28+%5Bx%2Cy%5D%2C+z+%29+%3D+K%28+y%2C+%5Bz%2Cx%5D+%29+&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

![\displaystyle = K( y, \alpha(z) x ) ](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%3D+K%28+y%2C+%5Calpha%28z%29+x+%29+&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

![\displaystyle = \alpha( K(x,y) z ) ](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%3D+%5Calpha%28+K%28x%2Cy%29+z+%29+&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

![\displaystyle = K( K(x,y) t_\alpha, z )](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%3D+K%28+K%28x%2Cy%29+t_%5Calpha%2C+z+%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

and thus by the non-degeneracy of ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) we obtain the useful formula

![\displaystyle [x,y] = K(x,y) t_\alpha \ \ \ \ \ (13)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Bx%2Cy%5D+%3D+K%28x%2Cy%29+t_%5Calpha+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2813%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for ![{x \in {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{y \in {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{-\alpha}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B-%5Calpha%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

As ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate, we can find ![{X = X_\alpha \in {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX+%3D+X_%5Calpha+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{Y = Y_\alpha \in {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{-\alpha}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BY+%3D+Y_%5Calpha+%5Cin+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B-%5Calpha%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{K(X,Y) \neq 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%28X%2CY%29+%5Cneq+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (which can be found as ![{K}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BK%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-degenerate). We divide into two cases depending on whether ![{\alpha(t_\alpha)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%28t_%5Calpha%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) vanishes or not. If ![{\alpha(t_\alpha)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%28t_%5Calpha%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) vanishes, then ![{[X,Y]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5BX%2CY%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-trivial but commutes with ![{X}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{Y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BY%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and so ![{\hbox{ad} X,\hbox{ad} Y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+X%2C%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+Y%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) generate a solvable algebra. By Lie’s theorem, this algebra is upper-triangular in some basis, and so ![{\hbox{ad} [X,Y]}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+%5BX%2CY%5D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent, hence ![{\hbox{ad} t_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+t_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is nilpotent; but by Proposition [19](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#poa)![{\hbox{ad} t_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+t_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is also semisimple, contradicting the non-zero nature of ![{t_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bt_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (and the semisimple nature of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)). Thus ![{\alpha(t_\alpha)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%28t_%5Calpha%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is non-vanishing. If we then scale ![{X,Y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX%2CY%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) so that ![{[X,Y] = H}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5BX%2CY%5D+%3D+H%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), where ![{H = H_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH+%3D+H_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the [co-root](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-root#Dual_root_system_and_coroots) of ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), defined as the element of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) given by the formula

![\displaystyle H := \frac{2}{\alpha(t_\alpha)} t_\alpha](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+H+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B2%7D%7B%5Calpha%28t_%5Calpha%29%7D+t_%5Calpha&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

so that

![\displaystyle \alpha(H) = 2, \ \ \ \ \ (14)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Calpha%28H%29+%3D+2%2C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2814%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

then ![{X,Y,H}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX%2CY%2CH%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) obey the relations [(8)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#concrete) and thus generate a copy of ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), rather than a solvable algebra. The representation theory of ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) can then be applied to the space

![\displaystyle \bigcup_{n \in S_\alpha} {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{n\alpha/2}, \ \ \ \ \ (15)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Cbigcup_%7Bn+%5Cin+S_%5Calpha%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7Bn%5Calpha%2F2%7D%2C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2815%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

where ![{S_\alpha := \{ n \in {\bf R}: n \alpha/2 \in \Phi \cup \{0\}\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BS_%5Calpha+%3A%3D+%5C%7B+n+%5Cin+%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%3A+n+%5Calpha%2F2+%5Cin+%5CPhi+%5Ccup+%5C%7B0%5C%7D%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By [(19)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#add), this space is invariant with respect to ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{y}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7By%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and hence to the copy of ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and by [(11)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#gha), [(14)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#alpha-h) each ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{n\alpha/2}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7Bn%5Calpha%2F2%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the weight space of ![{H}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of weight ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for each ![{n \in S}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn+%5Cin+S%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). By Theorem [20](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sl2), we conclude that the set ![{S}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BS%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) consists of integers. On the other hand, from [(13)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#k-bracket) we see that any copy of the representation ![{\sigma_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csigma_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) a positive even integer must have its ![{0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) weight space contained in the span of ![{t_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bt_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and so there is only one such representation in [(15)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#ss). As ![{X, Y, H}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX%2C+Y%2C+H%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) already give a copy of ![{\sigma_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csigma_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in [(15)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#ss), there are no other copies of ![{\sigma_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csigma_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) positive even, thus we have that ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is one-dimensional and that the only even multiples of ![{\alpha/2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2F2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are ![{\pm \alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cpm+%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). In particular, ![{2\alpha \not \in \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B2%5Calpha+%5Cnot+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) whenever ![{\alpha \in \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), which also implies that ![{\alpha/2 \not \in \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2F2+%5Cnot+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) whenever ![{\alpha \in \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Returning to Theorem [20](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sl2), we conclude that the set ![{S_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BS_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) contains no odd integers, and so ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{-\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B-%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are the only multiples of ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Next, let ![{\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be any non-zero element of ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) orthogonal to ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with respect to the inner product ![{\langle,\rangle}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle%2C%5Crangle%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that is dual to the restriction of the Killing form to ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and consider the space

![\displaystyle \bigcup_{n \in S_{\alpha,\beta}} {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{\beta+n\alpha/2} \ \ \ \ \ (16)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Cbigcup_%7Bn+%5Cin+S_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B%5Cbeta%2Bn%5Calpha%2F2%7D+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2816%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

where

![\displaystyle S_{\alpha,\beta} := \{ n \in {\bf R}: \beta + n \alpha/2 \in \Phi \}.](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+S_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D+%3A%3D+%5C%7B+n+%5Cin+%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%3A+%5Cbeta+%2B+n+%5Calpha%2F2+%5Cin+%5CPhi+%5C%7D.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

By [(19)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#add), this is again an ![{\mathfrak{sl}_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-invariant space, and by [(11)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#gha), [(14)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#alpha-h) each ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{\beta+n\alpha/2}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B%5Cbeta%2Bn%5Calpha%2F2%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the weight space of ![{H}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of weight ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). From Theorem [20](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sl2) we see that ![{S_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BS_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is an arithmetic progression ![{\{-m,-m+2,\ldots,m-2,m\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5C%7B-m%2C-m%2B2%2C%5Cldots%2Cm-2%2Cm%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of spacing ![{2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); in particular, ![{S_{\alpha,\beta}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BS_%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is symmetric around the origin and consists only of integers. This implies that the set ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is symmetric with respect to reflection across the hyperplane that is orthogonal to ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and also implies that

![\displaystyle 2 \frac{\langle \alpha,\beta\rangle}{\langle \alpha,\alpha \rangle} \in {\bf Z}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+2+%5Cfrac%7B%5Clangle+%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%5Crangle%7D%7B%5Clangle+%5Calpha%2C%5Calpha+%5Crangle%7D+%5Cin+%7B%5Cbf+Z%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for all roots ![{\alpha,\beta \in \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta+%5Cin+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

We summarise the various geometric properties of ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as follows:

> **Proposition 23 (Root systems)** Let ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a semisimple Lie algebra, let ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a Cartan subalgebra, and let ![{\langle,\rangle}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle%2C%5Crangle%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the inner product on ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that is dual to the Killing form restricted to ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Let ![{\Phi \subset {\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the set of roots. Then:
>
> - (i) ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) does not contain zero.
> - (ii) If ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a root, then ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is symmetric with respect to the reflection operation ![{s_\alpha: {\mathfrak h}^* \rightarrow {\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Calpha%3A+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A+%5Crightarrow+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) across the hyperplane orthogonal to ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); in particular, ![{-\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B-%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is also a root.
> - (iii) If ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a root, then no multiple of ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) other than ![{\pm \alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cpm+%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are roots.
> - (iv) If ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are roots, then ![{\frac{\langle \alpha,\beta \rangle}{\langle \alpha,\alpha \rangle}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cfrac%7B%5Clangle+%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta+%5Crangle%7D%7B%5Clangle+%5Calpha%2C%5Calpha+%5Crangle%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is an integer or half-integer. Equivalently, ![{s_\alpha(\beta) = \beta + m \alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Calpha%28%5Cbeta%29+%3D+%5Cbeta+%2B+m+%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for some integer ![{m}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bm%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).
> - (v) ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) spans ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

A set of vectors ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) obeying the above axioms (i)-(v) is known as a [root system](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_system) on ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (viewed as a finite dimensional complex Hilbert space with the inner product ![{\langle,\rangle}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle%2C%5Crangle%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)).

> **Remark 24** A short calculation reveals the remarkable fact that if ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a root system, then the associated system of co-roots ![{\{H_\alpha: \alpha \in \Phi\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5C%7BH_%5Calpha%3A+%5Calpha+%5Cin+%5CPhi%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is also a root system. This is one of the starting points for the deep phenomenon of [Langlands duality](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_dual), which we will not discuss here.

When ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is simple, one can impose a useful additional axiom on ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Say that a root system ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is _irreducible_ if ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) cannot be covered by the union ![{V \cup W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV+%5Ccup+W%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of two orthogonal proper subspaces of ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

> **Lemma 25** If ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is a simple Lie algebra, then the root system of ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is irreducible.
