# Chapter 1 Introduction 

K¨ ahler geometry represents what is as close as one can get to an exact intersection between algebraic geometry and differential geometry. This is reflected in the definition: K¨ ahler manifolds are complex manifolds with a Riemannian structure with respect to which the complex structure is orthogonal and parallel. Accordingly, through K¨ ahler metrics we often see a kind of dictionary appearing between the curvature of K¨ ahler manifolds and their vector bundles, and the algebraic geometry of the underlying manifold. The part of this dictionary concerning the Ricci curvature of K¨ ahler metrics has been very fruitfully investigated. First of all, the Ricci form of a K¨ ahler metric can be viewed as the curvature of the anti-canonical bundle induced by the K¨ ahler metric, and therefore represents (a positive multiple of) the first Chern class. Yau’s resolution of the Calabi conjecture gives that any form which represents the first Chern class can be realized as the Ricci form of a unique K¨ ahler metric in each K¨ ahler class [37]. Together with the Kodaira embedding theorem that ampleness of a line bundle corresponds to the existence of a positive Hermitian metric [22], this says the complex manifolds which furnish K¨ ahler metrics with positive Ricci curvature are precisely the smooth Fano varieties, an algebro-geometric characterization. This work by Yau also answered the negative and scalar-flat K¨ ahler-Einstein problems as those manifolds whose canonical bundles are ample and unitary-flat, respectively, and the positive case was settled by Chen, Donaldson, and Sun in terms of a geometric invariant theory-type algebro-geometric stability condition [10] [11] [12]. The scalar curvature of a (in most cases we are restricting to the compact case) K¨ ahler manifold ( M, ω ), 

S(ω) := −ωij ∂i∂j log det( ωpq ),

certainly has a similarly strong relationship with algebraic geometry as the Ricci curvature. For example, there is the formula

However, for some reasons including, technically, that scalar curvature is analytically much more difficult to study due to the prevalence of fourth order equations, and heuristically, that it is simply a much weaker and more flexible measure of curvature than Ricci curvature, there are many remaining open questions. The question of prescribed scalar curvature of K¨ ahler metrics is still completely open, including just the case of constant scalar curvature, though considerable progress on estimates for equations related to constant scalar curvature K¨ ahler metrics has been made in recent years [9]. The main motivation for this dissertation is the question of when a K¨ ahler manifold admits a K¨ ahler metric with scalar curvature positive everywhere. The starting point is an old result of Yau, which shows that even just positive total scalar curvature places quite a heavy restriction on the manifold, though still significantly less so than positive Ricci curvature: 

Proposition 1.0.1 (Yau [36]) . Let M be a compact complex manifold for which there exists a K¨ ahler metric of positive total scalar curvature. Then M has Kodaira dimension −∞ ,that is, the canonical bundle of M and all of its powers have no global holomorphic sections. Moreover, the converse is true in the case of compact K¨ ahler surfaces. 

This is done via a Bochner-type vanishing argument. The converse in the case of surfaces is due to the classification of complex surfaces, the first part of which says that any compact complex surface can be obtained from a minimal one (i.e. a smooth compact complex surface with no embedded P1 with self-intersection −1) via a finite sequence of blowups, a surgery which produces a new complex manifold by replacing a point with an embedded projective space of codimension 1. Secondly, for compact K¨ ahler surfaces of negative Kodaira dimension, it gives the minimal surfaces as being the projective plane, or a holomorphic P1-bundle over a compact Riemann surface. A positive scalar curvature K¨ ahler metric can be constructed explicitly on all such minimal surfaces, and then showing that positive total scalar curvature is preserved under blowup is just a cohomological calculation. Naturally, the place to get started on the problem of pointwise positive scalar curvature K¨ ahler metrics is K¨ ahler surfaces, where we have this nice classification of negative Kodaira dimension. The minimal surfaces are already taken care of, so we really are pushed onto the questions of whether one can construct a K¨ ahler metric on the blowup whose scalar curva-ture closely approximates that of the original K¨ ahler metric. Hitchin first investigated this question specifically for positive scalar curvature K¨ ahler metrics, with slight improvements in the surface case made by Sung: 

Proposition 1.0.2 (Hitchin [20], Sung [30]) . Let M be a compact complex manifold for which there exists a K¨ ahler metric of positive scalar curvature. Then if M has complex dimension at least 3, the blowup of any point also furnishes such a metric. In the case of complex dimension 2, any negative Kodaira dimension surface that can be obtained by no more than two successive blowups of a minimal surface has a positive scalar curvature K¨ ahler metric. M has a K¨ ahler metric of positive scalar curvature. 3. M has a Riemannian metric of positive scalar curvature. 4. M has Kodaira dimension −∞ .5. # Chapter 3 Analysis on K¨ ahler Blowups 

Compact algebraic surfaces are classified up to a surgery operation known as blowing up .This operation leaves the manifold unchanged everywhere but a point, with that point being replaced by smooth hypersurface isomorphic to projective space. The local and explicit nature of this operation leaves it malleable to an analytic approach. In this chapter we describe the blowup of a complex manifold, how to construct K¨ ahler metrics with nice analytic properties on the blowup of a K¨ ahler manifold, and finally we define function spaces with the geometry of the blowup in mind, so that they will be well suited to the analysis required to perturb the metrics constructed on the blowup to metrics with our desired scalar curvature properties.
