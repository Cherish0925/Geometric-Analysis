## Deep-Reasoning

This step changed the research state from accepting the restricted Ishida-LeBrun analytic bridge as essentially assembled to a sharper and more useful picture: the sign-general cutoff-flow existence theorem from Step 65 is a real reusable analytic capability, but it does not by itself solve the terminal calibration and global-quantifier problems that separate a restricted branch theorem from Yau 26. Under the active displayed formulation, proving one normalized sign branch in each dimension would suffice to prove the original interval statement, while a disproof must defeat all same-sign and cross-zero interval choices; hence neither the restricted negative IL gap nor a failed local gluing channel can replace the full problem. The new 20260912 research manuscript strengthens this orientation. Its opening explicitly treats the accumulated work as reusable mathematics rather than a completed theorem, gives the normalized branch language, separates local near-Einstein constructions from global Einstein nonexistence, and emphasizes the central scale problem: residual size only matters relative to the inverse, finite-dimensional obstruction, boundary flux, and nonlinear remainder at the same scale. The manuscript also sharpens the four-dimensional topology filter: if \(\sigma\operatorname{Ric}\) is positive and the pointwise Ricci eigenvalue ratio is at most \(3\), equivalently certainly when \(\delta_\sigma\le 1/2\), then strict Hitchin-Thorpe follows. Thus \(\#_4\mathbb{CP}^2\) as well as \(\#_5\mathbb{CP}^2\) and stronger violators cannot host arbitrarily tight \(\pm1\)-Ricci pinching; positive-Ricci connected-sum metrics on these topologies are negative controls, not live counterexample families. The same chapter gives a positive-branch theorem for complex or symplectic four-manifolds under this ratio hypothesis via Chen-LeBrun-Weber-type classification and existence, which is a real positive branch advance but still covers only those smooth types carrying such structure. The manuscript also repositions the IL route: it records explicit Seiberg-Witten lower bounds and the defect identity leading to the forbidden Derdzinski spectrum, but its listed unrestricted gap statements still require extra geometric hypotheses such as noncollapse, full-curvature bound, or Bach-flat constant-scalar input. This agrees with the main concern exposed by TTCS: the decisive interface is not merely per-datum flow existence, but whether the terminal metric after the cutoff flow has small calibrated density on pullback balls. There may be a route to close this by reapplying the metric-independent Seiberg-Witten and Chern-Weil identities to the terminal metric, rather than transporting the initial Weyl density through time; that derivation must be checked line by line under the current convention and geography. The 20260912 long-collar model and Weyl/source identities also alter route taste. A good \(L^2\) spectral gap can coexist with a logarithmically growing \(C^r\to C^0\) inverse even after quotienting true kernels, so any global inverse-function or gluing route that controls only energy norms is mathematically too weak for the maximum Ricci defect demanded by Yau 26. The Weyl chapter shows that exact negative minimum-eigenline balance gives equal nonnegative integrals rather than automatic vanishing, that near-closed self-dual forms cannot erase harmonic modes, and that with a source one needs the relative scale \(|q|=O(\eta\sqrt N)\), not just \(q\to0\). These formulas are valuable because they identify why previous shortcuts from small divergence, topology of torus slices, or formal kernel removal miss the actual obstruction. A03's new Koiso-Cauchy local theorem is especially important: analytic scalar \(-6\) metrics on \(T^3\) with totally geodesic data produce exact local \(\operatorname{Ric}=-3g\) four-dimensional germs whose mean curvature reverses through the torus, so any local no-reversal theorem based only on torus topology, scalar curvature, or self-adjointness is false. The graph-neck problem is therefore not closed; it has been transformed into a global half-core and matching problem. A02's mixed \(A_1\) work likewise remains useful only with the right interpretation: symmetry and spectral structure refine the local obstruction calculation, but a Green-operator compatibility computation is a first local input unless a full nonlinear desingularization is actually produced, and a local obstruction does not exclude unrelated Einstein metrics on the same smooth manifold. The strongest next work is therefore a critical mathematical reconciliation of three layers: the Step 65 per-datum sign-general flow, the Steps 55-63 IL smoothing machinery, and the new 20260912 formulas. The hard question is whether the restricted IL theorem can be stated without an extra terminal-density hypothesis; if not, the theorem must be renamed with its real hypotheses and the research should pivot toward the new global mechanisms: positive branch via orbifold compactness/desingularization plus collapse, graph-neck half-core matching after Koiso reopening, curve-bundle near-Einstein constructions with full Einstein nonexistence still missing, and possible higher-dimensional curvature-characteristic or charge quantization mechanisms.
