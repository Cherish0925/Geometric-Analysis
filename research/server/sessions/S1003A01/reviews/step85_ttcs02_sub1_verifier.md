## 1. Proposition 85.1

### 1.1 Exact norm identity

For a Kähler surface with complex orientation,
\[
\Lambda^+=\mathbb R\omega\oplus \operatorname{Re}\Lambda^{2,0},
\qquad |\omega|^2=2.
\]
The summands are pointwise orthogonal. Hence for \(\beta=\operatorname{Re}\theta\),
\[
|a\omega+\beta|^2
=a^2|\omega|^2+2a\langle\omega,\beta\rangle+|\beta|^2
=2a^2+|\beta|^2.
\]
This is valid exactly as written. With the usual complex-form convention,
\[
|\operatorname{Re}\theta|^2=\frac12|\theta|^2.
\]

### 1.2 Constant norm and triviality of \(K_V\)

If \(|\beta|\) is constant and \(\beta\neq0\), then \(|\theta|\) is a positive constant. Thus \(\theta\) is a nowhere-zero holomorphic section of \(K_V\), giving a holomorphic trivialization of \(K_V\). On a KE surface,
\[
\rho=E\omega,\qquad
c_1(V)=\frac{E}{2\pi}[\omega].
\]
Since \(E<0\) and \(\int_V\omega^2>0\), this real cohomology class is nonzero. Thus \(K_V\) cannot be trivial. Therefore constant \(|\beta|\) forces \(\beta=0\).

The \(E<0\) assumption is essential. For a Ricci-flat K3 surface or flat complex torus, the holomorphic symplectic form has constant norm and supplies a nonzero \(\beta\) of constant length.

### 1.3 Orbifold version

The same argument works for a compact Kähler orbifold:

* orbifold Hodge theory gives
  \[
  H_g^+=\mathbb R\omega\oplus\operatorname{Re}H^{2,0}_{\mathrm{orb}};
  \]
* a nowhere-zero orbifold section trivializes the orbifold line bundle;
* \(\rho/(2\pi)\) represents the orbifold first Chern class in real orbifold cohomology.

At an \(A_1\) point, the isotropy is generated by \(-1\in SU(2)\), and
\[
(-1)^*(dz_1\wedge dz_2)=dz_1\wedge dz_2.
\]
Thus a canonical section may be nonzero at the orbifold point, exactly as stated.

### 1.4 Quadratic estimate

Let
\[
Q_0(\beta)=|\beta|^2-\fint_V|\beta|^2.
\]
This is continuous and homogeneous of degree two on the finite-dimensional real space \(\operatorname{Re}H^{2,0}\). Its zero set is \(\{0\}\) by the preceding argument. Consequently,
\[
c_V:=\min_{\|\beta\|_{L^2}=1}\|Q_0(\beta)\|_{L^2}>0,
\]
and homogeneity gives
\[
\|Q_0(\beta)\|_{L^2}\ge c_V\|\beta\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
Proposition 85.1 is fully proved.

## 2. Theorem 85.A

## 2.1 Use of Nagy’s normal form for formal jets

Nagy states Theorem 1.6 for a smooth Einstein family, but the proof of Theorems 3.9 and 4.21 uses only the equations obtained from the first two Taylor coefficients. Therefore it applies to a formal jet satisfying
\[
\operatorname{Ric}_{g_t}-Eg_t=O(t^3),
\]
after formal volume and diffeomorphism normalization.

The resulting metric coefficients are
\[
H_1\in\mathscr E(V,g),\qquad
H_2^+=H_1^2,
\]
while \(H_2^-\) solves Nagy’s inhomogeneous equation and is determined modulo
\(\ker\Delta_E\). Thus the sentence in Step 85 should not suggest that \(H_2^-\) is arbitrary; only its \(J\)-type is needed for the coupled-form calculation.

Nagy’s paper treats manifolds. The orbifold extension is not directly furnished by that citation. The local tensor identities transfer immediately, while the global argument requires orbifold elliptic decomposition, orbifold Moser normalization, and orbifold Kähler identities. These are standard for compact Kähler orbifolds, but a short orbifold lemma should be included if the theorem is presented as proved there.

## 2.2 Pointwise algebra A1–A5

The script uses the correct conventions:

* \(G_t=I+tH+\cdots\) represents \(g^{-1}g_t\) at a \(g\)-orthonormal point;
* the two-form norm is
  \[
  |F|^2=\frac12F_{ab}F_{cd}G^{ac}G^{bd};
  \]
* the Hodge star uses the chosen orientation;
* \(P^-=(1-*)/2\).

The exact identities are:

\[
\left.\frac d{dt}\right|_0|\omega|_{g+tH}^2=-\operatorname{tr}H,
\]
and, for \(\beta\in\operatorname{Re}\Lambda^{2,0}\),
\[
\left.\partial_s\partial_t\right|_{0}
|\omega+t\beta|_{g+sH}^2=0.
\]
The second formula holds for every symmetric \(H\): the metric variation pairs \(H\) with an antisymmetric product of the two anticommuting self-dual endomorphisms.

For \(H\) anti-\(J\),
\[
DP^-_\omega[H]\omega=0,
\]
while a primitive \(J\)-invariant \(H\) generally produces a nonzero anti-self-dual component. Conformal changes leave the Hodge star on two-forms unchanged in dimension four.

For A5, write
\[
A(t)=I+tH_1+\frac{t^2}{2}H_2,\qquad
K_t=A(t)^{-1}J.
\]
If \(H_1J+JH_1=0\), direct expansion gives
\[
K_t^2
=-I+t^2\bigl(H_2^+-H_1^2\bigr)+O(t^3).
\]
Thus \(H_2^+=H_1^2\) is precisely the second-order compatibility relation ensuring that the fixed \(\omega\) remains self-dual and has norm \(\sqrt2\) through order two.

The script verifies A1–A4 symbolically for arbitrary coefficients. Its A5 calculation uses one rational sample, so the script alone does not prove A5 universally; the displayed expansion of \(K_t^2\) supplies the universal proof.

## 2.3 First-order coupled equations

After simultaneous gauge normalization,
\[
H_1\in\mathscr E(V,g)
=\{H\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2,-}:\bar\partial H=0,\ \delta H=0\}.
\]
Since \(DP^-[H_1]\omega=0\),
\[
d\beta_1=0,\qquad \beta_1^-=0.
\]
Hence \(\beta_1\) is harmonic and self-dual:
\[
\beta_1=c\omega+\operatorname{Re}\theta.
\]
The first length equation is
\[
2\langle\omega,\beta_1\rangle-\operatorname{tr}H_1=0.
\]
Since \(\operatorname{tr}H_1=0\), one gets \(c=0\). This part is correct.

## 2.4 Holomorphic transport of the mixed term

Set
\[
J'=JH_1,\qquad J_s=J+sJ',
\qquad g_s=\omega(\,\cdot\,,J_s\,\cdot\,).
\]
Because \(H_1\) anticommutes with \(J\),
\[
J'J+JJ'=0.
\]
The equation \(\bar\partial H_1=0\) is the linearized integrability equation for the associated Beltrami differential \(\phi\).

For \(\theta\in H^{2,0}\), first-order type preservation prescribes
\[
(\theta')^{1,1}=\iota_\phi\theta
\]
up to a convention-dependent sign. Since
\[
\bar\partial(\iota_\phi\theta)=0,
\]
the form \(\partial(\iota_\phi\theta)\) is both \(\partial\)-exact and \(\bar\partial\)-closed. The \(\partial\bar\partial\)-lemma yields a \((1,0)\)-form \(\xi\) with
\[
\partial(\iota_\phi\theta)=\partial\bar\partial\xi.
\]
Taking
\[
(\theta')^{2,0}=\partial\xi
\]
gives
\[
\bar\partial(\theta')^{2,0}
=-\partial(\iota_\phi\theta),
\]
so \(d(\theta+s\theta')=O(s^2)\).

For \(\beta_s=\operatorname{Re}(\theta+s\theta')\), one has to first order:

* \(d\beta_s=0\);
* \(\beta_s\) is \(g_s\)-self-dual;
* \(\langle\omega,\beta_s\rangle_{g_s}=0\) pointwise.

Differentiating the last identity gives
\[
\langle\omega,\beta'\rangle_g
+D_g\langle\omega,\beta_1\rangle[H_1]=0.
\]
The second term is zero by A2, so
\[
\langle\omega,\beta'\rangle=0
\]
pointwise. This is correct.

## 2.5 Second-order form equation and length equation

Expanding the anti-self-dual equation gives
\[
d\beta_2=0,\qquad
\beta_2^-=-2\,DP^-[H_1]\beta_1.
\]
The transported derivative satisfies
\[
(\beta')^-=-DP^-[H_1]\beta_1.
\]
Therefore every solution is
\[
\beta_2=2\beta'+\gamma,
\qquad
\gamma\in H_g^+
=\mathbb R\omega\oplus\operatorname{Re}H^{2,0}.
\]
Writing \(\gamma=c_\gamma\omega+\eta\),
\[
\langle\omega,\beta_2\rangle
=2c_\gamma,
\]
a constant. Conversely, every real constant is obtained by choosing \(c_\gamma\). Thus the admissible length corrections compatible with closedness and self-duality are exactly the constants.

The norm expansion is
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\omega_t|_{g_t}^2
={}&|\omega|_{g_t}^2
+2t\langle\omega,\beta_1\rangle_{g_t}\\
&+t^2\bigl(|\beta_1|_g^2+\langle\omega,\beta_2\rangle_g\bigr)
+O(t^3).
\end{aligned}
\]
A5 removes the pure metric term through order two, and A2 removes the mixed metric–\(\beta_1\) term. Hence solvability is equivalent to
\[
|\beta_1|^2=\text{constant}.
\]
Proposition 85.1 then gives \(\beta_1=0\). No second-order term is missing.

The theorem’s first sentence is slightly circular: a “formal second-order AKE jet” is already a solved second-order object. The mathematically precise formulation is:

> A first-order coupled infinitesimal pair \((H_1,\beta_1)\) extends to order two if and only if \(\beta_1=0\), with \(H_1\) arbitrary in \(\mathscr E(V,g)\).

For manifolds, Theorem 85.A is fully proved after this wording repair. For orbifolds, the same result follows from a standard orbifold adaptation, but that adaptation is not supplied by Nagy’s theorem itself.

## 3. Theorem 85.B

## 3.1 Flat indicial calculation

Write a self-dual form on flat \(\mathbb R^4\setminus\{0\}\) as
\[
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^3f_i\omega_i
\]
in a parallel self-dual frame. If \(d\alpha=0\), then \(\delta\alpha=0\), so \(\Delta\alpha=0\), and each \(f_i\) is harmonic.

The closed homogeneous modes occur in degrees
\[
d\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\cup\{-4,-5,-6,\ldots\}.
\]
There are no modes in degrees \(-2\) and \(-3\). More precisely,
\[
\dim\mathcal H_d^{\mathrm{closed},+}
=(d+1)(d+3),\qquad d\ge0,
\]
and
\[
\dim\mathcal H_{-4-m}^{\mathrm{closed},+}
=(m+1)(m+3),\qquad m\ge0.
\]
This agrees with the script values \(3,8,15,24\) and \(3,8\).

The script only computes the first four growing scalar degrees and their Kelvin transforms. The all-degree statement needs the general spherical-harmonic argument; it is correct, but it is not established solely by the finite script output.

There are no logarithmic modes because the scalar harmonic indicial roots \(l\) and \(-l-2\) never coincide for an actual spherical degree \(l\ge0\).

If \(\Gamma\subset SU(2)\) acts trivially on \(\Lambda^+\), invariance is imposed only on the scalar coefficients. A nontrivial free action on \(S^3\) has no invariant linear functions, so degree one disappears. If \(-1\in\Gamma\), all odd degrees disappear. For \(A_1\),
\[
\{\ldots,-6,-4\}\cup\{0,2,4,\ldots\}
\]
is correct.

## 3.2 Constant mode across a neck

On an exact \(A_1\) cone, a bounded solution decomposes into:

* one constant mode;
* growing modes beginning at degree \(2\);
* decaying modes beginning at degree \(-4\).

For a perturbed cone, a rigorous estimate has the form
\[
|c(\tau_2)-c(\tau_1)|
\le
C\|\alpha\|_{L^\infty}
\int_{\tau_1}^{\tau_2}
\bigl(|e|+r|\nabla e|\bigr)\,d\tau,
\qquad \tau=\log r,
\]
together with spectral estimates for all nonconstant modes. The note states this mechanism but does not derive the projected first-order system or its long-cylinder estimate.

The phrase “uniformly on the neck” also needs a moving-neck convention. If the outer neck radius is fixed, a smooth orbifold harmonic form generally differs from its value at \(p\) by \(O(r^2)\), which does not tend to zero at that fixed outer end. A correct one-parameter formulation uses
\[
a_nR_n\le r\le \rho_n,\qquad
R_n\to\infty,\quad \rho_n\to0,
\]
and obtains schematically
\[
|\omega_n-c_n|
\le
C\left(r^2+\left(\frac{a_n}{r}\right)^4+\varepsilon_n\right),
\]
so
\[
\sup_{\text{neck}}|\omega_n-c_n|
\le C\bigl(\rho_n^2+R_n^{-4}+\varepsilon_n\bigr)\to0.
\]

This gives an explicit counterexample to the fixed-outer-radius reading: even the limiting orbifold form can have a nonzero quadratic Taylor term, leaving an \(O(\rho^2)\) variation independent of \(n\).

## 3.3 Relation to Ozuch’s weighted estimate

Ozuch’s quoted weighted \(C^{2,\alpha}_{\beta,*}\)-closeness plausibly supplies the required integrated smallness after choosing a moving neck. The naive model errors have the form
\[
O(r^2)+O(a_n^4r^{-4}),
\]
and therefore
\[
\int_{a_nR_n}^{\rho_n}
\left(r^2+a_n^4r^{-4}\right)\frac{dr}{r}
=O(\rho_n^2+R_n^{-4}).
\]
A perturbation \(O(\delta_nr^\beta)\), \(\beta>0\), has logarithmic integral \(O(\delta_n/\beta)\). What is absent from Step 85 is the explicit conversion from Ozuch’s weighted norm to the displayed \(e,\nabla e\) estimate.

## 3.4 Liouville arguments

On a hyperkähler ALE space, \(\Lambda^+\) has a global parallel frame. A bounded harmonic self-dual form has harmonic scalar coefficients. Since the ALE metric is complete and \(\operatorname{Ric}=0\), any bounded harmonic scalar function is constant, by applying Yau’s theorem to a positive shift. Hence every bounded harmonic self-dual form is parallel.

Likewise, a bounded closed self-dual form on the entire flat cone is constant by the indicial classification. These statements are correct.

## 3.5 Uniform \(L^\infty\) estimate and \(b^+\)

The blow-up argument for
\[
\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}\le C\|\omega\|_{L^2}
\]
has the right structure:

1. an exterior maximum gives a nonzero smooth limit with zero \(L^2\) mass;
2. a bubble maximum gives a nonzero parallel bubble limit;
3. a neck maximum gives a nonzero constant cone limit;
4. the neck trace transports that constant to a region of fixed macroscopic volume.

The last step depends on the quantitative neck estimate above.

The equality
\[
b^+(M)=b^+_{\mathrm{orb}}(V)
\]
also uses topology, not only local PDE. Mayer–Vietoris adds the ALE intersection lattice, which has sign \(-\) in the hyperkähler orientation. Thus no new \(+\) directions appear. Once dimensions agree and the \(L^\infty\) estimate excludes concentration, convergence of the harmonic spaces follows.

**Status:** the indicial and Liouville components are fully correct. The full neck theorem is highly plausible under the sharpened moving-neck formulation, but Step 85 does not contain the weighted mode estimate needed for a complete proof.

Corollary 85.B′ follows once that theorem is available: uniform \(L^\infty\) control gives \(L^4\) convergence, vanishing core volume removes lost mass, and the limiting form lies in \(\operatorname{Re}H^{2,0}_{\mathrm{orb}}\).

## 4. The \(A_1\) period interpretation in 85.D

At a KE point on a Kähler surface,
\[
\mathcal R^+=W^++\frac{s}{12}\operatorname{Id}.
\]
With \(I=\omega/\sqrt2\),
\[
W^+=\frac{s}{6}I\otimes I-\frac{s}{12}
(\operatorname{Id}-I\otimes I),
\]
hence
\[
\mathcal R^+=\frac{s}{4}I\otimes I.
\]
Therefore
\[
\ker\mathcal R^+=I^\perp.
\]

The quoted first desingularization obstruction
\[
\mathcal R^+(p)\widehat\zeta\to0
\]
forces
\[
\langle\widehat\zeta,I\rangle\to0.
\]
The neck trace identifies the asymptotic bubble form with \(I\). Consequently,
\[
\langle\omega_b(\infty),\widehat\zeta\rangle=0.
\]

For Eguchi–Hanson, the period vector satisfies
\[
\int_S\omega_i=C\zeta_i.
\]
Thus for the selected bubble form \(\omega_n=n_i\omega_i\),
\[
\int_S\omega_n=C\,n\cdot\zeta=o(|\zeta|).
\]
Since \(\operatorname{Area}(S)\asymp|\zeta|\), the exceptional sphere is asymptotically Lagrangian for the selected complex structure.

The geometric wording can be sharpened:

* in complex structure \(\widehat\zeta\), the sphere is holomorphic and the form in a direction \(n\perp\widehat\zeta\) is the real or imaginary part of the holomorphic symplectic form;
* in complex structure \(n\), the sphere is a special Lagrangian vanishing cycle, which is the smoothing picture.

Thus “smoothing rather than resolution” is correct asymptotically, not necessarily as an exact statement at each finite \(n\).

### Eguchi–Hanson tail

The expansion
\[
\phi(u)
=\sqrt{u^2+a^4}
+a^2\log\frac{u}{a^2+\sqrt{u^2+a^4}}

\[
\omega_1
=\omega_1^0-\frac{a^4}{2}i\partial\bar\partial(r^{-2})

Since \(r^{-2}\) is harmonic on \(\mathbb R^4\setminus\{0\}\),

has no such tail in the standard asymptotic gauge. This part is correct.

## 5. \(T_+(M)=T_+(V)\) and the action formula

For an Einstein four-metric,
\[
4\pi^2(2\chi+3\tau)
=
\int\left(2|W^+|^2+\frac{s^2}{24}\right)d\mu,
\]
with orbifold characteristic numbers on \(V\).

An orientation-matched Eguchi–Hanson bubble has
\[
s=0,\qquad W^+=0,
\]
so its relative \(+\)-charge is zero. Therefore the orbifold Chern–Weil quantity satisfies
\[
T_+(M)=T_+(V).
\]

It is important that \(T_+(V)\) use orbifold characteristic numbers. If one instead uses the ordinary invariants of the underlying singular topological space, the equality is false. Resolving an \(A_1\) point adds one exceptional class of sign \(-\):
\[
\Delta\chi=1,\qquad \Delta\tau=-1,
\qquad \Delta(2\chi+3\tau)=-1.
\]
The orbifold correction restores equality:
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(V)=\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(V)-\frac12,
\]
while the \(A_1\) signature correction is zero, giving the same \(2\chi+3\tau\) value as the smooth resolution.

At a KE orbifold,
\[
|W^+|^2=\frac{s^2}{24},
\]
so
\[
T_+(V)=\frac{\widehat S^2(V)}8
\]
in the normalization
\(\widehat S^2=\int s^2d\mu\). With the Step-83 normalization
\[
D(g)=\frac{\widehat S^2(g)}{16}-\frac{T_+}{2},
\]
one obtains
\[
D(g)=\frac{\widehat S^2(g)-\widehat S^2(V)}{16}.
\]
Thus formula (5.1) is correct with orbifold \(T_+\).

## 6. Conditional Theorem 85.M

## 6.1 Raw versus normalized obstruction

There is a normalization ambiguity in the note. If the raw obstruction is
\[
\Lambda_I(\zeta,y)
=
c_0\frac{s}{4}\,\zeta_I
+O(|\zeta|^{1+\delta}),
\]
then the natural \(C^1\) estimate is
\[
\partial_{\zeta_I}\Lambda_I
=
c_0\frac{s}{4}+o(1).
\]

If instead
\[
\lambda_I=\frac{\Lambda_I}{|\zeta|},
\]
then the leading term is
\[
\lambda_I
=
c_0\frac{s}{4}\frac{\zeta_I}{|\zeta|}.
\]
Its exact derivative is
\[
\partial_{\zeta_I}\lambda_I
=
c_0\frac{s}{4}
\frac{|\zeta_\perp|^2}{|\zeta|^3},
\]
not \(c_0s/(4|\zeta|)\) throughout a full neighborhood. The latter asymptotic holds in a conic tube where
\[
\zeta_I=o(|\zeta|),
\]
as is expected near the smoothing locus. Along the pure normal ray \(\zeta_\perp=0\), the derivative of \(\zeta_I/|\zeta|\) is zero away from the origin.

Thus hypothesis (b) is correctly scaled only for a normalized obstruction in a thin tube around the smoothing graph. For a uniform statement in all nearby directions, the raw obstruction formulation is cleaner.

## 6.2 Mean-value argument

Suppose
\[
\mathcal K=\{\zeta_I=\kappa(\zeta_\perp,y)\}
\]
and \(\Lambda_I=0\) on \(\mathcal K\). If
\[
\partial_{\zeta_I}\Lambda_I
=
c_0\frac{s}{4}+o(1)
\]
has a fixed nonzero sign in a tube around \(\mathcal K\), then
\[
\Lambda_I(\zeta,y)
=
(\zeta_I-\kappa)\,
\partial_{\zeta_I}\Lambda_I(\xi,\zeta_\perp,y)
\]
for an intermediate \(\xi\). Therefore \(\Lambda_I=0\) forces
\[
\zeta_I=\kappa.
\]
The note’s displayed factor should be evaluated at the intermediate point rather than automatically at the endpoint \(\zeta\). This does not alter the zero-set conclusion.

Directions outside the tube are handled by the leading angular obstruction:
\[
\left|\frac{\zeta_I}{|\zeta|}\right|\ge\varepsilon
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|\lambda_I|\ge c\varepsilon+o(1).
\]
Combining the angular estimate with the normal derivative estimate covers a punctured neighborhood of the orbifold point.

## 6.3 Dimension of the Kähler smoothing locus

For an \(A_1\) model, the local smoothing parameter is one complex number, corresponding to the real plane \(\zeta_\perp\). The remaining real period component \(\zeta_I\) is the resolution direction. Hence a graph
\[
\zeta_I=\kappa(\zeta_\perp,y)
\]
has real codimension one in the full \((\zeta,y)\)-space.

However, global \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein smoothability plus smoothness of the total deformation base does not by itself give such a graph for every pair \((\zeta_\perp,y)\). One also needs the global-to-local deformation map to be locally onto the \(A_1\) smoothing parameter and compatible with the \(y\)-directions. The graph and existence of KE smoothings should therefore remain explicit hypotheses rather than consequences of the two shorter phrases.

**Status:** the local mean-value mechanism is correct after distinguishing raw and normalized obstructions. The \(1/|\zeta|\) derivative formula is not valid in a full neighborhood of all period directions. The graph has codimension one once the local-to-global smoothing and period-map assumptions are included.
