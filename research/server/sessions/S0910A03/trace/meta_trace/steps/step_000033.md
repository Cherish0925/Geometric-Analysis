## Deep-Reasoning

This step uncovered a consequential mathematical error that had propagated through Steps 29-31 and across the session group: the Moser absorption coefficient for the weighted cutoff flow is \(\gamma = S^2 H_2^{1/2}\), not \(\beta = S H_2^{1/2}\). The product \(\beta = S \sqrt{H_2} = (r^2/\sqrt{V})(K_0 V)^{1/2} = r^2\sqrt{K_0}\) is genuinely volume-independent because the single power of \(S\) cancels against \(\sqrt{V}\) from \(H_2\). But the actual \(p=2\) Moser absorption step produces \(K_3 \le K_2^{1/2} \|Z_2\|_4^2 \le K_2^{1/2} S^2(D_2 + r^{-2}K_2)\), where the Sobolev embedding \(\|Z_2\|_4^2 \le S^2(\|\nabla Z_2\|_2^2 + r^{-2}\|Z_2\|_2^2)\) introduces two powers of \(S\). The coefficient to be absorbed is therefore \(\gamma = S^2 K_2^{1/2} = S\beta = r^4 K_0^{1/2}/\sqrt{V}\), which diverges as \(V(B_{6r}) \to 0\) under collapse. This means the weighted Moser iteration fails on collapsing balls, and the claimed family-uniform gap theorem from Step 29 is not established.

The correction preserves a meaningful per-manifold result. For a fixed manifold \(M\) in the controlled Ishida-LeBrun family: CGB gives total volume \(V(M) \ge c_0 A > 0\); Bishop-Gromov with bounded total volume gives diameter \(D(M) \le C(\chi(M))\); BG monotonicity then gives ball volume \(V(B_{6r}) \ge v(M) > 0\) depending only on \(\chi(M)\). Hence \(\gamma \le C(M)\) is bounded, the Moser iteration converges with \(C(M)\)-dependent constants, and the full chain (curvature Moser \(\to\) defect Moser \(\to\) integrable drift \(\to\) metric equivalence \(\to\) terminal calibration \(\to\) Einstein germ contradiction) produces a per-manifold gap \(\delta_0(M) > 0\).

The Strategist's separation of requirements was implemented and found to be productive. The zero-order bootstrap (\(\|aF\|_\infty\), \(\|aE\|_\infty\), metric drift) works per-manifold with \(C(M)\)-dependent constants. The higher-order continuation (\(m=2\) term, coupled Gronwall, DeTurck existence) is per-metric: for each fixed smooth datum, all derivatives remain finite, with constants depending on the individual metric. The plateau curvature \(|\mathrm{Rm}| \le C(M)\) is per-manifold. The global \(Q = \int|E|^2\) propagation is topology-uniform (\(Q(T) \le C\delta^2 A\), no Sobolev constant). The calibration inequality is metric-by-metric. The terminal exponential-pullback averaging uses the per-manifold curvature bound to define charts, and the topology-uniform \(Q\) to control the average calibration defect.

The deeper question exposed by this correction is whether the per-manifold result can be upgraded to family-uniform or dimension-only. Three potential routes deserve investigation: (1) Relative Sobolev inequalities (Li-Wang 2002) that may avoid the volume-dependent constant; their isoperimetric input could come from the Ricci lower bound directly rather than through ball volumes. (2) Topological constraints on collapse: for the standard IL subfamily with \(\pi_1 = 0\), Cheeger-Gromov-Taylor noncollapse under bounded sectional curvature would eliminate collapse entirely, but the extension from Ricci to sectional bounds is nontrivial. (3) Direct use of Li's smoothing theorem: Li gives \(|\mathrm{Rm}| \le C/t\) from \(r^2\int|\mathrm{Rm}|^2 \le \epsilon_0\) with volume-independent constants; if the terminal argument could tolerate the \(1/t\) singularity rather than requiring the uniform Moser endpoint, the family-uniform chain might close. Each of these bypasses the Sobolev-constant bottleneck from a different direction.

For the full Yau 26 problem, the current restricted result shows that no individual IL manifold can serve as a negative-branch counterexample with tight enough pinching. But the gap depends on the manifold, which is weaker than the required dimension-only constants. The positive branch, arbitrary four-dimensional topology, dimensions \(n \ge 5\), and Lean formalization remain entirely open.
