# Round 20: a different bundle target and the limits of existing controls

22 September 2026.

This round does not produce a new near-Einstein closed metric family, settle
Einstein existence on a previously undecided smooth manifold, or solve a
Yau26 sign branch. It obtains a restricted quantitative estimate and an exact
period-domain criterion, and specifies a different bundle problem using
classical subgroup constructions. The positive-branch work is a classical
smooth-topology comparison, not a new obstruction theorem.

## 1. A topology test not covered by the free-quotient construction

There is an actual flat smooth bundle

\[
\Sigma_6\longrightarrow M_\rho\longrightarrow\Sigma_2
\]

with faithful mapping-class monodromy in the subgroup generated by separating
twists. The representation lifts to area-preserving diffeomorphisms fixing a
disk. Consequently it has a square-zero section and

\[
\chi(M_\rho)=20,\qquad \tau(M_\rho)=0,\qquad
b_1=16,\quad b_2^+=b_2^-=25.
\]

The construction combines a genus-two surface subgroup of the pentagon
right-angled Artin group with high powers of five separating twists on the
genus-six fibre. Disjoint supports realize the Artin relations as actual
diffeomorphism identities. The surface relation therefore holds before
passing to mapping classes. The full construction and original subgroup
references are in [the construction note, Section 9](round20_construction.md).

Faithfulness has a useful consequence: this monodromy cannot factor through
any free group, including after a finite base cover. Otherwise a closed
surface group would embed in a free group. In contrast, the arbitrary
free-quotient monodromy construction was already included in Round 11; neither
noncommutativity nor pseudo-Anosov monodromy alone was a new extension.

This separates two conditions previously easy to conflate:

\[
\text{zero signature}\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{monodromy factors through a free group}.
\]

The signature obstruction below vanishes on this bundle, but the established
free-quotient metric construction does not apply to its specified projection.
Whether its total smooth manifold has another Round-11 decomposition remains
unknown. In particular this is a concrete research target, not a new
diffeomorphism-type classification or a near-Einstein example.

The unresolved construction question is now precise: can this fixed total
space admit \(\delta_-(g_j)\to0\)? A negative answer restricted to its given
submersion would not decide that question for arbitrary metrics. A positive
answer would enlarge the input construction only after checking whether the
total smooth type is already covered in a different way. Its unrestricted
Einstein existence is a separate question. No representation-theoretic name
or vanishing characteristic number supplies either missing metric theorem.

## 2. Quantitative cost of nonzero signature in the adapted class

For a surface bundle with minimal fibres, intrinsic fibre curvature
\(-\kappa\), and base curvature \(-\kappa\), with no flatness or injectivity
radius assumption, the construction note proves

\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac{\|g^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}_g+\kappa I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}}{\kappa}
\ge\frac{3|\tau(M)|}{2\chi(M)}.
\quad}
\]

In the real metric-tensor normalization, the moduli energy satisfies
\(\int|df|^2\ge48\pi^2|\tau|\). TT projection converts this to
\(\int Q\ge12\pi^2|\tau|/\kappa\), where
\(Q=\sum_a\operatorname{tr}S_a^2\) is the fibre-shape energy. The horizontal
Ricci equation gives \(Q+2|A|^2\le2\delta\). Thus connection curvature cannot
cancel this contribution. The product-normalized volume then gives the bound.

If base curvature is variable, the corresponding statement is

\[
(1+\delta/\kappa)\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}{V_0}
\ge1+\frac{3|\tau|}{2\chi},\qquad
V_0=\frac{4\pi^2\chi}{\kappa^2}.
\]

The moduli-signature formula, Ricci blocks, and exact submersion rigidity
were already in the old Skill. The incremental deduction is this linear
defect/volume inequality under its stated geometry, not a new general
signature obstruction. In particular nonzero-signature Kodaira fibrations
with exact negative Kähler–Einstein metrics rule out extrapolation to all
metrics. [Full proof and comparison](round20_construction.md).

## 3. What simultaneous monopole projections actually control

The rigidity note extracts simultaneous projection bounds from the classical
curvature inequality and the inherited integral gap without adding scalar
energy saturation. It then proves the exact finite-dimensional criterion:

> For a finite set of classes, the maximum of their positive-projection
> squares is a proper function on the positive-plane domain if and only if
> their real span contains a maximal positive or a maximal negative subspace.

This includes a proof of necessity for degenerate spans. It gives a usable
criterion, not automatic compactness of the space of Einstein metrics.

A fixed product \(\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h\) provides an exact geometric
calibration. Its complete set of special actual monopole classes is
\(\{\pm K\}\), and its hyperbolic product metrics have zero Einstein defect
and exact scalar-energy saturation. Pinching a nonseparating curve nevertheless
drives a primitive integral two-class to zero Hodge norm. The period planes
escape even modulo integral isometries, not just by changing a marking.

Thus sharper bounds on a small monopole span do not control the harmonic
directions it misses. The finite-dimensional argument and product degeneration
are classical mechanisms; the improved projection radius comes from the
earlier integral theorem. [Criterion and proofs](round20_rigidity.md).

## 4. Positive candidates: homeomorphism does not supply the exact output

The standard \(\#_r(S^2\times S^2)\), \(r\ge2\), and the listed mixed
product resolutions have vanishing Seiberg–Witten invariants in both
orientations. Taubes' theorem excludes symplectic structures, and their even
first Betti number then excludes complex structures. Hence a homeomorphic
projective Kähler–Einstein surface cannot be diffeomorphic to these standard
models.

An explicit classical control is Teicher's simply connected spin surface
\(Y_0(7,4)\): it has ample canonical bundle and signature zero, and is
homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to
\(\#_{1225\cdot56!-1}(S^2\times S^2)\). It admits a negative
Kähler–Einstein metric; that metric does not transfer to the standard sum.
The note also preserves the pending analytic qualification of the inherited
all-rank ADE approximation, rather than treating it as an established A1
theorem. [Exact comparison and sources](round20_global.md).

This is a classical application identifying a genuine smooth-structure
mismatch. It neither rules out non-Kähler Einstein metrics on the standard
models nor supplies them. It is not counted as a new Yau26 obstruction.

## 5. Research consequence and verification

There is no new reason this round to improve the Berger length constant,
revisit \(\#_4\mathbb{CP}^2\), or seek larger budgets already excluded by
Round 19. Nor is arbitrary free-quotient monodromy a new construction goal.
The faithful Torelli bundle instead isolates a zero-signature surface relation
not handled by that construction. Its metric problem cannot be solved merely
by substituting more complicated images of free generators in the old proof.
A different construction, or a different decomposition of its total space,
would be needed to obtain a new input from this target.

Four executable checks accompany the proofs. They verify coordinate Ricci
identities with both flat and nonflat connections, normalization factors,
period-domain algebra, incidence data, and characteristic-number arithmetic.
They do not replace the subgroup theorems, monopole input, or global geometric
proofs. See [verification](round20_verification.md). All changes are confined
to this local research worktree; installed Skills and running Sessions are
unchanged.
