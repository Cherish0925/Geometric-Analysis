This is real coercivity, but normalized local Moser estimates still require localization of that excess; they do not automatically yield the near-sharp ceiling 6+O(δ). At c=1/2 the zero-defect reaction level is 9, below the reflected-center average at least 12; its perturbed root is 9+5δ+(8/9)δ²+O(δ³). Thus incorporating the area equation creates a larger order-one separation than the orbit energy alone.

This matches the exact round-cusp benchmark, where the radial Dirichlet spectrum tends to 9/4 rather than zero as collar length grows. The resulting bootstrap is not yet closed: its global spectral estimate depends on B, and a coarse scalar layer can balance an O(W²) reaction by an O(δ√W) flux over thickness O(δW^(−3/2)). What may exclude that layer is precisely what the scalar model omits: the gradient coercivity, the target-map Maurer–Cartan integrability, the sharp trace/traceless defect relation, and the horizontal Einstein constraints. Establishing their combined effect, or constructing them compatibly, is now the difficult center of this restricted negative-branch route.
