Corollary 4.1 Let $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underline{H}, \underline{J})$ be a partially integrable contact almost CR manifold that admits an infinitesimal transverse symmetry $\underline{e}_{0}$. Let $\underline{\theta}^{0}$ be the contact form dual to $\underline{e}_{0}$ and $\underline{\nabla}$ its corresponding Webster-Tanaka connection. Denote by $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underset{\sim}{h}, \underset{\sim}{J})$ the almost Kähler (local) leaf space of the foliation defined by $\underline{e}_{0}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] The Ricci tensor of the Levi-Civita connection $\underset{\sim}{\nabla}$ of $\underset{\sim}{h}$ commutes with $\underset{\sim}{J}$, i.e. ${\underset{\sim}{J}}_{(i}{ }^{k}{\underset{\operatorname{Ric}}{j}}_{j) k}=0$, if and only if $\underline{\nabla}^{\gamma} \underline{N}_{\gamma(\alpha \beta)}=0$.

\section*{5 Almost Robinson structures}

Further details on the content of this section can be found in［47，74，75，18］．Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c})$ be a time－ oriented and oriented Lorentzian conformal manifold of dimension $2 m+2$ ．An almost Robinson structure on $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c})$ consists of a pair $(N, K)$ where $N$ is a complex distribution of rank $m+1$ ，
totally null with respect to $\mathbf{c}$, and $K$ is a real null line distribution such that ${ }^{\mathbf{C}} K=N \cap \bar{N}$. When $N$ is involutive or integrable, ${ }^{3}$ i.e. $[N, N] \subset N$, we say that $(N, K)$ is a Robinson structure. The quadruple $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, N, K)$ is referred to as an almost Robinson manifold or almost Robinson geometry, and as a Robinson manifold or Robinson geometry when $N$ is integrable.

The complex distribution $N$ in the definition above is also referred to as an almost null structure [68]. It is said to have (regular) real index one: at every point $p$ of $\mathcal{M}$, the dimension of the real span of $N_{p} \cap \bar{N}_{p}$ is one [29].

One can equivalently describe an almost Robinson structure as an optical structure whose screen bundle $H_{K}=K^{\perp} / K$ is equipped with a bundle complex structure $J$ compatible with the screen bundle conformal structure. Here, we identify the eigenbundles $H_{K}^{(1,0)}$ and $H_{K}^{(0,1)}$ of $J$ with the subbundles $\bar{N} /{ }^{\mathbf{C}} K$ and $N /{ }^{\mathbf{C}} K$ of ${ }^{\mathbf{C}} H_{K}$. Under certain conditions, $(N, K)$ induces an almost CR structure $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$ on the (local) leaf space of $\mathcal{K}$, and one can show that the involutivity of $(N, K)$ is equivalent to the involutivity of $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$.

An almost Robinson structure clearly defines an optical structure $K$ and a congruence of null curves $\mathcal{K}$ associated to it. But in general, not every optical geometry is endowed with a distinguished almost Robinson structure. There is however one exception that is particularly relevant to the present article: let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ be an optical geometry of dimension $2 m+2$ with twisting congruence of oriented null geodesics $\mathcal{K}$. Let $k$ be an optical vector field, and suppose that its twist satisfies
$$\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i k} \boldsymbol{\tau}^{k}{ }_{j}+\frac{1}{2 m} \boldsymbol{\tau}_{k \ell} \boldsymbol{\tau}^{k \ell} \boldsymbol{h}_{i j}=0 .$$
Then we can always find an optical vector field whose twist defines a bundle complex structure $J$ on the screen bundle $H_{K}$, compatible with the conformal structure there [17, 18]. In other words, the twist induces an almost Robinson structure on $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c})$. We shall refer to an almost Robinson structure arising in this way as a twist-induced almost Robinson structure.

As shown in [17, 18], a twist-induced almost Robinson structure with non-shearing congruence of null geodesics $\mathcal{K}$ induces a partially integrable contact almost CR structure $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$ on the leaf space $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$ of $\mathcal{K}$. Further, locally there is a one-to-one correspondence between metrics in ${ }^{\text {n.e. }}$ and contact forms in $\operatorname{Ann}(\underline{H})$. More specifically, there is a unique optical vector field $k$ whose twist is normalised to $2 m$ for any metric $g$ in $\stackrel{\text { n.e. }}{\mathbf{c}}$. In particular, for every $g$ in $\stackrel{\text { n.e. }}{\mathbf{c}}$, the 1-form $\kappa=g(k, \cdot)$ satisfies $£_{k} \kappa=0$, and descends to a contact form $2 \underline{\theta}^{0}$ on $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underline{H}, \underline{J})$ - the factor of 2 has been added for later convenience. We can choose a coframe $\left\{\underline{\theta}^{0}, \underline{\theta}^{\alpha}, \underline{\theta}^{\bar{\alpha}}\right\}$ adapted to $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$, which we then pull back to $\mathcal{M}$. Similarly, the Levi form $\underline{h}_{\alpha \bar{\beta}}$ can be pulled back to $\mathcal{M}$, and can be identified with the screen bundle metric induced from $g$. Denoting by $\varpi$ the local surjective submersion from $\mathcal{M}$ to $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$, we can then express the metric $g$ as
$$g=2 \theta^{0} \odot \theta_{0}+2 h_{\alpha \bar{\beta}} \theta^{\alpha} \odot \bar{\theta}^{\bar{\beta}}$$
where
$$\kappa=\theta^{0}=2 \varpi^{*} \underline{\theta}^{0}, \quad \theta^{\alpha}=\varpi^{*} \underline{\theta}^{\alpha}, \quad \bar{\theta}^{\bar{\alpha}}=\varpi^{*} \bar{\theta}^{\bar{\alpha}}, \quad h_{\alpha \bar{\beta}}=\varpi^{*} \underline{h}_{\alpha \bar{\beta}} .$$

\section*{6 Optical geometries, almost Robinson structures and almost CR structures}

We are now in a position to return to our study of non-shearing congruences of null geodesics. We assume $n=2 m$ with $m>1$. For clarity, we project the integrability condition (3.5) onto the screen
bundle:
$$W^{0}{ }_{i j}{ }^{0}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i k} \boldsymbol{\tau}^{k}{ }_{j}+\frac{1}{2 m} \boldsymbol{\tau}_{k l} \boldsymbol{\tau}^{k l} \boldsymbol{h}_{i j} .$$
With reference to the algebraic constraint (5.1) on the twist, we conclude that the LHS of (6.1) vanishes, i.e. $W^{0}{ }_{i}{ }^{0}{ }_{j}=0$, if and only if the twist of $\mathcal{K}$ induces an almost Robinson structure. Therefore, following the discussion of Section 5 on the relation between almost CR structures and almost Robinson structures, we immediately arrive at the first main result, Theorem 1.1, where we note that the curvature condition (1.2) is equivalent to
$$\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{[a} W_{b] e f[c} \boldsymbol{\kappa}_{d]} k^{e} k^{f}=0, \quad \text { for any optical vector field } k, \text { with } \boldsymbol{\kappa}=\boldsymbol{g}(k, \cdot) .$$
If any of the equivalent conditions of Theorem 1.1 is satisfied, we can then choose a metric $g$ in $\stackrel{n \cdot e}{\mathbf{c}}$, cast it in the form (5.2), and use the computation of the curvature tensors for $g$ given in Appendix A. Theorem 6.1 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, N, K)$ be a $(2 m+2)$-dimensional twist-induced almost Robinson geometry, where $m>1$, with non-shearing congruence of null geodesics $\mathcal{K}$. Denote by $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$ the induced partially integrable contact almost CR structure on the (local) leaf space $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$ of $\mathcal{K}$. The following statements are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] In addition to conditions (6.2), the Weyl tensor satisfies
$$W^{0}{ }_{\alpha \beta \gamma}=0 .$$
\item[2.] The almost Robinson structure $(N, K)$ is integrable.
\item[3.] The almost CR structure ( $\underline{H}, \underline{J}$ ) is integrable.
\end{itemize}

If any of these conditions holds, the Weyl tensor also satisfies
$$\begin{array}{r}
W_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}=0, \\
\left(W_{\bar{\gamma} \delta \alpha \beta}\right)_{\circ}=\left(W_{\bar{\gamma} \delta \alpha \beta}\right)_{\circ}=0 .
\end{array}$$

Remark 6.1 The combined equations (6.2a), (6.4) and (6.5a) can be rewritten as
$$W(u, v, w, z)=0, \quad \text { for any sections } u, v, w \text { and } z \text { of } N,$$
and constitute the integrability condition of an almost Robinson structure regardless of whether the associated congruence of null curves is geodesic and non-shearing, or not - see [27, 68, 67]. Thus, from the remaining conditions in (6.5b), we see that the degeneracy of the Weyl curvature is stronger when the almost Robinson structure is induced from a twisting non-shearing geodetic congruence. On the other hand, condition (6.4) is sufficient to establish the involutivity of the almost Robinson structure.

By inspection of (6.3d), we prove:
Theorem 6.2 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, N, K)$ be a $(2 m+2)$-dimensional twist-induced almost Robinson geometry, where $m>1$, with non-shearing congruence of null geodesics $\mathcal{K}$. Denote by $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$ the induced partially integrable contact almost CR structure on the (local) leaf space $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$ of $\mathcal{K}$. The following statements are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] In addition to conditions (6.2), the Weyl tensor satisfies
$$\left(W_{(\alpha}{ }^{(\beta)}{ }^{\delta)}\right)_{\circ}=0 .$$
\item[2.] The Chern-Moser tensor and the Nijenhuis tensor of $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$ satisfy
$$\underline{S}_{\alpha}{ }^{\gamma}{ }_{\beta}{ }^{\delta}+\left(\underline{N}_{\epsilon}(\alpha \beta) \underline{N}^{\epsilon}(\gamma \delta)\right)_{\circ}=0 .$$
\end{itemize}

Remark 6.2 In dimension five, i.e. $m=2$, condition (6.6) reduces to $\underline{S}_{\alpha}{ }^{\gamma}{ }_{\beta}{ }^{\delta}=0$.
Combining Theorem 6.1 and Theorem 6.2 proves:
Theorem 6.3 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, N, K)$ be a $(2 m+2)$-dimensional twist-induced almost Robinson geometry, where $m>1$, with non-shearing congruence of null geodesics $\mathcal{K}$. Denote by $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$ the induced partially integrable contact almost CR structure on the (local) leaf space $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$ of $\mathcal{K}$. The following statements are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] In addition to conditions (6.2), the Weyl tensor satisfies
$$W^{0}{ }_{\alpha \beta \gamma}=\left(W_{(\alpha}{ }^{(\beta}{ }_{\gamma)}{ }^{\delta)}\right)_{\circ}=0 .$$
\item[2.] $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$ is locally flat, i.e. the Chern-Moser tensor and the Nijenhuis tensor of $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$ vanish, i.e.
$$\underline{S}_{\alpha \bar{\gamma} \beta \bar{\delta}}=\underline{N}_{\alpha \beta \gamma}=0 .$$
If any of these conditions holds, the Weyl tensor also satisfies conditions (6.5).
\end{itemize}

The previous theorem immediately yields:
Corollary 6.1 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c})$ be an oriented locally conformally flat manifold of even dimension greater than four. Suppose that it admits an optical structure $K$ with twisting non-shearing congruence of geodesics $\mathcal{K}$. Then the twist of $\mathcal{K}$ induces a flat contact $C R$ structure on the (local) leaf space of $\mathcal{K}$.

Remark 6.3 Theorem 6.3 and Corollary 6.1 should be contrasted with the situation in dimension four where:
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] CR flatness cannot be inferred from the Petrov types alone. For instance, the so-called Robinson congruence whose underlying CR structure is flat, occurs in Minkowski space (Petrov type O), the Taub-NUT metric (Petrov type D) and Hauser's waves of Petrov type N - see [47, 74].
\item[2.] Conformal flatness does not imply CR flatness. Indeed, the Kerr theorem asserts that any analytic non-shearing congruence of null geodesics in conformally flat spacetime arises as the intersection of a complex submanifold of complex projective 3 -space $\mathbf{C P}^{3}$ and the real five-dimensional CR hyperquadric embedded therein. There are many examples of such complex submanifolds that do not give rise to a flat CR structure: for instance, the locus of a homogeneous polynomial of degree two in $\mathbf{C P}^{3}$ - see [53, 54, 47, 74].
\end{itemize}

\section*{7 Algebraic conditions on the Weyl tensor}

The purpose of this section is to justify an additional assumption on the Weyl tensor that we will be using when solving the Einstein field equations. We start by recalling the well-known fact [13, 55] that at any point $p$ of a four-dimensional Lorentzian conformal manifold $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c})$, the Weyl tensor determines four null directions, and thus four optical structures in a neighbourhood of $p$ : any null vector $k$ that defines such a null direction is a solution of
$$\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{[a} W_{b] e f[c} \boldsymbol{\kappa}_{d]} k^{e} k^{f}=0, \quad \text { where } \boldsymbol{\kappa}=\boldsymbol{g}(k, \cdot),$$
and (the span of) $k$ is said to be a principal null direction of the Weyl tensor at $p$. If $k$ satisfies the stronger condition
$$k^{d} W_{d a e[b} \boldsymbol{\kappa}_{c]} k^{e}=0, \quad \text { where } \boldsymbol{\kappa}=\boldsymbol{g}(k, \cdot),$$
the corresponding null direction is said to be repeated. In this case, we say that the Weyl tensor is algebraically special at $p$, or of Petrov type II or more degenerate at $p$. This condition is particularly important in general relativity especially in relation to the Goldberg-Sachs theorem [19, 20] as explained in the introduction.

In the present context, our starting point is an optical geometry ( $\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K$ ) of dimension $2 m+2$ greater than four, with twisting non-shearing congruence of null geodesics $\mathcal{K}$. As in dimension four, the line distribution $K$ provides a number of criteria on the basis of which we may describe the algebraic degeneracy of the Weyl tensor as is discussed in e.g. [41, 48]. One such degeneracy criterion is that for any optical vector field $k$, the Weyl tensor satisfies
$$\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{[a} W_{b c] f[d} \boldsymbol{\kappa}_{e]} k^{f}=0, \quad \text { where } \boldsymbol{\kappa}=\boldsymbol{g}(k, \cdot) .$$
Taking the trace and the tracefree part of condition (7.1) yields the two respective weaker conditions
$$\begin{aligned}
k^{d} W_{d a e[b} \boldsymbol{\kappa}_{c]} k^{e} & =0 \\
\left(\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{[a} W_{b c] f[d} \boldsymbol{\kappa}_{e]} k^{f}\right)_{\circ} & =0
\end{aligned}$$
Condition (7.3) has no analogue in dimension four, and turns out to be too strong under general assumptions. Instead, we shall show that condition (7.2) will be sufficient to facilitate our computations.

Let us examine the consequence of each of these conditions in the present context. As before, we assume the setting of Theorem 1.1 and work with a metric $g$ given by (5.2) in an adapted frame.

\subsection*{7.1 Degeneracy condition (7.2)}

Let us rewrite condition (7.2) as
$$\begin{aligned}
W_{\alpha}{ }^{0}{ }_{\beta}{ }^{0}= & W_{\alpha}{ }^{0} \bar{\beta}^{0}=0, \\
& W_{\alpha}{ }^{0}{ }_{0}^{0}=0 .
\end{aligned}$$
Conditions (7.4) is trivially satisfied by virtue of Theorem 1.1. On the other hand, from equations (A.3) and (A.18), the LHS of (7.5) yields
$$W_{\alpha}{ }^{00}{ }_{0}=\frac{1}{2 m}\left((2 m-1) \dot{E}_{\alpha}-(2 m-4) \mathrm{i} E_{\alpha}\right) .$$
We thus see that condition (7.2) is equivalent to
$$\dot{E}_{\alpha}=\frac{2 m-4}{2 m-1} \mathrm{i} E_{\alpha} \quad \text { i.e. } \quad \ddot{\lambda}_{\alpha}=\frac{2 m-4}{2 m-1} \mathrm{i} \dot{\lambda}_{\alpha} \text {. }$$
This ODE has general solution
$$\begin{array}{ll}
\lambda_{\alpha}=-2 \mathrm{i} \frac{2 m-1}{2 m-4} \underline{E}_{\alpha} \mathrm{e}^{\frac{2 m-4}{2 m-1} \mathrm{i} \phi}+\underline{\lambda}_{\alpha}, & m \neq 2, \\
\lambda_{\alpha}=2 \underline{E}_{\alpha} \phi+\underline{\lambda}_{\alpha}, & m=2,
\end{array}$$
for some functions $\underline{E}_{\alpha}$ and $\underline{\lambda}_{\alpha}$ on $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underline{H}, \underline{J})$.

\subsection*{7.2 Degeneracy condition (7.3)}

Let us now rewrite the LHS of (7.3) as $\left(W^{0}{ }_{i j k}\right)_{\circ}$. This tensor splits into three components under the structure group of the almost Robinson manifold [67]:
\begin{itemize}
\item[-] $\left(W^{0}{ }_{\alpha \beta \bar{\gamma}}\right)_{0}$, which vanishes - see (6.2c) of Proposition 6.1, or (A.5),
\item[-] $W^{0}{ }_{\alpha \beta \gamma}$, which is proportional to the Nijenhuis tensor - see (6.3a) or (A.4),
\item[-] and the trace of $\left(W^{0}{ }_{i j k}\right)_{0}$ with respect to the bundle Hermitian form $\omega_{i j}$ induced from the twist of $k$, and which is proportional to $E_{i}$ by a constant factor, as follows from (A.3), (A.5) and (A.18).
\end{itemize}

Hence, given our assumptions, condition (7.3) holds if and only if $\underline{\mathrm{N}}_{\alpha \beta \gamma}=0$ and $E_{\alpha}=0$. In the context of finding solutions to Einstein field equations, such a condition is too strong since we will allow the almost Robinson structure to be non-integrable, i.e. $\underline{\mathrm{N}}_{\alpha \beta \gamma}$ does not necessarily vanish. We shall show however at Step 2 of Section 8.1 that $\lambda_{\alpha}$, and thus $E_{\alpha}$, must eventually vanish.

\section*{8 Einstein metrics}

Throughout this section, we assume that $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, N, K)$ is a twist-induced almost Robinson geometry of dimension $2 m+2$ with non-shearing congruence of null geodesics $\mathcal{K}$ as in Theorem 1.1. We assume $m>1$, only pointing out notable differences when $m=1$. We now seek a metric in the conformal class c, which satisfies the Einstein field equations (8.1) below.

We proceed as follows. Let $g$ and $\widehat{g}$ be two metrics in c related by (2.1). For convenience, and with no loss of generality, we take $g$ to be in the conformal subclass ${ }^{n \cdot e}$ so that it takes the form (5.2) and we can use the computation of the curvature given in Appendix A. The reader should also refer to Section 5 for the general setup and notation. The unknown metric $\widehat{g}$ must satisfy the Einstein field equation with pure radiation
$$\widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}_{a b}=\Lambda \widehat{g}_{a b}+\Phi \widehat{\kappa}_{a} \widehat{\kappa}_{b},$$
where $\Lambda$ is the cosmological constant and $\Phi$ a smooth function on $\mathcal{M}$, which may or may not be identically zero. In terms of the Schouten tensor, these read as
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{a b}=\frac{1}{2(2 m+1)} \Lambda \widehat{g}_{a b}+\frac{1}{2 m} \Phi \widehat{\kappa}_{a} \widehat{\kappa}_{b} .$$
At Step 2 below, we shall also impose condition (7.2) on the Weyl tensor.

\subsection*{8.1 Computations}

Following the strategy set up in [37], we shall integrate the components of the Einstein field equations (8.2) successively. The computation of the components of $\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{a b}$ will be achieved using the transformation law (2.5) between the Schouten tensors of $g$ and $\widehat{g}$, and the components of the curvature tensors given in Appendix A. The computation is lengthy and we have deliberately left out many details.
- Step 1:
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}^{00}=0 .$$

We compute
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}^{00}=1-\ddot{\varphi}+\dot{\varphi}^{2} .$$
Hence, equation (8.3) holds if and only if
$$\ddot{\varphi}-\dot{\varphi}^{2}=1 .$$
The general solution to this equation is $\varphi=\frac{1}{2} \underline{\varphi}-\ln |\cos (\phi+\underline{\psi})|$ where $\underline{\psi}$ and $\underline{\varphi}$ are functions on $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$. Hence the conformal factor is given by $\mathrm{e}^{2 \varphi}=\frac{e^{\underline{\varphi}}}{\cos ^{2}(\phi+\underline{\psi})}$. We can use $\underline{\varphi}$ to change adapted CR frames by absorbing $\mathrm{e}^{\underline{\varphi}}$ into $\underline{\theta}^{0}$. Further, a change of affine parameter along the geodesics of $k$ together with some redefinitions of the functions $\lambda_{\alpha}$ and $\lambda_{0}$ can be used to eliminate the function $\underline{\psi}$. Thus, with no loss of generality, we shall take
$$\varphi=-\ln |\cos \phi|,$$
Hence, the conformal factor can be taken to be
$$\mathrm{e}^{2 \varphi}=\frac{1}{\cos ^{2} \phi}=\sec ^{2} \phi .$$
For future use, we record $\dot{\varphi}=\tan \phi$ and $\ddot{\varphi}=\sec ^{2} \phi$.
Remark 8.1 The inverse of the conformal factor is a periodic function of period $\pi$, and one can already anticipate that the metric $\widehat{g}$ is defined on a circle bundle over $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$. With no loss of generality, we may choose $\phi$ to lie in the interval $\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$. This is particularly relevant to the relation with Fefferman-Einstein metrics given in Section 9.1.
- Step 2:
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha}{ }^{0}=0 .$$
We find
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha}{ }^{0}=\frac{1}{2 m}\left(\dot{E}_{\alpha}-4 \mathrm{i} E_{\alpha}\right)+(1-\mathrm{i} \dot{\varphi}) \lambda_{\alpha}+\dot{\varphi} E_{\alpha}$$
Hence, equation (8.4) holds if and only if
$$\ddot{\lambda}_{\alpha}-(4 \mathrm{i}-2 m \tan \phi) \dot{\lambda}_{\alpha}+4 m(1-\mathrm{i} \tan \phi) \lambda_{\alpha}=0 .$$
The general solution of this second-order linear ODE will be treated elsewhere. Instead, at this stage, we shall impose the condition (7.2) so that $\lambda_{\alpha}$ takes the form (7.7). Plugging (7.7) into the above equation and assuming $m>1$ yields
$$\lambda_{\alpha}=0 .$$

Remark 8.2 In dimension four, that is, when $m=1$, the situation is remarkably different. For one, if one assumes the Einstein condition, the degeneracy condition (7.2) on the Weyl tensor follows from the Goldberg-Sachs theorem. In addition, the general solution of the system of ODEs (7.6) and (8.5) is given by
$$\lambda_{\alpha}=\left(1+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{e}^{-2 \mathrm{i} \phi}\right) \underline{\lambda}_{\alpha},$$
for some smooth functions $\underline{\lambda}_{\alpha}$ on $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$. We thus recover the result already obtained in [37]. The fact that $\lambda_{\alpha}$ may be non-zero here allows many more possible solutions of the Einstein field equations.
- Step 3:
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha \beta}=0 .$$
We compute
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha \beta}=\frac{1}{m} \underline{\nabla}^{\gamma} \underline{\mathrm{N}}_{\gamma(\alpha \beta)}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(1+\mathrm{i} \dot{\varphi}) \underline{A}_{\alpha \beta} .$$
Hence, equation (8.6) holds if and only if
$$\begin{aligned}
\underline{A}_{\alpha \beta} & =0, \\
\underline{\nabla}^{\gamma} \underline{N}_{\gamma(\alpha \beta)} & =0 .
\end{aligned}$$
- Step 4:
$$\left(\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha \bar{\beta}}\right)_{\circ}=0 .$$
We compute
$$\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha}{ }^{\beta}=\frac{1}{2 m}\left(\underline{\operatorname{Ric}}_{\alpha}{ }^{\beta}-\underline{\mathrm{N}}_{\alpha \delta \gamma}\right. & \left.\underline{\mathrm{N}}^{\beta \delta \gamma}\right) \\
& -\frac{1}{2 m(2 m+1)}\left(\frac{1}{2} \ddot{\lambda}_{0}+\left(2(m+1)^{2}-m(2 m+1) \ddot{\varphi}\right) \lambda_{0}+m \underline{\Lambda}\right) \delta_{\alpha}^{\beta},
\end{aligned}$$
where we have defined
$$\underline{\Lambda}:=\frac{1}{m}\left(\underline{\mathrm{Sc}}-\underline{\mathrm{N}}_{\alpha \delta \gamma} \underline{\mathrm{N}}^{\alpha \delta \gamma}\right) .$$
Taking the tracefree part of (8.9), we immediately conclude that (8.8) holds if and only if
$$\underline{\operatorname{Ric}}_{\alpha}{ }^{\beta}-\underline{\mathrm{N}}_{\alpha \delta \gamma} \underline{\mathrm{N}}^{\beta \delta \gamma}=\underline{\Lambda} \delta_{\alpha}^{\beta} .$$
Condition (8.10) together with conditions (8.7) allows us to conclude immediately that the almost CR manifold is CR-Einstein, and in particular, $\underline{\Lambda}$ is constant.
- Step 5:
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}=\frac{m+1}{2 m+1} \Lambda .$$
We take the trace of equation (8.9) to find
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha}^{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2(2 m+1)}\left(\frac{1}{2} \ddot{\lambda}_{0}+\left(2(m+1)^{2}-m(2 m+1) \ddot{\varphi}\right) \lambda_{0}\right)+\frac{m+1}{2(2 m+1)} \underline{\Lambda},$$
and we compute
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{0}^{0}=-\frac{1}{2(2 m+1)} \underline{\Lambda}+\frac{1}{2(2 m+1)} \ddot{\lambda}_{0}+\frac{1}{2} \dot{\varphi} \dot{\lambda}_{0}+\left(\frac{m+1}{2 m+1}+\frac{1}{2} \ddot{\varphi}\right) \lambda_{0} .$$
Hence, using the fact that $\widehat{\mathrm{P}}=2 \mathrm{e}^{-2 \varphi}\left(\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{0}{ }^{0}+\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha}{ }^{\alpha}\right)$, we find
$$\mathrm{e}^{2 \varphi} \widehat{\mathrm{P}}=\frac{1}{2(2 m+1)} \ddot{\lambda}_{0}+\dot{\varphi} \dot{\lambda}_{0}+\left(-\frac{2 m(m+1)}{2 m+1}+(m+1) \ddot{\varphi}\right) \lambda_{0}+\frac{m}{2 m+1} \underline{\Lambda} .$$

Therefore, equation (8.11) holds if and only if
$$\begin{aligned}
\ddot{\lambda}_{0}+2(2 m+1) \tan \phi \dot{\lambda}_{0}+\left(-4 m(m+1)+2(m+1)(2 m+1) \sec ^{2} \phi\right) \lambda_{0} & = \\
2(m+1) \Lambda \sec ^{2} \phi-2 m \underline{\Lambda} . &
\end{aligned}$$
Equation (8.14) is a second-order linear ODE with general solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{2 m+2} \underline{\Lambda}+\left(\frac{1}{2 m+1} \Lambda-\frac{1}{2 m+2} \underline{\Lambda}\right) \sum_{j=0}^{m} a_{j} \cos ^{2 j} \phi & \\
& +\underline{b} \cos ^{2 m+2} \phi+\underline{c} \cos ^{2 m+1} \phi \sin \phi
\end{aligned}$$
where the $a_{i}$ are given by
$$a_{0}=1, \quad a_{j}=\frac{2 m-2 j+4}{2 m-2 j+1} a_{j-1}, \quad j=1, \ldots, m,$$
and $\underline{b}$ and $\underline{c}$ are arbitrary functions on $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$.
- Step 6:
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{0}^{0}=\frac{1}{2(2 m+1)} \Lambda \mathrm{e}^{2 \varphi} .$$
From equation (8.13), we conclude that equation (8.16) holds if and only if
$$\ddot{\lambda}_{0}+(2 m+1) \tan \phi \dot{\lambda}_{0}+\left(2(m+1)+(2 m+1) \sec ^{2} \phi\right) \lambda_{0}=\Lambda \sec ^{2} \phi+\underline{\Lambda} .$$
Since the function (8.15) is also a solution of (8.17), we will be able to reduce the number of arbitrary functions to one. To facilitate the computation, and for future use, we reduce the second-order ODEs to a first-order ODE by plugging equation (8.14) into (8.17). We find
$$\tan \phi \dot{\lambda}_{0}-\left(2 m+2-(2 m+1) \sec ^{2} \phi\right) \lambda_{0}=\Lambda \sec ^{2} \phi-\underline{\Lambda} .$$
It remains to plug the solution (8.15) into (8.18), which gives the relation
$$\underline{b}=-2\left(\frac{1}{2 m+1} \Lambda-\frac{1}{2 m+2} \underline{\Lambda}\right) a_{m} .$$
At this stage, all the coefficients of the function $\lambda_{0}$ with the exception of $\underline{c}$ have been determined.
Remark 8.3 One can alternatively use $\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha}{ }^{\alpha}=\frac{m}{2(2 m+1)} \Lambda \mathrm{e}^{2 \varphi}$. In this case, equation (8.12) tells us that $\lambda_{0}$ must satisfy
$$\ddot{\lambda}_{0}+\left(4(m+1)^{2}-2 m(2 m+1) \sec ^{2} \phi\right) \lambda_{0}=-2 m \Lambda \sec ^{2} \phi+2(m+1) \underline{\Lambda} .$$
Plugging (8.20) into (8.14) also yields (8.18), from which the same conclusion (8.19) follows.
- Step 7:
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha 0}=0 .$$
We compute
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{\alpha 0}=\left(-\frac{1}{4 m} \cos ^{2 m} \phi+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2 m} \cos ^{2 m+1} \phi \sin \phi+\frac{1}{2 m} \cos ^{2 m+2} \phi\right) \underline{\nabla}_{\alpha} \underline{c} .$$

Hence, equation (8.21) holds if and only if $\underline{\nabla}_{\alpha} \underline{c}=0$. We can take a covariant derivative $\underline{\nabla}_{\bar{\beta}}$ of this expression and commute the derivatives so that the first of the commutation relations (4.2) together with the fact that $\underline{c}$ is real allows us to conclude $\underline{\nabla}_{0} \underline{c}=0$, i.e. $\underline{c}$ is constant.

We have now completely determined the function $\lambda_{0}$, and thus the 1-form $\lambda$ and metric $\widehat{g}$. There remains to check, in the final step, under which conditions the metric $\widehat{g}$ is consistent with the remaining component of (8.2).
- Step 8:
$$\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{00}=\Phi \mathrm{e}^{4 \varphi} .$$
Our previous findings lead us to assert that $\widehat{\mathrm{P}}_{00}=0$. Thus, equation (8.22) holds if and only if $\Phi=0$, i.e. our hypotheses do not allow for the presence of pure radiation on $(\mathcal{M}, \widehat{g})$.

\subsection*{8.2 Conclusions}

From the computations of Section 8.1, we extract the following results:
Theorem 8.1 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ be a $(2 m+2)$-dimensional conformal optical geometry, where $m>1$, with twisting non-shearing congruence of null geodesics $\mathcal{K}$. Suppose that the Weyl tensor satisfies
$$W(k, v, k, \cdot)=0, \quad \text { for any sections } k \text { of } K, v \text { of } K^{\perp}$$
so that $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ admits a twist-induced almost Robinson structure $(N, K)$. Denote by $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$ the induced partially integrable contact almost CR structure of positive definite signature on the (local) leaf space $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$ of $\mathcal{K}$, and by $\varpi$ the natural projection from $\mathcal{M}$ to $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$.

$$\widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}(w, w)=0, \quad \text { for any sections } w \text { of } N .$$
Then $\mathcal{M}$ is locally diffeomorphic to $\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right) \times \underline{\mathcal{M}}$, and there is a distinguished contact form $\underline{\theta}^{0}$ for $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$ with Levi form $\underline{h}$ such that the pseudo-Hermitian torsion tensor $\underline{A}_{\alpha \beta}$ and the Nijenhuis tensor $\underline{N}_{\gamma \alpha \beta}$ satisfy $\underline{A}_{\alpha \beta}=\underline{\nabla}^{\gamma} \underline{N}_{\gamma(\alpha \beta)}=0$, and the metric $\widehat{g}$ takes the form
$$\widehat{g}=\sec ^{2} \phi g, \quad \text { for }-\frac{\pi}{2}<\phi<\frac{\pi}{2},$$
where
$$\begin{aligned}
& g=2 \kappa \odot \lambda+h, \\
& \kappa=2 \varpi^{*} \underline{\theta}^{0}, \quad h=\varpi^{*} \underline{h}, \quad \lambda=\mathrm{d} \phi+\lambda_{0} \varpi^{*} \underline{\theta}^{0},
\end{aligned}$$
for some smooth function $\lambda_{0}$ on $\mathcal{M}$.
2. Suppose that $\mathbf{c}$ contains a metric $\widehat{g}$ whose Ricci tensor satisfies
$$\begin{array}{ll}
\widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}(v, v)=\Lambda \widehat{g}(v, v), & \text { for any section } v \text { of } K^{\perp}, \\
\widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}(k, \cdot)=\Lambda \widehat{g}(k, \cdot), & \text { for any section } k \text { of } K,
\end{array}$$
for some constant $\Lambda$, i.e. $\kappa_{[a} \widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}_{b][c} \kappa_{d]}=\Lambda \kappa_{[a} \widehat{g}_{b][c} \kappa_{d]}$ and $k^{a} \widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}_{a}{ }^{b}=\Lambda k^{b}$, for any optical 1-form $\kappa_{a}$ and optical vector field $k^{a}$. Then part (1) holds. In addition, the almost pseudo-Hermitian structure ( $\underline{H}, \underline{J}, \underline{\theta}^{0}$ ) is almost CR-Einstein, and the function $\lambda_{0}$ is now given by
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{0}=\frac{\underline{\Lambda}}{2 m+2}+\left(\frac{\Lambda}{2 m+1}-\frac{\underline{\Lambda}}{2 m+2}\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m} a_{j} \cos ^{2 j} \phi-2 a_{m} \cos ^{2 m+2} \phi\right. & ) \\
& +\underline{c} \cos ^{2 m+1} \phi \sin \phi
\end{aligned}$$
where $\underline{\Lambda}:=\frac{1}{m}\left(\underline{\mathrm{Sc}}-\underline{N}_{\alpha \delta \gamma} \underline{N}^{\alpha \delta \gamma}\right)$, $\underline{c}$ is a smooth function on $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$, and
$$a_{0}=1, \quad \quad a_{j}=\frac{2 m-2 j+4}{2 m-2 j+1} a_{j-1}, \quad \quad j=1, \ldots, m .$$
3. Suppose that $\mathbf{c}$ contains a metric $\widehat{g}$ whose Ricci tensor satisfies
$$\widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}(v, \cdot)=\Lambda \widehat{g}(v, \cdot), \quad \text { for any section } v \text { of } K^{\perp},$$
i.e. $\kappa_{[a} \widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}_{b] c}=\Lambda \kappa_{[a} \widehat{g}_{b] c}$ for any optical 1-form $\kappa_{a}$. Then part (2) holds. In addition, the function $\underline{\mathrm{c}}$ in $\lambda_{0}$ is now a constant.
Further, $\widehat{g}$ must be Einstein. In other words, c cannot contain a metric $\widehat{g}$ that satisfies the Einstein field equation with pure radiation (8.1) with non-zero $\Phi$.

Proof. Part 1 follow from Steps 1,2 and 3 of Section 8.1. Part 2 from Steps 1 to 6, while including Steps 7 and 8 as well proves part 3. \(\square\)

Combining part 3 of Theorem 8.1 with Theorem 6.1 now gives Theorem 1.2 where we have written $\|\underline{\mathrm{N}}\|_{\underline{h}}^{2}=\underline{\mathrm{N}}_{\alpha \beta \gamma} \underline{\mathrm{N}}^{\alpha \beta \gamma}$.

Remark 8.4 To construct examples illustrating Theorems 1.2 and 8.1, it suffices to choose a known almost Kähler-Einstein manifold $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underset{\sim}{h}, \underset{\sim}{J})$ as discussed at the end of Section 4.3, extend $(\underset{\sim}{h}, \underset{\sim}{J})$ to an almost CR-Einstein structure $\left(\underline{H}, \underline{J}, \underline{\theta}^{0}\right)$ on a rank-1 associated bundle $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$ as in Proposition 4.4. which we then lift to $\mathcal{M}=\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right) \times \underline{\mathcal{M}}$ as an almost Robinson structure $(N, K)$ for the Einstein metric $\widehat{g}$ as in Theorem 1.2.

Under the assumptions of Theorem 1.2, the covariant derivatives of the coframe 1-forms (5.4) with respect to the Levi-Civita connection ∇ of $g$ reduce to
$$\begin{aligned}

\nabla \theta^{\alpha} & =\underline{\nabla} \theta^{\alpha}-\underline{\mathrm{N}}^{\alpha}{ }_{\bar{\beta}} \bar{\theta}^{\bar{\gamma}} \otimes \bar{\theta}^{\bar{\beta}}-2 \mathrm{i} \lambda \odot \theta^{\alpha}+\mathrm{i} \lambda_{0} \kappa \odot \theta^{\alpha}, \\

The covariant derivative with respect to the Levi-Civita connection $\widehat{\nabla}$ of $\widehat{g}$ can easily be obtained using formula (2.2) with $\Upsilon=\tan \phi\left(\lambda-\frac{1}{2} \lambda_{0} \kappa\right)$. For instance, one can easily verify that the expansion of $k=g^{-1}(\kappa, \cdot)$ with respect to $\widehat{g}$ is given by $\widehat{\epsilon}=2 m \tan \phi$.

$$\begin{aligned}

from which we can obtain non-zero expressions for $W_{0}^{0}{ }_{0}^{\alpha}$ and $W_{\alpha \gamma}{ }^{\delta \beta}$ using the Bianchi identities. When the almost Robinson structure is integrable, i.e. $\underline{\mathrm{N}}_{\alpha \beta \gamma}=0$, the Weyl tensor satisfies the 'Goldberg-Sachs'-type curvature condition put forward in [66]:
$$W(v, w, u, \cdot)=0, \quad \text { for all } v, w, u \in \Gamma(N) .$$
A more detailed study of the curvature is given in [67]. For the time being, we draw the following conclusion:

Proposition 8.1 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ be the optical geometry of Theorem 1.2. The following statements are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ is (locally) conformally flat;
\item[2.] the almost Robinson structure ( $N, K$ ) is integrable, the almost pseudo-Hermi-tian structure ( $\underline{H}, \underline{J}, \underline{\theta}^{0}$ ) flat, and the metric $\widehat{g}$ flat (and in particular Ricci-flat).
\end{itemize}

Proof. The equivalence follows essentially from equations (8.24). See Theorem 6.1 for the integrability of $(N, K)$, and Theorem 6.3 for CR flatness. Now, equation (8.24g) tells us that $\lambda_{0}=0$, which means that $\Lambda=\underline{\Lambda}=\underline{c}=0$. But this is equivalent to $\widehat{g}$ being Ricci-flat. In addition, all the pseudo-Hermitian torsion and curvature invariants of $\underline{\theta}^{0}$ vanish, i.e. $\left(\underline{H}, \underline{J}, \underline{\theta}^{0}\right)$ is flat. \(\square\)

\section*{9 Special cases}

\subsection*{9.1 Fefferman-Einstein metrics: $(2 m+2) \Lambda=(2 m+1) \underline{\Lambda}$ and $\underline{c}=0$}

To any contact almost CR manifold $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underline{H}, \underline{J})$ of positive definite signature, one can associate a canonical Lorentzian conformal manifold $\left(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}_{F}\right)$ on the total space of a circle bundle associated to the canonical bundle of $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underline{H}, \underline{J})$. The construction was originally due to Fefferman [15, 16], and $\left(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}_{F}\right)$ has since been known as the Fefferman space of $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underline{H}, \underline{J})$ - see also [31]. Leitner subsequently generalised Fefferman's construction to partially integrable contact almost CR structures and allowed the inclusion of an additional gauge field [34]. Regardless of the integrability
and the presence of a gauge field, the resulting conformal structure admits a null conformal Killing field, which generates a maximally twisting non-shearing congruence of null geodesics $\mathcal{K}$ having the almost CR manifold $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underline{H}, \underline{J})$ as its (local) leaf space, and thus a twist-induced almost Robinson structure. Fefferman spaces thus fall into the class of geometries considered in the present article. In the integrable case, a characterisation of conformal structures as Fefferman spaces was presented in [24, 12]. The non-integrable case will be given in 65.

A Fefferman-Einstein metric is a metric $g_{F E}$ in $\mathbf{c}_{F}$ that is Einstein, at least on some open subset of $\mathcal{M}$ [33] - in the terminology of [22, 11], such a metric arises from an almost Einstein structure. We shall refer to $\left(\mathcal{M}, g_{F E}\right)$ as a Fefferman-Einstein space. In this definition, we shall also include Einstein metrics in gauged Fefferman conformal structures over partially integrable contact almost CR manifolds.

We now show that for some values of the parameters of the family of metrics found in Theorem 1.2, the resulting metric is a Fefferman-Einstein metric. The proof of the implication (1) ⇒ (5) is only given in the integrable case, but can also be obtained in the non-integrable case by appealing to the results of [65].

Theorem 9.1 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ be the optical geometry of Theorem 1.2. The following statements are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] $k=g^{-1}(\kappa, \cdot)$ is a conformal Killing field;
\item[2.] $\ell=g^{-1}(\lambda, \cdot)$ is a conformal Killing field;
\item[3.] $\lambda_{0}$ is constant;
\item[4.] the parameters satisfy $(2 m+2) \Lambda=(2 m+1) \underline{\Lambda}$ and $\underline{c}=0$;
\item[5.] $(\mathcal{M}, \widehat{g})$ is locally isometric to a Fefferman-Einstein space.
\end{itemize}

Proof. The equivalence of statements (1), (2), (3) and (4) is immediate from inspection of equations (8.23a), (8.23c) and the definition of $\lambda_{0}$ in Theorem 1.2. That (5) implies (1) follows from [24, 12, 34]. To show that condition (1) implies (5), we assume that the underlying almost CR structure is integrable, and we can check the criteria for the characterisation of the Fefferman space given in Corollary 3.1 of [12] - see also [24]: under the assumption that $k$ is a null conformal Killing field, we must have, for any metric $g$ in c,
$$\begin{aligned}
& k^{a} W_{a b c d}=0, \\
& k^{c} Y_{a b c}=0, \\
& \frac{1}{(2 m+2)^{2}}\left(\nabla_{a} k^{a}\right)^{2}-k^{a} k^{b} \mathrm{P}_{a b}-\frac{1}{2 m+2} k^{a} \nabla_{a} \nabla_{b} k^{b}<0 .
\end{aligned}$$
Here $Y_{a b c}:=2 \nabla_{[b} \mathrm{P}_{c] a}$ is the Cotton tensor of $g$. The set of conditions (9.1) are conformally invariant. Condition (9.1a) is satisfied by (8.24a), (8.24e) and (8.24f). Since c contains an Einstein metric $\widehat{g}$, we have $\widehat{Y}_{a b c}=0$, so condition (9.1b) holds. Finally, since for any metric $g$ in ${ }^{n} \mathbf{c}$, $\nabla_{a} k^{a}=0$ and $k^{a} k^{b} \mathrm{P}_{a b}=1$ by equation (A.17), we have that the LHS of equation (9.1c) can be computed to be -1, which is indeed less than zero. This establishes (5). \(\square\)

Note that for a Fefferman-Einstein metric, $\Lambda$ is determined by $\underline{\Lambda}$. As a direct consequence of Theorem 9.1, we obtain the following two corollaries.

Corollary 9.1 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ be the optical geometry of Theorem 1.2. Then $\widehat{g}$ is a Ricci-flat Fefferman-Einstein metric if and only if $\lambda_{0}=0$, i.e. $\Lambda=\underline{\Lambda}=\underline{c}=0$.

Corollary 9.2 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ be the optical geometry given in Theorem 1.2. Then, the Einstein metric $\widehat{g}$ can be cast into the Kerr-Schild form
$$\widehat{g}=g_{F E}+\sec ^{2} \phi\left(\lambda_{0}-\frac{1}{2 m+2} \underline{\Lambda}\right) \kappa \otimes \kappa, \quad \text { for }-\frac{\pi}{2}<\phi<\frac{\pi}{2},$$
where $g_{F E}$ is the Fefferman-Einstein metric associated to the underlying almost CR-Einstein structure ( $\underline{H}, \underline{J}, \underline{\theta}^{0}$ ).

The previous corollary comes as no surprise considering that the conformal factor involved in $\widehat{g}$ (see Remark 8.1) and the function $\lambda_{0}$ depend on periodic functions of period $\pi$, which indeed suggests these Einstein metrics live on a circle bundle.

\subsection*{9.2 Taub-NUT-type spacetimes: $\underline{\Lambda} \neq 0$}

Originally discovered in dimension four by Taub [70], and independently, by Newman, Unti, Tamburino [42], the Taub-NUT spacetime is an Einstein Lorentzian manifold of dimension $2 m+2$ associated to any $2 m$-dimensional Kähler-Einstein metric [9, 7, 3]. We shall presently show that the construction can be generalised to the non-integrable case and is locally isometric to the class of metrics of Theorem 1.2 for which $\underline{\Lambda} \neq 0$.

Let $(\mathcal{M}, \underset{\sim}{h}, \underset{\sim}{J})$ be a $2 m$-dimensional almost Kähler-Einstein manifold with non-zero Ricci scalar $2 m \underset{\sim}{\Lambda}$. By Proposition 4.4, the circle bundle $\mathcal{M} \longrightarrow \mathcal{M}$ defined by (4.18) in Section 4.3 admits an almost CR-Einstein structure ( $\underline{H}, \underline{J}, \underline{\theta}^{0}$ ). Following our previous conventions, we write $\underline{\Lambda}$ for $\underset{\sim}{\Lambda}$, and we omit pullback maps for clarity. We can then equip the radial extension $\mathcal{M}_{T N}=\mathbf{R} \times \underline{\mathcal{M}}$ of $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$, with coordinate $r$ on R, with the Lorentzian metric
$$g_{T N}=\frac{1}{\underline{\Lambda}^{2} F(r)} \mathrm{d} r \otimes \mathrm{~d} r-4 F(r) \underline{\theta}^{0} \otimes \underline{\theta}^{0}+\frac{r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}}{\underline{\Lambda}^{2}} \underline{h},$$
where $F(r)$ is a smooth function which satisfies
$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{~d} r}\left(\frac{\left(r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}\right)^{m}}{r} F(r)\right)=\frac{\left(r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}\right)^{m}}{r^{2}} \underline{\Lambda}-\frac{\left(r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}\right)^{m+1}}{r^{2}} \frac{\Lambda}{\underline{\Lambda}^{2}} .$$
for some constant $\Lambda$. We shall denote the constant of integration by $M$.
Up to rescaling of the constants by factors involving $\underline{\Lambda}$, the metric (9.2) corresponds to the one given in e.g. [7] when $(\mathcal{M}, \underset{\sim}{h}, \underset{\sim}{J})$ is merely Kähler-Einstein, and the choice of function $F(r)$ satisfying (9.3) is to ensure that $g_{T N}$ is Einstein. We shall show that this also holds true when $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underset{\sim}{h}, \underset{\sim}{J})$ is strictly almost Kähler.

Definition 9.1 We call the Lorentzian manifold $\left(\mathcal{M}_{T N}, g_{T N}\right)$ constructed above a Taub-NUT-type spacetime.

A Taub-NUT-type spacetime is sometimes referred to more specifically as Taub-NUT-AdS when $\Lambda<0$, as Taub-NUT when $\Lambda=0$, and as Taub-NUT-dS when $\Lambda>0$. Here, AdS stands for anti-de Sitter, and dS for de Sitter. The respective physical interpretation of the constants $\Lambda$ and
$M$ are that of a cosmological constant and a mass. The parameter $\underline{\Lambda}$ is referred to as the NUT (Newman-Unti-Tamburino) parameter.

Let us recast the metric (9.2) as
$$g_{T N}=\frac{r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}}{\underline{\Lambda}^{2}}(2 \kappa \odot \lambda+\underline{h}),$$
where
$$\kappa=2 \underline{\theta}^{0}+\frac{1}{\underline{\Lambda} F(r)} \mathrm{d} r, \quad \lambda=\frac{\underline{\Lambda}}{r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}} \mathrm{~d} r-\frac{1}{2} \frac{\underline{\Lambda}^{2}}{r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}} F(r) \kappa .$$
We note also $\lambda=\frac{1}{2} \frac{\underline{\Lambda}^{2}}{r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}}\left(-2 F(r) \underline{\theta}^{0}+\frac{1}{\underline{\Lambda}} \mathrm{~d} r\right)$. We now perform the change of variables
$$r=\underline{\Lambda} \tan \phi, \quad \text { i.e. } \quad \phi=\arctan \frac{r}{\underline{\Lambda}} .$$
We note that $r \rightarrow \pm \infty$ as $\phi \rightarrow \pm \frac{\pi}{2}$. This defines a diffeomorphism for $\phi \in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$. Now
$$r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}=\underline{\Lambda}^{2} \sec ^{2} \phi, \quad \frac{\mathrm{~d}}{\mathrm{~d} \phi}=\frac{r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}}{\underline{\Lambda}} \frac{\mathrm{~d}}{\mathrm{~d} r}, \quad \mathrm{~d} r=\underline{\Lambda} \sec ^{2} \phi \mathrm{~d} \phi .$$
Hence, defining
$$F(r)=-\frac{r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}}{\underline{\Lambda}^{2}} \lambda_{0}, \quad \quad \text { i.e. } \quad \lambda_{0}=-\frac{\underline{\Lambda}^{2}}{r^{2}+\underline{\Lambda}^{2}} F(r)$$
we have that $\kappa=2 \underline{\theta}^{0}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}} \mathrm{~d} \phi$. The last term is clearly closed, and thus locally exact, i.e. $\kappa$ is gauge equivalent to $2 \underline{\theta}^{0}$. We can thus assume that $\kappa=2 \underline{\theta}^{0}$ by means of a local diffeomorphism. It can then be checked that $\lambda=\mathrm{d} \phi+\lambda_{0} \underline{\theta}^{0}$ as in Theorem 1.2. In addition, we check that $F(r)$ satisfies (9.3) if and only if $\lambda_{0}$ satisfies
$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{~d} \phi}\left(\frac{\sec ^{2 m+2} \phi}{\tan \phi} \lambda_{0}\right)=\frac{\sec ^{2 m+4} \phi}{\tan ^{2} \phi} \Lambda-\frac{\sec ^{2 m+2} \phi}{\tan ^{2} \phi} \underline{\Lambda},$$
which is none other than equation (8.18). To ensure consistency of these equations, we must have $M=-\underline{\Lambda}^{2 m-1} \underline{c}$. This proves the following result.

Theorem 9.2 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ be the optical geometry of Theorem 1.2. Assume $\underline{\Lambda}$ to be nonzero. Then the Einstein manifold $(\mathcal{M}, \widehat{g})$ is locally isometric to the Taub-NUT-type spacetime $\left(\mathcal{M}_{T N}, g_{T N}\right)$. In particular, $g_{T N}$ is Einstein with Ricci scalar $2(m+1) \Lambda$.

\section*{10 Further geometric properties}

Viewed as a $G$-structure, the almost Robinson structure $(N, K)$ of Theorem 1.2 can easily be described in terms of its intrinsic torsion as can be gleaned from equations (8.23). We shall not pursue the matter here, which is dealt with in [18]. Instead, we focus on the existence of additional geometric structures arising from the Einstein condition.

\subsection*{10.1 A distinguished conformal Killing field}

Proposition 10.1 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ be the optical geometry of Theorem 1.2. Then the vector field $v=g(\alpha, \cdot)$, where $\alpha:=\lambda+\frac{1}{2} \lambda_{0} \kappa$, is a Killing field for the metric $g$. Further, $v$ descends to the infinitesimal transverse symmetry $\underline{e}_{0}$ of the almost CR-Einstein structure ( $\underline{H}, \underline{J}, \underline{\theta}^{0}$ ) on the (local) leaf space $\underline{\mathcal{M}}$ of $\mathcal{K}$.

This conformal Killing field is null if and only if $\widehat{g}$ is a Ricci-flat Fefferman-Einstein metric.
Proof. Using equations (8.23a) and (8.23c) together with the Leibniz rule, we find
$$\nabla \alpha=2 \mathrm{i} \lambda_{0} \underline{h}_{\alpha \bar{\beta}} \theta^{\alpha} \wedge \bar{\theta}^{\bar{\beta}}-\dot{\lambda}_{0} \kappa \wedge \lambda,$$
i.e. $\nabla \alpha=\mathrm{d} \alpha$, which shows that $v$ is a Killing field for $g$ as claimed. Further, since $£_{k} v=0$, the vector field $v$ projects down to a vector field $\underline{v}$ on $(\underline{\mathcal{M}}, \underline{H}, \underline{J})$. It is then clear that $\underline{v}$ is the Reeb vector field $\underline{e}_{0}$ of the contact form $\underline{\theta}^{0}$. Thus, by definition of almost CR-Einstein, it must be an infinitesimal symmetry of $(\underline{H}, \underline{J})$. Finally, we note that $g(v, v)=\lambda_{0}$, and the last claim follows from Corollary 9.1. \(\square\)

\subsection*{10.2 A second almost Robinson structure}

Under the assumption of Theorem 1.2, the existence of the Einstein metric $\widehat{g}$ is essentially equivalent to the existence of the distinguished 1-form $\lambda$. This not only determines a second optical structure $L$, where $L^{\perp}=\operatorname{Ann}(\lambda)$, but also a second almost Robinson structure which we now describe, carefully distinguishing between the cases $\lambda_{0} \neq 0$ and $\lambda_{0}=0$. When $\lambda_{0} \neq 0$, it will be convenient to apply a boost transformation to $\kappa$ and $\lambda$ to obtain
$$\lambda^{\prime}:=2 \frac{1}{\lambda_{0}} \lambda, \quad \kappa^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2} \lambda_{0} \kappa .$$
These 1-forms satisfy
$$\begin{aligned}
& \nabla \lambda^{\prime}=2 \underline{i}_{\underline{h}_{\alpha \bar{\beta}} \theta^{\alpha} \wedge \bar{\theta}^{\bar{\beta}}-\frac{1}{2} \dot{\lambda}_{0} \lambda^{\prime} \otimes \lambda^{\prime}} \\
& \nabla \kappa^{\prime}=\mathrm{i} \lambda_{0} \underline{h}_{\alpha \bar{\beta}} \theta^{\alpha} \wedge \bar{\theta}^{\bar{\beta}}+\frac{1}{2} \dot{\lambda}_{0} \lambda^{\prime} \otimes \kappa^{\prime}
\end{aligned}$$
respectively.
Proposition 10.2 Let $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, N, K)$ be the twist-induced almost Robinson manifold of Theorem 1.2. Then the null 1 -form $\lambda$ defines an optical structure $L$ with non-shearing congruence of null geodesics $\mathcal{L}$, non-expanding with respect to $g$, and an almost Robinson structure $\left(N^{*}, L\right)$ dual to $(N, K)$, where
$$N^{*} \cong\left(\bar{N} \cap^{\mathbf{C}} H_{K, L}\right) \oplus^{\mathbf{C}} L, \quad H_{K, L}=K^{\perp} \cap L^{\perp} .$$
These enjoy the following properties.
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] If $\widehat{g}$ is not a Ricci-flat Fefferman-Einstein metric, i.e. $\lambda_{0} \neq 0$, then $\mathcal{L}$ is twisting and $\left(N^{*}, L\right)$ is twist-induced. Further, $\left(N^{*}, L\right)$, with $\lambda^{\prime}$ defined in (10.1), induces on the (local) leaf space of $\mathcal{L}$ an almost CR-Einstein structure equivalent to $\left(\underline{H}, \underline{J}, \underline{\theta}^{0}\right)$.
\end{itemize}
\begin{itemize}
\item[2.] If $\widehat{g}$ is a Ricci-flat Fefferman-Einstein metric, i.e. $\lambda_{0}=0$, then $\lambda$ is a null 1 -form parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection ∇ of $g$, and in particular, $\mathcal{L}$ is non-twisting. Further, $\left(N^{*}, L\right)$ induces a local foliation by $2 m$-dimensional Ricci-flat almost Kähler-Einstein manifolds on the (local) leaf space of $\mathcal{L}$.
\end{itemize}

Proof. The existence of the dual almost Robinson structure is self-explanatory, and that $\mathcal{L}$ is a non-shearing congruence of null geodesics, non-expanding with respect to $g$, non-twisting when $\lambda_{0}=0$, twisting otherwise, follows directly from equation (8.23c).
\begin{itemize}
\item[1.] When $\lambda_{0} \neq 0$, we can work with an adapted coframe $\left\{\kappa^{\prime}, \theta^{\alpha}, \bar{\theta}^{\bar{\alpha}}, \lambda^{\prime}\right\}$ where $\kappa^{\prime}$ and $\lambda^{\prime}$ are defined by (10.1). By inspection of (10.2), we note that the sets of 1 -forms $\left\{\lambda^{\prime}, \theta^{\alpha}\right\}$ and $\left\{\kappa, \theta^{\alpha}\right\}$ are on the same geometric footing. Both sets can be viewed as the pullbacks of adapted CR coframes on the respective (local) leaf spaces of $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{K}$. There, their respective structure equations are clearly determined by the same almost pseudo-Hermitian invariants. In particular, their respective pseudo-Hermitian structures must be equivalent.
\item[2.] When $\lambda_{0}=0$, the existence of a foliation $\underline{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{L}}$, say, by almost Kähler manifolds on the leaf space $\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{L}}$ of $\mathcal{L}$ follows from the analysis given in [17, 18]. Note that the tensor $h$ in Theorem 1.2 can also be viewed as the pullback of a tensor field on $\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{L}}$, which restricts to an almost Kähler metric on each leaf of $\underline{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{L}}$. That these metrics are Ricci-flat can be checked by restricting the Ricci tensor of $g$ to projectable vector fields tangent to $H_{K, L}$ using the computation of Appendix A. \(\square\)
\end{itemize}

Remark 10.1 By Proposition 8.1, if $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ of Theorem 1.2 is conformally flat, then $\widehat{g}$ is a Ricci-flat Fefferman-Einstein metric so that the null 1-form $\lambda$ defines a non-twisting non-shearing congruence of null geodesics.

Remark 10.2 When $\lambda_{0} \neq 0$, the situation of Proposition 10.1 with respect to the almost Robinson structure ( $N^{*}, L$ ) is completely analogous, and one can check that $£_{\ell^{\prime}} v=0$ where $\ell^{\prime}=g^{-1}\left(\lambda^{\prime}, \cdot\right)$ with $\lambda^{\prime}$ given by (10.1), i.e. $v$ descends to the transverse infinitesimal symmetry of the almost CR-Einstein induced by $\left(N^{*}, L\right)$ on the leaf space $\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{L}}$ of $\mathcal{L}$. In fact, we can rewrite $\alpha=\kappa^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2} \lambda_{0} \lambda^{\prime}$.

On the other hand, when $\lambda_{0}=0, v=\ell=g^{-1}(\lambda, \cdot)$ is tangent to $\mathcal{L}$ and thus projects down to the zero vector field on $\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{L}}$. However, $k$ now commutes with $\ell$ and descends to a transverse infinitesimal symmetry of the almost Kähler-Einstein foliation described in Proposition 10.2. One can check that the quotient by this symmetry yields an almost Kähler-Einstein manifold.

Remark 10.3 Note that the distribution $K+L$ is involutive, and thus $(\mathcal{M}, \mathbf{c}, K)$ is foliated by two-dimensional leaves. It is straightforward to check that one recovers an almost Kähler-Einstein manifold on the leaf space of this foliation. This was already noted in [33] in the integrable case, where the Fefferman-Einstein space is described as a 2-torus fibration over a Kähler-Einstein manifold.
