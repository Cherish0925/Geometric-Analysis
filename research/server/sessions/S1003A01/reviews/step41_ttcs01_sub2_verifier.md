## 5. Conformal Chern–Gauss–Bonnet identity and interpretation

### The identity

Weyl energy is conformally invariant and \(\chi\) is fixed. Since \(-\tfrac12\int|\mathrm{Ric}^\circ|^2+\tfrac1{24}\int s^2\) is invariant,
\[
P(\widehat g)-P(g)=\frac{S(\widehat g)-S(g)}{12}.
\]
On the cell this needs equal collar jets. That holds because \(f\equiv1\) (host Kähler form, \(|\omega|=\sqrt2\)) on the exact collar, so the transgression terms agree. The data confirm it: \(p^2(\widehat P-P)=0.14592\) against \(C_E/12=0.14595\).

### A usable transfer formula

\[
P(\widehat g_p)-P(g_p)=\int\Big(3\Big(\frac{\Delta v}{v}\Big)^2-s\frac{\Delta v}{v}\Big)d\mu_{g_p}\sim\frac{C_E}{12\,p^2}.
\]
Any AK-side lower bound for \(P(\widehat g_p)\) transfers to \(g_p\) only after subtracting this term. Since \(J(g_p)\to J(g_*)<1\) smoothly, every such AK bound must contain a term diverging like \(p^{-2}\).

The note's reading is therefore correct: the divergence belongs to the conformal representative, not to the Einstein defect of \(g_p\).

### Structure of the singular p = 0 limit

\(\widehat g_0=\kappa r\,g\) is not a cone along E. Points at \(r=0\) are at finite \(\widehat g_0\)-distance, \(\sim r^{3/2}\), and the \(\sigma_{2,3}\) directions shrink like \(r^{1/2}\). The completion therefore collapses the bolt sphere to a point. This is consistent with \(\mathrm{Area}_{\widehat g_p}(E)=|p|\to0\).
