# Step 36 Preceptor interface review: fixed-$J_6$ retained, existence/continuation/Q not yet closed

## 0. Purpose

This review checks the Step 35 handoff at the interfaces where a conditional
energy estimate becomes a genuine flow-to-terminal argument for the
four-dimensional negative Ishida--LeBrun route.  The conclusion is deliberately
mixed:

- the unregularized fixed-$J_6$ persistence mechanism is a real and useful
  advance;
- the Step 35 existence and continuation notes are not proofs of the needed
  analytic platform;
- terminal calibration still requires a separate global unweighted
  $Q(t)=\int_M |E(t)|^2\,d\mu_t$ estimate.

The review uses the Step 35 notes, the Step 27 shifted-curvature/transported
DeTurck material, Yang's Section 8 local Ricci-flow construction, the Step 23
single-support $Q$ and $J_6$ bridge, A01's Step 34 fixed-exponent note, and TTCS
verification `trace/ttcs/results/step36_ttcs01_verifier1_integration.md`.  The
small script `workspace/scripts/verify_step36_interface_algebra.py` checks only
algebraic sanity points: Lie-derivative product terms, coefficient transport,
and the fact that $J_6$ and $aE$ can miss unweighted $Q$ near $a=0$.

## 1. What survives from Step 35: fixed $J_6$

Let
\[
 a=\chi^2,
 \qquad E=\operatorname{Ric}_g+g,
 \qquad s=|E|^2,
\]
and on one buffered support define
\[
 J_6=\int \eta^2 a^4 s^3,
 \qquad
 X_6=\int \eta^2a^5s^2|\nabla E|^2,
 \qquad
 w_6=\eta a^{5/2}s^{3/2}=\eta\chi^5|E|^3 .
\]
The important simplification is sound: $s^3$ and the test
$q=3\eta^2a^4s^2$ are smooth polynomial functions of the tensor $E$, so the
fixed $J_6$ layer needs no Kato/norm regularization.  The exact chain rule
\[
(\partial_t-a\Delta)s^3
=3s^2(\partial_t-a\Delta)s-6as|\nabla s|^2
\]
adds a favorable nonpositive term.  The Hessian-$a$ contribution must be
integrated by parts as a signed tensor.  Its defect contraction is quadratic in
$E$,
\[
 B_0^{ij}=2g^{ij}|E|^2+2(\operatorname{tr}E)E^{ij}-4E^{ik}E_k{}^j,
\]
so $B_0=O(s)$ and $\nabla B_0=O(s^{1/2}|\nabla E|)$.  After one integration by
parts all such terms reduce to
\[
 a^3|da|^2s^3
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 a^4|da|s^{5/2}|\nabla E|,
\]
and hence to $\varepsilon X_6+C_\varepsilon r^{-2}J_6$, using
$|da|^2/a=4|d\chi|^2$.

The defect reaction is linear in curvature.  With
\[
P_6=\int \eta^2a^5Fs^3=\int Fw_6^2,
\]
Hölder and the local Sobolev inequality give
\[
 P_6\le \|F\|_{2,U}\|w_6\|_4^2
 \le C\mathcal S K_2^{1/2}(X_6+r^{-2}J_6)
 =C\gamma(X_6+r^{-2}J_6),
\]
where $K_2=\int_U F^2$ is the same localized active-region curvature energy
and $\gamma=\mathcal S K_2^{1/2}$.  Thus, as long as the first-exit interval
supplies smooth existence, moving Sobolev comparability, the stated support
geometry, and small $\gamma(t)$,
\[
 J_6'+cX_6\le C(1+r^{-2})J_6+CP_6
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 J_6(t)\le e^{C(1+r^{-2})t}J_6(0).
\]
This is independent of the $q=3$ curvature seed $Y=\int aF^3$.  The $q=3$
layer remains necessary for curvature Moser and for the positive-time potential
in the general defect Moser recurrence, but not for persistence of this fixed
seed.

Two textual corrections must be kept with the lemma.  The first-derivative
cutoff contribution in Step 35 §3.6 should have power
$a^4|da|s^{5/2}|\nabla E|$, not $s^{3/2}$.  Also, the malformed schematic
metric-variation index in Step 35 §2.2 should be replaced by the simple bound
$a\operatorname{Rm}*E*E$ in the evolution of $|E|^2$.  These are notation
repairs, not changes to the surviving estimate.

## 2. The Step 35 existence note is not a proof

The target equation is the fixed-cutoff normalized local flow
\[
 \partial_t g=-2a(\operatorname{Ric}_g+g),\qquad a=\chi^2.
\]
A DeTurck-modified equation with a fixed coefficient and a gauge term
$a\mathcal L_Wg$ does not pull back to the target equation by a diffeomorphism
generated by $aW$.  The product rule is
\[
 \mathcal L_{aW}g
 =a\mathcal L_Wg+da\otimes W^\flat+W^\flat\otimes da.
\]
Therefore $a\mathcal L_Wg$ lacks exactly the first-derivative coefficient terms
needed for cancellation.  The pullback also transports the scalar coefficient.
This is not a cosmetic issue; it changes the equation solved by the limiting
metric.

The correct local-flow formulation is the transported-coefficient DeTurck
system already identified in Step 27.  With a background metric $s$ and DeTurck
field $W(\widehat g,s)$, solve the hatted system
\[
 \partial_t\widehat g
 =-2\widehat a(\operatorname{Ric}_{\widehat g}+\widehat g)
   +\mathcal L_{\widehat a W(\widehat g,s)}\widehat g,
\]
coupled to
\[
 \widehat a=a\circ\phi_t^{-1},
 \qquad
 \partial_t\phi_t\circ\phi_t^{-1}=-\widehat a W(\widehat g,s).
\]
Equivalently $\partial_t\widehat a=(\widehat aW)\widehat a$.  Then
$\phi_t^*\widehat a=a$, and
\[
 {d\over dt}\phi_t^*\widehat g
 =\phi_t^*(\partial_t\widehat g+
          \mathcal L_{\partial_t\phi_t\circ\phi_t^{-1}}\widehat g)
 =-2a(\operatorname{Ric}_g+g).
\]
The normalization term $-2\widehat a\widehat g$ is zeroth order and should not
alter Yang's principal/tame mechanism, but it must be included in the actual
transported system.

The second problem is the $\varepsilon$ argument in the Step 35 note.  For each
fixed $\varepsilon>0$, replacing $a$ by $a+\varepsilon$ in a gauged equation
makes a strictly parabolic problem.  But ordinary parabolic Schauder constants
depend on the lower ellipticity bound, hence usually on $\varepsilon^{-1}$.
Splitting into $\{a>a_0\}$ and $\{a<2a_0\}$ does not close uniform smooth
existence: the displayed estimate on the small-$a$ region contains an unclosed
positive high-derivative term of the form
\[
 C(2a_0+\varepsilon)\|h\|_{H^{k+1}}^2,
\]
and interface terms are not accounted for.  Yang's Section 8 construction
(line 811--818 in the local source) instead obtains tame estimates adapted to
the degenerate square-cutoff structure and then passes from regularized
linearized equations.  The current session has not written that proof for the
normalized transported system.

Thus the correct existence proof target is:

1. formulate the transported-coefficient normalized DeTurck system above;
2. linearize it, retaining the coupled variation of $\widehat a$ and the
   extra zeroth-order normalized term;
3. prove the same degenerate energy/tame estimates as Yang's local Ricci flow
   for the linearized pair $(h,\psi)$, with square coefficient $\chi^2$ and
   without relying on ellipticity constants independent of $\varepsilon$;
4. pass to a smooth short-time solution and pull it back to the fixed-cutoff
   equation.

For one fixed smooth datum the time and constants may depend on the datum, but
that allowance does not replace the missing degenerate energy construction.

## 3. The Step 35 continuation note is not an extension criterion

Continuation must be stated on a maximal interval $[0,T_{\max})$.  A proof may
assume a bound such as
\[
 \sup_{M\times[0,T)} |a\operatorname{Rm}|<\infty
\]
for every $T<T_{\max}$ or near a candidate finite terminal time and then show
smooth extension.  The Step 35 note instead argues on an already smooth closed
interval and inserts coefficients
\[
 \|T(t)\|_\infty,
 \qquad
 \|a\nabla\operatorname{Rm}(t)\|_\infty,
 \qquad T=\operatorname{Rm}+G,
\]
then declares them finite because the solution is smooth.  That does not
control their integrability as $t\uparrow T_{\max}$.  The zero-order Moser
bound controls $|aF|$, not unweighted $|T|$ near $a=0$, and it supplies no
first-derivative pointwise bound such as $|a\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|$.
The assertion that $T=U/a$ recovers unweighted bounds near $a=0$ is precisely
what the shifted formulation was designed to avoid.

The viable continuation route is the shifted-curvature Wang induction.  Define
\[
 G={g\owedge g\over 2(n-1)},
 \qquad T=\operatorname{Rm}+G,
 \qquad U=aT.
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}(T)=E,
 \qquad \operatorname{Ric}(U)=aE,
 \qquad \partial_tg=-2\operatorname{Ric}(U),
\]
so
\[
 \partial_t\Gamma=g^{-1}*\nabla\operatorname{Ric}(U),
\]
not a split expression involving uncontrolled $da*F+a\nabla\operatorname{Rm}$.
This contraction must be preserved in the cutoff-jet equation
\[
 \partial_t\nabla^r\chi
 =\sum_{q=0}^{r-2}\nabla^q(\partial_t\Gamma)*\nabla^{r-1-q}\chi.
\]

At the lowest coupled level the real target is to estimate the pair
\[
 (\nabla U,\nabla^2\chi)
\]
from quantities already available under $\sup |U|<\infty$ and finite
unweighted $L^p$ norms of $T$, not from $\|T\|_\infty$ or pointwise
$a\nabla\operatorname{Rm}$.  The differentiated $U$ equation contains the raw
terms
\[
 \chi\nabla^3\chi*U,
 \quad \nabla\chi*\nabla^2\chi*U,
 \quad \chi\nabla^2\chi*\nabla U,
 \quad \chi\nabla\chi*\nabla^2U.
\]
In the $L^p$ energy for $\nabla U$, these must be treated as a signed group:
one integration by parts moves the derivative off $\nabla^3\chi$; terms with
$\nabla^2U$ keep a factor $\chi$ and are absorbed by the weighted dissipation;
the residual terms couple to $\|\nabla^2\chi\|_{L^p}$ and $\|\nabla U\|_{L^p}$.
The companion cutoff equation for $\nabla^2\chi$ is driven by
$\nabla\operatorname{Ric}(U)*\nabla\chi$, so the same pair can plausibly close
by Wang's Volterra/staggered-exponent method.  But this term-level base-pair
proof is not present in the Step 35 continuation note.

For higher orders the safe dependency remains the Step 27 triangle:
\[
 \nabla^mU \longleftrightarrow \nabla^{m+1}\chi,
 \qquad
 \nabla^{m-1}T \text{ is obtained after the previous cutoff level.}
\]
All $G$-terms from the normalization must be written through this induction.
The older theorem-level Step 27 note and the Codex ledger provide a credible
roadmap, not an independently checked terminal extension theorem.

## 4. Terminal calibration requires separate global $Q$

The terminal Ishida--LeBrun calibration and exponential-pullback selection use
an unweighted global defect energy
\[
 Q(t)=\int_M |E(t)|^2\,d\mu_t.
\]
This cannot be replaced by $J_6=\int\eta^2a^4|E|^6$ or by
$\|aE\|_\infty$.  Near the zero set of $a$ these weighted quantities may be
small while $|E|^2$ is large.  The toy model $a=x^2$, $E\sim x^{-1}$ has
bounded $aE$ and finite $a^4|E|^6$ in one dimension near $0$, but divergent
unweighted $|E|^2$; the script records this only as a sanity example, not as a
smooth geometric construction.

The separate $Q$ route is the homogeneous defect energy identity from Steps 14,
20, and 23.  Globally or componentwise on the disjoint selected supports,
\[
 Q'+c\int a|\nabla E|^2
 \le C\int\left({|da|^2\over a}+a\right)|E|^2
    +C\int a|\operatorname{Rm}|\,|E|^2 .
\]
There is no curvature-only source.  On each support,
\[
 \int \chi_j^2 F|E|^2
 =\int F(\chi_jE)^2
 \le \|F\|_{2,U_j}\|\chi_jE\|_4^2
 \le C\gamma_j\left(\int a|\nabla E|^2+r^{-2}\int |E|^2\right).
\]
For small $\gamma_j$, the gradient term is absorbed, giving
\[
 Q'(t)\le Cr^{-2}Q(t),
 \qquad Q(t)\le e^{C\theta}Q(0)
 \quad (t\le\theta r^2),
\]
with outside-support contribution stationary.  This estimate must be carried
as its own first-exit component.  Its role is distinct:

- $Q(t)$ feeds the terminal Chern--Weil/monopole calibration;
- $J_6(t)$ seeds the defect Moser iteration;
- $\|aE\|_\infty$ controls metric drift and moving Sobolev/support comparison.

Step 35's phrase that global $L^2$ defect control follows from $J_6$ persistence
is therefore incorrect.
