## Deep-Reasoning

This step produced the most structurally clarifying mathematical objects yet for Yau Problem 26. The central insight—building on the Strategist's sign-blind observation and S0910A01's quantitative analysis—is a complete per-sign Einstein census for dimension 4 that dramatically constrains the viable research routes.

The sign-blind CGB lemma (Proposition 3.2 in the dossier) shows that tight Ricci pinching from EITHER direction forces strict Hitchin-Thorpe 2χ > 3|τ| because s² (not s) enters the identity. This eliminates all HT-violating manifolds from both branches of counterexample search.

The sign census reveals a fundamental asymmetry in dimension 4. Positive Einstein (λ>0) is excluded by the Seiberg-Witten/gauge-theoretic mechanism: non-trivial SW invariants prevent positive scalar curvature, hence positive Einstein. This is NOT an integral curvature inequality and it is INCOMPATIBLE with tight positive pinching (which requires s ≈ +4 > 0). Consequence: LeBrun manifolds have δ₊ ≥ 1; they CANNOT serve as positive-branch counterexamples. Zero Einstein (λ=0) is excluded by topology: Ricci-flat 4-manifolds must be K3 or flat (T⁴ quotients), and LeBrun manifolds have wrong topology. No constraint on δ. Negative Einstein (λ<0) is excluded by SW + CGB integral curvature inequality: the combination forces ∫|W⁺|² < 0 on a hypothetical Einstein metric, contradiction. Crucially, for non-Einstein input metrics, the |E|² term absorbs this negative quantity, so the mechanism gives only a weak constraint on δ₋.

I derived the LeBrun mechanism from first principles: using the SW basic class κ² = A−k (where A = c₁²(X) and M = X#k\overline{CP²}), the CGB gives 4∫|W⁺|² = 8π²(A−k) − 16λ²Vol/3, and the SW bound 16λ²Vol ≥ 32π²(A−k) forces 4∫|W⁺|² ≤ −8π²(A−k)/3 < 0 for all k < A. This excludes ALL negative Einstein, not just k > 2A/3. I raised a discrepancy with S0910A01's formula λ²V ≥ 2π²A (using A rather than A−k) in message #204 and asked for clarification on whether π*K_X is a SW basic class of M.

The computational verification with `lebrun_delta_bound.py` confirms that for varying LeBrun manifolds with A → ∞ and k ≈ 2A/3, the δ₋ lower bound tends to 0 like 1/√A. The formula's derivation remains a source obligation.

The most original construction is the analysis of dimension ≥ 5: I could not find (nor can existing literature definitively identify) ANY closed manifold of dimension ≥ 5 that provably admits no Einstein metric of any sign. HT and SW are four-dimensional tools; the Lichnerowicz α-invariant only excludes λ > 0 (positive scalar curvature) on certain spin manifolds but not λ ≤ 0. This means refuting Q_n for n ≥ 5 requires solving a strictly harder open problem first: finding a high-dimensional no-Einstein manifold.

The Berger sphere computation (`berger_collapse_ricci.py`) shows that Hopf-fiber collapse on S^{2n+1} sends the vertical Ricci eigenvalue to 0 while horizontal goes to 2(n+1), producing δ₊ → 1. This is a concrete, computable piece of evidence that non-quotient collapse is incompatible with tight positive Ricci pinching, supporting P_n⁺. The O'Neill formula generalization suggests that any Riemannian submersion with shrinking fibers creates Ricci splitting, with the exception of flat finite quotients that preserve Einstein.

The negative branch dichotomy composes: CGB volume ceiling + Min-Oo escape (d²max|K| → ∞) + L² curvature budget → curvature concentration → ALE bubbles (noncollapsed) or collapse. The Calabi/MV computation from Step 1 shows ALE bubbles create order-1 Ricci defect unless first obstruction λ = 0. The collapse case is the genuine frontier.

Overall, evidence strongly tilts toward Q_n TRUE. The most promising existence route is P_n⁺ via a 'no bad collapse' argument: show that tight positive Ricci pinching excludes all non-quotient collapse (supported by Berger computation), then use noncollapsed compactness to get Einstein. The refutation route faces multiple independent obstacles: no high-dimensional no-Einstein manifolds, positive branch protected by SW incompatibility, negative branch constrained by the dichotomy.
