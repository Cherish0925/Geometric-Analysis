# Twisted torus connectors: conserved charge, a geometric counterexample, and a quantitative necessary twist

Date: 2026-09-19. Local research continuation. The Skill was reread in full. Live Skill files and research Sessions were only read.

The new results here are an explicit exact geometric test of the asymptotic-charge issue, a quantitative necessary lower bound for twist in the full free-T² connector class, and a weighted transport obstruction. They do not construct a two-ended connector or decide general Einstein existence on a graph manifold.

## 1. Conventions and the exact charge law

On the principal region of a free isometric T² action, write

\[
 g=\gamma+G_{ab}(d\xi^a+\mathcal A^a)(d\xi^b+\mathcal A^b),\qquad
 G=\rho M,\quad\det M=1,\quad\rho=e^\sigma.
\]

If `dA^a=f^a dmu_gamma`, define the coordinate charge and physical twist energy by

\[
 q=\rho Gf=\rho^2Mf,\qquad
 Q=\frac12 f^TGf=\frac12\rho^{-3}q^TM^{-1}q.
\tag{1}
\]

The target metric for the orbit shape `U` is `|dU|²=(1/2)tr(M^{-1}dM M^{-1}dM)`. Put `E=Ric+3g` and `epsilon=||g^{-1}E||_op,infinity`.

The mixed Ricci equation is, in the manuscript's physical normalization,

\[
 \mathscr B=\rho^{-3/2}M^{-1/2}dq,\qquad
 |\mathscr B|^2=4|E_{\rm mix}|_{\rm HS}^2,
 \qquad |\mathscr B|\le2\sqrt2\epsilon.
\tag{2}
\]

The norm of the mixed block in (2) counts one horizontal-by-vertical block, not both transposed blocks of the full Ricci tensor. These definitions and coefficients are in `20260916_global.tex:1885–1912`.

Consequently `dq=0` for an exact Einstein metric. On a connected principal region, one exact orthogonally transitive open set forces `q=0` everywhere, hence `dA=0` everywhere. A flat connection may still have global holonomy; local orthogonal transitivity and the base equations require only `dA=0`.

For the two-complete-expanding-end hypotheses of the manuscript's OT theorem, this observation removes the separate OT assumption at *zero* Ricci defect: any full free-T² exact Einstein connector with an exact standard hyperbolic open end would reduce to the forbidden OT case. It does not imply that a small nonzero defect makes twist small.

## 2. An actual Einstein metric: flat conformal infinity does not force zero charge

The underlying metric is the classical toral AdS black-hole metric, not a
new Einstein construction; see [Anderson, equations (1.9)--(1.11)](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~anderson/dehn.pdf).
The calculation here concerns its free torus marking, nonzero reduced
charge, and the distinction between exact and merely asymptotic end data.

### Proposition 1: a smooth one-ended twisted Einstein filling

For `a>0`, set `m=a³` and

\[
 V(r)=r^2-\frac{m}{r},\qquad
 g=\frac{dr^2}{V(r)}+V(r)d\theta^2+r^2(dx^2+dy^2),\quad r\ge a.
\tag{3}
\]

Give `theta` period `P=4pi/(3a)`, give `x` the same period, and give `y` any positive period. Then (3) extends smoothly over `r=a` to a complete metric on `D²×T²`, satisfies `Ric=-3g`, and has one flat conformal torus infinity.

Choose any nonzero integer `c`. The torus action generated by `partial_x+c partial_theta` and `partial_y` is free even at the bolt. For this action its conserved coordinate charge is

\[
 q=(3cm,0)\ne0,
\tag{4}
\]

while `Q` tends to zero at conformal infinity.

**All Ricci blocks.** View (3) as a warped product with two-dimensional base `h=dr²/V+Vdtheta²` and flat two-dimensional fibre of warp factor `r`. Direct differentiation gives

\[
 K_h=-V''/2,\quad\operatorname{Hess}_h r=(V'/2)h,
 \quad\Delta_h r=V',\quad|dr|_h^2=V.
\]

The radial and theta blocks of Ricci are both

\[
 (-V''/2-V'/r)h=-3h.
\]

The two fibre components are

\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_{xx}=\operatorname{Ric}_{yy}=-(rV'+V)=-3r^2,
\]

and all mixed blocks vanish. These identities use `V'=2r+m/r²` and `V''=2-2m/r³`. The accompanying script also computes all coordinate Ricci components directly, including after the torus-coordinate shear below.

**Smoothness and freeness.** At the bolt, write `u=2sqrt((r-a)/(3a))`. The normal two-plane is

\[
 du^2+(3a/2)^2u^2d\theta^2+O(u^2)du^2+O(u^4)d\theta^2.
\]

The stated period gives a smooth polar disk. The other two circle directions have nonzero size there. Translation in `x` prevents `partial_x+c partial_theta` from acquiring isotropy when the theta circle collapses. The integer condition makes the torus marking globally integral.

**Charge computation.** Set `theta=s+c xi`, `x=xi`, `y=eta`. This is an integral change of the `(theta,x)` torus coordinates with determinant one. On `r>a`, put `D=r²+c²V`. Then

\[
 G=\operatorname{diag}(D,r^2),\quad
 \rho=r\sqrt D,\quad
 \gamma=\frac{dr^2}{V}+\frac{Vr^2}{D}ds^2,
 \quad\mathcal A_s=(cV/D,0).
\]

Since `dmu_gamma=(r/sqrt(D))dr ds`,

\[
 \partial_r\mathcal A_s^1=\frac{3cm}{D^2},\qquad
 f^1=\frac{3cm}{rD^{3/2}},\qquad
 \rho G_{11}f^1=3cm.
\]

In particular,

\[
 Q=\frac{9c^2m^2}{2r^2D^2}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad Q(a)=\frac{9c^2}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]

**Flat conformal infinity.** With `z=1/r`, the compactified metric is

\[
 z^2g=\frac{dz^2}{1-mz^3}
 +(1-mz^3)(ds+c\,d\xi)^2+d\xi^2+d\eta^2.
\]

Its boundary metric is flat. Nevertheless its connection has expansion

\[
 \mathcal A_s^1
 =\frac{c}{1+c^2}-\frac{cm}{(1+c^2)^2}z^3+O(z^6).
\tag{6}
\]

The nonzero charge is carried by this cubic asymptotic coefficient. The example is one-ended, with disk quotient, and is not a solution of the cylindrical two-ended problem. Doubling it does not preserve the bolt/end geometry or the second fundamental form matching. No connector follows from the example.

### What asymptotics actually force charge to vanish

For an exact Einstein end with

\[
 \gamma=dt^2+b_0^2e^{2t}(1+o(1))ds^2,\quad
 \rho=\rho_0e^{2t}(1+o(1)),\quad M\to M_\infty>0,
\]

in radial connection gauge, the charge law gives

\[
 \partial_t\mathcal A_s
 =b_0\rho_0^{-2}e^{-3t}M_\infty^{-1}q+o(e^{-3t}),
\]

and hence

\[
 \mathcal A_s=\mathcal A_\infty
 -\frac{b_0}{3\rho_0^2}e^{-3t}M_\infty^{-1}q+o(e^{-3t}).
\tag{7}
\]

The differentiable asymptotic conditions needed for these expansions are understood. Flat leading boundary data or `Q→0` do not kill the coefficient in (7). Requiring `partial_t A_s=o(e^{-3t})` does kill it and forces `q=0`.

In contrast, on a cusp where `rho→0`, `M` remains uniformly positive definite, and `Q` is bounded, a nonzero constant charge would make `Q` diverge. Such an exact cusp forces zero charge. The expanding and shrinking ends therefore have different charge-detection properties.

## 3. A quantitative non-small-twist theorem beyond OT

Assume now that the complete metric has a free isometric T² action, cylindrical quotient, and two ends which are exactly standard outward-expanding hyperbolic ends outside a compact set. Flat connection holonomy is allowed on these ends. Orthogonal transitivity in the interior is **not** assumed. Let

\[
 e=|dU|^2,\quad Q_*=\sup_BQ,\quad
 K=3+\epsilon,\quad f_*=2\epsilon+Q_*.
\]

The quantities `e` and `Q` vanish on the exact ends, so their suprema are finite.

### Proposition 2: a necessary twist threshold

Under these hypotheses,

\[
 12-8\epsilon\le
 \left[\frac{2f_*}{\sqrt K}
       +\sqrt{2K+\frac{5f_*^2}{K}}\right]^2.
\tag{8}
\]

In particular, for `epsilon` below the manuscript's OT threshold (approximately `0.24625`),

\[
 \boxed{Q_*\ge q_*(\epsilon):=
 \sqrt{3+\epsilon}\left(\sqrt{54-42\epsilon}
                  -2\sqrt{12-8\epsilon}\right)-2\epsilon>0.}
\tag{9}
\]

Thus every hypothetical sequence of full free-T² connectors with `epsilon→0` must satisfy

\[
 \liminf Q_*\ge 9\sqrt2-12\approx0.7279220614.
\tag{10}
\]

This is a necessary order-one twist energy. It excludes a perturbative extension of the OT connector scheme whose twist tends uniformly to zero. It is not a full nonexistence theorem for arbitrary twist.

**Proof: separator lower bound.** The same shortest-loop argument in the complete auxiliary metric `rho² gamma` gives a minimal invariant separator. Its intrinsic scalar formula is unchanged by a connection restricted to a one-dimensional base curve (locally that connection is gauged away). Twist adds a nonnegative contribution to the second fundamental form. The Gauss identity therefore still gives

\[
 \sup_Be\ge12-8\epsilon.
\tag{11}
\]

See the formulas with twist explicitly retained in `20260916_global.tex:1915–1933`. Global flat holonomy does not alter this local scalar computation.

**Proof: charged upper bound without differentiating the charge.** The horizontal and shape equations give

\[
 \operatorname{Ric}^4_\sigma
 :=\operatorname{Ric}_B-\nabla^2\sigma-\tfrac12d\sigma^2
 =-3\gamma+\tfrac12U^*h+Q\gamma+E_B\ge-K\gamma,
\]

\[
 F_U:=\tau_\sigma(U)=\mathscr Z-\nabla_UQ,
 \qquad|\mathscr Z|\le2\epsilon,\qquad|\nabla_UQ|=Q.
\tag{12}
\]

The last equality follows from the target identity `|grad F|=F` for the fixed coordinate-charge quadratic form; differentiation here is in the target variable, holding charge fixed. It is valid pointwise even when the charge varies over the base. Thus `|F_U|<=f_*`.

The weighted Bochner calculation, retaining the whole tension as a divergence source, gives

\[
 \frac12Le\ge\operatorname{div}_\sigma V-f_*^2
       +\tfrac12e^2-Ke,
 \qquad V(X)=\langle dU(X),F_U\rangle,
 \quad |V|\le f_*\sqrt e.
\tag{13}
\]

Here the nonnegative `Qe` term from the horizontal equation is simply dropped. We do not assert that differentiating `grad_U Q` has a favorable sign. The identity used is exactly `⟨dU,DF_U⟩=div_sigma V-|F_U|²`.

Put `kappa=2K`. Completing the square in (13) gives

\[
 (-L+\kappa)e\le\kappa^2+2f_*^2-2\operatorname{div}_\sigma V.
\]

The complete weighted base satisfies `CD(-K,4)`. Here is the resolvent estimate, including its dimensional constant. For its conservative heat kernel `p_t(x,y)`, let

\[
 I(t)=\int p_t|\nabla_y\log p_t|^2d\mu_\sigma.
\]

The curvature-dimension inequality and integration by parts give

\[
 I'=-2\int p_t\Gamma_2(\log p_t)
 \le2KI-\tfrac12 I^2,
 \qquad I(t)\le\frac{4K}{1-e^{-2Kt}}.
\]

The last bound follows by positive-time regularization and comparison with the Riccati solution having infinite initial value. Consequently, for `G_{2K}=integral_0^infinity exp(-2Kt)p_t dt`, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 \sup_x\int|\nabla_yG_{2K}(x,y)|d\mu_\sigma(y)\le\frac2{\sqrt K}.
\]

Indeed the upper bound is `integral_0^infinity exp(-2Kt)sqrt(4K/(1-exp(-2Kt)))dt=2/sqrt(K)`. These calculations use the complete weighted curvature-dimension heat calculus, as in the Skill's OT proof; they do not require a lower injectivity-radius bound.

The compact support of `e,V` outside the exact ends removes boundary contributions. Applying the resolvent and putting `B=sup e` gives

\[
 B\le2K+\frac{f_*^2}{K}+\frac{4f_*}{\sqrt K}\sqrt B.
\]

Solving this scalar quadratic yields the upper bound in (8). This argument uses the full charged equations and requires no derivative bound on the Ricci residual or on charge.

To invert (8), let `L0=12-8epsilon`. On the stated small-defect range `L0>2K`. The increasing upper bound reaches `L0` at

\[
 f_{\rm crit}=\sqrt K\bigl(\sqrt{5L_0-2K}-2\sqrt{L_0}\bigr).
\]

Since `f_*=2epsilon+Q_*`, (9) follows. At zero defect it reduces to (10). Setting `Q_*=0` in (8) recovers exactly the manuscript's OT bound and its threshold, providing a coefficient check.

## 4. The quantitative escape needed to remove charge at both ends

Let `Z={q=0}`; it contains both exact end regions. For a point `x` and a unit-speed base path `Gamma` from `x` to `Z`, define

\[
 H(\Gamma;x)=\int_\Gamma
 \left(\frac{\rho(y)}{\rho(x)}\right)^{3/2}
 \|M(x)^{-1/2}M(y)^{1/2}\|_{\rm op}\,ds_\gamma(y),
\]

\[
 H(x)=\inf_{\Gamma:x\to Z}H(\Gamma;x),\qquad H_*=\sup_BH(x).
\tag{14}
\]

This uses the fixed Lie-algebra marking of the T² action. The relative matrix norm, and hence the condition, is invariant under constant integral basis changes. It measures physical orbit-area and shape variation as well as path length; coordinate collar length is not a substitute.

### Proposition 3: uniform transport control yields a full twisted-class gap

The mixed equation implies

\[
 \sqrt{2Q(x)}\le2\sqrt2\epsilon H(x),\qquad
 Q_*\le4\epsilon^2H_*^2.
\tag{15}
\]

Combining with Proposition 2,

\[
 \boxed{H_*\ge\frac{\sqrt{q_*(\epsilon)}}{2\epsilon}}
\tag{16}
\]

whenever `q_*(epsilon)>0`. In particular, a family with uniformly bounded `H_*` has a positive Ricci-defect gap, even with nonzero connection twist and collapse. Conversely a hypothetical vanishing-defect connector sequence needs weighted transport growing at least as `1/epsilon`.

**Proof.** Parallel identification of the torus Lie algebra gives `q(x)=-integral_Gamma dq`. Multiply by `rho(x)^(-3/2)M(x)^(-1/2)`, use (2), and apply the triangle inequality. Squaring gives (15). This proof deliberately uses the stated Hilbert--Schmidt estimate from (2), without claiming an optimized directional constant.

A sufficient geometric control is: every `x` can be joined to `Z` by a path of length at most `D`, with `rho(y)/rho(x)<=A` and the relative shape norm at most `S`. Then `H_*<=D A^(3/2) S`. At least one of these controls must fail along a sequence whose defect tends to zero. Arbitrarily long collars and large orbit-area excursions are therefore a quantified escape, not a uniform charge-smallness theorem.

On a pure expanding model with `rho(r)/rho(0)=e^(2r)` and constant shape, a radial cutoff at `R` has transport integral

\[
 H(\Gamma;0)=\frac{e^{3R}-1}{3}.
\tag{17}
\]

This makes small mixed residual compatible with removing a fixed coordinate charge far out. It does not produce a connector: all horizontal and shape equations, lattice data, and volume budgets still apply. The manuscript's existing scalar redshift countermodel is consistent with (17), and not being upgraded to a full geometric solution.

For a uniformly comparable radial tube with fixed central physical cross-section volume, its volume and (17) both grow like `e^(3R)`. Thus a bounded global volume would bound this particular escape. If the cross-section covolume collapses, or its lattice varies, that inference fails: weighted transport can increase without a comparable unnormalized volume increase. This states the precise remaining interaction with the relative CGB budget, not a proof of a universal positive connector-volume cost.

## 5. What has advanced and what remains open

The exact soliton demonstrates that flat conformal infinity and vanishing physical twist energy do not force zero conserved charge, even for a smooth complete Einstein metric. The obstruction for exact hyperbolic open ends is stronger and must not be conflated with that asymptotic statement.

The full charged Bochner estimate proves a uniform non-small-twist requirement for every candidate in the specified two-end invariant class. Together with the mixed equation it gives a genuine positive-gap theorem under the explicit transport bound (14), and a necessary `1/epsilon` escape when that bound is absent. The unresolved general case permits order-one twist and diverging weighted transport. Neither local stable charged germs nor a large cover independently resolves the remaining coupled equations.

`round2_global_checks.py` directly verifies every soliton Ricci component before and after the torus shear, its constant charge and physical twist energy, its cubic asymptotics, the resolvent-threshold inversion, and recovery of the OT expression. These are exact symbolic checks. The analytic propositions additionally use the manuscript's stated complete weighted resolvent estimate and the explicitly reproduced separator and Bochner arguments.
