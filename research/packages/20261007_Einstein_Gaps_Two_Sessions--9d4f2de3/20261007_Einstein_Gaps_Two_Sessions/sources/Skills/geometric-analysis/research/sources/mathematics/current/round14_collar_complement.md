# Two radial modes and a uniform Dirichlet complement on a collapsing throat

## 1. Statement and role in the Einstein equation

Let \((F,h)\) be a fixed closed connected oriented surface of curvature
\(-k^2\), where \(k>0\). Set
\[
 B_{c,R}=(-R,R)\times\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z),\qquad
 a(t)=c\cosh(kt),\qquad g_0=dt^2+a^2d\phi^2+h.
\]
We let \(R\to\infty\), \(c\to0\), and require
\(a_{\max}=c\cosh(kR)\to0\). These conditions hold on the fixed-fiber
subcollars of the closed construction in Round 13. All tensor boundary
conditions below are homogeneous Dirichlet conditions at \(t=\pm R\).
No conditions are imposed at an artificial interior face.

Use \(\Delta=\nabla^*\nabla\), negative divergence \(\delta\), and
\[
 P_g=D(\operatorname{Ric}+k^2g)_g+\delta_g^*
          (\delta_g+\tfrac12d\operatorname{tr}_g)
      =\tfrac12\Delta_{L,g}+k^2.
                                                        \tag{1.1}
\]
On the product Einstein background this is
\(P_0=\frac12\nabla^*\nabla-\mathring R\). It acts on **all** symmetric
two-tensors, not only invariant or transverse-traceless tensors.

Write \(f(t)=\operatorname{sech}^2(kt)\) and define
\[
 \begin{split}
 H_1&=f(dt^2-a^2d\phi^2),\\
 H_2&=f\{dt\otimes(ad\phi)+(ad\phi)\otimes dt\}.
 \end{split}                                             \tag{1.2}
\]
The restrictions of these tensors need not satisfy the Dirichlet condition;
they define two continuous linear functionals by \(L^2(g_0)\) pairing.
Let \(\lambda_1(F)>0\) be the first positive eigenvalue of the positive
scalar Laplacian on \(F\), and put
\[
 \mu=\min\{k^2/8,\lambda_1(F)/2\}>0.                     \tag{1.3}
\]

**Theorem 1 (full Dirichlet complement).** For sufficiently small
\(a_{\max}\), every \(q\in H^1_0(B_{c,R}\times F;\operatorname{Sym}^2)\)
with \(\langle q,H_1\rangle=\langle q,H_2\rangle=0\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\langle P_0q,q\rangle_{L^2}\ge\mu\|q\|_{L^2}^2.} \tag{1.4}
\]
The constants depend on the fixed fiber but not on \(c,R\). The product
operator is nonnegative on the full Dirichlet space. It has exactly two
eigenvalues tending to zero in this limit; all remaining eigenvalues are
at least \(\mu\).

This determines the entire small **Dirichlet collar** sector. It does not
say that the closed manifold has only two small modes per edge: variations
of the adjacent blocks and their matching data are not Dirichlet data.
The two real polarizations are familiar length/twist directions of a
hyperbolic collar. The point here is an explicit uniform complement estimate
for the complete tensor operator in the current collapsing construction.

## 2. The exact radial operator

Use the orthonormal frame \(T=\partial_t\), \(U=a^{-1}\partial_\phi\)
on the cylinder, and \(\kappa=a'/a=k\tanh(kt)\). Its only nonzero frame
derivatives are
\(\nabla_U T=\kappa U\), \(\nabla_U U=-\kappa T\).
Let \(D=dt^2-a^2d\phi^2\) and
\(O=dt\otimes(ad\phi)+(ad\phi)\otimes dt\). Directly,
\[
 \nabla_UD=2\kappa O,\qquad \nabla_UO=-2\kappa D.
\]
Thus on either circle-invariant polarization
\[
 P_0(vD)=(L_wv)D,\quad P_0(vO)=(L_wv)O,
\quad L_wv=-\tfrac12(v''+\kappa v')+(2\kappa^2-k^2)v.
                                                        \tag{2.1}
\]
The two tensors in (1.2) are trace-free and divergence-free. In particular,
both solve \(P_0H_i=0\) on the complete cylinder. Omitting the second
polarization would make (1.4) false.

The scalar radial measure is \(a(t)dt\). Conjugation by its square root
gives an elementary factorization:
\[
 2\sqrt a L_wa^{-1/2}
 =-\partial_t^2+k^2\{9/4-(15/4)\operatorname{sech}^2(kt)\}
 =A_{3/2}^*A_{3/2},\quad
 A_s=\partial_t+sk\tanh(kt).                             \tag{2.2}
\]
The kernel is generated by \(u_0=\operatorname{sech}^{3/2}(kt)\).
Moreover,
\[
 A_{3/2}A_{3/2}^*=A_{1/2}^*A_{1/2}+2k^2\ge2k^2.        \tag{2.3}
\]
This proves, by the closed-range decomposition of \(A_{3/2}\),
\[
 \langle L_wv,v\rangle_{a\,dt}
 \ge k^2\|v\|_{a\,dt}^2\quad\hbox{if }v\perp f.        \tag{2.4}
\]
For completeness, (2.3) makes \(A_{3/2}^*\) bounded below; hence its
range is closed and equals \((\ker A_{3/2})^\perp\).
For \(u=A_{3/2}^*w\), the spectral inequality
\((AA^*)^2\ge2k^2AA^*\) proves
\(\|Au\|^2\ge2k^2\|u\|^2\), then density gives (2.4).
No numerical spectral truncation is used.

A Dirichlet function on \((-R,R)\) extends by zero to an \(H^1\)
function on the whole line. Its pairing with the restricted \(f\) is
exactly the whole-line pairing. Thus (2.4) applies unchanged to the finite
interval. The factorization also proves nonnegativity without the
orthogonality condition.

## 3. All circle-invariant tensor sectors

The parallel product decomposition splits \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\) into
the two factor trace lines, the trace-free tensors on each factor, and
\(T^*B\odot T^*F\). The operator preserves these summands.

### 3.1 Cylinder trace-free tensors with fiber dependence

Expand their coefficients in scalar eigenfunctions on \(F\). The operator
for eigenvalue \(\lambda_j\) is \(L_w+\lambda_j/2\), on each real
polarization. Nonconstant fiber modes have lower bound \(\lambda_1/2\);
constant fiber modes have lower bound \(k^2\) after (1.2) is removed.

### 3.2 Fiber trace-free tensors

On a hyperbolic surface the covariant Weitzenbock identity gives
\[
 \langle(\nabla_F^*\nabla_F-2k^2)Q,Q\rangle
 =\|d^{\nabla_F}Q\|^2+\|\delta_FQ\|^2\ge0.              \tag{3.1}
\]
Here the exterior covariant derivative uses the standard exterior-power
norm. Only the nonnegative identity is needed; numerical factors change if
the derivative is instead normed as an unnormalized three-tensor.
See [Delay, Section 2 and Lemma 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0802.3174)
for the same convention, where the surface scalar curvature is \(-2k^2\).

The remaining radial operator is
\(L_s=-\frac12(\partial_t^2+\kappa\partial_t)\), and
\[
 2\sqrt a L_sa^{-1/2}
 =-\partial_t^2+\frac{k^2}{4}
                    (1+\operatorname{sech}^2(kt))
 \ge k^2/4.                                             \tag{3.2}
\]
Consequently this entire tensor sector has lower bound \(k^2/8\), even
when its fiber component is an exact Teichmuller zero mode.
There is no conflict with the nonzero boundary flux in
`round14_shear.md`: that flux belongs to the nonhomogeneous matching
problem, whereas the present estimate imposes zero boundary values.

### 3.3 Traces and mixed tensors

On either factor trace line the operator is
\(\frac12\Delta_{B\times F}+k^2\), with lower bound \(k^2\).
On the mixed bundle it is
\(\frac12(\nabla_B^*\nabla_B+\nabla_F^*\nabla_F)\).
The one-form Bochner identity on each hyperbolic factor is
\[
 \nabla^*\nabla=\Delta_H+k^2.
\]
The Hodge Laplacian has nonnegative quadratic form, including for the
zero-extended Dirichlet forms on the cylinder. Thus the mixed sector also
has lower bound \(k^2\). Combining these facts proves (1.4) on the
circle-invariant subspace.

## 4. Nonzero angular modes: why finite collapsing collars matter

Decompose tensor coefficients in the invariant unit frame into
\(e^{im\phi}\), \(m\in\mathbb Z\). These Fourier subspaces are
orthogonal and preserved by \(P_0\). On a mode \(m\ne0\),
\[
 \nabla_Uq=(im/a)q+\Gamma q,\qquad \|\Gamma\|\le Ck.
\]
Young's inequality therefore gives
\[
 \|\nabla_Uq\|^2\ge
      \frac{m^2}{2a_{\max}^2}\|q\|^2-Ck^2\|q\|^2,
\qquad
 \langle P_0q,q\rangle\ge
   \left(\frac{1}{4a_{\max}^2}-C'k^2\right)\|q\|^2.
                                                        \tag{4.1}
\]
The curvature endomorphism is uniformly bounded. Thus all nonzero angular
modes lie above \(\mu\) for sufficiently small \(a_{\max}\).
This proves the theorem on the full tensor space.

The condition \(a_{\max}\to0\) is essential for this argument. On the
complete hyperbolic funnel the circle becomes large, and nonzero angular
TT modes can also occur. We have not identified the full-line, all-angular
kernel with the two-dimensional axisymmetric kernel.

Nonnegativity on the full Dirichlet space also follows without smallness:
use (3.1) on the cylinder trace-free factor, its fiber counterpart, and the
trace/mixed identities above. These identities are legitimate after zero
extension and approximation by smooth compactly supported tensors.

## 5. The two small Dirichlet eigenvalues

Let \(\chi_R\) be one on \(|t|\le R-1\), zero near the two endpoints,
with fixed derivative bounds. The two trial tensors \(\chi_RH_i\) are
orthogonal, and
\[
 \|\chi_RH_i\|_2^2\asymp c,
 \quad
 \langle P_0(\chi_RH_i),\chi_RH_i\rangle
       \le Cc e^{-3kR}.                                 \tag{5.1}
\]
Indeed the uncut tensors are kernels; integration by parts leaves
\(\frac12\int|d\chi_R|^2|H_i|^2\), and
\(a f^2=c\operatorname{sech}^3(kt)\) on the cutoff bands.
Every linear combination has the same estimate. The min--max principle
gives \(0\le\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le Ce^{-3kR}\).
The codimension-two estimate (1.4) gives \(\lambda_3\ge\mu\).

The twist tensor is locally a Lie derivative: take
\(X=b(t)\partial_\phi\) with \(b'=f/a\). Its two endpoint rotations
differ by \(c^{-1}\int_{-R}^R\operatorname{sech}^3(kt)dt\).
Thus it is not generated by such a vector field vanishing at both marked
ends. Whether a global closed-manifold twist can be removed by a gauge
transformation involves the exterior blocks. The Dirichlet spectral
statement does not assume an answer to that separate question.

## 6. Stability on the actual sheared plateau

On the same collar let
\[
 g_z=dt^2+a^2(d\phi+z(t)\beta)^2+h,
                                                        \tag{6.1}
\]
where the fiber, the smooth one-form \(\beta\), \(z\), and its first
three derivatives have uniform bounds. This includes both the old
compactly supported transition and the smoothed Maxwell profiles of
`round14_shear.md`. Geometric covariant differentiation relative to
\(g_0\) gives
\[
 \|g_z-g_0\|_{C^2(g_0)}\le C a_{\max}.                   \tag{6.2}
\]
In particular (6.2) concerns unit-frame components; it has no hidden
coordinate factor \(a^{-1}\). Angular derivatives of invariant tensors
only introduce the bounded frame connection \(\kappa\).

Put \(\epsilon=a_{\max}\). Comparing the quadratic forms in (1.1),
including their measures and inner products, yields
\[
 |\langle P_{g_z}q,q\rangle_{g_z}
                  -\langle P_0q,q\rangle_0|
 \le C\epsilon\{\|\nabla^0q\|_0^2+\|q\|_0^2\}.
                                                        \tag{6.3}
\]
This follows by comparing inverse metrics, volume, connections and
curvature, which depend on at most two derivatives of (6.2). Also
\(\|\nabla^0q\|^2\le2\langle P_0q,q\rangle+C\|q\|^2\).
Thus for small \(\epsilon\),
\[
 \boxed{\langle P_{g_z}q,q\rangle_{g_z}
       \ge(\mu/2)\|q\|_{g_z}^2
       \quad\hbox{if }\langle q,H_i\rangle_0=0.}        \tag{6.4}
\]
On this codimension-two subspace the form also controls the full
\(H^1\) norm, by (6.3), (6.4), and the preceding gradient bound.
Lax--Milgram therefore provides a uniformly bounded inverse for the
**compressed Dirichlet operator** from the dual of that \(H^1_0\)
subspace to the subspace. This is not an inverse on the whole tensor space.

The min--max proof and (6.3) additionally give
\[
 -C\epsilon\le\lambda_1(P_{g_z})\le
 \lambda_2(P_{g_z})\le C(\epsilon+e^{-3kR}),\qquad
 \lambda_3(P_{g_z})\ge\mu/2.                            \tag{6.5}
\]
Hence exactly two Dirichlet eigenvalues tend to zero; their signs need
not be positive for the approximate background. A disjoint union of
\(m\) such collars has exactly \(2m\) small Dirichlet modes, provided
the finitely many fixed fibers have a positive minimum \(\lambda_1\).

## 7. What this contributes to a full repair problem

Round 13 supplied an explicit almost-kernel but not a complementary
bound. The present proof identifies both radial polarizations and controls
all remaining **Dirichlet collar** modes, including noninvariant ones.
The separate Maxwell calculation determines nonzero residual projections
and exported fiber/scalar flux. These two pieces now fit: eliminating the
Dirichlet complement cannot justify discarding the radial modes or the
boundary response.

To obtain an inverse on the closed manifold one must still couple the
collar Dirichlet-to-Neumann map to the actual adjacent blocks, retain
their Teichmuller and other low modes, and solve the resulting finite or
low-energy system. The current proof does not supply that boundary map,
a uniform maximum-norm inverse on the closed geometry, or a nonlinear
Einstein solution. It supplies a proved local component rather than
postulating a full complement estimate.

The factorization and all-sector argument are given explicitly here;
`round14_collar_check.py` independently checks the radial tensor operator,
both polarizations, and scalar conjugation. The integral inequalities and
min--max argument are analytic proofs, not consequences of those finite
checks.
