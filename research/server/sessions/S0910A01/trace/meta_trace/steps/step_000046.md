## Deep-Reasoning

This Explore step corrected the strategic meaning of the newly consolidated four-dimensional negative Ishida--LeBrun result. The restricted IL theorem remains a substantial mathematical asset: it isolates a local calibration-cost principle, in which a Q-controlled nonnegative density \(\Phi\) cannot have vanishing mean on bounded-curvature noncollapsed charts because the limit would be the forbidden Derdziński Einstein germ. But this step showed that the principle is not a universal four-dimensional negative branch machine. It needs a separate curvature-to-volume budget and a separate obstruction density. Step 45 had transferred the calibrated IL relation \(\operatorname{Vol}/A\to32\pi^2/9\) to arbitrary almost-negative-Einstein 4-manifolds; that transfer is mathematically invalid. Chern--Gauss--Bonnet gives \(\int |\mathrm{Rm}|^2=8\pi^2\chi+\int |\mathrm{Ric}^\circ|^2\), while \(\mathrm{Ric}\approx-g\) controls the scalar and trace-free Ricci terms but does not bound \(\chi/\operatorname{Vol}\) from above in the needed way. Strict Hitchin--Thorpe controls characteristic-number ratios, not a missing geometric volume ratio. The constant computation made this concrete: exact negative Einstein metrics with \(\tau=0\) already have different normalized volume ratios, since real hyperbolic 4-manifolds rescaled to \(\mathrm{Ric}=-g\) satisfy \(\operatorname{Vol}/\chi=12\pi^2\), whereas products of closed hyperbolic surfaces with the product metric satisfy \(\operatorname{Vol}/\chi=4\pi^2\). The IL ratio \((32/9)\pi^2\) is a calibrated relation involving \(A\), not a universal replacement for \(\operatorname{Vol}/\chi\). This restores the right abstraction: Module S should be treated as a smoothing theorem under an explicit mean critical-curvature budget, not as a budget creator for arbitrary topology. The step also withdrew the Step 45 assertion that the universal Hitchin--Thorpe obstruction plus the IL geography covers all known strict-Hitchin--Thorpe no-Einstein examples. Source reading immediately contradicted it. LeBrun's 1998 paper states in its introduction that simplicial-volume and entropy estimates give additional obstructions for spaces with infinite fundamental group, separate from Seiberg--Witten estimates. LeBrun's 1995 paper on four-manifolds without Einstein metrics has a footnote saying Sambusetti used Besson--Courtois--Gallot entropy inequalities to show, for example, that the connected sum of two copies of any real-hyperbolic 4-manifold never admits Einstein metrics while satisfying strict Hitchin--Thorpe. Brunnbauer--Ishida--Suárez-Serrato further summarize the strengthened Gromov--Hitchin--Thorpe inequality \(2\chi-3|\tau|\ge \lambda(X)^4/(54\pi^2)\) for Einstein 4-manifolds, where \(\lambda\) is minimal volume entropy, and explicitly place Gromov, Besson--Courtois--Gallot, Sambusetti, and Kotschick among homotopy-invariant obstruction mechanisms. Thus the broader 4D negative landscape contains at least three structurally different mechanisms: characteristic-number inequalities, smooth-structure-sensitive monopole curvature estimates, and infinite-fundamental-group entropy or simplicial-volume inequalities. These mechanisms do not all produce the same kind of local forbidden profile. Entropy/Sambusetti obstructions appear fundamentally global: for a map of nonzero degree to a locally symmetric target, BCG natural maps give entropy-volume lower bounds and equality rigidity; in the hyperbolic target case, Bessières--Besson--Courtois--Gallot give a Ricci-lower-bound volume gap for \(X\#X\). Combined with Chern--Gauss--Bonnet upper bounds for a hypothetical negative Einstein metric, this yields a contradiction without requiring a calibrated Weyl spectrum on a local chart. This suggests a promising new Yau-26 route: prove quantitative negative Ricci pinching gaps for entropy-obstructed connected sums by combining scaled BCG volume inequalities with the scalar part of Chern--Gauss--Bonnet. It would be a global-volume route rather than an IL-style local calibration route, and it may cover real-hyperbolic connected sums and perhaps broader degree-to-locally-symmetric families. At the same time, it does not settle the full problem: near equality in entropy may reconstruct global maps or coverings, while controlled local Einstein charts alone do not reconstruct a global Einstein metric. The regularization module for the IL theorem also remains live: the current source reading and A03 agreement do not by themselves produce the fixed-\(\varepsilon\) transported DeTurck estimates, common-time smooth compactness, and \(\varepsilon\to0\) passage needed before stationary-collar identities can be used. The research state is therefore sharper: the restricted IL theorem is valuable and should be completed as an intermediate theorem, but broader progress requires comparing obstruction mechanisms on their own terms rather than forcing them into one local-profile template.
