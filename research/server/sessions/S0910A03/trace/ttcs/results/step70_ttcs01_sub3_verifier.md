## Task

Verify the peripheral algebraic reduction for the anisotropic quotient construction. The claim: for a one-cusped orientable hyperbolic 4-manifold piece P with cusp lattice Λ≅Z³ and a primitive character φ:π₁(P)→Z with φ|Λ=dℓ (ℓ primitive, d≥1), the cyclic mod-N cover (N coprime to d) has one cusp with peripheral lattice ker(dℓ mod N) = {v∈Λ: d ℓ(v)≡0 mod N}, which has type (N,1,1) in the basis {ℓ,e₂,e₃}. VERIFY: (1) Is this algebraic claim correct? (2) Does the resulting cover remain a single-cusped manifold (i.e., does the peripheral inclusion being surjective mod N guarantee one cusp)? (3) For the long exact sequence H₁(T³)→H₁(M̄)→H₁(M̄,T³)→0 with Poincaré-Lefschetz H₁(M̄,T³)≅H³(M̄), derive necessary and sufficient conditions on M̄ for the peripheral image in H₁(M̄;Q) to be nonzero. (4) For the Slavich orientable one-cusped 24-cell manifold with H₁≅Z⊕Z₃, determine whether the peripheral image is nonzero.

### 1. Cyclic-cover algebra

Let
\[
G=\pi_1(P),\qquad \Lambda\cong \mathbb Z^3,
\]
and define
\[
q_N:G\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow \mathbb Z/N,
\qquad H_N=\ker q_N.
\]
Primitivity of \(\phi\) means that \(\phi\) is onto, so \(H_N\) defines a connected regular \(N\)-fold cover with deck group \(\mathbb Z/N\).

On \(\Lambda\),
\[
q_N(v)=d\,\ell(v)\pmod N.
\]
If \(\gcd(d,N)=1\), multiplication by \(d\) is invertible modulo \(N\), hence
\[
H_N\cap\Lambda
=\ker(d\ell\bmod N)
=\ker(\ell\bmod N).
\]

There is a minor notation issue: \(\ell\in\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb Z)\) is a covector, not a lattice vector. Choose an adapted basis \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) such that
\[
\ell(e_1)=1,\qquad e_2,e_3\in\ker\ell.
\]
Then
\[
H_N\cap\Lambda
=\langle Ne_1,e_2,e_3\rangle.
\]
Thus its geometric directional type is \((N,1,1)\); its Smith invariant factors are conventionally written \((1,1,N)\).

More generally, if \(g=\gcd(d,N)\), then
\[
H_N\cap\Lambda
=\left\langle \frac{N}{g}e_1,e_2,e_3\right\rangle.
\]

### 2. Number of cusps in the cover

Components above the original cusp are indexed by
\[
H_N\backslash G/\Lambda.
\]
Since \(H_N\triangleleft G\), this set is naturally
\[
(\mathbb Z/N)/q_N(\Lambda).
\]
Now \(q_N(\Lambda)\) is the subgroup generated by \(d\bmod N\), so
\[
\#\{\text{cusps upstairs}\}
=[\mathbb Z/N:q_N(\Lambda)]
=\gcd(d,N).
\]

Therefore:

- if \(\gcd(d,N)=1\), the cover has exactly one cusp;
- its cusp cross-section covers the original one with degree \(N\);
- its peripheral lattice is \(\langle Ne_1,e_2,e_3\rangle\).

Thus surjectivity of the peripheral subgroup onto the deck group does indeed imply that the total preimage of the unique cusp is connected.

### 3. Exact homological condition for a nonzero peripheral image

Let \(i:T^3\hookrightarrow\bar M\). With rational coefficients, the pair sequence contains
\[
H_1(T^3)\xrightarrow{i_*}H_1(\bar M)
\xrightarrow{j}H_1(\bar M,T^3)
\longrightarrow H_0(T^3)\longrightarrow H_0(\bar M).
\]
Both spaces are connected, so the last map is an isomorphism. Hence \(j\) is onto and
\[
\operatorname{im}i_*=\ker j.
\]

Poincaré–Lefschetz duality gives
\[
H_1(\bar M,T^3;\mathbb Q)\cong H^3(\bar M;\mathbb Q),
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{\;
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{im}i_*
=b_1(\bar M)-b_3(\bar M).
\;}
\]

The following statements are consequently equivalent:

\[
\operatorname{im}\bigl(H_1(T^3;\mathbb Q)\to H_1(\bar M;\mathbb Q)\bigr)\ne0,
\]
\[
b_1(\bar M)>b_3(\bar M),
\]
\[
H^1(\bar M;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow H^1(T^3;\mathbb Q)
\quad\text{is nonzero},
\]
and the existence of a primitive integral character
\[
\phi:\pi_1(\bar M)\to\mathbb Z
\]
whose peripheral restriction is nonzero. Such a restriction automatically has the form \(d\ell\), with \(d\ge1\) and \(\ell\) primitive.

### 4. Slavich’s one-cusped example

The supplied datum
\[
H_1(\bar M;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z_3
\]
only gives \(b_1(\bar M)=1\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{im}i_*
=1-b_3(\bar M).
\]
There are two possibilities:

- \(b_3(\bar M)=0\): the peripheral image has rank one, so the desired primitive character exists.
- \(b_3(\bar M)=1\): the peripheral image is zero over \(\mathbb Q\). It may still land in the \(\mathbb Z_3\) summand, but every homomorphism to \(\mathbb Z\) kills that torsion.

Thus \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z_3\) alone does not determine the answer for the Slavich manifold. The supplied files contain neither \(b_3\) nor the abelianized cusp generators.

Two concrete computations would settle this exact example:

1. A cellular homology computation giving \(b_3\). Since \(b_4=0\),

2. If \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) generate the cusp group, abelianize them in
   \[
   H_1(\bar M)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z_3.

   Writing their free coordinates as \(m_1,m_2,m_3\), the peripheral image is nonzero exactly when
   \[
   (m_1,m_2,m_3)\ne(0,0,0).

   \[
