It then passed 33/33 and wrote `Sessions/S0910A01/workspace/data/step70_support_lemma_verify.json`. The restricted IL assembly verifier also passed 30/30 and wrote `Sessions/S0910A01/workspace/data/step68_assembly_verify.json`. These runs do not make the restricted theorem a solution of Yau 26, but they confirm that the recent local regressions in the IL modules have been removed from the active files before using them again.

The main new execution work concerned the graph-neck route. The Strategist and A03 corrected the meaning of the reflection identity: for a Gaussian torus collar with shape operator \(S\), \(H=\operatorname{tr}S\), \(B=S-I\), \(b=H-3\), and \(E=\operatorname{Ric}+3g\), the exact identity is \(e^{2R}b(R)-b(0)+\int_0^R e^{2t}|B|^2dt=-\int_0^R e^{2t}E_{00}dt\). Thus a reflection-center to cusp-end match with \(S(0)=0\) and \(H(R)=3\) requires \(3+\int_0^R e^{2t}|S-I|^2dt=-\int_0^R e^{2t}E_{00}dt\), and any pointwise defect bound must satisfy \(\delta\ge 2(3+\int e^{2t}|S-I|^2dt)/(e^{2R}-1)\). This is stronger and more informative than the earlier shorthand: \(H<3\) gives only a lower bound on the deficit, not convergence to a cusp, because weighted accumulated shear can dominate.

I wrote and ran `Sessions/S0910A01/workspace/scripts/step72_graph_neck_shear_budget.py`, producing `Sessions/S0910A01/workspace/data/step72_graph_neck_shear_budget.json`, and synthesized the result in `Sessions/S0910A01/workspace/notes/step72_graph_neck_weighted_shear_budget.md`. The script measures both the weighted normal balance and the full displayed Ricci residual for A03-style low-mode diagonal \(T^2\)-invariant boundary-value samples. A first version over-counted residuals by measuring unconstrained high Fourier modes in the collocation state; I fixed it by projecting shape variables to the Galerkin mode set before measuring. The corrected results show three important things. First, the normal-equation profile \(S=\tanh(t)I\) has \(E_{00}=0\) and the endpoint deficit needed to force \(H(R)=3\) decays from about \(2.20\times10^{-1}\) at \(R=2\) to \(4.03\times10^{-3}\) at \(R=4\) and \(1.35\times10^{-6}\) at \(R=8\), but it never matches a finite cusp because \(H(R)<3\). Second, adding a compact endpoint patch to force \(H(R)=3\) shows the scalar normal balance alone does not block \(\delta\to0\): for patch length \(L=1\), the measured \(|E_{00}|_\infty\) is about \(2.97\times10^{-1}\) at \(R=2\), \(5.69\times10^{-3}\) at \(R=4\), and \(1.91\times10^{-6}\) at \(R=8\). This eliminates an overly simple no-go interpretation of the shear identity. Third, profiles that remain near the geodesic center for most of the neck and transition only in a fixed final interval keep order-one normalized shear cost as \(R\) grows: for final transition lengths \(L=0.5,1,2\), the costs at \(R=8\) are approximately \(1.63,0.914,0.321\), nearly the same as at \(R=12\). Whole-neck smooth interpolation can make the weighted average small, but its pointwise \(|E_{00}|\) stays around 3, so it cannot give operator-norm Ricci pinching.

The A03-style spatial Dirichlet samples are also now read more sharply. Even when `solve_bvp` converges for the spatial block, the full residual is not small: the anisotropic-expand case at \(R=1\) has full component sup about 23.3, with \(E_{00}=23.3\) and \(E_{01}=5.28\); the round cusp target at \(R=1\) has full sup about \(5.39\times10^3\). These are not approximate Einstein necks. The scientific meaning is that the graph route remains live but cannot be advanced by extending the same spatial-only construction or by relying on neck length alone. The next construction must incorporate the normal and mixed equations, gauge-fixing, reflection parity, and constraint-compatible boundary data together, while recording both \(\|\operatorname{Ric}+3g\|_{\mathrm{op},\infty}\) and the weighted shear balance. I sent this distinction and the file paths to A03 in peer replies/messages #497 and #498.
