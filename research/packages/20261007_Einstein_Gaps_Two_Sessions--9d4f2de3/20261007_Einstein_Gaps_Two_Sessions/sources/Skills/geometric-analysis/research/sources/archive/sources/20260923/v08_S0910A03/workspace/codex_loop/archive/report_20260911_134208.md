# Research Report: normalized fixed-cutoff smooth extension

## Task

This run investigated whether Wang's smooth-extension induction for
\(\partial_tg=-2\chi^2\operatorname{Ric}\) transfers to

\[
\partial_tg=-2a(\operatorname{Ric}+g),\qquad a=\chi^2,
\]

under only
\(\sup_{M\times[0,T)}|a\operatorname{Rm}|<\infty\), with constants allowed
to depend on the individual smooth \(g_0,\chi,T\). This is the last analytic
bridge in the session's restricted four-dimensional negative-branch route;
it does not by itself solve the full Yau problem.

## Verdict

The transfer succeeds on a closed \(M^n\), \(n\ge2\), for finite \(T\).
The earlier specification's treatment of \(-2ag\) as generically “lower
order” was insufficient because the full curvature variation contains
Hessian-cutoff terms. The repair is to shift the curvature by the parallel
constant-curvature tensor

\[
G=\frac1{2(n-1)}g\owedge g,\qquad
\widetilde R=\operatorname{Rm}+G,\qquad U=a\widetilde R.
\]

With the convention fixed in the main derivation,
\(\operatorname{Ric}(G)=g\), so

\[
\operatorname{Ric}(\widetilde R)=\operatorname{Ric}+g,\qquad
\partial_tg=-2\operatorname{Ric}(U).
\]

This exact identity restores Wang's connection/cutoff derivative mechanism.
The shifted evolution has Wang's highest-order structure, while normalization
adds only explicitly estimable algebraic \(G\)-linear terms. Consequently all
\(\nabla^mU\), hence all \(\nabla^m(a\operatorname{Rm})\), are uniformly
bounded; \(g(t)\) converges smoothly at \(T\) and can be restarted.

## Work performed

### Convention and exact variation

I fixed

\[
(h\owedge k)_{ijkl}
=h_{ik}k_{jl}+h_{jl}k_{ik}-h_{il}k_{jk}-h_{jk}k_{il}
\]

and the contraction
\(\operatorname{Ric}(S)_{jl}=g^{ik}S_{ijkl}\). The resulting identities are

\[
\nabla G=0,\qquad \operatorname{Ric}(G)=g,\qquad
\partial_tG=\frac1{n-1}(\partial_tg)\owedge g
=-\frac{2a}{n-1}(\operatorname{Ric}+g)\owedge g.
\]

The derivation also differentiates the inverse metric in
\(\partial_t\operatorname{Ric}(G)\), avoiding a common all-covariant/mixed
index error.

Starting from the exact connection and mixed-curvature variations, I isolated
the component Hessian term

\[
\mathcal H^\ell{}_{kij}
=-g^{\ell p}
(a_{ik}E_{jp}-a_{ip}E_{jk}-a_{jk}E_{ip}+a_{jp}E_{ik}),
\quad E=\operatorname{Ric}+g,
\]

and recorded the full \(da*\nabla E\) component expression. Thus the cutoff
terms are genuinely
\((\nabla^2a)*\operatorname{Ric}(\widetilde R)\) and
\(da*\nabla\operatorname{Ric}(\widetilde R)\), followed by the usual
algebraic and Laplacian terms.

### Shifted and weighted evolution

The reliable all-covariant schematic equation obtained is

\[
(\partial_t-a\Delta)\widetilde R
=a(\widetilde R*\widetilde R+G*\widetilde R)
+\nabla a*\nabla\widetilde R+\nabla^2a*\widetilde R.
\]

Every right-hand term vanishes when \(\widetilde R=0\), as required because a
hyperbolic metric is stationary for the normalized flow for every fixed
cutoff. Differentiation gives, without suppressing the top cutoff order,

\[
\partial_t\nabla^m\widetilde R
=a\Delta\nabla^m\widetilde R
+\sum_{\substack{r+k=m+2\\r\ge1,\ k\le m+1}}
\nabla^ra*\nabla^k\widetilde R
+\nabla^{\le m}a*\nabla^{\le m}\widetilde R*
 \nabla^{\le m}\widetilde R
+\sum_{i+k=m}\nabla^ia*G*\nabla^k\widetilde R.
\]

In particular, \(\nabla^{m+2}a*\widetilde R\) is present.

For \(U=a\widetilde R\), the derivative evolution is Wang's Lemma 4.5
structure plus

\[
\mathcal N_m=\sum_{i+j=m}\nabla^ia*G*\nabla^jU.
\]

The four raw high terms are

\[
\chi\nabla^{m+2}\chi*U,\quad
\nabla\chi*\nabla^{m+1}\chi*U,\quad
\chi\nabla^{m+1}\chi*\nabla U,\quad
\chi\nabla\chi*\nabla^{m+1}U.
\]

The cutoff itself satisfies the same triangular equation as in Wang:

\[
\partial_t|\nabla^m\chi|
\le C_m\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}|\nabla^rU|\,|\nabla^{m-r}\chi|.
\]

### Base \(L^p\) estimate

The curvature hypothesis bounds \(U\) and \(a(\operatorname{Ric}+g)\), hence
gives uniform metric equivalence, volume control, Sobolev control, and a
uniform first derivative bound for the fixed cutoff. Testing the shifted
equation against
\(|\widetilde R|^{p-2}\widetilde R\), the apparent
\(\nabla^2a\) requirement is removed by one integration by parts. Young's
inequality uses the nonsingular identity

\[
\frac{|\nabla a|^2}{a}=4|\nabla\chi|^2
\]

with its continuous interpretation on \(\{\chi=0\}\). This gives

\[
\frac d{dt}\int|\widetilde R|^p
+c_p\int a|\nabla\widetilde R|^2|\widetilde R|^{p-2}
\le C_p\int|\widetilde R|^p,
\]

so \(\widetilde R\) has the required unweighted \(L^p\) base bound without
assuming \(\nabla^2\chi\).

### Full triangular induction and the \(G\)-terms

Writing \(A_m=\|\nabla^mU\|_{L^p}\),
\(B_m=\|\nabla^m\widetilde R\|_{L^p}\), and
\(C_m=\|\nabla^m\chi\|_{L^p}\), with sufficiently large Hölder exponents run
in parallel, the closing order is

\[
A_{m-1}\longrightarrow C_m\longrightarrow B_{m-1}
\longrightarrow A_m.
\]

The \(m=1\) weighted estimate and the \(m=2\) cutoff equation form Wang's
coupled \((\nabla U,\nabla^2\chi)\) Gronwall base. For the general weighted
step, the grouped integrations by parts in Wang Proposition 4.14 apply
verbatim because the high-cutoff contraction structure is unchanged. They
reduce the first three high terms to diffusion-absorbable pieces and the
single residual product

\[
\nabla^2\chi*\nabla^m\chi*U*
\nabla^mU|\nabla^mU|^{p-2},
\]

which is controlled from the current triangular inputs. The fourth high term
is absorbed directly by weighted diffusion.

The new normalization term was not merely labeled lower order. If its
\(U\)-derivative order is \(m\), its tested integral is at most
\(C\|\nabla^mU\|_p^p\). If its \(U\)-derivative order is below \(m\), Hölder
and Young bound it by \(C(1+\|\nabla^mU\|_p^p)\) using already available
cutoff and weighted-curvature derivatives. The analogous unweighted
\(G\)-terms satisfy the same estimates and require no cutoff derivative above
\(m+1\). Thus normalization adds no node or backward edge to the induction.

Uniform Sobolev applied after the all-\(p\), all-order induction yields
\(\sup|\nabla^mU|<\infty\) for every \(m\).

### Extension and normalized DeTurck restart

Because \(\nabla G=0\),

\[
\nabla^m(a\operatorname{Rm})=\nabla^mU-(\nabla^ma)*G,
\]

so all weighted ordinary-curvature derivatives are bounded. Also
\(\nabla^m\partial_tg\) is a contraction of \(\nabla^mU\). Wang's
connection-difference/Hamilton extension argument therefore gives a smooth
terminal metric.

The correct gauge system transports the coefficient in front of both terms:

\[
\partial_t\widehat g
=-2\widehat a(\operatorname{Ric}(\widehat g)+\widehat g)
+\mathcal L_{\widehat aW}\widehat g,\qquad
\widehat a=a\circ\phi^{-1},\qquad
\partial_t\phi\circ\phi^{-1}=-\widehat aW.
\]

For \(g=\phi^*\widehat g\), the Lie terms cancel and
\(\phi^*\widehat a=a\), recovering the fixed spatial coefficient in the
original normalized equation. Yang's degenerate local Ricci--DeTurck
construction still applies: the added
\(-2\widehat a\,\widehat g\) is zeroth order and changes neither the principal
symbol nor the transported-coefficient equation.
