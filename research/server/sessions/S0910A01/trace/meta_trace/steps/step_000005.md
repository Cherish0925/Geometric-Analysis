Second, the Kähler-Einstein exclusion and Armstrong-LeBrun smooth-convergence contradiction were established. More decisively: if a near-equality sequence converged smoothly, the limit would be Einstein (from Ric convergence), would saturate the mixed estimate (D_a = 0), would be almost-Kähler with ω in the lowest W⁺ eigenspace (LeBrun Prop 2.4), would therefore be KE by Armstrong's theorem (Corollary 2.5), but KE requires |W⁺|² = 2/3 while the near-equality target is 1/24—a contradiction. S0910A03 reached the same conclusion independently.

AAD's compact integrability theorem (Gray's third condition → Kähler) uses unique continuation for the Nijenhuis tensor, opposite almost-Hermitian structure, and complex surface classification—qualitative tools without quantitative stability estimates. Li-Zhang's W⁺ middle-eigenvalue condition λ₂ ≥ −S/12 applies only for S > 0 (the condition fails for negative KE). Therefore existing rigidity tools do not directly bridge the gap between near-equality and exact equality.

The research is now at a structural decision point. The near-equality regime is characterized to high precision. The escape must involve non-smooth degeneration: collapse, curvature concentration, or ALE bubbling. Three routes compete for the next investment: (A) Weitzenböck/spinor compactness using the SW equation under Ricci pinching, which could provide the C^α regularity needed to apply Armstrong's rigidity on most of the manifold; (B) quantitative Taubes exceptional-sphere area estimates, which could give a direct contradiction if the Gromov energy quantum stays bounded; (C) the positive branch, where noncollapsing is free from Myers' theorem and Cheeger-Gromov compactness is more standard. The positive branch may be more tractable for an existence proof (either sign suffices for the proposition), while the negative branch has the deepest structural understanding.

The most promising single advance would be a compactness argument showing that the SW spinor equation plus Ricci pinching forces C^α regularity of the spinor norm (hence of the almost-Kähler structure) away from a discrete singular set. This would reduce the problem to Einstein orbifold desingularization, where the Ozuch obstruction theory applies.
