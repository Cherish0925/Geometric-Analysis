## Deep-Reasoning

Step 6 advanced the positive-branch side of Yau Problem 26 by replacing two misleading Step-5 proposed computations with sharper discriminators. The exact target remains unchanged: a proof of one sign branch in every dimension would prove the original interval proposition, while any refutation must defeat both sign branches in one dimension. The positive branch is still attractive because Ric≈+g gives Myers diameter, volume upper bound, b1=0, finite fundamental group, and in dimensions 2 and 3 existing structure theorems solve the branch. But Step 5 was right to retract the false global noncollapsed convergence theorem: bounded Ricci plus noncollapse does not give a global harmonic-radius bound or same-topology Einstein metric in dimensions at least 4. This step sharpened that retraction rather than leaving it vague. Cheeger-Naber gives measure-theoretic regular patches under bounded Ricci and noncollapse in dimension 4, so one can extract local Einstein limits where harmonic radius is definite and Ric-g tends to zero; but this does not recover topology, remove bubbles, or solve the Yau26 existence problem. Naber-Zhang gives collapsed bounded-Ricci harmonic-radius and topology control on local covers under maximal nilpotency rank, but pointwise curvature bounds require Einstein or ∇Ric control. The essential obstacle is therefore structural and PDE-level: a pointwise algebraic Ricci pinching Ric=g+E with |E|≤δ is not an elliptic Einstein equation and gives no derivative control on Ricci or Weyl. The computational/model advance was the Kaluza-Klein correction to the collapse experiment. Fixed-connection fibre shrinkage is already known to force δ_+→1 and is irrelevant because round lens spaces collapse with δ_+=0. The pinching-compatible principal-circle scaling is phi=tF=O(1). In the constant-fibre-length connection metric model g=π*g_B+t^2 alpha^2 over a closed 3-dimensional base, O'Neill/Kaluza-Klein formulas with standard form norm give Ric(U,U)=|phi|²/2, Ric_hor=Ric_B minus one half phi∘phi, and Ric_mix=±d*phi/2. Writing phi=*b, exact Ric=g would force |b|²=2, db=0, δb=0, and Ric_B=2g_B minus one half b⊗b. Then b is a nonzero harmonic one-form and Ric_B(b,b)=|b|²>0, contradicting Bochner on the closed base. The pinched version gives a quantitative contradiction for this constant-length free-circle model when δ<1/(1+sqrt2)≈0.414213562. This does not prove all smooth free collapse impossible, because nonconstant fibre length, mean-curvature, dilaton, and inhomogeneous collapse terms remain unanalyzed, but it shows the Step-5 fixed-connection catalogue was the wrong direction and that the simplest pinching-compatible free circle model is also obstructed. Together with χ(M)=0 for global free S1 actions versus χ>0 under tight positive four-dimensional CGB accounting, this moves the four-dimensional positive-collapse enemy toward singular orbit strata, fixed-point strata, orbifold quotient collapse, curvature-concentration caps, or more general warped free-collapse equations. The normalization repairs also matter: round S4 with Ric=g has radius sqrt(3) and volume 24π²≈236.8705, not the unit-radius value 8π²/3≈26.3189. It saturates beta(0)Vol=8π²χ exactly. A cheap rotational ansatz search for S4 metrics with Ricci eigenvalues in [0.9,1.1] found only the near-constant-curvature rescaling endpoint, with δ≈0.1001 and volume≈195.93, far from volume collapse; this is only ansatz-level falsification, not a theorem. The ALE energy normalization was also fixed: Ricci-flat ALE bubbles carry the orbifold Gauss-Bonnet defect (1/8π²)∫|Rm|²=χ(X)-1/|Γ|, so Eguchi-Hanson contributes 12π², not 8π². Overall, this step did not prove or refute Yau26, but it refined the positive-branch research object: regular patches are locally Einstein but globally insufficient; constant-length regular free circle collapse is obstructed; four-dimensional collapse must be coupled to concentration or singular strata; and the missing bridge is an approximate-pinching analogue of Einstein epsilon-regularity/gluing or a new collapse-compatibility theorem.
