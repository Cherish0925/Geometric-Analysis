# Per-datum existence for the degenerate cutoff flow

Session: S0910A01. Agent2 / Constructor. Step 65, Execute.

This note proves the per-datum existence theorem for the exact degenerate
cutoff flow on a closed manifold.  It is logically upstream of the
Ishida–LeBrun obstruction apparatus: neither Ricci pinching, Seiberg–Witten
calibration, nor the geometric first-exit estimates are used.

The construction follows A03 Step 56 (`S0910A03/workspace/notes/
step56_tame_direct_flow_repair.md`) for the tame hierarchy and
regularization scheme, with three additions: (i) the fixed-parameter
existence is pinned to a specific standard quasilinear parabolic theorem
with explicit strong-parabolicity verification, (ii) the normalization sign
$\sigma$ is general, and (iii) all conventions use the canonical
shifted-curvature from `workspace/notes/step64_convention_consistency_sweep.md`.

---

## 1. Setting and conventions

### 1.1 Manifold and data

$(M^n,\bar g)$ is a closed (compact, no boundary) smooth Riemannian
manifold of dimension $n\ge2$.  Fix a smooth positive metric $g_0$ on $M$
and a smooth function $\chi:M\to[0,1]$.  Fix $\sigma\in\mathbb R$.

### 1.2 Canonical curvature convention

With Hamilton's Ricci contraction $R_{jk}=g^{il}R_{ijkl}$ and the
standard Kulkarni–Nomizu product
\[
(\varphi\owedge\psi)_{ijkl}
=\varphi_{ik}\psi_{jl}+\varphi_{jl}\psi_{ik}
-\varphi_{il}\psi_{jk}-\varphi_{jk}\psi_{il},
\tag{1.1}
\]
define the constant-curvature reference tensor and shifted curvature
\[
G=\frac{g\owedge g}{2(n-1)},
\qquad
T=\operatorname{Rm}-G.
\tag{1.2}
\]
Then $\operatorname{Ric}(G)=-g$, $\operatorname{Ric}(T)=\operatorname{Ric}+g$,
and $\nabla T=\nabla\operatorname{Rm}$.  The operator-normalized curvature
norm gives $|G|^2=n/(2(n-1))$.  These conventions appear in the scope
section but play no role in the existence proof itself: the per-datum theorem
concerns only the metric $G$ and scalar $\beta$, not the shifted curvature.

### 1.3 The geometric equation

The degenerate cutoff flow is
\[
\partial_tg=-2\chi^2(\operatorname{Ric}_g-\sigma g),
\qquad g(0)=g_0.
\tag{1.3}
\]
For $\sigma=-1$, this is $\partial_tg=-2\chi^2(\operatorname{Ric}+g)$,
the negative-Einstein normalization.  For $\sigma>0$ (resp. $\sigma=0$),
the stationary condition is positive-Einstein (resp. Ricci-flat).  The
velocity vanishes identically on $\{\chi=0\}$.

## 2. Transported system and doubly regularized approximation

### 2.1 DeTurck gauge

Fix the smooth background metric $\bar g$ and its Levi-Civita connection
$D=\nabla^{\bar g}$.  For a positive metric $G$ on $M$, define the DeTurck
vector field
\[
W^k(G)=G^{ab}\bigl(\Gamma(G)^k_{ab}-\Gamma(\bar g)^k_{ab}\bigr).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Let $\varphi_t$ be the one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms generated by
$-\beta^2 W(G)$:
\[
\partial_t\varphi_t=-\beta(t,\varphi_t)^2\,W(G)(t,\varphi_t),
\qquad\varphi_0=\operatorname{id}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Setting $G=(\varphi_t^{-1})^*g$ and $\beta=\chi\circ\varphi_t^{-1}$,
the geometric equation (1.3) transforms to the transported-cutoff DeTurck
system
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\partial_tG&=\beta^2\bigl[G^{-1}D^2G+R(G,\bar g)+Q(G,DG)+2\sigma G\bigr]
  +2\operatorname{sym}\bigl(d(\beta^2)\otimes W(G)^\flat_G\bigr),\\
\partial_t\beta&=\beta^2W(G)\cdot D\beta,
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
where $R(G,\bar g)$ is algebraic in $G$ and $\bar{\operatorname{Rm}}$, and
$Q(G,DG)$ is quadratic in $DG$ with coefficients smooth in $G^{\pm1}$.
The principal part of the metric equation is
$\beta^2 G^{ab}D_aD_bG_{ij}$, which degenerates where $\beta=0$.  The
scalar equation is first-order and therefore not parabolic on its own.

### 2.2 Doubly regularized system

For $\varepsilon,\nu\in(0,1]$, define
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\partial_tG&=(\beta^2+\varepsilon)
  \bigl[G^{-1}D^2G+R(G,\bar g)+Q(G,DG)\bigr]
  +2\sigma\beta^2G\\
  &\quad+2\operatorname{sym}\bigl(d(\beta^2)\otimes W(G)^\flat_G\bigr),\\
\partial_t\beta&=\beta^2W(G)\cdot D\beta+\nu\bar\Delta\beta.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
Relative to (2.3), two changes have been made:

(a) The metric principal coefficient $\beta^2$ is replaced by
$\beta^2+\varepsilon$, and the same factor multiplies $R$ and $Q$.  This
makes the metric block uniformly elliptic.  The extra
$\varepsilon(R+Q)$ vanishes in the $\varepsilon\to0$ limit.

(b) A viscosity term $\nu\bar\Delta\beta$ is added.  This makes the scalar
equation parabolic.  It vanishes in the $\nu\to0$ limit.

The zero-order term $2\sigma\beta^2 G$ is kept with $\beta^2$ (not
$\beta^2+\varepsilon$) to preserve the structure of the geometric equation;
the difference $2\sigma\varepsilon G$ could equally be absorbed into either
form without affecting the estimates.

### 2.3 Maximum principle for $\beta$

At a spatial maximum of $\beta$, $D\beta=0$ and $\bar\Delta\beta\le0$, so
\[
\partial_t\beta=\beta^2W\cdot 0+\nu(\le0)\le0.
\]
At a spatial minimum, $D\beta=0$ and $\bar\Delta\beta\ge0$, giving
$\partial_t\beta\ge0$.  Therefore, for smooth solutions of (2.4),
\[
0\le\beta(t)\le 1
\qquad\text{whenever }0\le\beta(0)=\chi\le1.
\tag{2.5}
\]
This holds for every $(\varepsilon,\nu)\in(0,1]^2$ and is inherited by
the limit.

## 3. Strong parabolicity of the regularized system

### 3.1 Principal symbol

Write the unknown as $u=(G_{ij},\beta)$, with $G_{ij}$ symmetric
($N=n(n+1)/2$ components) and $\beta$ scalar ($1$ component).  In local
coordinates where $\bar g_{ab}=\delta_{ab}$ at a point,  the second-order
terms in (2.4) are:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{metric block:}\quad
  &(\beta^2+\varepsilon)\,G^{ab}\,\partial_a\partial_bG_{ij}
  +\text{(lower order in }G,\beta,DG,D\beta\text{)},\\
\text{scalar block:}\quad
  &\nu\,\bar g^{ab}\,\partial_a\partial_b\beta
  +\text{(lower order in }G,\beta,DG,D\beta\text{)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Cross-coupling at second order: the metric equation has no
$\partial^2\beta$ term (the $d(\beta^2)$ Lie term is first-order in $\beta$),
and the scalar equation has no $\partial^2G$ term ($W\cdot D\beta$ involves
only first derivatives of both $G$ and $\beta$).  Hence the principal symbol
is **diagonal**:
\[
\sigma_{\rm princ}(\xi)
=\operatorname{diag}\Bigl(
  (\beta^2+\varepsilon)\,G^{ab}\xi_a\xi_b\cdot I_{N},\;
  \nu\,\bar g^{ab}\xi_a\xi_b
\Bigr).
\tag{3.2}
\]

### 3.2 Strong parabolicity verification

**Metric block.**  Since $G$ is positive definite, its eigenvalues relative
to $\bar g$ lie in an interval $[\lambda,\lambda^{-1}]$ for some
$\lambda=\lambda(G)>0$.  Hence
\[
G^{ab}\xi_a\xi_b\ge\lambda\,|\xi|^2_{\bar g},
\qquad
(\beta^2+\varepsilon)\,G^{ab}\xi_a\xi_b\ge\varepsilon\lambda\,|\xi|^2_{\bar g}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

**Scalar block.**
\[
\nu\,\bar g^{ab}\xi_a\xi_b\ge\nu\,|\xi|^2_{\bar g}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

**Combined Legendre–Hadamard condition.**  For $\xi\in T^*_xM\setminus\{0\}$
and $(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathbb R^N\times\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$,
\[
\sigma_{\rm princ}(\xi)\bigl[(\eta,\zeta),(\eta,\zeta)\bigr]
\ge\min(\varepsilon\lambda,\,\nu)\,
  |\xi|^2_{\bar g}\,\bigl(|\eta|^2+|\zeta|^2\bigr)
>0.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Thus, for each fixed $(\varepsilon,\nu)\in(0,1]^2$ and each positive metric
$G$ with $\lambda\bar g\le G\le\lambda^{-1}\bar g$, the system (2.4)
satisfies the strong Legendre–Hadamard (strong parabolicity) condition with
ellipticity constant $\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\nu,\lambda}=\min(\varepsilon\lambda,\nu)>0$.

### 3.3 Named existence theorem

**Theorem A (Quasilinear strongly parabolic systems on closed manifolds).**
*Let $M$ be a closed smooth manifold and consider a quasilinear system*
\[
\partial_tu^I=a^{IJ,ab}(x,u,Du)\,D_aD_bu^J+f^I(x,u,Du)
\tag{A}
\]
*with smooth coefficients satisfying the strong Legendre–Hadamard condition
on an open admissible domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^{N+1}$.  For smooth
initial data $u_0$ with $(u_0(x),Du_0(x))\in\Omega$ for all $x\in M$,
there exists a maximal time $T_{\max}>0$ and a unique smooth solution
$u\in C^\infty(M\times[0,T_{\max}))$.  If $T_{\max}<\infty$, then the
solution leaves every compact subset of the admissible domain; equivalently,
either the pointwise state $(u,Du)$ approaches $\partial\Omega$ or
$\|u(t)\|_{C^k}\to\infty$ for some finite $k$.*

This is a standard result in the theory of quasilinear parabolic equations.
In the geometric PDE literature, it is contained in the local
well-posedness theory for the Ricci–DeTurck flow (DeTurck 1983, Hamilton
1982 §6) and its generalizations to coupled metric-scalar systems
(Hamilton 1993).  In the abstract PDE setting, it follows from
Amann (1995, *Linear and Quasilinear Parabolic Problems*, Vol. I,
Birkhäuser, Ch. V) or Taylor (2011, *PDE III*, §16.1).  The closed-manifold
case avoids boundary regularity; the proof reduces to Sobolev energy
estimates, a contraction argument in $H^s$ for $s>n/2+2$, and standard
parabolic Schauder estimates for smoothing.

### 3.4 Application to the regularized system

**Hypotheses of Theorem A for system (2.4).**

1. *Principal coefficients smooth in $(G,DG,\beta,D\beta)$:*
   $(\beta^2+\varepsilon)G^{ab}$ is smooth in $(G,\beta)$, and
   $\nu\bar g^{ab}$ is constant.  $\checkmark$

2. *Strong Legendre–Hadamard:* Verified in (3.5) with
   $\Lambda=\min(\varepsilon\lambda,\nu)>0$.  $\checkmark$

3. *Admissible domain:* $\Omega=\{G\text{ positive definite}\}\times\mathbb R
   =\{\lambda(G)>0\}$, open.  $\checkmark$

4. *Smooth initial data:* $(G(0),\beta(0))=(g_0,\chi)$ is smooth with
   $g_0$ positive definite.  $\checkmark$

5. *Lower-order terms smooth:* $R(G,\bar g)$, $Q(G,DG)$, $2\sigma\beta^2G$,
   $\operatorname{sym}(d(\beta^2)\otimes W^\flat)$, and $\beta^2W\cdot D\beta$
   are smooth in $(G,DG,\beta,D\beta)$ and the fixed background
   $(\bar g,\bar\Gamma,\bar{\operatorname{Rm}})$.  $\checkmark$

**Conclusion.**  For each $(\varepsilon,\nu)\in(0,1]^2$, Theorem A gives
a unique smooth maximal solution
$(G^{(\varepsilon,\nu)},\beta^{(\varepsilon,\nu)})$ of (2.4) on
$M\times[0,T_{\max}(\varepsilon,\nu))$.  If $T_{\max}<\infty$, then
$G$ ceases to be positive definite or a $C^k$ norm diverges.

## 4. Regularization-uniform tame hierarchy

### 4.1 Energy and dissipation

Choose
\[
s_0=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor+5,
\tag{4.1}
\]
so that $H^{s_0}(M)\hookrightarrow C^4(M)$ by Sobolev embedding.  For
$m\ge s_0$, define
\[
\mathcal E_m=\|G\|_{H^m}^2+A_m\|\beta\|_{H^m}^2,
\qquad
\mathcal D_m^G=\sum_{k\le m}\int_M(\beta^2+\varepsilon)|D^{k+1}G|^2\,d\mu_{\bar g},
\qquad
\mathcal D_m^\beta=\nu\|D^{m+1}\beta\|_2^2,
\tag{4.2}
\]
where $A_m>0$ are fixed coupling constants and all norms use $\bar g$.

### 4.2 Tame estimate (Proposition 3.1 of A03 Step 56)

On any time interval where $G$ stays in a fixed metric neighborhood
$\lambda\bar g\le G\le\lambda^{-1}\bar g$ with
$\|G\|_{H^{s_0}}+\|\beta\|_{H^{s_0}}\le R$, every smooth solution of (2.4)
satisfies, for all $m\ge s_0$,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal E_m+c_{m,\lambda}\mathcal D_m^G
+c_m\mathcal D_m^\beta
\le C_{m,R,|\sigma|}(1+\mathcal E_m).
\tag{4.3}
\]

**The constants $C_{m,R,|\sigma|}$ and $c_{m,\lambda}$ are independent of
$(\varepsilon,\nu)\in(0,1]^2$.**

This is the central property of the regularization.  The proof is given
in A03 Step 56 §§3.1–3.4 and is verified independently below; only the
$\sigma$-extension is new.

### 4.3 Verification of $(\varepsilon,\nu)$-independence

Every instance where $\varepsilon$ appears in the principal coefficient
is handled by the ratio identity
\[
\frac{\beta^{2k}}{(\beta^2+\varepsilon)^j}\le1
\qquad\text{for }k\ge j\ge0\text{ and }\beta\in[0,1].
\tag{4.4}
\]
Specifically:

- **Principal metric term (A03 §3.1).**  The first coefficient derivative
  is $DA=2\beta D\beta\cdot G^{-1}+(\beta^2+\varepsilon)D(G^{-1})$.
  Weighted Young uses $\beta^2/(\beta^2+\varepsilon)\le1$.  No inverse $\varepsilon$.

- **Lie cross term (A03 §3.3).**  The critical coupling (3.9) involves
  $\beta D^{m+1}\beta$ paired with $D^mG$ and $W(G)$.  Integration by
  parts moves the top derivative to produce $\beta D^{m+1}G$, absorbed by
  $\mathcal D_m^G$ via weighted Young:
  \[
  |I_{\rm top}|\le\zeta\int(\beta^2+\varepsilon)|D^{m+1}G|^2
  +C_\zeta\int\frac{\beta^2}{\beta^2+\varepsilon}|D^m\beta|^2|W|^2.
  \]
  The weight is $\beta^2/(\beta^2+\varepsilon)\le1$, not $\beta^4$.
  No inverse $\varepsilon$.

- **Transport coupling (A03 §3.4, eq. 3.12).**  The scalar equation's
  highest metric coupling $\int D^m\beta\cdot\beta^2 A(G)D^{m+1}G\cdot D\beta$
  absorbs into $\mathcal D_m^G$ with weight
  $\beta^4/(\beta^2+\varepsilon)\le\beta^2\le1$.  This is the term that
  uses the fourth power; it is distinct from the Lie cross term above.

- **Transport and viscosity (A03 §3.4).**  $\beta^2W\cdot D\beta$ has no
  $\varepsilon$ dependence.  The viscosity contributes
  $-\nu\|D^{m+1}\beta\|^2+C_m\nu\|\beta\|_{H^m}^2$; since $\nu\le1$,
  the lower-order term is bounded by $C_m\|\beta\|_{H^m}^2$.

- **Sign-general zero-order term.**  The $+2\sigma\beta^2G$ term contributes
  \[
  \bigl|\langle D^mG,\,D^m(2\sigma\beta^2G)\rangle\bigr|
  \le C_{m,R}|\sigma|(1+\mathcal E_m)
  \tag{4.5}
  \]
  by the Moser–Hamilton tame product estimate (A03 eq. 2.3) and
  $\|\beta\|_{L^\infty}\le1$.  No $(\varepsilon,\nu)$ dependence.

### 4.4 Common per-datum time interval

At $m=s_0$, the inequality (4.3) holds with the positivity constraint
checked dynamically.  The time-derivative bound
\[
\|\partial_tG\|_{H^{s_0-2}}+\|\partial_t\beta\|_{H^{s_0-2}}
\le P_{s_0,|\sigma|}(1+\mathcal E_{s_0}),
\tag{4.6}
\]
(using only $0<\varepsilon,\nu\le1$, never dividing by them), together with
Sobolev embedding ($s_0-2>n/2$ gives $H^{s_0-2}\hookrightarrow C^0$) and
the initial positivity $G(0)=g_0\ge\lambda_0\bar g$, yields
\[
\|G(t)-g_0\|_{C^0}\le CK_R\,t,
\]
so $G(t)\ge(\lambda_0/2)\bar g$ for $t\le T_0$ with $T_0$ chosen so that
$CK_RT_0\le\lambda_0/2$.  The upper eigenvalue bound follows from
$H^{s_0}\hookrightarrow C^0$.  An ODE comparison on $\mathcal E_{s_0}$
(with $(\varepsilon,\nu)$-independent coefficients) gives a uniform
bound $\mathcal E_{s_0}(t)\le R^2$ on $[0,T_0]$, where
\[
T_0=T_0(g_0,\chi,\bar g,\sigma,n)>0
\tag{4.7}
\]
depends only on the smooth initial data, the background, $|\sigma|$, and $n$.

**All-order continuation argument.**  Suppose for contradiction that
$T_{\max}(\varepsilon,\nu)<T_0$ for some fixed $(\varepsilon,\nu)$.  On the
interval $[0,T_{\max})$, the $s_0$-level bound and metric positivity are
maintained.  For every $m>s_0$, the tame inequality (4.3) then applies with
coefficient $C_{m,R,|\sigma|}$ controlled by the fixed $s_0$-level bound.
Grönwall gives
\[
\sup_{t<T_{\max}}\mathcal E_m(t)
\le(\mathcal E_m(0)+1)\exp(C_{m,R,|\sigma|}T_0)-1.
\tag{4.7a}
\]
Since the initial data are smooth, $\mathcal E_m(0)<\infty$ for every $m$,
so all $H^m$ norms stay bounded.  Sobolev embedding gives
$\|u(t)\|_{C^k}\le C_k$ for all $k$.  Together with metric positivity,
this means the solution at $t\uparrow T_{\max}$ has a smooth, positive-definite
limit, from which Theorem A restarts the flow — contradicting
$T_{\max}$ being maximal.  Therefore
\[
T_{\max}(\varepsilon,\nu)\ge T_0
\qquad\text{for all }(\varepsilon,\nu)\in(0,1]^2.
\tag{4.8}
\]
The solution exists on $[0,T_0)$ and the all-order bound gives a smooth
limit at $t=T_0$; the closed interval $[0,T_0]$ is obtained by restarting
once more and restricting.

### 4.5 Higher-order propagation on the common interval

For any $m>s_0$, on the interval $[0,T_0]$ the coefficient $C_{m,R,|\sigma|}$
in (4.3) is determined by the $s_0$-level bound $R$ and the smooth initial
data.  Grönwall gives the regularization-uniform bound
\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T_0}\mathcal E_m(t)
\le (\mathcal E_m(0)+1)\exp(C_{m,R,|\sigma|}T_0)-1
=:R_m^2.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Thus every spatial Sobolev level is controlled on the same per-datum interval,
with constants depending on the datum and on the chosen order, but not on
$(\varepsilon,\nu)$.

## 5. Limit $(\varepsilon,\nu)\to(0,0)$

### 5.1 Convergence

From (2.4), (4.9), and induction on time derivatives (using the equation
and higher spatial bounds, never dividing by $\varepsilon$ or $\nu$),
\[
\|D^k_t D^j_x G^{(\varepsilon,\nu)}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|D^k_t D^j_x\beta^{(\varepsilon,\nu)}\|_{L^\infty}
\le C_{k,j}(g_0,\chi,\bar g,\sigma)
\tag{5.1}
\]
for all $k,j\ge0$, uniformly on $M\times[0,T_0]$.  By Arzelà–Ascoli, a
diagonal subsequence $(\varepsilon_\ell,\nu_\ell)\to(0,0)$ converges in
$C^\infty(M\times[0,T_0])$ to a smooth pair $(G_\infty,\beta_\infty)$.

### 5.2 Limit equation

The regularization residuals satisfy
\[
\varepsilon_\ell\bigl[G_\ell^{-1}D^2G_\ell+R(G_\ell)+Q(G_\ell,DG_\ell)\bigr]
\to0,
\qquad
\nu_\ell\bar\Delta\beta_\ell\to0
\tag{5.2}
\]
in every $C^k$ norm (bounded by $\varepsilon_\ell C_k$ and $\nu_\ell C_k$
respectively).  Thus $(G_\infty,\beta_\infty)$ is a smooth solution of the
exact transported-cutoff DeTurck system (2.3) on $M\times[0,T_0]$.

### 5.3 Maximum principle in the limit

Since $0\le\beta^{(\varepsilon,\nu)}(t)\le1$ for every
$(\varepsilon,\nu)$ by (2.5), the $C^0$ convergence gives
\[
0\le\beta_\infty(t)\le1\qquad\text{on }M\times[0,T_0].
\tag{5.3}
\]

### 5.4 Geometric pullback

With $G=G_\infty$ and $\beta=\beta_\infty$ smooth on $[0,T_0]$, the
DeTurck ODE
\[
\partial_t\varphi(t,x)=-\beta(t,\varphi(t,x))^2\,W(G)(t,\varphi(t,x)),
\qquad\varphi(0,x)=x
\tag{5.4}
\]
has a unique smooth solution $\varphi_t:M\to M$ on $[0,T_0]$ (standard ODE
on a compact manifold with smooth vector field).  Then
$\beta(t,\varphi_t(x))=\chi(x)$ by the transport identity, and
$g(t)=\varphi_t^*G(t)$ satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\partial_tg=-2\chi^2(\operatorname{Ric}_g-\sigma g),
\qquad g(0)=g_0,
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
on $M\times[0,T_0]$.

## 6. Main theorem

**Theorem (Per-datum existence for the degenerate cutoff flow).**
*Let $(M^n,\bar g)$ be a closed Riemannian manifold ($n\ge2$), $g_0$ a
smooth positive metric on $M$, $\chi:M\to[0,1]$ smooth, and
$\sigma\in\mathbb R$.  There exist*

- *a time $T_0=T_0(g_0,\chi,\bar g,\sigma,n)>0$,*
- *a smooth metric $g(t)$ on $M\times[0,T_0]$,*
- *a smooth family of diffeomorphisms $\varphi_t:M\to M$ with $\varphi_0=\operatorname{id}$,*

*such that:*

1. $g$ *solves* $\partial_tg=-2\chi^2(\operatorname{Ric}_g-\sigma g)$
   *with* $g(0)=g_0$.
2. $G=(\varphi_t^{-1})^*g$ *and* $\beta=\chi\circ\varphi_t^{-1}$
   *satisfy the transported system (2.3).*
3. $0\le\beta(t)\le1$.
4. *For every integer $m\ge0$,*
   $\|G(t)\|_{H^m}+\|\beta(t)\|_{H^m}\le C_m(g_0,\chi,\bar g,\sigma)$
   *on $[0,T_0]$.*

*The construction uses no Ricci pinching, curvature scale, topological data,
or IL first-exit estimates.  Uniqueness of $g(t)$ as a solution of (1.3) is
not asserted; the IL application requires only existence and restart.*

## 7. Restart and autonomous splice

**Corollary (Restart from smooth endpoint).**  If $(g,\varphi)$ is a smooth
solution of the system above on $[0,T]$ for some $T>0$, then the theorem
applied to the datum $(g(T),\chi,\bar g,\sigma,n)$ produces a smooth
extension to $[0,T+T_1]$ with $T_1=T_0(g(T),\chi,\bar g,\sigma,n)>0$.

This restart does not require uniqueness of the geometric equation.  The
DeTurck gauge is re-initialized at $t=T$: the new DeTurck ODE uses
$\psi_s$ starting from $\psi_0=\operatorname{id}$, and the global
diffeomorphism is $\varphi_{T+s}=\varphi_T\circ\psi_s$.  The transported
cutoff resets to $\tilde\beta(0)=\chi\circ\varphi_T^{-1}$, which is smooth
and takes values in $[0,1]$.  Since all data are smooth, all hypotheses of
Theorem 6 are satisfied.

For the IL application: the first-exit estimates provide curvature and
Sobolev control on positive-time slabs, which keeps $T_1$ bounded below
and extends the solution to the common observation time $\theta r^2$.  That
mechanism is the content of Steps 55–62 and §6 of A03 Step 56; it is not
needed here.

## 8. Three levels of uniformity

The following distinctions are essential for the restricted IL theorem and
the broader Yau-26 problem.

**Level 1: One datum, all regularizations.**  $T_0$ in Theorem 6 depends
on the full smooth datum $(g_0,\chi,\bar g,\sigma,n)$, including high
Sobolev norms and the positivity constant of $g_0$.  This is the per-datum
existence proved here.

**Level 2: Family-uniform time (restricted IL).**  Over the Ishida–LeBrun
family with controlled Ricci defect, $\delta_i\to0$, the initial metrics
have varying topology and varying Sobolev norms.  A common observation time
is obtained by the first-exit bootstrap (A01 Steps 55–62) in the following
weaker form: if a per-datum solution were to stop before $\theta r^2$, then
on any positive-time slab ending at that time the weighted curvature/defect
estimates, together with the separate continuation hierarchy, produce a
smooth positive endpoint with datum-dependent higher bounds.  The autonomous
geometric equation can then be restarted there with matching time jets.  This
requires no family-uniform higher derivative bound and no concatenation of a
single DeTurck gauge across restarts.  It does not follow from the per-datum
theorem alone.

**Level 3: Dimension-only constants (Yau 26).**  The original problem asks
for $c_n,C_n$ depending only on $n$.  Neither Level 1 nor Level 2 achieves
this; it requires a global mechanism linking Ricci pinching to Einstein
existence across all closed $n$-manifolds.

## 9. Scope and limitations

1. **Sign generality.**  The theorem holds for all $\sigma\in\mathbb R$.
   The only $\sigma$-dependent quantity is $T_0$, through the zero-order
   contribution $C|\sigma|(1+\mathcal E_m)$ to the Grönwall coefficient.
   For the IL application, $\sigma=-1$; for the positive branch, $\sigma=1$;
   for the Ricci-flat normalization, $\sigma=0$.  The same analytic
   construction applies to all three.

2. **Uniqueness.**  The theorem produces at least one smooth solution; it does
     not claim that (1.3) has a unique solution.  For the DeTurck-gauged
     system (2.3), each fixed-$(\varepsilon,\nu)$ solution is unique by Theorem A,
     but the degenerate limit is constructed by subsequential compactness and
     need not be the unique limit of all subsequences.  A separate argument
     (e.g. a Grönwall estimate on the difference of two solutions of (2.3))
     would be needed to establish uniqueness of the degenerate transported
     system, and the ungauged geometric equation may further have distinct
     solutions related by time-dependent diffeomorphisms.  The IL argument
     needs existence and restart, not canonical uniqueness.
3. **Convention independence.**  The shifted curvature $T=\operatorname{Rm}-G$
   and the IL curvature energies play no role in §§2–7.  This theorem is
   valid independently of the convention choice for the shifted curvature.

4. **Degenerate coefficient.**  On the collar $\{\chi=0\}$, the geometric
   flow is trivially stationary.  The regularization $\beta^2+\varepsilon$
   makes the transported system uniformly elliptic on all of $M$; the
   degenerate limit is taken only after regularization-uniform bounds are
   established.  No standard parabolic theorem is cited for the exact
   degenerate system.

5. **Dimension.**  The result holds for all $n\ge2$.  The Sobolev threshold
   $s_0$ increases with $n$ (roughly $n/2+5$), but the proof structure is
   dimension-independent.
