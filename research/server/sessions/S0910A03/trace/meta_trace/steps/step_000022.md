## Deep-Reasoning

This step achieved a structural clarification that sharpens the entire remaining PDE program. The Step 21 TTCS generator proposed a Nash semigroup route that would bypass the weighted Moser hierarchy. The Strategist identified the decisive counterexample: the 4D Hardy potential V = κ/|x|² with 0 < κ < 1 satisfies the small form bound ∫Vφ² ≤ κ∫|∇φ|² but the heat semigroup is NOT ultracontractive—the kernel has a singular spatial weight r^{-σ} with σ = 1-√(1-κ) > 0. This was independently confirmed by TTCS via the Milman–Semenov on-diagonal estimate.

The counterexample reveals a deeper structural principle. In four dimensions, n/2 = 2 is the critical Sobolev exponent. A curvature potential in L² has only a fixed form bound (critical), which the Hardy example shows is insufficient for ultracontractivity. A potential in L³ has form-relative bound zero (subcritical), which allows standard Moser iteration with convergent constants. The Wang hierarchy H₃(t) ≤ Cγ³/(A₀⁴t) provides exactly this critical-to-subcritical upgrade at positive time. This is the essential mechanism: the SINGLE Moser step from H₂ to H₃ is the hard part, and everything after it (higher exponents, L∞ bound, defect propagation) is standard subcritical theory.

The two-stage architecture is now clean: Stage 1 (critical, hard) uses the Wang-type energy inequality to bootstrap H₂ → H₃ through critical Sobolev absorption with the volume-independent β = C√ε_Li. Stage 2 (subcritical, standard) uses standard Moser from L³ to L∞ with convergent constants. The Nash approach can at most compress Stage 2 (which doesn't need compression) and cannot touch Stage 1.

I verified the complete absorption mechanism algebraically: Cauchy–Schwarz factoring H₃ ≤ H₂^{1/2}·||w||₄², outer cutoff Sobolev, graph maximum principle on the finite-neighbor system, self-improvement of γ, and dissipation integral → terminal H₃ bound. All steps close under the small-γ hypothesis. The TTCS verification confirmed all five items with important corrections: the normalized bound is A₀⁴H₃ ≤ Cγ³/t (not just H₃), the conformal-Hardy zero-order term has genuine a⁻² singularity but is screened by natural test functions, and the outer cutoff graph mechanism is algebraically correct but the tensor evolution inequality is not yet proved.

A transcription correction was also established: the correct quadratic form uses ∇(a^{-1/2}φ), not ∇(a⁻¹φ). The leading gradient weight is a⁻¹ (one power) not a⁻² (two powers). This matters near {a=0} and propagates through all subsequent formulas.

The precise critical energy lemma has been formulated: it reduces the entire remaining PDE problem to the tensor evolution of F² under ∂_tg = -2χ²(Ric+g), followed by testing with η² and integration by parts. Everything downstream—subcritical Moser, defect hierarchy, metric comparability, Q preservation, exponential chart, projector contradiction—is either proved or standard.

A02's positive-branch finding adds important dimensional structure: codimension-1 positive Einstein collapse exists in every n ≥ 5 via KK constructions (L(q,1)×N^d), while n=4 is blocked by Bochner. This means the positive branch of Yau 26 in high dimensions cannot rely on collapse obstruction.

The research capital is now concentrated on a single, well-defined PDE problem: the localized evolution inequality for the regularized curvature under the degenerate normalized cutoff flow.
