# Cycle deformation, monodromy detection, and exact bidisk realization

24 September 2026. This note supplies a global transition test for the
allowed product branch. Small deformation on individual balls is replaced
by a bound accumulated around actual generators of the base fundamental
group. The test detects finite mapping-class monodromy; it does not assume
it, assume a nearby Einstein metric, or require solving the tensor equation
near the input metric. Classical surface theory then realizes an exact
Einstein metric on the original smooth total space.

The retained [Round 18 theorem](../mathematics/current/round18_twisted_operator.md)
starts with finite monodromy and describes the complete Einstein operator
and nearby solutions. Its conclusion is not being rediscovered here. The
additional bridge is from a differential cycle budget to that global
topological hypothesis. Two uses of the inherited Dehn-twist construction
show independently why local smallness and degeneration of the fiber cannot
be ignored. No claim about the nonexistence of general Einstein metrics on
those examples is made.

## 1. An intrinsic cycle budget

Let
\[
                 F_f\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\pi}B_b
                 \qquad(f,b\ge2)
\tag{1}
\]
be a smooth oriented bundle of closed connected oriented surfaces. Let
\(g\) be any smooth metric on \(M\), let \(V=\ker d\pi\), and use the
orthogonal Ehresmann connection \(H=V^\perp\). A Riemannian submersion,
horizontal integrability, or constant fiber curvature is not required in
the main theorem. Compactness makes horizontal lifting complete along
each finite path.

For a piecewise smooth based loop \(\gamma:[0,1]\to B\), let
\(\Phi_t:F_0\to F_{\gamma(t)}\) be horizontal transport, with
\(F_0=F_{\gamma(0)}\). Define metrics on the fixed surface \(F_0\) by
\[
 h_t=\Phi_t^*(g|_{F_{\gamma(t)}}),\qquad
 A_t=h_t^{-1}\dot h_t,\qquad
 A_t^0=A_t-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A_t)I,
\]
and define the conformal cycle variation
\[
 \mathcal J_g(\gamma)
       =\frac12\int_0^1\sup_{y\in F_0}
                          \|A_t^0(y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,dt.
\tag{2}
\]
The expression is invariant under orientation-preserving reparametrization
of the loop and under a fixed change of marking of \(F_0\). It measures
variation of its conformal structure, not a change in total fiber area.
No choice of a representative of a formal monodromy class replaces the
actual transport in (2).

Let \(\Gamma=(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_N)\) be based loops at a **common**
point \(b_0\), whose homotopy classes generate \(\pi_1(B,b_0)\). Put
\[
                    \mathcal J_g(\Gamma)=\max_i\mathcal J_g(\gamma_i).
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(x_0\in\mathcal T_f\) be the marked conformal class of
\(g|_{F_0}\). The thickness condition below refers to the systole of the
unique curvature \(-1\) metric in this conformal class. It is not a
lower bound for four-dimensional balls or an injectivity-radius bound for
the given metric.

**Theorem A (global monodromy detection and realization).** For every
\(f\ge2\) and \(\iota>0\), there is \(\varepsilon_{f,\iota}>0\)
such that the following holds. If (1) has a metric and generating loops
with
\[
 \operatorname{sys}(x_0)\ge\iota,
 \qquad \mathcal J_g(\Gamma)<\varepsilon_{f,\iota},
\tag{4}
\]
then its mapping-class monodromy has finite image \(G\). The original
smooth bundle is smoothly isomorphic to
\[
                   (\widehat B\times F_f)/G\longrightarrow B,
\tag{5}
\]
where \(\widehat B\to B\) is the connected regular cover associated
to the monodromy kernel, \(G\) acts freely on \(\widehat B\), and its
action on \(F_f\) is by orientation-preserving hyperbolic isometries.
Consequently the same smooth \(M\) carries an exact metric \(h\) with
\[
                \operatorname{Ric}_h=-h,
       \qquad (\widetilde M,\widetilde h)
               \cong\mathbb H^2_{-1}\times\mathbb H^2_{-1}.
\tag{6}
\]
It is Kähler for two opposite orientations. No closeness between \(g\)
and \(h\) is asserted. The threshold is independent of the genus of
the base, the number of generators, diameter, total volume, and the
order of the eventually detected finite group.

In particular, a pointwise near-Einstein input satisfying (4) has the
Einstein output required by Yau26 in this input class. In fact (4) alone
is sufficient: the theorem does not need the near-Einstein hypothesis.
Deriving (4) from a weaker original geometric input is a separate task;
Sections 4--6 identify what data it must retain. The companion
[Ricci-to-monodromy theorem](20260924_ricci_monodromy_realization.md)
performs this step for minimal surface submersions, using the horizontal
Ricci identity and a based short-generator argument. Its hypothesis is
\(2\operatorname{diam}(B)\sqrt{\delta+\|(K_B+1)_+\|_\infty}
<\varepsilon_{f,\iota}\), with the same constant as below.

## 2. The exact Teichmüller displacement estimate

We use \(d_T(x,y)=\frac12\inf\log K(\phi)\), where the infimum is
over quasiconformal maps in the prescribed marking class. Write
\(\rho:\pi_1(B)\to\operatorname{Mod}(F_f)\) for the monodromy.

**Lemma 1.** For every based loop,
\[
                    d_T(x_0,\rho(\gamma)x_0)
                          \le\mathcal J_g(\gamma).
\tag{7}
\]
The convention for the mapping-class action may replace \(\rho(\gamma)\)
by its inverse; the displacement is unchanged.

**Proof.** For any two nonzero fixed tangent vectors \(v,w\) at a point
of \(F_0\), differentiating the ratio of their squared lengths gives
\[
 \left|\frac d{dt}\log\frac{h_t(v,v)}{h_t(w,w)}\right|
                    \le2\|A_t^0\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{8}
\]
At a fixed point take vectors attaining the largest and smallest length
ratios between \(h_1\) and \(h_0\). Integration of (8) implies that
the logarithm of the quasiconformal dilatation of the identity between
these two conformal metrics is at most
\(\int\sup\|A_t^0\|_{\mathrm{op}}dt\). The metric \(h_1\) is
the pullback of \(g|_{F_0}\) by the actual return map \(\Phi_1\),
whose mapping class is \(\rho(\gamma)\). The definition of \(d_T\)
therefore gives (7). This proof does not discard rotations of the
principal directions or require that the matrices \(A_t\) commute.
\(\square\)

Although the connection need not be flat, isotopy classes of its return
maps are invariant under based homotopy of loops and obey the group
relations. Indeed horizontal lifting over a smooth homotopy gives an
isotopy of the resulting fiber diffeomorphisms. Thus the actual classes
used in (7) form the monodromy representation, not an unrelated list of
almost-isometries.

## 3. Small moves in a thick fiber force one finite group

**Lemma 2 (uniform finite-isotropy neighbourhood).** For fixed
\(f,\iota\), some \(\varepsilon_{f,\iota}>0\) has the following
property. If \(x\in\mathcal T_f\), \(\operatorname{sys}(x)\ge\iota\),
and every member of a set \(S\subset\operatorname{Mod}(F_f)\) moves
\(x\) by less than \(\varepsilon_{f,\iota}\), then \(\langle S\rangle\)
is finite. The number of elements of \(S\) need not be bounded.

**Proof.** We use two classical inputs: the mapping-class action on
Teichmüller space is proper, with finite stabilizers, and the quotient of
the fixed-systole thick part is compact by Mumford's compactness theorem.
Thus a compact set \(K\subset\mathcal T_f\) meets every thick orbit.

If the lemma failed, after simultaneous conjugation there would be
\(x_j\in K\), \(x_j\to x\), and sets \(S_j\) generating infinite
groups, with \(d_T(x_j,sx_j)<1/j\) for all \(s\in S_j\). For these
elements,
\[
              d_T(x,sx)\le2d_T(x,x_j)+1/j\longrightarrow0
\tag{9}
\]
uniformly in \(s\). Properness gives a neighbourhood \(U\) of \(x\)
such that only finitely many group elements can carry a smaller compact
neighbourhood into one meeting \(U\). Equivalently, since \(d_T\) is
proper, the set \(\{s:d_T(x,sx)\le1\}\) is finite. Its nonzero
displacements have a positive minimum. Hence, for large \(j\), every
member of \(S_j\) lies in \(\operatorname{Stab}(x)\). This finite
group contains \(\langle S_j\rangle\), a contradiction. A possible
finite kernel of the action, including the genus-two hyperelliptic
involution, causes no problem. \(\square\)

**Proof of Theorem A.** Lemmas 1--2 place all the generating monodromies
in a single finite group. It is insufficient to check that each generator
has finite order separately; Lemma 2 is precisely what controls the group
they generate.

Kerckhoff's Nielsen realization theorem realizes the finite image \(G\)
as isometries of one curvature \(-1\) metric on the fiber. Give \(B\)
any hyperbolic metric and \(\widehat B\) its lift. The diagonal action
on \(\widehat B\times F_f\) is free because its first component is
a deck action, even if its fiber action has fixed points. The product
metric descends smoothly and satisfies (6).

It remains to identify the smooth total space, not merely its group.
The identity component \(\operatorname{Diff}_0^+(F_f)\) is contractible
by Earle--Eells. Therefore
\(B\operatorname{Diff}^+(F_f)\simeq B\operatorname{Mod}(F_f)\), and
oriented smooth surface bundles over \(B\) are classified by conjugacy
classes of their monodromy representations. The original bundle and the
constructed suspension have the same representation, hence are smoothly
bundle-isomorphic. Pulling back the descended metric proves existence on
the original \(M\). The two product complex structures are invariant
under the orientation-preserving diagonal action, so both descend.
\(\square\)

This proof is a composition with classical global surface theory, not a
new Nielsen realization theorem. It uses neither a global tensor inverse
nor the assumption that a candidate Einstein metric preserves the input
bundle. It constructs one that does; that suffices for existence.

## 4. Differential and opposite-form versions

Let \(\mathrm{II}(Y,Z)=(\nabla_YZ)^H\) for vertical \(Y,Z\). For a
horizontal vector \(X\), define the symmetric vertical endomorphism
\(S_X\) by
\[
                  g(S_XY,Z)=g(\mathrm{II}(Y,Z),X).
\]
For the projectable horizontal field that lifts \(\dot\gamma\),
\[
 (\mathcal L_Xg)|_{V\times V}=-2g(S_X\cdot,\cdot),
 \quad
 \mathcal J_g(\gamma)=\int_0^1
               \sup_{F_{\gamma(t)}}\|S_X^0\|_{\mathrm{op}}dt.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed metric compatibility gives
\(g(\nabla_YX,Z)=-g(X,\nabla_YZ)\); the symmetric sum is the Lie
derivative. Pulling this identity back by \(\Phi_t\) gives (10).

Consequently, for a Riemannian submersion onto \((B,k)\),
\[
 \mathcal J_g(\Gamma)
 \le\Lambda_k(\Gamma)
       \sup_{|X|=1,\ X\in H}\|S_X^0\|_{\mathrm{op}},
 \qquad \Lambda_k(\Gamma)=\max_i\operatorname{Length}_k(\gamma_i).
\tag{11}
\]
Both factors matter. One cannot replace the right side by the local
deformation amplitude alone on a base with arbitrarily long essential
loops.

Here is a form-level version that keeps exact integrability explicit.
Suppose two smooth forms satisfy
\[
 *\omega_+=\omega_+,\quad *\omega_-=-\omega_-,\quad
 |\omega_\pm|^2=2,\quad d\omega_\pm=0.
\tag{12}
\]
Put \(\alpha=(\omega_++\omega_-)/2\) and
\(\beta=(\omega_+-\omega_-)/2\). Then \(\alpha,\beta\) have
rank two, \(\alpha^2=\beta^2=0\), and \(\alpha\wedge\beta\)
never vanishes, up to the choice of orientation/sign. Their kernel
distributions are transverse, orthogonal, and integrable: if a closed
constant-rank two-form annihilates \(Y,Z\), Cartan's formula gives
\(\iota_{[Y,Z]}\alpha=0\). This is an exact symplectic pair, not an
inference from approximate closedness.

Assume one distribution is the vertical tangent bundle of a smooth
surface bundle as in (1). This compact-leaf/leaf-space condition is not
automatic. If \(P\) is its orthogonal projection and \(J_\pm\) are
the endomorphisms associated with \(\omega_\pm\), then, with one
of the two signs,
\[
 P=\tfrac12(I\pm J_+J_-),\qquad
 |\nabla P|\le\frac1{\sqrt2}
                     (|\nabla\omega_+|+|\nabla\omega_-|).
\tag{13}
\]
We use Hilbert--Schmidt norms for tensors and the standard exterior norm
for forms; \(|\nabla J|^2=2|\nabla\omega|^2\). Also
\(\|S_X^0\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le |X||\nabla P|\). Thus, under the
Riemannian-submersion hypothesis, Theorem A applies if
\[
 \frac{\Lambda_k(\Gamma)}{\sqrt2}
       \bigl(\|\nabla\omega_+\|_\infty+
             \|\nabla\omega_-\|_\infty\bigr)
                              <\varepsilon_{f,\iota}.
\tag{14}
\]
The sharper loopwise test (10) is preferable when deformation is
inhomogeneous. No bound on second derivatives of these forms is used.

The actual-form supply theorem currently gives mean derivative bounds;
the collapsed-bulk theorem gives local limiting parallel forms. Neither
alone proves (12), the existence of the global fibration, or the cycle
estimate (10). The present theorem specifies a genuine sufficient
global input rather than declaring those missing implications automatic.

## 5. Sharpness I: thick fibers and uniform near-parallelism do not suffice

We extract additional consequences of the inherited fixed bundle \(E_D\),
not a newly constructed smooth object. Fix \(f,b\ge2\), a positive
Dehn twist \(D\) about a nonseparating curve of \(F_f\), and the
epimorphism \(u:\pi_1(B_b)\to\mathbb Z\) that sends one standard
generator to 1 and all others to 0. Its monodromy is \([D]^{u(\gamma)}\).
Choose a basepoint of the fiber off the twist support, so the suspension
has a section and
\[
 \pi_1(E_D)=\pi_1(F_f)\rtimes\pi_1(B_b),\qquad
 b_1(E_D)=2f+2b-1.
\tag{15}
\]
The last equality follows because a nonseparating twist acts by a
rank-one transvection on \(H_1(F_f;\mathbb R)\); its coinvariant
space has dimension \(2f-1\). In particular \(E_D\) admits no
Kähler metric, since the first Betti number of a compact Kähler manifold
is even. This simple obstruction is sufficient here; the stronger
inherited finite-cover obstructions are not needed.

Use the inherited area-preserving hyperbolic twist path on a fixed fiber:
\[
 h_z=du^2+a(u)^2(ds+z\zeta'(u)du)^2,
 \quad a(u)=a_0\cosh u,
 \quad h_1=D^*h_0,
 \quad dA_{h_z}=a(u)du\wedge ds.
\tag{16}
\]
It is extended by the fixed hyperbolic metric outside the collar.
The path \(z\in[0,1]\) is compact, so all its hyperbolic systoles
have a common positive lower bound. These are not globally trivial
deformations: an intermediate collar rotation is not a global isotopy
that removes \(D\).

On a long pinching base collar set
\[
 g_L=dt^2+b_L(t)^2d\theta^2+h_{z(t)},\qquad
 b_L(t)=\ell\cosh t,\quad z(t)=\eta(t/L),
\tag{17}
\]
where \(\eta\) is monotone from 0 to 1 and constant near the ends.
The source construction closes (17) by the marked Dehn twist, on the
fixed \(E_D\), and proves
\[
 \|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_L}^{\sharp}+I\|_\infty=O(L^{-1}),
 \qquad \operatorname{Vol}(g_L)=16\pi^2(f-1)(b-1).
\tag{18}
\]
No region-volume argument is used for this maximum-norm estimate.

The horizontal and vertical area forms \(\alpha_L,\beta\) glue
globally, are closed, and satisfy
\[
 \omega_{+,L}=\alpha_L+\beta,\qquad
 \omega_{-,L}=\alpha_L-\beta,
 \quad *\omega_{\pm,L}=\pm\omega_{\pm,L},
 \quad |\omega_{\pm,L}|^2=2.
\tag{19}
\]
Closedness uses the **fixed pointwise fiber area form** in (16), not
just its fixed integral. The marked gluing preserves that form. The
horizontal distribution is flat, and the only mixed second fundamental
terms are produced by \(\partial_th_{z(t)}\). Writing
\(Q=a\zeta'\), \(v=Qz'\), their orthonormal matrix is
\[
                   S_{\partial_t}
                    =-\frac v2\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus \(|\nabla P|=|v|\), and the connection on the two rank-two
blocks gives \(|\nabla\omega_{\pm,L}|\le C|v|\). Therefore
\[
                 \|\nabla\omega_{\pm,L}\|_\infty=O(L^{-1}).
\tag{21}
\]
The forms are also harmonic, by (19) and closedness.

Nevertheless (15) prohibits any exact Kähler output. Along a loop that
crosses the twist collar once and otherwise remains in the product
region, (20) and monotonicity give the **nonvanishing** cycle budget
\[
                  \mathcal J_{g_L}(\gamma)
                             =\frac12\|Q\|_\infty.
\tag{22}
\]
In particular it is at least \(d_T(x_0,Dx_0)>0\). An infinite-order
twist cannot fix a hyperbolic conformal structure, whose automorphism
group is finite. The deformation amplitude in (21) vanishes, but the
length over which it accumulates diverges. At least one essential cycle
retains the global information.

Thus even **closed, nowhere-zero, exactly normalized opposite forms**,
uniform near-parallelism, pointwise near-Einstein curvature, and thick
fiber conformal classes do not imply global KE realization without
control of transition cycles. This is stronger than merely observing
that an exceptional set in an average theorem might carry topology.

## 6. Sharpness II: vanishing cycle distortion does not replace fiber thickness

Keep the same smooth bundle and now keep a fixed hyperbolic base and a
fixed base collar. Pinch the twisting curve in the fiber to length
\(\lambda\downarrow0\). In its hyperbolic collar use the angular
period 1 and write \(a_\lambda(u)=\lambda\cosh u\). Fix a nonnegative
smooth \(\chi\), supported in \((-u_0,u_0)\), with positive integral,
and choose
\[
 C_\chi=\int_{-u_0}^{u_0}\chi(u)\operatorname{sech}u\,du,
 \qquad
 \zeta'(u)=\frac{\chi(u)}{C_\chi\cosh u}.
\tag{23}
\]
The integral of \(\zeta'\) is 1, so the return map is still one full
Dehn twist, not a small fractional topological twist. But
\[
                     Q_\lambda=a_\lambda\zeta'
                            =\lambda\chi/C_\chi
\tag{24}
\]
and all its fixed-order \(u\)-derivatives are \(O(\lambda)\).
The increasingly wide hyperbolic collar contains this fixed support.
Extend the hyperbolic fiber metrics using any fixed marked pinching
family; the construction remains on the same smooth bundle because its
mapping class remains \([D]\).

Take a fixed smooth monotone \(z(t)\) across the fixed base collar.
For completeness, in the orthonormal frame of (17) the inherited exact
Ricci defect is
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}+g=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -v^2/2&0&0&(v_u+2jv)/2\\
 0&0&0&0\\
 0&0&-v^2/2&-(v_t+kv)/2\\
 (v_u+2jv)/2&0&-(v_t+kv)/2&v^2/2
 \end{pmatrix},
 \quad j=\tanh u,\ k=b'/b,\ v=Q_\lambda z'.
\tag{25}
\]
All the functions on the fixed base collar have bounded derivatives.
Equations (24)--(25), together with the product metric off the supports,
therefore give
\[
 \delta_-(g_\lambda)=O(\lambda),\qquad
 \|\nabla\omega_{\pm,\lambda}\|_\infty=O(\lambda),\qquad
 \mathcal J_{g_\lambda}(\Gamma)=O(\lambda).
\tag{26}
\]
A fixed standard based generating system has bounded base lengths. Its
only nontrivial return map is \(D\); the other loops can be chosen to
avoid the active collar or to run along it at constant \(t\). In (26)
the forms still satisfy all the exact conditions (19).

The image of monodromy is nevertheless infinite cyclic, and (15) still
rules out Kähler geometry. The missing hypothesis is precisely the
fiber conformal thickness: its systole is at most \(\lambda\). This
is an explicit small-displacement failure at the boundary of moduli
space, not a failure of Lemma 2. Both circle collapse and long fiber
diameter are genuine; the base geometry does not degenerate.

### These are also actual bilateral near-saturation examples

Both constructions have symplectic structures (19) in the two
orientations. They have
\[
 \tau(E_D)=0,\quad A=2\chi(E_D)=8(f-1)(b-1)>0,
 \quad V=2\pi^2A,
 \quad p_\pm\ge A.
\tag{27}
\]
For the signature, the chosen monodromy extends over a handlebody
because it factors through one free generator; the surface bundle is
the boundary of the resulting oriented five-dimensional bundle. For
the class supply, \(b_+=b_->1\), so Taubes's symplectic canonical
classes have nonzero ordinary SW invariants in the two orientations.
They are actual for every metric and throughout the conformal weight
construction used in the inherited first-line estimates. Their squares
are \(A\), hence their positive projected squares are at least \(A\).
These class-supply facts and the weighted-solvability passage are the
same inherited classical inputs, not a new gauge theorem here.

Write \(q_\pm\) for the bilateral normalized deficits of I25/I30.
The exact inherited energy identity gives
\[
 q_\pm=\frac9{2V}
       \left[\int|\operatorname{Ric}|^2-8\pi^2(2p_\pm-A)\right].
\]
Since \(\fint|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\le4(1+\delta_-)^2\),
\[
                    0\le q_\pm\le36\delta_-+18\delta_-^2\to0.
\tag{28}
\]
Thus the two sharpness examples lie in the actual bilateral gauge
near-saturation regime, not only in an abstract almost-product class.
They do **not** disprove Yau26: arbitrary non-Kähler Einstein existence
on \(E_D\) remains undecided by these statements.

## 7. What this bridge changes

The inputs (4) are nonempty and not merely the equation for an existing
Einstein metric. Every finite-monodromy bundle has a connection and
invariant fiber metric with \(\mathcal J=0\); the base metric and its
diameter can be arbitrary. Smooth changes of the metric and connection
retain (4) for a fixed generating system when sufficiently small, while
the input need not be Einstein. More generally, trace changes of fiber
metrics do not affect (2), so input scalar curvature and scale are not
required to be close to a product normalization.

Unlike Round 18, one need not know the monodromy image in advance, supply
an exact reference metric, or discard slow Teichmüller coefficient
functions. Their cumulative deformation is precisely what (2) measures.
The proof constructs the global quotient only after this data has forced
finite monodromy. It does not classify all nearby Einstein metrics or
provide a uniform inverse on the quotient; those remain distinct claims
in Round 18.

For the first line, the resulting mathematical choice is specific:
local product estimates must be accompanied by a mechanism controlling
global cycle distortion and escape toward the boundary of fiber moduli.
Making every local form derivative small, even uniformly with exact
closedness, does not remove those two independent alternatives. The
actual examples above rule out that proposed shortcut while Theorem A
gives a sufficient global replacement. No new smooth topology or
world-first priority is asserted for this composition.

# From pointwise Ricci control to finite monodromy and exact realization

24 September 2026. This is a composition theorem for a specified geometric
class, not a theorem that arbitrary near-Einstein four-manifolds admit a
surface fibration. Its global input is the thick-fiber cycle theorem in
[the companion note](20260924_cycle_holonomy_realization.md).
Its curvature identity is classical and was already used in
[Round 20, Sections 5–7](../mathematics/current/round20_construction.md).
The new project-level conclusion is a quantitative **sufficient condition
for exact existence**, including zero-signature bundles: small pointwise
Ricci error detects finite mapping-class monodromy, instead of assuming it.

## 1. Statement

Let
\[
        \Sigma_f\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\pi}B
\]
be a smooth oriented surface bundle, with fiber genus \(f\ge2\) and closed
oriented base of genus at least two. A Riemannian metric \(g\) makes
\(\pi:(M,g)\to(B,b)\) a Riemannian submersion. Write \(h_y\) for its
vertical metric, \(\mathcal H\) for its orthogonal horizontal distribution,
and \(K_b\) for the Gaussian curvature of the base. Assume the fibers are
minimal. They need not be totally geodesic, intrinsically hyperbolic, or
mutually isometric. The horizontal distribution need not be integrable.

Fix a reference fiber over \(y_0\). Uniformize its conformal class to
curvature \(-1\), and assume its hyperbolic systole is at least \(\iota>0\).
Define
\[
\begin{split}
 \delta&=\|\operatorname{Ric}_g^{\sharp}+I\|_{L^\infty,\mathrm{op}},\\
 \eta&=\|(K_b+1)_+\|_{L^\infty(B)},\qquad
 D_B=\operatorname{diam}(B,b).
\end{split}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The positive part in \(\eta\) is intentional; no two-sided curvature
closeness of the base is needed for the estimate below.

**Theorem 1 (Ricci-to-monodromy realization).** There is
\(\epsilon_{f,\iota}>0\), depending only on fiber genus and the reference
systole, such that
\[
                 2D_B\sqrt{\delta+\eta}<\epsilon_{f,\iota}       \tag{2}
\]
implies that the mapping-class monodromy of the bundle has finite image.
Consequently the **same oriented smooth manifold** \(M\) carries a
negative Kähler–Einstein metric \(\widehat g\), with
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_{\widehat g}=-\widehat g,
 \qquad (\widetilde M,\widetilde{\widehat g})
          \cong\mathbb H^2\times\mathbb H^2.                  \tag{3}
\]
The theorem imposes no bound on total-space diameter, total volume,
injectivity radius, full curvature, or a spectral inverse. It does not
assert closeness of \(\widehat g\) to \(g\).

In particular, when \(K_b=-1\), condition (2) is simply
\[
          \delta<\frac{\epsilon_{f,\iota}^2}{4D_B^2}.          \tag{4}
\]
Thus, within this explicitly specified input class, the output is already
the Einstein existence requested by the negative side of Yau26. The
additional fibration, minimality, thickness and base-scale hypotheses are
the restriction; obtaining KE rather than a general Einstein metric is
not a defect of the existence conclusion.

## 2. Horizontal Ricci controls conformal deformation

For a horizontal vector \(X\), define the symmetric vertical endomorphism
\(S_X\) by
\[
            h(S_XU,V)=\langle(\nabla_UV)^{\mathcal H},X\rangle.
\]
Let \(A_XY=\tfrac12[X,Y]^{\mathcal V}\) for basic horizontal vector
fields. Minimality is exactly \(\operatorname{tr}S_X=0\) for every
horizontal \(X\). For a unit horizontal vector, O'Neill's contracted
curvature formulas give
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_g(X,X)
       =K_b-|S_X|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
                -2\sum_a|A_Xe_a|^2.                         \tag{5}
\]
Here \(e_a\) is a horizontal orthonormal basis. The derivative of the
fiber mean-curvature vector in the general formula is zero because that
vector is identically zero, not because its integral vanishes. To check
the coefficients, horizontal sectional curvatures contribute
\(K_b-3\sum_a|A_Xe_a|^2\); the mixed sectional curvatures contribute
\(\sum_a|A_Xe_a|^2-|S_X|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\). This is the same identity
used in Round 20; our sign choice for \(S\) has no effect on (5).
The primary source is O'Neill,
[*The fundamental equations of a submersion*](https://doi.org/10.1307/mmj/1028999604).

Since the horizontal compression of \(\operatorname{Ric}_g+g\) has
operator norm at most \(\delta\), (5) yields, pointwise,
\[
\begin{split}
 |S_X|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+2\sum_a|A_Xe_a|^2
   &=(K_b+1)-(\operatorname{Ric}_g+g)(X,X)\\
   &\le\eta+\delta.
\end{split}                                                \tag{6}
\]
In particular, for an arbitrary horizontal vector,
\[
                 |S_X|_{\mathrm{op}}
           \le\sqrt{\delta+\eta}\,|X|.                    \tag{7}
\]
No vertical or mixed Ricci equation is discarded in the hypothesis: the
full Ricci defect in (1) implies the horizontal bound. Conversely we
make no claim that the horizontal bound alone makes an arbitrary trial
metric near-Einstein. Intrinsic fiber curvature is absent from (5), which
is why no fiber uniformization hypothesis is required away from \(y_0\).

## 3. Integrating the actual deformation around a loop

Let \(\gamma:[0,1]\to B\) be a piecewise smooth loop based at \(y_0\).
Its horizontal transport \(P_t:F_{y_0}\to F_{\gamma(t)}\) is defined for
the entire interval because the bundle is compact. Set
\(k_t=P_t^*h_{\gamma(t)}\). Differentiating on the fixed marked fiber,
\[
                 k_t^{-1}\dot k_t=-2P_t^*S_{\dot\gamma^{\mathcal H}}.
                                                                  \tag{8}
\]
The trace is zero by minimality. Write \(x_t\) for the corresponding
point in Teichmüller space, with metric normalized by
\(d_T=\tfrac12\inf\log K\).

For clarity the deformation estimate does not require differentiating
the uniformization map. For any nonzero tangent vector on the reference
fiber,
\[
 \left|\frac{d}{dt}\log |v|_{k_t}\right|
       \le\sup_{F_{\gamma(t)}}|S_{\dot\gamma^{\mathcal H}}|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
After integration, the ratio between the largest and smallest singular
stretch of the identity map is at most
\(\exp(2\int\sup|S|\,dt)\). This ratio is its quasiconformal
dilatation. It follows that
\[
\begin{split}
 d_T(x_0,x_1)
 &\le\int_0^1\sup_{F_{\gamma(t)}}
            |S_{\dot\gamma^{\mathcal H}}|_{\mathrm{op}}\,dt\\
 &\le\sqrt{\delta+\eta}\,\operatorname{Length}_b(\gamma).
\end{split}                                                     \tag{9}
\]
The endpoint is the action on \(x_0\) of the monodromy class, or its
inverse according to the marking convention. Distances are the same.
Connection holonomy itself need not be a homomorphism into
diffeomorphisms when the connection is curved; its **mapping-class**
holonomy is the topological monodromy homomorphism. Formula (9) controls
that homomorphism without falsely imposing flatness of the connection.

## 4. Short generators and a common finite group

We use the following elementary global fact.

**Lemma 2.** For a closed connected Riemannian manifold \(B\), any
basepoint \(y_0\), and \(a>0\), its fundamental group has a finite
generating set represented by loops of length less than
\(2\operatorname{diam}(B)+a\).

**Proof.** Subdivide any based loop into arcs of length less than \(a\).
Join each subdivision vertex to \(y_0\) by a minimizing path of length
at most \(D_B\). The original loop is the product of the resulting
triangular based loops; cancellation is literal along the joining paths.
Each triangular loop has length less than \(2D_B+a\). Only finitely
many based homotopy classes admit a representative with that length
bound: lifting to the complete universal cover associates each class to
a distinct deck translate of a fixed lift of \(y_0\) in a compact ball.
The deck orbit is discrete and the action is proper. Thus the collection
of all these short classes is a finite generating set. \(\square\)

The companion theorem supplies the following precise Teichmüller fact:
for every \(f,\iota\), there exists \(\epsilon_{f,\iota}>0\) such that,
at every marked hyperbolic surface of systole at least \(\iota\), the
subgroup generated by **all** mapping classes moving it less than
\(\epsilon_{f,\iota}\) is finite. This is stronger than saying that
each such class separately has finite order. It follows from proper
discontinuity of the mapping-class action and compactness of the thick
moduli quotient, using neighborhoods of a **common** finite stabilizer.
The companion note proves the uniform statement and treats the possible
ineffective finite kernel in genus two.

By the strict inequality (2), choose \(a>0\) sufficiently small that
\[
              (2D_B+a)\sqrt{\delta+\eta}<\epsilon_{f,\iota}.
\]
Apply (9) to the finite generating set of Lemma 2. All its monodromy
elements lie in the same finite subgroup supplied by the thick-point
lemma. Therefore the entire monodromy image is finite. This is the
global step that a pointwise estimate for \(S\) alone does not provide.

## 5. Realization on the original smooth bundle

Let \(G\) be the finite monodromy image. Nielsen realization gives a
hyperbolic metric \(h_F\) on the model fiber and an action
\(G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Isom}^+(\Sigma_f,h_F)\) realizing
those mapping classes. Let \(\widehat B\to B\) be the connected regular
cover corresponding to the kernel of monodromy. Its deck group is
\(G\). Uniformize any conformal structure on the oriented base to a
hyperbolic metric \(\widehat b\) and lift it to \(\widehat B\).
Then
\[
          (\widehat B\times\Sigma_f)/G                     \tag{10}
\]
is a smooth diagonal quotient; the action is free because it is free on
the base factor. It carries the quotient of the product metric, with
Ricci tensor equal to minus the metric, and the quotient complex
structure is Kähler.

The identity component of the orientation-preserving diffeomorphism
group of a closed surface of genus at least two is contractible. Thus
oriented smooth surface bundles are classified by their mapping-class
monodromy; there is no additional identity-component twisting over
\(B\). The quotient bundle (10) and the original bundle are smoothly
isomorphic, since their monodromy representations coincide. Pulling
back the quotient metric proves (3) on the original \(M\).
The companion note gives the classical Nielsen and surface-diffeomorphism
references. Neither the original horizontal distribution nor its full
diffeomorphism holonomy has to agree with the resulting product quotient.

## 6. A quantified escape alternative

Taking the contrapositive gives a useful theorem about actual inputs,
not an assertion of general Einstein nonexistence.

**Corollary 3.** If the mapping-class monodromy image is infinite and
the reference fiber is \(\iota\)-thick, then every minimal-submersion
metric as above satisfies
\[
          (\delta+\eta)D_B^2\ge\frac{\epsilon_{f,\iota}^2}{4}.
                                                               \tag{11}
\]
For hyperbolic bases and \(\delta_j\to0\), a sequence of such metrics
with fixed fiber genus and a uniformly thick reference fiber therefore
has
\[
          D_{B,j}\ge\frac{\epsilon_{f,\iota}}{2\sqrt{\delta_j}}
                   \longrightarrow\infty.                  \tag{12}
\]
Since a Riemannian submersion is distance nonincreasing,
\(\operatorname{diam}(M,g)\ge D_B\). Alternatively, with bounded
\(D_B\), infinite monodromy and vanishing defect force the reference
conformal systole to approach zero along any subsequence for which it
could otherwise have a positive lower bound.

These alternatives distinguish two real escape mechanisms: long
horizontal transport and thin fiber conformal geometry. The companion
note supplies Dehn-twist examples separating them. They do not say that
the same smooth manifold has no Einstein metric outside this submersion
class, nor that every near-Einstein manifold has either of these two
geometric descriptions.

## 7. What changes, and what does not

Round 20 already proved a signature-energy obstruction for minimal
hyperbolic-fiber submersions and recovered the classical exact adapted
Einstein classification. For a signature-zero bundle its signature
lower bound vanishes. The present argument instead uses the actual
mapping-class action and one thick conformal fiber. It can detect
infinite monodromy even when signature is zero; more importantly, it
proves existence when the smallness threshold is met.

This is not a new construction of bidisk quotients: those are classical.
It is a geometric criterion that **recognizes** their smooth bundle
types from the input metric, with finite monodromy deduced rather than
assumed. No assertion of worldwide priority is made. The first-line
connection is that local or average Kähler-type structure requires
global transport information to produce existence. Here that
information is obtained from a definite horizontal Ricci identity and
a thick-point monodromy threshold, rather than from another local
calibration coefficient.

The remaining step toward arbitrary Yau26 inputs is substantial:
neither an actual surface submersion nor fiber minimality nor thickness
is supplied by the general pointwise Ricci hypothesis. The existing
collapsed-bulk theorem does not automatically supply them across its
exceptional set. This theorem completes the existence argument for the
stated input class without claiming that missing classification.

# Einstein repair with an integrable kernel: a sharp cyclic-cover theorem

24 September 2026. The positive-coercivity hypothesis in the
[uniformly local repair theorem](20260924_uniformly_local_repair.md)
excludes products of hyperbolic surfaces. Here all their genuine
Teichmüller parameters are retained. On a specified cyclic-cover family
we prove a complete nonlinear repair theorem with the correct quadratic
loss, and actual near-Einstein metrics showing that loss cannot be
replaced by a uniform constant. The reduced equation is proved to vanish;
its solvability is not an assumption.

## 1. Objects, norms and statements

Fix closed oriented hyperbolic surfaces \((B,b)\), \((F,f)\), of curvature
\(-1\), and a primitive epimorphism \(\pi_1(B)\to\mathbb Z\).
Let \((B_m,b_m)\) be the degree-\(m\) cyclic cover. Put
\[
 M_m=B_m\times F,\qquad h_m=b_m+f,\qquad
 \operatorname{Ric}_{h_m}=-h_m.
\]
All constants below depend on these fixed surfaces, the cyclic cover and
\(0<\alpha<1\), but not on \(m\). Use the uniform lifted product atlas
to define \(X_m=C^{3,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}\) and
\(Y_m=C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}\). These are supremum norms, not sums
over charts. The choice of one extra derivative makes the global factor
projections and gauge estimates explicit.

For a metric \(g\), let
\[
 W_m(g)^k=g^{ij}\bigl(\Gamma(g)^k_{ij}-\Gamma(h_m)^k_{ij}\bigr),
 \quad
 \Phi_m(g)=\operatorname{Ric}_g+g-\tfrac12\mathcal L_{W_m(g)}g.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
The condition \(W_m(g)=0\) says that the identity map to \((M_m,h_m)\)
is harmonic. At \(h_m\),
\[
 D\Phi_m=L_m=\tfrac12\Delta_{L,h_m}+I
            =\tfrac12\nabla^*\nabla-\mathring R.
\]
Write \(T_\Sigma\) for the transverse-traceless tensors on a hyperbolic
surface, and set
\[
 K_m=T_{B_m}\oplus T_F=\ker L_m,
 \qquad \Pi_m=\text{the }L^2(h_m)\text{-projection onto }K_m.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
Every vector in \(K_m\) is an actual infinitesimal factor deformation;
its dimension grows with \(m\). None is discarded.

**Theorem A (quantified repair to the full Einstein moduli).** There are
\(c,C,r_0>0\) and smooth families
\[
 H_m(k)=h_m+k+R_m(k),\qquad k\in K_m,\quad \|k\|_{X_m}<r_0,
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
of exact product Einstein metrics in harmonic gauge, with
\(\Pi_mR_m(k)=0\) and
\[
 \|R_m(k)\|_{X_m}\le C\|k\|_{X_m}^2,\qquad
 \|DR_m(k)\|\le C\|k\|_{X_m}.
                                                               \tag{4}
\]
If \(W_m(g)=0\) and \(\|g-h_m\|_{X_m}<c m^{-2}\), then
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|g-H_m(\Pi_m(g-h_m))\|_{X_m}
       \le C m^2\|\operatorname{Ric}_g+g\|_{Y_m}.
 \quad}                                                       \tag{5}
\]
In particular every exact Einstein metric in this harmonic-gauge
neighborhood belongs to (3). The assertion permits every tensor sector
in the input metric, not only product or fiber-preserving perturbations.

**Theorem B (the loss is realized nonlinearly).** There are smooth
metrics \(g_m\), uniformly noncollapsed with uniformly bounded
curvature derivatives of every fixed order, such that
\[
 \|g_m-h_m\|_{X_m}\le C m^{-2},\qquad
 \|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_m}+g_m\|_{Y_m}\le C m^{-4},
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
but, writing \(\mathscr E(M_m)=\{h:\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\}\),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \inf_{h\in\mathscr E(M_m)}\|h-g_m\|_{X_m}\ge c m^{-2}.
 \quad}                                                       \tag{7}
\]
The infimum includes all normalized Einstein metrics and their
diffeomorphic pullbacks, not just the displayed product family. Thus a
uniform residual-to-distance estimate with exponent \(\theta>1/2\)
is false on this family, even though all exact kernel directions are
integrable. No optimality of the exponent \(1/2\) is asserted.

These manifolds already admit \(h_m\). Theorem B is a limitation of
uniform perturbative repair, not an Einstein-nonexistence result or a
counterexample to Yau26.

## 2. The scalar inverse costs exactly \(m^2\)

Let \(\Delta\ge0\). We first prove
\[
 \|\Delta_{B_m}^{-1}v\|_{C^0}\le C m^2\|v\|_{C^0},
 \qquad \int_{B_m}v=0,
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
with the inverse chosen to have mean zero. Cut the fixed base along a
nonseparating curve dual to the epimorphism, and use bounded overlapping
enlargements of its lifted cells. Adjacent cells and these enlargements
have uniform Poincaré constants and bounded overlap. For a solution
\(\Delta u=v\), differences of consecutive cell means are bounded by
the local \(L^2\) gradient. Local elliptic estimates, after subtracting
a cell mean, give
\[
 \sup_{\text{cell }j}|u-u_j|
 \le C\bigl(\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\text{enlarged cell }j)}
                  +\|v\|_\infty\bigr).
\]
To justify this estimate, cover the cell by a fixed chain of interior
balls, apply the inhomogeneous local \(L^2\)-to-\(C^0\) estimate on
each ball, and use Poincaré on the enlargement. No estimate at an
artificial cut boundary is used.

Summing along at most \(m\) cells and applying Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
 O:=\operatorname{osc}u\le C\sqrt m\|\nabla u\|_2+C\|v\|_\infty.
\]
Since \(\operatorname{Vol}B_m=m\operatorname{Vol}B\), integration by
parts, with any constant subtracted from \(u\), gives
\[
 \|\nabla u\|_2^2=\int uv\le C m\|v\|_\infty O.
\]
Substitution and Young's inequality prove (8). Uniform local Schauder
estimates then give its \(C^{j,\alpha}\)-to-\(C^{j+2,\alpha}\)
version, with the same \(m^2\) loss.

For sharpness choose the harmonic one-form \(\eta\) in the primitive
integral class. Its lift to the infinite cyclic cover is \(dz\), with
\(z(\tau x)=z(x)+1\) and \(\Delta z=0\). For \(m\ge2\),
\[
 v_m=\cos(2\pi z/m)
\]
is a smooth function on \(B_m\). Summing over the deck orbit proves
\(\int v_m=0\), while \(\|v_m\|_\infty=1\). The chain rule gives
\[
 \Delta v_m=\frac{4\pi^2}{m^2}|\eta|^2v_m,
 \qquad \|\Delta v_m\|_{C^{j,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}\le C_jm^{-2}.
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
All local derivatives of \(v_m\) are uniformly bounded. Thus the
order in (8) is attained on an actual harmonic-circle construction,
not merely inferred from a discrete graph model.

## 3. Uniform projections and the full transverse inverse

The classical product decomposition gives two slow channels
\[
 T_{B_m}\widehat\otimes L^2(F),\qquad
 L^2(B_m)\widehat\otimes T_F,
\]
on which \(L_m\) is respectively \(\tfrac12\Delta_F\) and
\(\tfrac12\Delta_{B_m}\). The remaining trace, conformal-Killing and
mixed sectors have positive shifts. These are the complete sectors,
as in [Kröncke, Proposition 4.1 and Lemma 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1508.00721)
and [the project's surface calculation](../mathematics/current/round17_product_operator.md),
rescaled from curvature \(-3\) to \(-1\). In Kröncke's notation the
Einstein operator \(\Delta_E=\nabla^*\nabla-2\mathring R\) is
\(2L_m\), not \(L_m\). In our nonnegative Hodge convention,
\(\Delta_H=\nabla^*\nabla-1\) on surface one-forms. Thus the exact
intertwining needed below is
\[
 L_{B_m\times F}(D_Bz)
       =D_B\bigl(\tfrac12\Delta_{H,B}+1+\tfrac12\Delta_F\bigr)z
       =D_B(A_B+\tfrac12\Delta_F)z.
\]
This also follows directly from the surface identities
\(L_B=D_B\delta_B\) on trace-free tensors and
\(\delta_BD_B=A_B\). The accompanying coordinate check verifies
both identities for arbitrary one-form coefficients in the
curvature-\(-1\) half-plane metric; no Hodge sign is inferred from a
change of notation in the cited source.

We need Hölder, not just Hilbert-space, control of these projectors.
For a hyperbolic surface let
\[
 D\xi=\delta^*\xi+\tfrac12(\delta\xi)b,
 \qquad A=\delta D=\tfrac12\nabla^*\nabla+\tfrac12.
\]
For a trace-free tensor \(s\), its TT projection is
\(P_Ts=s-D A^{-1}\delta s\). This formula has uniform bounds on
the present surfaces, including as an operator from \(C^0\) to
local \(C^{j,\alpha}\) for every fixed \(j\).

Here is a proof which does not assume a bound for a large-rank projection.
Put \(w=A^{-1}\delta s\). The cyclic surfaces have
\(\operatorname{Vol}B_R(x)\le C(1+R)\), by counting translates of a
fixed cell in the infinite cyclic cover. Test \(Aw=\delta s\) with
\(\phi_x^2w\), where \(\phi_x=e^{-a\operatorname{dist}(x,\cdot)}\)
and \(a>0\) is fixed and sufficiently small. Integrate the divergence
on the right onto \(\phi_x^2w\). The positive potential \(1/2\),
Young's inequality and \(|d\phi_x|\le a\phi_x\) give
\[
 \int\phi_x^2(|\nabla w|^2+|w|^2)
       \le C\int\phi_x^2|s|^2\le C\|s\|_\infty^2.        \tag{10}
\]
The last constant follows from the linear ball-growth bound. Thus
\(P_Ts=s-Dw\) has uniformly bounded local \(L^2\) norm. It is TT;
the first-order operator \(\delta:S^2_0\to T^*\Sigma\) is elliptic
in dimension two. Local homogeneous elliptic estimates therefore
bound every derivative of \(P_Ts\) by this local \(L^2\) norm.
This proves the claim, first for smooth \(s\), and then by density for
the stated bounds. The fixed surface \(F\) has the same property.

The estimates persist with a parameter in the other factor: the
projections commute with those derivatives, and their \(C^0\)-to-local-
derivative bounds apply to parameter differences. Hence the factor
projections, their product-sector extensions and \(\Pi_m\) are bounded
on \(X_m,Y_m\), uniformly in \(m\). For \(\Pi_m\), one additionally
averages over the other factor; normalized averaging has norm at most
one. This explains why a growing kernel dimension causes no projection
constant here.

We claim that
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|u\|_{X_m}\le C m^2\|L_mu\|_{Y_m},\qquad \Pi_mu=0.
 \quad}                                                       \tag{11}
\]
For the two slow channels, remove their true constant coefficient
modes only, and apply (8) or the fixed scalar Green operator on \(F\).
This gives the required \(C^0\) bound. For traces, the operator is
\(1+\tfrac12(\Delta_{B_m}+\Delta_F)\), so the maximum principle
gives a uniform bound.

On a conformal-Killing sector write the source as \(D_Bw\), where
\(w=A_B^{-1}\delta_Bs\). Its \(C^{0,\alpha}\) norm on the product is
bounded by \(C\|s\|_{Y_m}\), using the positive-potential inverse
and the preceding parameter estimates. Solve
\[
 (A_B+\tfrac12\Delta_F)z=w,
 \qquad u=D_Bz.
\]
This connection operator has potential \(1/2\); the maximum principle
and local Schauder estimates bound \(u\) in \(C^0\) uniformly.
The other conformal-Killing sector is identical with the factors
interchanged. This retains its response rather than setting it to zero.

On the mixed bundle,
\[
 L_m=\tfrac12\nabla^*\nabla,
 \qquad \langle L_mu,u\rangle_{L^2}\ge\|u\|_2^2,
\]
because each factor's rough one-form Laplacian is \(\Delta_H+1\).
Multiplication by a scalar preserves this entire bundle. The weighted
identity in the preceding repair theorem thus applies with gap one
and linear volume growth, giving a uniform local \(L^2\) bound and
then a \(C^0\) bound. We do not apply weighted coercivity to the
nonlocal kernel-orthogonal subspace: multiplication need not preserve
that subspace.

Finally, the full local elliptic estimate
\[
 \|u\|_{X_m}\le C(\|L_mu\|_{Y_m}+\|u\|_\infty)
\]
proves (11) after summing the finitely many sector types. Fredholm
theory gives surjectivity onto \(K_m^\perp\cap Y_m\). Applying (9)
to \(u=v_mq\), for any fixed nonzero \(q\in T_F\), shows that the
operator norm in (11) is at least \(c m^2\). These tensors are TT
and orthogonal to the **entire** kernel (2).

## 4. Integration of the whole kernel, uniformly in the cover

We give the uniform version of the fixed-geometry argument in
[Round 18, §7](../mathematics/current/round18_twisted_operator.md).
For \(q\in T_{B_m}\) small in the uniform norm, the metric \(b_m+q\)
is positive definite and
\(K(b_m+q)+1=O(\|q\|^2)\), because the curvature variation of a TT
tensor is zero. Solve
\[
 \Delta_{b_m+q}u+e^{2u}+K(b_m+q)=0.                         \tag{12}
\]
Its derivative at zero is \(\Delta_{b_m}+2\). The maximum principle
and uniform local Schauder estimates give a uniform inverse; the
nonlinear contraction therefore gives \(u=O(\|q\|^2)\) on a
uniform ball. Thus \(e^{2u}(b_m+q)\) has curvature \(-1\) and
derivative \(q\). TT elliptic estimates control all higher local
derivatives of \(q\) by its uniform norm, so this construction and
its first two parameter derivatives have uniform bounds. The same
construction applies on \(F\).

Place each hyperbolic family in harmonic-map gauge relative to its
reference surface. The Jacobi operator at the identity is
\(\nabla^*\nabla+1\). Its pointwise positive potential, the maximum
principle and local Schauder theory give uniform inverse bounds.
The implicit-function argument for maps to the fixed reference metric
therefore has a uniform radius. Its first variation is zero along TT
directions. Pushing the family forward by this small harmonic
diffeomorphism preserves curvature and changes it only quadratically.
Taking products gives actual Einstein metrics in the gauge (1).

Their kernel projection is \(q+O(q^2)\), with uniformly bounded
derivative. The inverse function theorem on \(K_m\), using its
uniform norm rather than coordinatewise parameter bounds, reparametrizes
the family to give (3)--(4). Constants do not depend on its dimension.

Let \(Q_m=I-\Pi_m\). The usual complementary equation is
\[
 Q_m\Phi_m(h_m+k+w)=0,\qquad w\in K_m^\perp.
\]
By (11) and the uniform quadratic Hölder estimate, it has a unique
small solution in the radius \(c m^{-2}\) under consideration.
The actual family already constructed is this graph:
\(w=R_m(k)\). Its remaining equation is therefore
\[
 \boxed{\ \Pi_m\Phi_m(h_m+k+R_m(k))=0\ }
\]
identically. This proves compatibility of every reduced equation;
it is stronger than omitting them or assuming integrability of only
selected tangent vectors.

To prove (5), take \(k=\Pi_m(g-h_m)\),
\(z=g-H_m(k)\in K_m^\perp\), and expand (1). The quadratic remainder
\(N_m\) satisfies a uniform Lipschitz bound
\[
 \|N_m(a)-N_m(b)\|_{Y_m}
 \le C(\|a\|_{X_m}+\|b\|_{X_m})\|a-b\|_{X_m}.
\]
Since \(\Phi_m(H_m(k))=0\), (11) gives
\[
 \|z\|_{X_m}\le C m^2\bigl(\|Q_m\Phi_m(g)\|_{Y_m}
                                +Ccm^{-2}\|z\|_{X_m}\bigr).
\]
Choose \(c\) small enough to absorb the last term. In harmonic gauge
\(\Phi_m(g)=\operatorname{Ric}_g+g\), proving Theorem A. The same
argument classifies every nearby gauged zero, not merely the solutions
obtained by a chosen iteration.

## 5. Actual small residuals separated from all Einstein outputs

Fix a nonzero \(q\in T_F\), let \(u_m=v_mq\) from (9), and first put
\(\widetilde g_m(t)=h_m+t u_m\). The tensors \(u_m\) have uniformly
bounded local derivatives of every fixed order, are TT, and satisfy
\[
 \Pi_mu_m=0,\quad \|u_m\|_{X_m}\ge c_0>0,
 \quad \|L_mu_m\|_{Y_m}\le C m^{-2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \|\operatorname{Ric}_{\widetilde g_m(t)}+\widetilde g_m(t)\|_{Y_m}
                         \le C(t m^{-2}+t^2).              \tag{13}
\]
This is the Taylor expansion of the actual Ricci tensor, not just a
linearized forcing. Because \(DW_m|_{h_m}(u_m)=0\), the harmonic-map
gauge adjustment described above is \(O(t^2)\), with bounds in every
fixed derivative norm. Denote the adjusted metric by \(g_m(t)\). Then
\[
 W_m(g_m(t))=0,\qquad
 g_m(t)=h_m+t u_m+O_{X_m}(t^2),                              \tag{14}
\]
and (13) still holds. Choose once and for all a sufficiently small
\(\theta_0>0\), and set \(t=t_m=\theta_0m^{-2}\).
These metrics lie in Theorem A's neighborhood and satisfy (6).

For any family member \(H_m(k)\) at distance \(O(t_m)\) from
\(g_m(t_m)\), the bounded projection and (14) imply
\(\|k\|_{X_m}\le C t_m\). Equations (3)--(4) then give
\[
 Q_m(g_m(t_m)-H_m(k))=t_m u_m+O_{X_m}(t_m^2).
\]
Thus its norm is at least \(c t_m\); boundedness of \(Q_m\) gives
the same lower bound for the metric distance. Members farther away
already obey that bound. By Theorem A this treats every gauged
Einstein metric within its neighborhood.

For clarity, an arbitrary nearby Einstein output cannot evade this
argument by a small change of gauge. If \(h\) is close to \(g_m(t_m)\)
in \(X_m\), solve the harmonic map equation from \((M_m,h)\) to
\((M_m,h_m)\) near the identity. At \(h_m\) its Jacobi operator on
vector fields is \(\nabla^*\nabla+1\), so the inverse is uniform;
small coefficient perturbations preserve that bound. Since the
identity is already harmonic for \(g_m(t_m)\), the solution \(F\) satisfies
\[
 \|F-\operatorname{id}\|_{C^{4,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}
                       \le C\|h-g_m(t_m)\|_{X_m}.
\]
It is a diffeomorphism. Its pushforward \(\bar h=(F^{-1})^*h\) is
Einstein and in the gauge (1). The metrics \(g_m(t_m)\) have uniformly
bounded \(C^{4,\alpha}\) norms, so writing
\[
 \bar h-g_m=(F^{-1})^*(h-g_m)
                       +\bigl((F^{-1})^*g_m-g_m\bigr)
\]
gives \(\|\bar h-g_m\|_{X_m}\le C\|h-g_m\|_{X_m}\), with no
derivative loss in this comparison. Taking \(\theta_0\) smaller if
necessary puts \(\bar h\) inside the classified neighborhood whenever
the proposed distance is less than a fixed small multiple of \(t_m\).
The gauged lower bound contradicts such a distance. Outputs outside
that small ball trivially satisfy (7). This proves Theorem B.

Combining (6)--(7), for every \(\theta>1/2\),
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{dist}_{X_m}(g_m,\mathscr E(M_m))}
      {\|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_m}+g_m\|_{Y_m}^{\theta}}
                       \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
In particular the \(m^2\) loss in the linear residual estimate (5)
has matching nonlinear examples.

## 6. Mathematical position

The product spectrum and the integration of surface Teichmüller
directions are classical; see Kröncke above and
[Rupflin--Topping on horizontal hyperbolic metrics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00526-018-1386-y).
Round 18 already proved local completeness at each fixed finite-monodromy
geometry. The increment here is the **uniformly quantified cyclic
regime**: a sharp Hölder-space transverse inverse, an explicit nonlinear
radius, all reduced equations solved by the actual full moduli, and a
nonlinear obstruction to volume-independent strong repair estimates.

There is no contradiction with the preceding positive-coercivity theorem.
This family has exact kernels and scalar slow channels, although its
local geometry is uniformly bounded and its ball growth is only linear.
Nor does this exclude a different existence proof, a weaker repair
modulus, or a theorem retaining additional global control of the slow
coefficients. It identifies a real geometric quantity that such a theorem
must handle; it does not identify another absent gauge zero mode.

`check_integrable_kernel_cyclic_repair.py` checks the curvature-\(-1\)
surface differential identities, the harmonic-circle chain rule,
deck-orbit cancellation and the nonlinear scaling. It also checks the
negative control that localization need not preserve kernel
orthogonality. These finite calculations do not verify the cellwise
elliptic estimates, uniform moduli integration or nonlinear existence
argument.

# Actual harmonic forms force whole-filling Milnor matching

24 September 2026. This note strengthens the realization criterion in
[Orbifold smoothing bridge](20260924_orbifold_smoothing_bridge.md), Theorem C.
It replaces its assumed relative diffeomorphism of singular fillings by a
condition on the **actual harmonic representative and the topology of the
whole filling**. It does not assume that a terminal ALE bubble is the whole
filling, or require a single-scale degeneration.

The new connection in this project is analytic-to-symplectic: actual-form
alignment gives canonical contact boundaries; a zero-free harmonic form
makes the entire filling symplectic; classical filling theorems then force
its relative smooth type. The filling classifications are not new results
of this project. Global Q-Gorenstein smoothability remains a separate input.

## 1. The input and the forced-matching theorem

Let \((M_j,g_j,a_j)\) satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem A of the orbifold
smoothing bridge. Explicitly, the \(M_j\) are closed connected oriented
smooth four-manifolds, each \(a_j\) is an actual conformally solvable
Spin\(^c\) class, and, for fixed \(D,A_0>0\),
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(M_j,g_j)\le D,\qquad
 p_j=(a_j^+)^2\ge A_0,\qquad
 \delta_j+q_j\longrightarrow0,                         \tag{1}
\]
where
\[
 \delta_j=\|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}^{\sharp}+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}},
 \quad q_j=\frac{\mathfrak D_2(g_j,a_j)}{V_j},
 \quad V_j=\operatorname{Vol}(M_j,g_j),
\]
\[
 \mathfrak D_2(g,a)
 =\frac32\int_M(s^2+12|W^+|^2)\,d\mu-72\pi^2(a^+)^2.
\]
The structure determining \(a_j\) is fixed while auxiliary positive
weights vary. No hypothesis on \(\pi_1\) or \(\pi_2\) is imposed.

Pass to the subsequence supplied by that theorem. Its limit is a compact
negative Kähler--Einstein orbifold \((X,h,J,\omega_\infty)\), with finitely
many isolated quotient singularities \(x_\nu\). Choose small disjoint
uniformizing neighborhoods \(U_\nu\), with spherical links \(Y_\nu\),
and retain a collar on each side of \(Y_\nu\) in the regular locus. The
regular-locus embeddings identify the exterior
\[
                  K=X\setminus\bigcup_\nu\operatorname{int}U_\nu
\]
with a submanifold of \(M_j\). Denote its complementary compact pieces by
\(B_{\nu,j}\); each has the actual boundary marking by \(Y_\nu\).
These are the **whole singular fillings**. Any smaller bubbles or necks
inside them are included.

Let \(H_j\) be the harmonic self-dual representative of \(a_j^+\), not
the harmonic representative of the whole class \(a_j\), and put
\[
                         \Omega_j=-2\pi H_j.           \tag{2}
\]

**Theorem 1 (harmonic/contact forced matching).** In the setting (1),
suppose that, for all sufficiently large \(j\),

1. \(H_j\) has no zero on any \(B_{\nu,j}\);
2. for each \(\nu\), either
   - \(x_\nu\) is an \(A_1\) singularity and
     \(b_2(B_{\nu,j};\mathbb Q)=1\), or
   - \(B_{\nu,j}\) is a rational homology four-ball.

Then every \(B_{\nu,j}\), with its actual boundary marking, is oriented
diffeomorphic relative to that marking to a local Q-Gorenstein Milnor
fiber of \((X,x_\nu)\). In the second alternative the singularity is
**necessarily a Wahl singularity**
\[
               \frac1{n_\nu^2}(1,n_\nu a_\nu-1),
 \qquad n_\nu\ge2,
 \quad 0<a_\nu<n_\nu,
 \quad \gcd(n_\nu,a_\nu)=1.                            \tag{3}
\]
In particular, cyclicity and local Q-Gorenstein smoothability need not
be separately assumed in the rational-ball alternative.

The zero-free condition is only qualitative. There is no required uniform
lower bound for \(|H_j|\) on the shrinking pieces. It is a condition on a
specified solution of a linear global elliptic equation, not a proposed
relative diffeomorphism and not a choice of a convenient ALE model.

The proof occupies Sections 2--5. The analytic hypotheses alone do not
presently prove condition 1. In contrast, nonvanishing and the canonical
contact structure on the **fixed regular collars** will be consequences
of (1), rather than additional assumptions.

## 2. Actual harmonic alignment upgrades on every regular collar

**Lemma 2 (strong convergence of the actual harmonic forms).** Using the
same regular-locus embeddings as for the metrics,
\[
             \Omega_j\longrightarrow\omega_\infty
                    \quad\hbox{in }C^{1,\beta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X_{\rm reg})
 \quad\text{for every }\beta<1.                       \tag{4}
\]
This needs neither the core zero-free assumption nor a negative-square
budget.

**Proof.** Set
\[
 r_j=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}+g_j|^2,
 \qquad \kappa_j=r_j+q_j\longrightarrow0.
\]
The inherited actual-form composition, Theorem A, supplies smooth
self-dual forms \(\omega_j\), with \(\|\omega_j\|_\infty\le2\sqrt3\),
such that, for \(\eta_j\downarrow0\),
\[
                \fint|\omega_j-\Omega_j|^2
                          \le49\kappa_j+\eta_j.        \tag{5}
\]
It also supplies the amplitude and first-derivative estimates used in the
orbifold compactification proof. Since \(V_j\le V_0(D)\), (5) is strong
unnormalized \(L^2\) alignment. That proof constructs
\(\omega_j\to\omega_\infty\) strongly in local \(L^2\). Thus the same
local \(L^2\) convergence holds for \(\Omega_j\), with uniform local
\(L^2\) bounds.

On nested regular harmonic-coordinate charts, the metrics converge in
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) and strongly in \(W^{2,p}\) for every finite \(p\).
The forms \(\Omega_j\) solve the actual homogeneous Hodge equation
\[
                  (d+d_{g_j}^*)\Omega_j=0.             \tag{6}
\]
Interior first-order elliptic estimates for (6), initially with exponent
2, give a uniform local \(W^{1,2}\) bound. Sobolev embedding gives a local
\(L^4\) bound. Iterating the same interior estimate gives
\(W^{1,4}\), hence \(L^p\) for every finite \(p\), and then
\(W^{1,p}\) for these \(p\). Constants are uniform on the chosen charts:
their leading coefficients are uniformly \(C^{1,\alpha}\), their
zero-order coefficients uniformly \(C^{0,\alpha}\), and ellipticity is
uniform. All these are properties of the fixed regular charts.

For completeness the last derivative can be obtained from the
Weitzenböck equation. In coordinates its principal coefficients are
uniformly \(C^{1,\alpha}\), its first-order coefficients are bounded, and
its zero-order coefficients consist of curvature and quadratic first
metric derivatives. They are uniformly \(L^p\) because the metrics are
uniformly \(W^{2,p}\). The already obtained \(W^{1,p}\) and \(L^\infty\)
bounds therefore put its right-hand side in \(L^p\). Interior
second-order elliptic estimates give uniform \(W^{2,p}\) bounds for
\(\Omega_j\). Choosing \(p\) large gives precompactness in
\(C^{1,\beta}\) on a smaller chart. Every limit equals
\(\omega_\infty\) by (5), so (4) follows. No derivative of the Ricci
error was taken. \(\square\)

This upgrade is specific to the harmonic representative. Applying the
same conclusion directly to the auxiliary \(\omega_j\), whose small
derivative is initially only an averaged estimate, would not be justified.

## 3. The geometric collar supplies a strong convex filling

**Lemma 3 (canonical contact boundary).** For each sufficiently small
fixed \(Y_\nu\), the restriction of \(\Omega_j\) to its pulled-back
collar is symplectic for large \(j\). It has a primitive whose Liouville
field points outward from \(B_{\nu,j}\), and the induced contact
structure on its boundary is contactomorphic to the canonical contact
structure of the complex quotient singularity \((X,x_\nu)\).
If \(H_j\) is zero-free throughout \(B_{\nu,j}\), then
\((B_{\nu,j},\Omega_j)\) is a strong symplectic filling with that
contact boundary and the quotient-link orientation.

**Proof.** A uniformizing chart is a ball in \(\mathbb C^2\) with a
finite subgroup \(\Gamma\subset U(2)\) acting freely away from the
origin. The limiting smooth invariant Kähler metric has an invariant
strictly plurisubharmonic local potential \(\varphi\). After subtracting
an invariant pluriharmonic term, \(\varphi\) has a positive quadratic
leading part. Small regular level sets provide links \(Y\) transverse
to the radial direction. The convention for \(d^c\) is chosen so that
\(\omega_\infty=dd^c\varphi\). The primitive
\(\alpha_\infty=d^c\varphi\) gives a positive contact form on \(Y\)
with an outward-pointing Liouville field. Its contact structure is the
canonical quotient contact structure: interpolation of the strictly
plurisubharmonic potentials on a sufficiently small chart and Gray
stability identify it with the link of the standard quadratic potential.

Fix a compact collar \(C=Y\times[-b,b]\) in the regular locus. Since
\(Y\) is a spherical space form,
\(H^2(C;\mathbb R)=H^2(Y;\mathbb R)=0\). On a slightly smaller collar
closed two-forms admit a continuous linear right inverse to \(d\).
One concrete construction uses a fixed Hodge primitive on a slice \(Y\)
and integration of the normal contraction along the collar coordinate.
Consequently the closed difference
\(\Omega_j-\omega_\infty\) has primitives \(\gamma_j\to0\) in
\(C^1\), by Lemma 2. Put
\[
                  \alpha_j=\alpha_\infty+\gamma_j,
             \qquad d\alpha_j=\Omega_j.               \tag{7}
\]
The contact and outward-transversality conditions are open; (4) and (7)
preserve them for large \(j\). Gray stability identifies the boundary
contact structures. The collar is fixed first and \(j\) tends to infinity
afterwards; no estimate uniform as its radius tends to zero is asserted.

Finally \(d\Omega_j=0\), and self-duality gives
\[
             \Omega_j\wedge\Omega_j=|\Omega_j|^2\,d\mu_{g_j}.
                                                                    \tag{8}
\]
If the form has no zero on \(B_{\nu,j}\), (8) makes it symplectic on
the entire filling in its existing orientation. Its strong convex boundary
is supplied by (7). In particular the orientation is the singularity-link
orientation, not its reverse. \(\square\)

## 4. Classical filling classification determines the whole manifold

**Lemma 4 (the two filling alternatives).** Let \(B\) be a strong
symplectic filling of the canonical contact link of an isolated complex
quotient surface singularity.

(a) If the singularity is \(A_1\) and \(b_2(B;\mathbb Q)=1\), then
\(B\) is oriented diffeomorphic to the \((-2)\)-disk bundle over
\(S^2\), hence to the \(A_1\) Milnor fiber.

(b) If \(B\) is a rational homology ball, the singularity is Wahl and
\(B\) is oriented diffeomorphic to its Q-Gorenstein Milnor rational
ball.

**Proof.** The link in (a) is canonical contact \(L(2,1)\). McDuff's
filling theorem, stated in the precise form used here in Lisca [L08,
Introduction], says that every such filling is an oriented smooth blowup
of the \((-2)\)-disk bundle. Each blowup increases \(b_2\) by one,
so the assumed value excludes every blowup. The quadratic \(A_1\)
Milnor fiber is the cotangent disk bundle of \(S^2\), with this oriented
smooth type.

For (b), Etnyre--Ozbagci--Tosun [EOT25, Theorem 1.18] applies to a spherical
link **with the quotient-singularity orientation**. It forces the oriented
link to be a lens space
\[
                        L(n^2,na-1),
            \qquad 0<a<n,\quad\gcd(n,a)=1.             \tag{9}
\]
The trivial spherical group is not at issue here, since the point is a
singularity. The fundamental group of the link is the quotient group,
so that group is cyclic. Its faithful complex representation is diagonal
and, because it acts freely on the three-sphere, the complex singularity
is \(1/P(1,Q)\). Oriented lens-space classification gives
\(P=n^2\) and
\[
 Q\equiv na-1\quad\hbox{or}\quad (na-1)^{-1}\pmod{n^2}.
\]
The inverse is \(n(n-a)-1\) modulo \(n^2\); either representation is
a Wahl singularity, differing at most by interchanging its coordinates.

For the standard contact structure on (9), the symplectic rational-ball
filling is unique up to symplectic deformation, in particular up to
oriented diffeomorphism: [GS22, Proposition A.2], derived from Lisca's
classification. Its known representative is the Wahl Milnor rational
ball. The latter is a Stein filling and the \(d=1\) case of the
Q-Gorenstein deformation model in [S11, Proposition 2.3]. This identifies
the entire \(B\), not one selected ALE subdomain. \(\square\)

## 5. Arbitrary actual boundary markings extend

Classification up to unmarked diffeomorphism alone would leave the old
gluing issue unresolved. In the preceding two alternatives that issue
can be settled explicitly.

**Lemma 5 (extension of the boundary correction).** Every
orientation-preserving self-diffeomorphism of the boundary of an
\(A_1\) Milnor fiber or a Wahl Milnor rational ball extends to an
orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of that filling.

**Proof.** For \(A_1\), the boundary is \(\mathbb{RP}^3\). Bonahon
[B83, Theorem 3(e)] implies that every orientation-preserving
self-diffeomorphism is isotopic to the identity: the other component
reverses orientation. Extend such an isotopy through a collar, taking
the identity farther inside.

For a Wahl point write \(P=n^2\), \(Q=na-1\). If \(n>2\),
\[
 Q^2\equiv1-2na\pmod{n^2},\qquad Q^2\not\equiv\pm1\pmod{n^2}.
                                                                    \tag{10}
\]
Indeed the plus case would imply \(n\mid2a\), hence \(n\mid2\),
and the minus case already implies \(n\mid2\) after reducing modulo
\(n\). Bonahon's Theorem 3(a) gives two mapping classes, represented
by the identity and simultaneous complex conjugation. For \(n=2\),
\(a=1\), \((P,Q)=(4,1)\), and Theorem 3(c) gives exactly the same
conclusion. Both maps preserve three-dimensional orientation.

Use the standard real-parameter Wahl Milnor model
\[
 F_{n,a,t}
  =\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb C^3:xy=z^n+t\}/\mu_n,
                 \qquad t>0,                           \tag{11}
\]
cut off by a sufficiently small invariant Milnor ball. The action is
\[
       \xi\cdot(x,y,z)=(\xi x,\xi^{-1}y,\xi^a z),
                  \qquad\xi^n=1.                       \tag{12}
\]
It is free on the smooth fiber. Complex conjugation preserves (11),
normalizes (12) by \(\xi\mapsto\xi^{-1}\), and preserves the cutoff.
It thus extends over the quotient fiber. It preserves the real
orientation because the fiber has complex dimension two. The usual
equivariant Milnor collar identification, along real parameters, identifies
its boundary action with conjugation on the quotient link. Such a collar
identification can be made equivariant by averaging its horizontal vector
field over conjugation before integrating along the real parameter. In particular
it acts by \(-1\) on \(H_1(L(n^2,na-1);\mathbb Z)\), and represents
the nontrivial class in Bonahon's list. Every boundary diffeomorphism is
isotopic to one of these two extendible maps; its remaining isotopy extends
through a collar. \(\square\)

**Completion of Theorem 1.** Lemmas 2 and 3 give canonical strong
symplectic fillings. Lemma 4 supplies oriented diffeomorphisms from those
whole fillings to the appropriate Milnor fibers. Compare the boundary
map of any such diffeomorphism with the actual exterior marking and the
Milnor marking. Their difference is an orientation-preserving
self-diffeomorphism of the link. Extend that correction by Lemma 5 and
compose. Collar adjustment makes the maps agree on neighborhoods of the
boundaries, so they glue smoothly to the given exterior identification.
This is exactly the relative matching required in the previous Theorem C.
\(\square\)

## 6. A realization theorem and a zero-set obstruction

**Corollary 6 (negative KE realization with forced local matching).**
Assume (1) and the two hypotheses of Theorem 1. If the limiting surface
\(X\) has a projective Q-Gorenstein smoothing, then every sufficiently
large \(M_j\) in this subsequence admits a negative Kähler--Einstein
metric, on its original smooth structure.

A separately checkable sufficient condition for this global smoothing is
\[
                 H^2(Z,T_Z(-\log E))=0,                \tag{13}
\]
where \(Z\to X\) is a resolution with reduced exceptional divisor
\(E\), as in Lee--Park's global smoothing criterion [LP07, Theorem 2.1].
The local class-T condition in that criterion holds by Theorem 1.

**Proof.** Fix a small regular parameter in a global smoothing. Its
exterior is diffeomorphic to \(K\) by Ehresmann trivialization, and the
local fibers are the \(A_1\) or Wahl fibers in Theorem 1. The boundary
extension argument makes its gluing match the actual gluing in \(M_j\).
Thus \(M_j\) is oriented diffeomorphic to the smooth algebraic fiber.
The negative KE orbifold metric makes \(K_X\) ample. A Cartier multiple
of the relative canonical class remains ample on nearby smooth fibers;
the Aubin--Yau theorem supplies their negative KE metrics. Pullback gives
the metric on \(M_j\). This is Theorem C of the preceding note with its
local matching premise now proved, rather than assumed. \(\square\)

**Corollary 7 (topology forces a zero of the actual harmonic form).**
Under (1), if a whole filling \(B_{\nu,j}\) is a rational homology ball
but the limiting singularity is not Wahl, then, for large \(j\),
\(H_j\) has a zero in the interior of that filling. Its boundary collar
is nevertheless zero-free.

**Proof.** Zero-freeness would give a strong symplectic rational-ball
filling by Lemma 3, contradicting Lemma 4(b). Lemma 2 excludes zeros on
the fixed collar. \(\square\)

This is a genuine restriction on how the actual class can pass through
singular topology. It is not an Einstein nonexistence theorem: a zero
inside a shrinking core can be consistent with all the averaged estimates.

## 7. Nonempty local models and the precise gain

The additional geometric condition occurs in actual smooth metrics, not
just formal cohomology. On an \(A_1\) Milnor fiber the Eguchi--Hanson
Kähler form is closed, self-dual and nowhere zero. It supplies the first
filling alternative. On each Wahl Milnor fiber, the Ricci-flat Kähler
metrics supplied by Şuvaina [S11, Theorem A] have the same properties and
supply the rational-ball alternative. Their Kähler forms have canonical
convex boundaries on sufficiently large truncated ends. These are
classical local calibration examples, not new global Yau26 constructions.

For a global negative KE input with its compatible canonical Spin\(^c\)
class, \(\Omega=-2\pi H\) is exactly the Kähler form and has no zeros;
the two defects in (1) vanish. Accordingly, whenever a classical negative
KE degeneration has \(A_1\) Milnor neighborhoods or Wahl Milnor rational
balls, this criterion passes its exact-geometry test. We do not infer a
bounded-diameter degenerating sequence merely from the existence of a
local ALE model.

The topological restrictions in Theorem 1 cannot simply be dropped from
the **symplectic classification step**. Canonical contact \(L(4,1)\)
has both the \((-4)\)-disk-bundle filling and the Wahl rational-ball
filling, as recorded by McDuff/Lisca [L08, Introduction]. Both are
symplectic but have different second Betti numbers. Contact geometry
alone therefore does not select the Milnor rational ball. This is not
being offered as a counterexample satisfying the global near-Einstein
hypotheses (1).

The present result goes beyond classifying a terminal bubble in three
specific ways: it controls the whole actual filling, derives the relevant
contact boundary from the actual gauge-theoretic form, and removes every
boundary-marking ambiguity in the two stated cases. Within these
topological alternatives, it leaves two additional questions: whether the
actual harmonic form is zero-free in the core, and whether the local
smoothings globalize. Neither is replaced by the assertion that an ALE
limit exists.

# Thin-fiber monodromy, global reduction, and Einstein realization

24 September 2026. This note removes the reference-fiber thickness
hypothesis from the preceding cycle theorem, at the cost of an explicit
alternative: finite monodromy or a common invariant essential multicurve.
For irreducible monodromy the alternative gives exact negative
Kähler–Einstein existence on the same smooth total space. For reducible
monodromy it gives an actual decomposition of the surface bundle, not an
assertion that its pieces already have compatible Einstein metrics.

The proof uses classical hyperbolic collars, quasiconformal length
distortion, and compactness of thick moduli. It does not invoke an
unverified Teichmüller Margulis theorem or claim novelty for these
classical facts. The project-level extension is their composition with
the actual cycle budget and pointwise Ricci input. The previous
[cycle theorem](20260924_cycle_holonomy_realization.md) and
[Ricci theorem](20260924_ricci_monodromy_realization.md) are inputs.
Their finite-monodromy realization is retained, not rediscovered.

## 1. Statements with original geometric inputs

Let
\[
 F_f\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\pi}B_b,\qquad f,b\ge2,
\tag{1}
\]
be a smooth oriented bundle of closed oriented surfaces. Its monodromy
is \(\rho:\pi_1(B,b_0)\to\operatorname{Mod}(F_f)\). A multicurve
means a finite nonempty set of distinct isotopy classes of disjoint
essential simple closed curves. Call the image of \(\rho\)
**irreducible** when it preserves no such multicurve setwise. This is
a property of the entire group; it is not a claim that each element is
pseudo-Anosov or that the image is infinite.

For any smooth metric \(g\) use its orthogonal Ehresmann connection.
For a loop \(\gamma\) based at \(b_0\), let \(\Phi_t\) denote
actual horizontal transport and set
\[
 h_t=\Phi_t^*(g|_{F_{\gamma(t)}}),\qquad
 A_t=h_t^{-1}\dot h_t,\qquad
 A_t^0=A_t-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A_t)I.
\]
For a finite based generating system \(\Gamma\) define
\[
 \mathcal J_g(\gamma)
   =\frac12\int_0^1\sup_{F_{b_0}}\|A_t^0\|_{\rm op}\,dt,
 \qquad
 \mathcal J_g(\Gamma)=\max_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathcal J_g(\gamma).
\tag{2}
\]
All loops in \(\Gamma\) have the same base point. No representative
chosen merely to make the mapping-class displacement small replaces
the transport in (2).

**Theorem A (global thin-fiber alternative).** There is a constant
\(\mu_f>0\), depending only on fiber genus, such that
\[
                         \mathcal J_g(\Gamma)<\mu_f             \tag{3}
\]
has the following consequence. Either the monodromy image is finite,
or it preserves a multicurve \(C\) with at most \(3f-3\) components.
In the latter case \(C\) can be chosen so that every component has
length less than \(1\) in the curvature \(-1\) uniformization of
the reference fiber.

If the monodromy is irreducible, it therefore has finite image and the
same smooth \(M\) carries a metric \(\widehat g\) with
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_{\widehat g}=-\widehat g,\qquad
 (\widetilde M,\widetilde{\widehat g})
     \cong\mathbb H^2_{-1}\times\mathbb H^2_{-1}.              \tag{4}
\]
The output is Kähler in both orientations. There is no reference
systole assumption, no prescribed Einstein background, and no claim
that the input metric is already Einstein or close to the output.
The constant is independent of base genus and generator count.

The alternatives need not be disjoint: a finite group can preserve a
multicurve. The assertion relevant to an infinite image is that one
**common** multicurve is preserved by every element, not merely that
each generator has its own reduction system.

**Theorem B (pointwise Ricci realization without a thick fiber).**
Suppose \(\pi:(M,g)\to(B,k)\) is a Riemannian submersion with
minimal fibers. Intrinsic fiber curvature is unrestricted and the
horizontal distribution need not be integrable. Define
\[
 \delta=\|\operatorname{Ric}_g^{\sharp}+I\|_{\infty,\rm op},
 \quad \eta=\|(K_k+1)_+\|_\infty,
 \quad D_B=\operatorname{diam}(B,k).
\tag{5}
\]
If
\[
                         2D_B\sqrt{\delta+\eta}<\mu_f,          \tag{6}
\]
then the finite-or-reducible conclusion of Theorem A holds. In
particular, if \(\rho\) is irreducible, (4) holds on the original
smooth total space. For a hyperbolic base \(K_k=-1\), this is the
sufficient condition
\[
                   \delta<\frac{\mu_f^2}{4D_B^2}.             \tag{7}
\]
Consequently every such submersion with **infinite irreducible**
monodromy satisfies the input obstruction
\[
                   (\delta+\eta)D_B^2\ge\frac{\mu_f^2}{4}.   \tag{8}
\]
This concerns the specified submersion metrics, not arbitrary metrics
on \(M\). In the irreducible small-input case it nevertheless proves
the exact Einstein existence requested by Yau26 for that input class.
No total-space diameter, volume, injectivity-radius or auxiliary
monopole assumption is used.

## 2. A simultaneous short-curve lemma

On Teichmüller space use
\(d_T(x,y)=\tfrac12\inf\log K\). For a curve class \(c\), write
\(\ell_x(c)\) for its hyperbolic geodesic length.

Two classical facts enter the proof. First, the collar theorem implies
that all simple geodesics of length at most \(1\) are pairwise
disjoint. Indeed the collar inequality for intersecting simple
geodesics gives
\(\sinh(\ell(c)/2)\sinh(\ell(d)/2)>1\), whereas
\(\sinh^2(1/2)<1\). Thus there are at most
\[
                              N_f=3f-3                         \tag{9}
\]
such classes. Primary sources are Keen,
[*Collars on Riemann surfaces*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400881642-021),
and Buser,
[*The collar theorem and examples*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01168048).

Second, Wolpert's quasiconformal length inequality gives
\[
 e^{-2d_T(x,y)}\ell_x(c)
       \le\ell_y(c)\le e^{2d_T(x,y)}\ell_x(c).                 \tag{10}
\]
See [*The length spectra as moduli for compact Riemann surfaces*](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1979/109-2/p03).
The needed normalization can also be checked directly. The cyclic
cover corresponding to \(c\), with core length \(\ell\), is a
conformal annulus of modulus \(\pi/\ell\). A \(K\)-quasiconformal
map lifts to the corresponding cyclic covers and changes annular
modulus by a factor between \(K^{-1}\) and \(K\). Take the infimum
over marked maps to obtain (10). This argument applies to every
essential simple class, not just a chosen pants decomposition.

Put
\[
 a_f=\frac{\log2}{N_f+1}=\frac{\log2}{3f-2},
 \qquad \nu_f=\frac{a_f}{4}.                                 \tag{11}
\]

**Lemma 1 (a common reduction, with no generator-count bound).**
Suppose \(\operatorname{sys}(x)<1/2\), and let \(S\) be any set
of mapping classes satisfying
\[
                          d_T(x,sx)<\nu_f\quad(s\in S).        \tag{12}
\]
Then \(\langle S\rangle\) preserves a nonempty multicurve all of
whose \(x\)-lengths are less than \(1\).

**Proof.** In the interval \([-\log2,0]\), list the logarithms of
the lengths in \([1/2,1]\). There are at most \(N_f\) entries,
including repetitions if distinct curves have equal lengths. Insert
the two endpoints. Among the resulting at most \(N_f+1\) gaps there
is one \((u,v)\) with
\[
                            v-u\ge a_f.                       \tag{13}
\]
There is no curve length with logarithm in this open interval. Let
\(t=\exp((u+v)/2)\) and define
\[
                            C=\{c:\ell_x(c)<t\}.              \tag{14}
\]
It is nonempty because the systole is less than \(1/2\), and it is
a multicurve by the collar theorem. Every member of \(C\) has
length at most \(e^u\). The action convention may invert \(s\)
in (10), but \(d_T(x,s^{-1}x)=d_T(x,sx)\), so
\[
 \ell_x(sc)\le e^{2d_T(x,sx)}\ell_x(c)
     < e^{a_f/2}e^u\le e^{(u+v)/2}=t.                        \tag{15}
\]
Hence \(sC\subset C\). Since \(s\) is a bijection of curve
classes and \(C\) is finite, \(sC=C\). The same \(C\) works for
all generators, and is therefore preserved by their entire group.
Repeated length values and curves lying at the endpoints of the gap
cause no problem: the strict inequality in (12) supplies the strict
inequality in (15). \(\square\)

This is a length-gap argument, not an assertion that small moves of
the generators make every group element a small move. Long words
may accumulate arbitrarily large displacement.

**Lemma 2 (thick finite-isotropy neighbourhood).** Some
\(\varepsilon_{f,1/2}>0\) has the property that, if
\(\operatorname{sys}(x)\ge1/2\) and
\(d_T(x,sx)<\varepsilon_{f,1/2}\) for every \(s\in S\), then
\(\langle S\rangle\) is finite.

**Proof.** Mumford compactness gives a compact set in Teichmüller
space meeting every orbit with this systole bound. If the assertion
failed, conjugate to obtain \(x_j\to x\) in that compact set and
sets \(S_j\) generating infinite groups, with all displacements
less than \(1/j\). Uniformly in \(s\in S_j\),
\[
                  d_T(x,sx)\le2d_T(x,x_j)+1/j\longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]
Properness of the mapping-class action and properness of the
finite-dimensional Teichmüller metric imply that the set of mapping
classes moving \(x\) by at most \(1\) is finite. Its positive
displacements have a positive minimum. Thus for large \(j\) every
member of \(S_j\) fixes \(x\). The stabilizer is finite, giving a
contradiction. A finite ineffective kernel, such as the genus-two
hyperelliptic involution, does not affect this argument. This is the
lemma proved in the preceding cycle note, with \(\iota=1/2\).
The classical compactness source is Mumford,
[*A remark on Mahler's compactness theorem*](https://doi.org/10.2307/2037802).
\(\square\)

Fix once and for all
\[
                \mu_f=\min\{\varepsilon_{f,1/2},\nu_f\}>0.   \tag{17}
\]
Lemmas 1–2 give the following purely geometric alternative for every
\(x\in\mathcal T_f\): the subgroup generated by any collection of
\(\mu_f\)-small moves is finite or has a common short multicurve.
The second conclusion is not replaced by “each generator is
reducible.”

## 3. From the actual connection to the group alternative

For completeness the differential-to-discrete step has no compactness
hypothesis. For two fixed nonzero tangent vectors on the reference
fiber,
\[
 \left|\frac d{dt}\log\frac{h_t(v,v)}{h_t(w,w)}\right|
                      \le2\|A_t^0\|_{\rm op}.                \tag{18}
\]
Integration bounds the logarithm of the quasiconformal dilatation of
the identity between \(h_0\) and \(h_1\) by
\(\int\sup\|A_t^0\|_{\rm op}\,dt\). The endpoint metric is
the pullback by the actual return map, which represents
\(\rho(\gamma)\). Therefore
\[
                 d_T(x_0,\rho(\gamma)x_0)
                                   \le\mathcal J_g(\gamma).  \tag{19}
\]
Noncommuting shape operators and a curved connection are allowed.
The mapping classes of return maps are invariant under based
homotopy and compose as monodromy, even though the actual return
diffeomorphisms need not form a representation.

Apply (17) to (19) for the generating system. This proves the
alternative in Theorem A. If \(\rho\) is irreducible, the short
multicurve is impossible, so the image \(G\) is finite. Kerckhoff's
[Nielsen realization theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1983/117-2/p02)
realizes \(G\) by orientation-preserving isometries of one hyperbolic
fiber. On the connected kernel cover \(\widehat B\to B\), use a
lifted hyperbolic base metric. The diagonal action on
\(\widehat B\times F_f\) is free, regardless of fixed points of
the fiber action, since its base action is free. The product metric
descends with Ricci tensor equal to minus the metric.

This construction gives the original smooth bundle, not just a
manifold with an isomorphic group. For \(f\ge2\), Earle–Eells
contractibility of \(\operatorname{Diff}_0^+(F_f)\) gives
\(B\operatorname{Diff}^+(F_f)\simeq B\operatorname{Mod}(F_f)\).
The two bundles with the same monodromy are therefore smoothly
bundle-isomorphic. Pull back the product-quotient metric. The
product complex structures with either sign on the fiber descend,
giving (4) in both orientations. This concludes Theorem A.
The primary contractibility source is Earle–Eells,
[*The diffeomorphism group of a compact Riemann surface*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9904-1967-11746-4).

## 4. Ricci input and the scale of irreducible monodromy

For a horizontal vector \(X\), write
\[
 g(S_XU,V)=\langle(\nabla_UV)^{\mathcal H},X\rangle,
 \qquad A_XY=\tfrac12[X,Y]^{\mathcal V}.
\]
Minimality gives \(\operatorname{tr}S_X=0\). The classical
horizontal Ricci identity, already used in retained Round 20, is
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_g(X,X)
      =K_k-|S_X|_{\rm HS}^2-2\sum_a|A_Xe_a|^2
                      \quad(|X|=1).                         \tag{20}
\]
The mean-curvature derivative term vanishes because the fibers are
pointwise minimal. The source is O'Neill,
[*The fundamental equations of a submersion*](https://doi.org/10.1307/mmj/1028999604).
Equation (5) consequently implies
\[
                         |S_X|_{\rm op}
                               \le\sqrt{\delta+\eta}\,|X|.  \tag{21}
\]
Horizontal transport differentiates the vertical metric by
\(h_t^{-1}\dot h_t=-2\Phi_t^*S_{\dot\gamma^{\mathcal H}}\).
Thus (2) gives
\[
                    \mathcal J_g(\gamma)
                          \le\sqrt{\delta+\eta}\,
                                      \operatorname{Length}_k(\gamma).
\tag{22}
\]

For each \(a>0\), a finite based generating system of a closed
Riemannian manifold can be chosen with lengths below
\(2\operatorname{diam}+a\). One direct proof is to subdivide any
loop into sufficiently short arcs and join the subdivision points
to the base point by paths of length at most the diameter. Each
resulting loop has length below \(2\operatorname{diam}+a\).
The set of their possible homotopy classes is finite: in the proper
universal cover it is represented by deck transformations whose
orbit points lie in a fixed compact ball. These classes generate
the fundamental group. No bound on their number is needed here.

Under (6), choose \(a>0\) small enough that
\((2D_B+a)\sqrt{\delta+\eta}<\mu_f\) and apply Theorem A to
(22). If \(\delta+\eta=0\), every cycle budget is zero and the
same argument applies directly. This proves Theorem B and its
contrapositive (8).

There is also an explicit thickness consequence, without the implicit
constant in Mumford compactness. If \(\rho\) is irreducible and
*some* fiber has conformal hyperbolic systole less than \(1/2\),
base the short generators there. Lemma 1 and (22) give
\[
 (\delta+\eta)D_B^2
                  \ge\frac{(\log2)^2}{64(3f-2)^2}.            \tag{23}
\]
Hence, below this scale, irreducibility forces every fiber to be
conformally thick. This is not a lower bound for four-dimensional
ball volumes. It identifies the missing information in the earlier
thick-fiber theorem: here a global topological property supplies the
fiber thickness needed for finite-monodromy detection.

## 5. The reducible alternative is a genuine global decomposition

**Proposition 3 (fiberwise realization of the invariant multicurve).**
If \(\rho\) preserves a multicurve \(C\) of size \(r\), then
\(M\) contains a smooth submanifold \(\mathcal C\) whose
projection to \(B\) is a bundle with fiber the corresponding union
of \(r\) circles. After a connected finite base cover of degree at
most \(2^r r!\), its circle components and orientations can be
labeled globally. Cutting the pulled-back total space along them
gives genuine surface bundles with boundary over that base cover.

**Proof.** Isotopy classes of curves in the fibers form a discrete
local system. The monodromy-invariant set \(C\) gives a global
section of the associated local system of multicurves. Uniformize
the vertical metric in each fiber to curvature \(-1\). These
metrics vary smoothly with the base: the conformal curvature equation
has an invertible linearization at its negative-curvature solution,
and uniqueness makes the local solutions agree.

In each fiber take the unique geodesic representatives of the
specified classes. In a local bundle chart the classes are fixed.
Their unparametrized geodesic representatives depend smoothly on
the hyperbolic metric: the periodic normal Jacobi operator along
a closed hyperbolic geodesic has no kernel. The representatives
are disjoint, and uniqueness makes the local families agree under
changes of chart. Their union is the required embedded smooth
\(\mathcal C\). It is compact and its projection is a submersion,
so it is a bundle by local triviality for proper submersions.

The action on oriented components maps \(\pi_1(B)\) to the
signed permutation group of order \(2^r r!\). Pass to the
connected cover associated with its kernel. An orientation of a
curve, together with the fiber orientation, orients its normal
line. Thus every boundary side obtained by cutting is labeled.
Every component of a cut connected surface meets some such boundary
side; consequently the components are also fixed. Fiberwise tubular
neighborhoods and cutting now produce the stated smooth bundles
with boundary. No assertion that the circle bundles are trivial is
needed or made. \(\square\)

This decomposition keeps rather than discards residual monodromy.
After the finite cover, let \(S_v\) be the cut fiber components and
let \(\overline S_v\) be the punctured surfaces obtained by capping
their boundary circles with marked punctures. Restriction defines
\[
 \rho_v:\pi_1(\overline B)\longrightarrow
                              \operatorname{PMod}(\overline S_v).
\tag{24}
\]
For the subgroup fixing curves and their sides, the kernel of the
product restriction map is precisely the free abelian group
\[
                         \mathcal T_C\cong\mathbb Z^r        \tag{25}
\]
of Dehn twists about \(C\). A representative trivial after capping
can be isotoped to the identity off annular neighborhoods; its
remaining annular mapping classes are integer twists. Conversely
all those twists vanish after capping, and twists in distinct
essential nonparallel curves are independent. This is the standard
multicurve restriction sequence; see Hubbard–Koch, Proposition 2.5
of [*An analytic construction of the Deligne–Mumford compactification
of the moduli space of curves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.0062).

Thus the remaining data are the representations (24) **and** the
annular twist data (25). If the joint image in (24) is finite, a
further finite base cover reduces the image to \(\mathcal T_C\).
Theorems A–B do not prove that additional premise. A common
multicurve alone cannot replace it: a mapping class supported on a
once-punctured torus with homological action
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
is pseudo-Anosov on that component and extends by the identity
across its boundary. It preserves the boundary curve but is not
virtually a multitwist. This example explains the retained
restriction data; it is not claimed to satisfy the smallness
constant in (3).

In particular, neither this reduction nor a local bidisk description
solves the Einstein matching problem along \(\mathcal C\).
No invariant multicurve is silently promoted to a global marked
product or to finite monodromy.

## 6. Actual tests of both alternatives

### 6.1 A nonempty finite irreducible realization class

Finite image is compatible with irreducibility. On the smooth genus-two
curve
\[
                           y^2=x^5-1
\tag{26}
\]
consider \(\phi(x,y)=(\zeta_5x,y)\). Its action on holomorphic
one-forms \(dx/y,x\,dx/y\) has eigenvalues \(\zeta_5,\zeta_5^2\),
and its action on \(H^1(F;\mathbb Q)\) has the irreducible
characteristic polynomial
\(\Phi_5(t)=t^4+t^3+t^2+t+1\).

If a multicurve were preserved, its at most three components would
each be fixed by this order-five action. A nonseparating component
would give an invariant rational line in \(H_1\), impossible by
irreducibility of \(\Phi_5\). A separating component divides the
genus-two surface into two one-holed tori. The order-five action
cannot exchange these two sides, and their homology would give a
proper invariant rational two-plane, also impossible. Thus
\(\langle\phi\rangle\) is irreducible.

Map a surface group onto this cyclic group, take its kernel cover,
and form the diagonal product quotient. It satisfies (1) with
nontrivial finite irreducible monodromy. The quotient product metric
has \(\delta=\eta=\mathcal J=0\). Small, base-dependent vertical
metric perturbations with fixed fiber area form, supported over a
base chart, give genuinely nonproduct minimal submersion inputs
with \(\delta\to0\). For example, exponentiate a smooth
trace-free self-adjoint vertical endomorphism with a small
base-dependent coefficient, leaving the horizontal and base
metrics fixed. The determinant and hence vertical area form stay
fixed; the full Ricci tensor varies smoothly. These inputs need
not be Einstein, but the conclusion still concerns the same
smooth bundle.

### 6.2 The thin Dehn-twist family realizes the other branch

The retained fixed bundle \(E_D\), with monodromy
\(\rho(\gamma)=D^{u(\gamma)}\) for one nonseparating Dehn twist,
provides an actual infinite reducible example. Section 6 of the
preceding cycle note gives, on a fixed hyperbolic base and with
minimal intrinsically hyperbolic fibers, metrics \(g_\lambda\)
such that
\[
 \delta_-(g_\lambda)=O(\lambda),\qquad
 \mathcal J_{g_\lambda}(\Gamma)=O(\lambda),\qquad
              \operatorname{sys}(x_\lambda)\le\lambda\to0.
\tag{27}
\]
The full Ricci matrix, not merely the horizontal block, was computed
there. These metrics also have actual opposite-orientation monopole
classes and bilateral near-saturation; they are not formal
almost-parallel tensor examples.

Here the common invariant curve is precisely available as the
twisting curve. The monodromy remains infinite cyclic however
small the budget becomes. With fiber genus \(f\) and base genus
\(b\), the split fundamental-group extension and the rank-one
transvection on fiber homology give
\[
                              b_1(E_D)=2f+2b-1.               \tag{28}
\]
It is odd, so \(E_D\) admits no Kähler structure. Consequently
one cannot replace the finite-or-reducible alternative by KE
realization for every small-budget bundle. This is not a claim
that \(E_D\) has no general Einstein metric. The retained
example is used as a test, not counted as a new construction.

### 6.3 A signature-zero infinite irreducible test

Choose a pants decomposition \(P\) of \(F_f\) and, for each
component \(p\), a simple transverse curve \(q_p\) supported in
the adjacent one-holed torus or four-holed sphere. The collection
\(P\cup\{q_p\}\) fills: a curve disjoint from \(P\) is
isotopic to a pants curve, and that class meets its transverse
curve. Let \(G\) be generated by the twists in this collection.

The group \(G\) is infinite and irreducible. Indeed, if it
preserved a finite multicurve \(C\), a power of each generating
twist would fix every member \(c\) of \(C\). The annular
intersection formula
\[
                    i(T_a^n c,c)=|n|\,i(a,c)^2               \tag{29}
\]
forces \(i(a,c)=0\) for every generating curve \(a\). The
filling property rules out such an essential \(c\). Infinitude
already follows from one nontrivial twist.

Realize this representation through a free quotient of a base
surface group: use one free generator for each twist, send one
member of each standard handle pair to that generator and the
other to the identity. Actual twist diffeomorphisms give a
suspension bundle. The representation extends over a handlebody,
so the total four-manifold bounds the associated oriented
five-dimensional bundle and has signature zero.

The inherited free-quotient construction in
[Round 20, Section 2](../mathematics/current/round20_construction.md)
already constructs \(\delta_-\to0\) metrics of the required
minimal hyperbolic-base submersion type on these fixed bundles.
No such family is new here. What (8) newly supplies in the project
is the thickness-independent bound
\[
             \operatorname{diam}(B,k_j)
                         \ge\frac{\mu_f}{2\sqrt{\delta_-(g_j)}}.
\tag{30}
\]
It remains valid even if one tries to degenerate the conformal
fibers simultaneously. The earlier signature barrier is zero on
these objects; the earlier cycle theorem required a thick
reference fiber. Irreducibility detects a global obstruction to
small-cycle deformation that both numerical signature and
uncontrolled fiber pinching miss. Equation (30) is an obstruction
to a bounded-base-scale input, not to Einstein existence on the
underlying total manifold.

## 7. What has changed in the first-line realization mechanism

The previous sufficient condition used a conformally thick fiber
to convert small cycle deformation into finite monodromy. The new
argument distinguishes two ways of escaping that step. In thin
moduli, small simultaneous displacement forces a common short
multicurve; in thick moduli it forces finite image. Thus global
irreducibility supplies the required thickness rather than
assuming it. Under the submersion hypotheses this is a genuine
Ricci-input-to-Einstein-output theorem, with a genus-only constant
and no fiber systole in the statement.

The reducible case produces a smooth global cutting locus and
retains both component and annular monodromy. It is the specific
alternative exhibited by the actual \(E_D\) near-Einstein
family. A further theorem controlling those remaining transition
data would be needed for an Einstein realization there. The
current conclusion neither presumes that such a theorem holds
nor treats the existence problem as settled by local products.

The substantive inputs and their roles are separate: hyperbolic
collars and quasiconformal length distortion give Lemma 1;
Mumford compactness gives Lemma 2; O'Neill gives (20);
Nielsen realization and contractibility of \(\operatorname{Diff}_0\)
give exact output once finite monodromy has actually been detected.
No new gauge solvability or compactness theorem is asserted by
this composition.

# Exact nonlinear slow-mode energy and fixed-amplitude adiabatic metrics

24 September 2026. This note replaces a linearized fiber deformation by an
actual horizontal curve of hyperbolic metrics. It determines every Ricci
block, the complete nonlinear slow equation, and its normal feedback. The
resulting energy identity remains valid at fixed, nonzero moduli amplitude.
On cyclic covers it produces pointwise and unnormalized integral Ricci
errors tending to zero while that amplitude does not shrink.

The full-mode theorem in
[I33](20260924_integrable_kernel_cyclic_repair.md) concerns arbitrary
tensor perturbations in a shrinking neighborhood and measures distance to
**all** normalized Einstein metrics. The present theorem treats a larger
nonlinear, geometrically specified family, but its fixed-distance conclusion
concerns fiber-preserving product outputs. Neither conclusion replaces the
other. The underlying smooth products already carry exact Einstein metrics.

The submersion identities and the exact adapted rigidity statement were
already available in [Round 20](../mathematics/current/round20_construction.md).
They are not counted as new results. The additions here are the exact
arclength equation, the full residual-energy identity including normal
acceleration, and its fixed-amplitude application. The companion
[actual-gauge identity](20260924_moduli_energy_gauge.md) places these metrics
inside the first line's actual bilateral monopole near-saturation regime.

## 1. An actual, horizontally marked hyperbolic family

Let \(F\) be a closed oriented surface of genus at least two. Fix a smooth
curve \(h_v\), defined on an open interval containing a compact interval
\(J\), of metrics of Gaussian curvature \(-1\). Require
\[
 k_v=\partial_vh_v,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}_{h_v}k_v=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{div}_{h_v}k_v=0,\qquad
 \int_F|k_v|_{h_v}^2\,dA_{h_v}=1.
 \tag{1}
\]
Such nonconstant curves exist. A regular curve in Teichmüller space has a
horizontal lift; after reparametrization by its real-tensor \(L^2\)
arclength it satisfies (1). Rupflin--Topping, Definition 1.1 and the
discussion of equation (1.1) in
[*Horizontal curves of hyperbolic metrics*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00526-018-1386-y),
give the horizontal decomposition. Horizontality is equivalently the real
part of a holomorphic quadratic differential. Its length is a fixed
normalization of Weil--Petersson length; no conventional WP constant is
silently inserted into (1).

Pointwise trace-freeness implies that all \(h_v\) have the **same area
form**, not just the same total area. Write this form as \(dA_F\), and set
\[
 V_F=\int_FdA_F=4\pi(\operatorname{genus}(F)-1),\qquad
 N_v=\partial_vk_v-k_vh_v^{-1}k_v,
\]
\[
 C_4(v)=\int_F|k_v|_{h_v}^4dA_F,\qquad
 C_N(v)=\int_F|N_v|_{h_v}^2dA_F,\qquad
 \Xi(v)=\frac{C_4(v)}{16}+\frac{C_N(v)}4.
 \tag{2}
\]
Here a juxtaposition such as \(k_vh_v^{-1}k_v\) denotes contraction as
bilinear forms. All these are tensors and functions of the actual marked
metrics on \(F\), not only formal Teichmüller coordinates. Differentiating
the trace and unit-speed conditions gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}_{h_v}N_v=0,\qquad
 \int_F\langle N_v,k_v\rangle_{h_v}dA_F=0,
 \qquad \Xi(v)\ge\frac1{16V_F}.
 \tag{3}
\]
For example, with \(A=h_v^{-1}k_v\), differentiation of the squared
\(L^2\) norm yields
\(0=2\int_F(\langle\partial_vk_v,k_v\rangle-\operatorname{tr}A^3)\).
This proves the middle identity. The last follows from Cauchy--Schwarz and
\(\int_F|k_v|^2=1\). These identities do not assume that the curve is a
WP geodesic. In particular, its normal acceleration is not discarded.

Let \((B,\gamma)\) be any closed connected oriented Riemannian surface,
and let \(v:B\to J\) be smooth. On the original smooth product define
\[
             G=\gamma+h_{v(x)},\qquad E=\operatorname{Ric}_G+G.
 \tag{4}
\]
The marking in (1) defines a flat horizontal distribution. Throughout,
\(\Delta_\gamma=-\operatorname{div}_\gamma\nabla\) is nonnegative,
and tensor norms without an indicated average are unnormalized norms.

## 2. Complete nonlinear equation and residual energy

**Theorem A.** For (1)--(4), all Ricci-error blocks are exactly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{HH}&=(K_\gamma+1)\gamma
                      -\tfrac14|k_v(y)|_{h_v}^2\,dv\otimes dv,\\
 E_{HV}&=0,\\
 E_{VV}&=\tfrac12 k_v\Delta_\gamma v
                         -\tfrac12N_v|dv|_\gamma^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5}
\]
Consequently the retained slow equation and its entire transverse
component along this family are
\[
 \int_F\langle E_{VV},k_v\rangle dA_F
            =\tfrac12\Delta_\gamma v,
 \qquad
 E_{VV}-k_v\int_F\langle E_{VV},k_v\rangle dA_F
            =-\tfrac12N_v|dv|^2.
 \tag{6}
\]
They give the exact global identity
\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \int_{B\times F}|E|_G^2dV_G
 =\int_B\bigg[&\frac14(\Delta_\gamma v)^2
  +2V_F\left(K_\gamma+1-\frac{|dv|^2}{8V_F}\right)^2\\
  &+\left(\Xi(v)-\frac1{32V_F}\right)|dv|^4\bigg]dA_\gamma.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{7}
\]
In particular, for an arbitrary base metric,
\[
 \int|E|^2\ge\frac14\int_B(\Delta_\gamma v)^2dA_\gamma
                  +\frac1{32V_F}\int_B|dv|^4dA_\gamma.
 \tag{8}
\]
If \(K_\gamma=-1\), the simpler identity is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \int|E|^2=\frac14\int_B(\Delta_\gamma v)^2dA_\gamma
                    +\int_B\Xi(v)|dv|^4dA_\gamma.
 \quad}
 \tag{9}
\]
There is no potential term depending only on \(v\), and no uncomputed
\(O(v^2)\) error. The exact family of hyperbolic fibers is responsible
for this cancellation.

**Proof.** In base coordinates \(x^i\) and fiber coordinates \(y^a\),
put \(S_i=\tfrac12h^{-1}\partial_i h=\tfrac12h^{-1}k_v\partial_i v\).
Its trace is zero. The mixed Christoffel symbols of the block metric are
\[
 \Gamma^a_{ib}=\tfrac12(h^{-1}k_v)^a{}_b\partial_i v,
 \qquad
 \Gamma^i_{ab}=-\tfrac12\gamma^{ij}(k_v)_{ab}\partial_jv;
 \tag{10}
\]
the remaining nonzero symbols within each factor are its intrinsic
Christoffel symbols. Substitution into Ricci, or the full flat-connection
identities in Round 20, gives
\[
 (\operatorname{Ric}_G)_{ij}
    =K_\gamma\gamma_{ij}-\operatorname{tr}(S_iS_j),
 \qquad (\operatorname{Ric}_G)_{ia}
    =(\operatorname{div}_{h_v}S_i)_a,
\]
\[
  (E_{VV})^\sharp=-\gamma^{ij}D_iS_j,
  \quad
  D_iS_j=\partial_iS_j+[S_i,S_j]-\Gamma^\ell_{ij}(\gamma)S_\ell.
 \tag{11}
\]
The mixed term vanishes by (1). The commutator vanishes because every
\(S_i\) is a scalar multiple of the same endomorphism. Differentiating
\(h^{-1}k_v\) gives \(h^{-1}N_v\). Since
\(\gamma^{ij}(\partial_i\partial_jv-\Gamma^\ell_{ij}\partial_\ell v)
=-\Delta_\gamma v\), (11) proves all of (5).

Equation (3) now proves (6) and
\[
 \int_F|E_{VV}|^2dA_F
           =\tfrac14(\Delta_\gamma v)^2
                          +\tfrac14C_N(v)|dv|^4.
 \tag{12}
\]
Set \(c=K_\gamma+1\), \(X=|dv|^2\). Since
\(|dv\otimes dv|^2=X^2\) and \(\langle\gamma,dv\otimes dv\rangle=X\),
the horizontal norm integrated over a fiber is
\[
 2V_Fc^2-\tfrac12cX+\tfrac1{16}C_4(v)X^2.
 \tag{13}
\]
Completing this scalar square and using (12) proves (7). Equations
(8)--(9) follow from (3) and \(c=0\), respectively. \(\square\)

**Corollary A1 (forced slow energy and exact classification).** Define
\(f(x)=\int_F\langle E_{VV},k_v\rangle dA_F\). Then, for any constant
\(c\),
\[
 \Delta_\gamma v=2f,\qquad
 \int_B|dv|^2dA_\gamma=2\int_B(v-c)f\,dA_\gamma,
\]
\[
 \|v-\bar v\|_{L^2(B)}
       \le\frac{2}{\lambda_1(B,\gamma)}\|E\|_{L^2(B\times F)}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Here \(\lambda_1>0\) is the first nonzero scalar eigenvalue, and the last
inequality follows from \(\|f\|_2\le\|E_{VV}\|_2\). No scalar spectral
gap has been silently made uniform in the base.

If \(G\) is Einstein with **any** constant \(\lambda\), then
\(\operatorname{tr}_{VV}\operatorname{Ric}_G=-2\) by (3),(5), so
\(\lambda=-1\). Equivalently, pairing the Einstein equation with \(k_v\)
in (6) already implies \(\Delta v=0\). Connectedness of the closed base
gives \(v\) constant, and then (5) forces \(K_\gamma=-1\). Thus the
exact solutions in this entire block family are precisely the products.
This recovers the relevant inherited adapted rigidity, while (6)--(14)
supply its quantitative nonlinear equation and full residual energy.

**Corollary A2 (local stress and global amplitude).** Put
\(\delta=\|G^{-1}E\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}\). For an arbitrary base,
\[
 |dv|^2\le8V_F\delta,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{osc}_B v\le\sqrt{8V_F\delta}\,
                                  \operatorname{diam}(B,\gamma).
 \tag{15}
\]
When \(K_\gamma=-1\), 8 can be replaced by 4. Indeed, the trace-free
part of the horizontal block has operator norm
\(|k_v|^2|dv|^2/8\); its norm is at most \(\delta\) in dimension two.
Taking a fiber maximum and using \(\int_F|k_v|^2=1\) proves the first
bound. For a hyperbolic base the whole horizontal error has norm
\(|k_v|^2|dv|^2/4\). Integrating along a minimizing base geodesic proves
the amplitude estimate. Base-curvature adjustment therefore cannot remove
the rank-one trace-free stress.

## 3. Fixed-amplitude cyclic-cover construction

Fix a closed hyperbolic surface \((B,b)\) and a primitive class in
\(H^1(B;\mathbb Z)\). Let \(\eta\) be its harmonic representative,
normalized to have integral periods. On the associated infinite cyclic
cover \(B_\infty\), write \(dz=\eta\), with
\(z(\tau x)=z(x)+1\) for a deck generator \(\tau\). The function \(z\)
is harmonic. Let \(B_m=B_\infty/\langle\tau^m\rangle\), with the lifted
hyperbolic metric \(b_m\).

Fix \(a>0\) with \([-a,a]\subset J\), and suppose the two marked
hyperbolic structures \([h_{-a}]\) and \([h_a]\) are different. This is
achievable on an arbitrarily short regular horizontal arc. Define the
actual metrics
\[
 v_m=a\cos(2\pi z/m),\qquad
 M_m=B_m\times F,\qquad G_m=b_m+h_{v_m}.
 \tag{16}
\]
The amplitude \(a\) is fixed independently of \(m\).

**Theorem B.** This sequence has uniform local smooth bounded geometry
and is noncollapsed on a fixed local scale. For every fixed integer
\(j\ge0\) and \(0<\alpha<1\), in the uniformly lifted product charts,
\[
 \|\operatorname{Ric}_{G_m}+G_m\|_{C^{j,\alpha}_{\mathrm{uloc}}}
                  \le C_{a,j,\alpha}m^{-2},
 \qquad
 c_a m^{-2}\le\delta_-(G_m)\le C_a m^{-2},
 \tag{17}
\]
\[
 c_a m^{-3}\le
       \int_{M_m}|\operatorname{Ric}_{G_m}+G_m|^2dV_{G_m}
                 \le C_a m^{-3},\qquad
 \operatorname{osc}_{B_m}v_m=2a.
 \tag{18}
\]
The constants depend on the fixed surfaces, harmonic form, and fixed
horizontal curve, not on the covering degree. The volume is exactly
\(m\operatorname{Area}(B,b)V_F\), and diameter is comparable to \(m\).
In particular, even unnormalized \(L^2\) Ricci error tends to zero.

**Proof.** The harmonic chain rule gives exactly
\[
 dv_m=-a\frac{2\pi}{m}\sin(2\pi z/m)\eta,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{b_m}v_m=\left(\frac{2\pi}{m}\right)^2
                                      |\eta|^2v_m.
 \tag{19}
\]
All derivatives of the lifted \(\eta\) are uniformly bounded. Every
fixed number of derivatives of \(|dv_m|^2\) and \(\Delta v_m\) is
therefore \(O(m^{-2})\). The compact smooth hyperbolic path has uniform
bounds of every fixed order, so (5) proves the first part of (17).
Although the Hessian of \(v_m\) need only be \(O(m^{-1})\), the complete
Ricci formula involves its **trace** in (19); there is no missing
\(O(m^{-1})\) mixed block, because horizontality makes it vanish exactly.

The metrics \(G_m\) are uniformly equivalent to \(b_m+h_0\), with all
local coefficient derivatives uniformly bounded. Lift a fixed finite
atlas on \(B\) and a fixed finite atlas on \(F\), shrinking its charts
once if necessary. These charts give local ellipticity, curvature bounds
and a fixed-radius ball-volume lower bound independent of \(m\). The
same argument provides uniform harmonic-coordinate bounds if those are
desired, but harmonic coordinates are not needed for (17).

A connected compact fundamental cell for \(B_\infty\) has bounded
diameter. The cyclic chain of \(m\) cells gives
\(\operatorname{diam}(B_m)\le C m\). The circle-valued function \(z\)
modulo \(m\) has uniformly bounded differential and is onto the circle
of length \(m\), giving the reverse bound. Projection to \(B_m\) is
distance nonincreasing; a horizontal path at fixed fiber coordinate and
then a uniformly bounded fiber path prove the corresponding statements
for \(G_m\). Surjectivity also gives \(\operatorname{osc}v_m=2a\).
Equation (15) now gives the lower bound in (17).

For completeness the integral rate in (18) is exact in order, not only
an upper bound. For \(m\ge5\), deck summation gives
\[
 \sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\cos^2(\theta+2\pi r/m)=\frac m2,
 \quad
 \sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\sin^4(\theta+2\pi r/m)=\frac{3m}{8}.
 \tag{20}
\]
Write \(I_\eta=\int_B|\eta|^4dA_b>0\). It follows that
\[
 \int_{B_m}(\Delta v_m)^2dA_{b_m}
       =\frac{a^2(2\pi)^4}{2m^3}I_\eta,
 \qquad
 \int_{B_m}|dv_m|^4dA_{b_m}
       =\frac{3a^4(2\pi)^4}{8m^3}I_\eta.
 \tag{21}
\]
Insert these into (9), using the positive lower and finite upper bounds
of \(\Xi\) on \([-a,a]\), to prove (18). \(\square\)

This construction is an actual nonlinear continuation of the low channel
\(q(y)f(x)\); it is not a new smooth-topology supply theorem. It sharpens
the earlier shrinking-amplitude test by eliminating the spurious quadratic
potential of a linear metric interpolation. Its residual has no fixed
amplitude floor because every constant parameter gives an exact Einstein
product. All normal acceleration nevertheless remains visible in (9).

## 4. A precise fixed-distance statement, and its scope

Let \(\mathscr P_m\) be the metrics on \(B_m\times F\) obtained by
pulling back a hyperbolic product \(\gamma'+h_*\) through any smooth
fiber-preserving diffeomorphism
\(\Phi(x,y)=(\varphi(x),\phi_x(y))\), with orientation-preserving fiber
maps. Neither the product parameters nor the diffeomorphisms are fixed.
Set
\[
 d_0(G_m,\mathscr P_m)
  =\inf_{H\in\mathscr P_m}\|G_m^{-1}H-I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}},
 \quad
 D_T=d_T([h_{-a}],[h_a])>0,
 \tag{22}
\]
where \(d_T=\tfrac12\inf\log K\) is Teichmüller distance.

**Theorem C.** For every \(m\),
\[
                       d_0(G_m,\mathscr P_m)\ge\tanh D_T>0.
 \tag{23}
\]
**Proof.** If \(\varepsilon=\|G_m^{-1}H-I\|_\infty\ge1\), the claim
is automatic. Otherwise \((1-\varepsilon)G_m\le H\le(1+\varepsilon)G_m\).
Restricting to a vertical tangent space gives the same comparison between
\(h_{v_m(x)}\) and \(\phi_x^*h_*\), even when \(H\) has cross terms.
The mapping class of \(\phi_x\) is constant on connected \(B_m\), so
all the latter fiber metrics represent a single marked Teichmüller point
\(t_*\). The identity on a fiber has dilatation at most
\(\sqrt{(1+\varepsilon)/(1-\varepsilon)}\), whence
\[
 d_T([h_{v_m(x)}],t_*)
             \le\frac14\log\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}.
 \tag{24}
\]
Use fibers where \(v_m=\pm a\) and the triangle inequality. Then
\(D_T\le\tfrac12\log((1+\varepsilon)/(1-\varepsilon))
=\operatorname{arctanh}\varepsilon\), proving (23). \(\square\)

Thus no estimate \(d_0(G,\mathscr P)\le\rho(\|\operatorname{Ric}_G+G\|)\)
with \(\rho(t)\to0\) can be uniform over these cyclic covers, even for
fixed arbitrarily small but nonzero moduli amplitude, uniform local
geometry, and unnormalized \(L^2\) residual tending to zero. The omitted
global information is the slow coefficient, not a local curvature spike.

Theorem C does **not** replace \(\mathscr P_m\) by the set of all Einstein
metrics or permit arbitrary non-fiber-preserving diffeomorphisms. Such a
replacement would need a theorem showing that every sufficiently close
Einstein metric has the required global structure. Round 18 proves local
completeness for each fixed background; I33 gives a shrinking uniform
neighborhood. Neither supplies that missing fixed-amplitude coverage.
The manifolds \(M_m\) have the explicit exact output \(b_m+h_0\), so
these examples do not refute any existence conclusion of Yau26.

## 5. What has been retained rather than projected away

The scalar equation (6) is exact for the actual geometric family. Its
normal term is retained in (5), contributes the nonnegative \(C_N\) term
in (7), and can be nonzero. Solving \(\Delta v=0\) alone is not asserted
to solve an arbitrary full tensor perturbation problem. Within this family,
it makes \(v\) constant and then all the written vertical and mixed
equations vanish; the remaining base equation is precisely \(K_\gamma=-1\).

This supplies a complete nonlinear energy for a genuine slow family and
a sharp global-size obstruction to uniform adapted repair. It is
compatible with the first-line obstruction/existence distinction:
local smallness and actual near-saturation may coexist with macroscopic
moduli oscillation, although the same smooth topology has an exact output.
The companion gauge calculation identifies the actual harmonic classes
and their curvature deficits; those are additional verified information,
not a presumption that every slow-moduli problem is controlled by them.

## 6. Reproducible finite checks

`check_nonlinear_slow_moduli.py` recomputes a four-dimensional Ricci matrix
from Christoffel symbols for a two-base-variable area-preserving family,
checks the normal-acceleration term, arclength pairing and completed-square
coefficients, verifies the harmonic-coordinate cancellation and the cyclic
averages in (20), and includes negative controls for dropping acceleration
or replacing the scalar Laplacian by a Hessian norm. The local coordinate
test is not a closed hyperbolic family; the global family is supplied by
the horizontal-lift argument above. These finite checks do not certify the
Teichmüller theory or any unrestricted Einstein classification.

# Actual monopole deficits are moduli energy in the nonlinear slow family

24 September 2026. This companion to the
[nonlinear slow-moduli theorem](20260924_nonlinear_slow_moduli.md)
identifies its actual gauge classes and computes their deficits exactly.
It connects a complete nonlinear metric family to the first-line
curvature-functional theory; the Chern--Weil identity used in the
connection is classical. This is part of the same slow-family result,
not an independent claim of a new general curvature inequality.

## 1. Geometric setting and actual classes

Let \((B,\gamma)\) and \(F\) be closed oriented surfaces of genera at
least two. The base has curvature \(-1\). Let \(h_v\), for \(v\) in a
compact interval, be a smooth path of curvature \(-1\) metrics on the
fixed marked surface \(F\), with horizontal tangent
\[
 k_v=\partial_vh_v,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}_{h_v}k_v=0,\quad\delta_{h_v}k_v=0,
 \quad\int_F|k_v|_{h_v}^2\,dA_{h_v}=1.                 \tag{1}
\]
This is the positive-speed, fiber \(L^2\)-arclength convention of the
companion, not a claim about a particular convention for a constant
multiple of the Weil--Petersson metric. For a smooth function
\(v:B\to I\), put
\[
 M=B\times F,\qquad G=\gamma+h_{v(x)},\quad
 A_F=\operatorname{Area}(F,h_v),\quad V=\operatorname{Vol}(M,G).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
The trace condition in (1) gives a **pointwise fixed** area form
\(dA_F\), so \(V=A_F\operatorname{Area}(B,\gamma)\).

**Proposition 1 (exact actual supply).** In the two orientations of
\(M\), there are fixed actual determinant classes \(a_\pm\) whose
harmonic representatives are
\[
 \omega_\pm=dA_B\pm dA_F,\qquad
 H_\pm=-\frac{\omega_\pm}{2\pi},\qquad
 p_\pm=(a_\pm^+)^2=a_\pm^2=A:=2\chi(M),\quad V=2\pi^2A.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
Here \(+\) on \(a_-^+\) refers to self-duality in the **opposite**
orientation. The forms \(\omega_\pm\) are closed, nondegenerate,
harmonic, and have norm squared two. They need not be parallel.

**Proof.** In the product orientation \(*_GdA_B=dA_F\), so
\(\omega_+\) is self-dual and \(\omega_-\) is anti-self-dual.
Both are closed because the fiber area form is independent of \(v\).
They are therefore harmonic, and \(\omega_-\) is self-dual after
orientation reversal. The compatible almost-complex structures are
\(J_B\oplus J_{h_v}\) and \(J_B\oplus(-J_{h_v})\). Their first
Chern classes are
\[
 a_+=c_1(TB)+c_1(TF),\qquad
 a_-=c_1(TB)-c_1(TF)=-[\omega_-]/(2\pi),
\]
and \(a_+=-[\omega_+]/(2\pi)\), by the curvature \(-1\)
normalizations. The varying complex structures are homotopic to the
constant ones along the parameter interval. In particular these are
integral determinant classes, not arbitrarily chosen real classes.

In either orientation \(b_+=1+2g(B)g(F)>1\). Taubes's symplectic
nonvanishing theorem supplies the actual Spin\(^c\) structures.
Metric-independent nonvanishing and the conformal rescaling argument
give the same structure for every auxiliary positive weight, as in the
[polarized closure proof](../mathematics/current/lebrun_stability_closure.md).
Since \(H_\pm\) already represents the entire class and is self-dual
in its corresponding orientation, there is no unaccounted-for
anti-self-dual projection. Finally \(\tau(M)=0\), and Gauss--Bonnet
on the factors gives (3). \(\square\)

## 2. Exact functional and Ricci-energy identities

Use tensor norms for Ricci and endomorphism norms on two-forms for
Weyl. Set
\[
 E=\operatorname{Ric}_G+G,\quad r=\fint_M|E|^2,\quad
 D(v)=\int_B|dv|^2\,dA_B,\quad \alpha=\frac{D(v)}{4V},
\]
\[
 \mathfrak D_\pm=\frac32\int_M(s^2+12|W^\pm|^2)-72\pi^2p_\pm,
 \qquad q_\pm=\mathfrak D_\pm/V.                       \tag{4}
\]

**Theorem 2 (gauge deficit equals slow energy plus full residual).**
For every metric (2), without a small-amplitude hypothesis,
\[
 \boxed{q_+=q_-=9\alpha+\frac92r
      =\frac9{4V}D(v)+\frac92\fint_M|\operatorname{Ric}_G+G|^2.}
                                                               \tag{5}
\]
The relative Einstein defect is exactly
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal E(G)=\frac{4r-\alpha^2}{4+2\alpha+r},
            \qquad r\ge\alpha^2.}                    \tag{6}
\]
Thus both \(q_\pm=0\) and \(\mathcal E(G)=0\) force \(v\) constant;
the resulting metric is a hyperbolic product. The assertion concerns
this full block family, not all metrics on \(B\times F\).
The derivative budget of the actual harmonic forms is also exact:
\[
 \int_M|\nabla\omega_\pm|^2=\tfrac12D(v),\qquad
 q_\pm=\tfrac92\left(\fint_M|\nabla\omega_\pm|^2+r\right).
                                                               \tag{5a}
\]

**Proof.** The exact Ricci calculation of the companion gives
\[
 s_G+4=-\frac14|k_v|^2|dv|^2,\qquad
 E_{HH}=-\frac14|k_v|^2dv\otimes dv.                    \tag{7}
\]
Unit fiber speed implies
\(\fint s=-4-\alpha\), and direct expansion gives
\[
 R:=\int_M|\operatorname{Ric}|^2=V(4+2\alpha+r).
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
LeBrun's Chern--Weil identity, separately in either orientation, is
\[
 \mathfrak D_\pm=\frac92\bigl[R-8\pi^2(2p_\pm-T_\pm)\bigr].
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
Here \(T_\pm=2\chi\pm3\tau=A=p_\pm\) and
\(8\pi^2A=4V\). Equations (8)--(9) prove (5).

Pointwise \(E_{HH}\) has rank at most one and its trace equals the
entire trace of \(E\); the vertical residual is trace-free. Hence
\(r\ge\fint(\operatorname{tr}E)^2\ge\alpha^2\).
Direct minimization over nonzero Einstein constants gives
\(\mathcal E=4-(\int s)^2/(RV)\). Inserting (8) proves (6).
Its numerator is at least \(3r\). Thus zero relative defect forces
\(r=\alpha=0\), and (1) forces \(dv=0\). This also rules out a
different nonzero Einstein normalization inside this family; its scalar
curvature is negative, so Ricci-flatness is not an omitted case.

For (5a), use orthonormal base vectors \(X_i\), fiber vectors \(e_a\),
and \(S_i=\tfrac12h^{-1}k_v\,\partial_iv\). The covariant derivatives
of \(\omega_\pm\) in horizontal directions vanish. In a vertical
direction their only nonzero components are mixed base--fiber ones.
Squaring these gives \(2\sum_i|S_i|^2\): the cross contraction
vanishes since the \(S_i\) are proportional to one symmetric
trace-free matrix, which is orthogonal to its product with the fiber
complex structure. Hence
\(|\nabla\omega_\pm|^2=\tfrac12|k_v|^2|dv|^2\).
Integrating the unit-speed condition proves (5a).
\(\square\)

## 3. The precise missing global quantity is a scalar spectral scale

Let \(\lambda_1=\lambda_1(B,\gamma)>0\) and
\(\operatorname{Var}_B(v)=\fint_B(v-\bar v)^2\).
The companion's full nonlinear energy identity implies
\[
 r\ge\frac1{4V}\int_B(\Delta_\gamma v)^2,
 \qquad \Delta_\gamma=-\operatorname{div}\nabla.        \tag{10}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{q_\pm\ge\frac{9\lambda_1}{4A_F}
                       \operatorname{Var}_B(v),\qquad
 r\ge\frac{\lambda_1^2}{4A_F}\operatorname{Var}_B(v).} \tag{11}
\]
Both powers of \(\lambda_1\) are attained in order by the actual
cyclic family below. These bounds do not replace \(\lambda_1\) by a
genus-independent constant.

There is also an input-to-saturation implication in this family:
\[
 \boxed{q_\pm\le\left(\frac92+\frac9{\lambda_1}\right)r.} 
                                                               \tag{12}
\]
Indeed \(\int(\Delta v)^2\ge\lambda_1D(v)\), so (10) gives
\(\alpha\le r/\lambda_1\); now use (5). If only the scale-free
relative defect is supplied, then
\[
 \mathcal E<\frac37
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 r\le\frac73\mathcal E,
\]
\[
 q_\pm\le\left(\frac{21}2+\frac{21}{\lambda_1}\right)\mathcal E,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_B(v)\le
          \frac{28A_F}{3\lambda_1^2}\mathcal E.         \tag{13}
\]
To check the implication, (6) and \(\alpha\le\sqrt r\) give
\(\mathcal E\ge3r/(4+2\sqrt r+r)\). This increasing function
equals \(3/7\) at \(r=1\); for \(r<1\), it is at least \(3r/7\).

Equations (11)--(13) distinguish curvature-functional saturation from
constancy of the actual global moduli parameter. They eliminate the
auxiliary-form supply assumption and the separate near-saturation
assumption here, using the genuine geometric family and its scalar
spectrum. They make no analogous claim for unrestricted near-Einstein
metrics without a surface fibration or without (1).

## 4. Variable base curvature and the complete cscK equality family

The same construction also allows an arbitrary base metric \(\gamma\)
on the fixed surface of genus at least two. Define
\[
 \lambda_B=-\fint_B K_\gamma
      =\frac{4\pi(g(B)-1)}{\operatorname{Area}(B,\gamma)}>0.
\]
The actual classes remain \(c_1(TB)\pm c_1(TF)\), but their entire
harmonic representatives are now
\(- (\lambda_BdA_B\pm dA_F)/(2\pi)\). Only their self-dual parts
in the corresponding orientations enter the functional:
\[
 H_\pm^+=-\frac{\lambda_B+1}{4\pi}\omega_\pm,\quad
 p_\pm=\frac{(\lambda_B+1)^2V}{8\pi^2},\quad
 T_\pm=A=\frac{\lambda_B V}{2\pi^2}.
                                                               \tag{17}
\]
In particular a nonzero negative projection is retained when the two
factor scales differ; it is not dropped as though every actual class
were self-dual.

Put \(X=|dv|^2\), and let \(\Xi(v)\ge1/(16A_F)\) be the full
normal-acceleration coefficient in the companion. Direct expansion of
all its Ricci blocks and (9) give
\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 q_+=q_-=\frac9{2V}\bigg[&2A_F\int_B(K_\gamma+\lambda_B)^2
       -\frac12\int_B K_\gamma X\\
       &+\frac14\int_B(\Delta v)^2+\int_B\Xi(v)X^2\bigg].
 \end{aligned}}                                               \tag{18}
\]
For verification before completing the square, the Ricci energy is
\[
 R=2V+2A_F\int_BK_\gamma^2-\tfrac12\int_BK_\gamma X
                 +\tfrac14\int_B(\Delta v)^2+\int_B\Xi(v)X^2,
\]
and \(8\pi^2(2p_\pm-T_\pm)=2(1+\lambda_B^2)V\).
These prove (18) without invoking an unproved geometric limit.

If \(K_\gamma\le-\kappa_0<0\), every displayed term in (18) is
nonnegative and
\[
 q_\pm\ge\frac{9A_F}{V}\int_B(K_\gamma+\lambda_B)^2
                       +\frac{9\kappa_0}{4V}\int_B|dv|^2.
                                                               \tag{19}
\]
Equality \(q_\pm=0\) is equivalent to constant \(v\) and constant
base curvature. The resulting product is cscK, and is Einstein only if
\(\lambda_B=1\). Thus the complete classical equality family is
preserved, rather than incorrectly replacing it by the KE family.

For the actual pointwise Yau26 input \(\delta_-(G)<1\), the horizontal
Ricci eigenvalue perpendicular to \(dv\) is exactly \(K_\gamma\).
Consequently \(|K_\gamma+1|\le\delta_-\), so (19) applies with
\(\kappa_0=1-\delta_-\). This also makes
\(1-\delta_-\le\lambda_B\le1+\delta_-\). Equations (9),(17) and
\(R/V\le4(1+\delta_-)^2\) yield the concrete upper bound
\[
                       q_\pm\le54\delta_-+9\delta_-^2.         \tag{20}
\]
Thus functional saturation is automatic from pointwise Ricci pinching
on this actual block family, even with variable base curvature. What is
not automatic is global constancy of its moduli parameter: the scalar
spectral scale in (11)--(13) still matters.

## 5. Fixed moduli oscillation with exact periods and vanishing defects

Fix a hyperbolic base \(B_0\) and a nonzero primitive integral harmonic
one-form \(\eta\). Let \(B_m\) be its connected degree-\(m\) cyclic
cover. On the infinite cyclic cover, choose \(z\) with
\(dz=\eta\), \(z(\tau x)=z(x)+1\). Take a fixed
\(a>0\) inside the interval (1), after translating its center, and set
\[
             v_m=a\cos(2\pi z/m).                      \tag{21}
\]
For \(m\ge3\), summing over the deck translates gives exactly
\[
 \bar v_m=0,\qquad\operatorname{Var}_{B_m}(v_m)=a^2/2,
\]
\[
 D(v_m)=\frac{2\pi^2a^2}{m}\int_{B_0}|\eta|^2,
\quad
 \int_{B_m}(\Delta v_m)^2
       =\frac{8\pi^4a^2}{m^3}\int_{B_0}|\eta|^4.       \tag{22}
\]
Write \(V_0=A_F\operatorname{Area}(B_0)\). The complete block
formula, with the fixed smooth path on a compact interval, gives
\[
 \delta_-(G_m)=O(m^{-2}),\quad r_m=\Theta(m^{-4}),\quad
 \mathcal E(G_m)=\Theta(m^{-4}),
\]
\[
 \boxed{q_{+,m}=q_{-,m}
  =\frac{9\pi^2a^2}{2V_0m^2}\int_{B_0}|\eta|^2+O(m^{-4}).}
                                                               \tag{23}
\]
The lower bound for \(r_m\) is (10) and (22); its upper bound follows
from \(|dv_m|+|\nabla^2v_m|=O(m^{-1})\) together with the sharper
\(\Delta v_m=O(m^{-2})\) and the exact absence of a mixed block.
Equivalently the full nonlinear identity has only
\((\Delta v_m)^2\) and bounded multiples of \(|dv_m|^4\).
The two-sided estimate for \(\mathcal E\) follows from (6).
The cyclic-cell estimate in
[I33](20260924_integrable_kernel_cyclic_repair.md) and the Rayleigh quotient
in (22) give \(\lambda_1(B_m)=\Theta(m^{-2})\), confirming the orders
in (11). There is no claim that \(v_m\) itself is an eigenfunction:
\(|\eta|^2\) need not be constant.

In particular all of the following hold simultaneously:

- the two gauge classes are actual and have no negative-square cost;
- their harmonic representatives are the **exact** nonvanishing
  constant-length forms (3), with no period error;
- pointwise Ricci defect and both normalized functional deficits vanish;
- even the unnormalized Ricci-error energy is \(O(m^{-3})\);
- the marked moduli oscillation in (22) stays fixed and nonzero.

This is a concrete sharpness statement for upgrading average gauge
structure to global constancy of the supplied fibration. It is not a
counterexample to Yau26: every \(B_m\times F\) already carries exact
hyperbolic product metrics. The companion proves separation from
fiber-preserving product outputs; neither note asserts separation from
**all** Einstein metrics and arbitrary diffeomorphisms. Exact periods,
absence of zeros, and small local derivative energy do not alone remove
the long scalar slow modes.

# Harmonic zeros have a fourth-order scale cost

24 September 2026. This note strengthens the whole-filling criterion in
[Harmonic/contact Milnor matching](20260924_harmonic_contact_milnor_matching.md).
Its qualitative hypothesis that the actual harmonic representative have no
zeros is replaced by a quantitative comparison between the original
Einstein/calibration defect and a metric coordinate scale on the **entire
filling**. No identification of a terminal ALE space with that filling is
made.

The analytic ingredient is an elementary local Hodge estimate, composed
with the inherited actual-form theorem. A classical local construction of
Taubes supplies the complementary example: even a fixed actual monopole
class, unchanged periods and boundary data, exact harmonicity, and vanishing
averaged structure errors do not by themselves prevent zero circles. The
example is not pointwise almost Einstein. These two statements specify
exactly which additional scale information the present bridge uses.

## 1. A metric scale and a local theorem

Fix \(0<\alpha<1\) and \(Q\ge 2\). A number \(0<\rho\le1\) is an
**admissible coordinate scale** at \(x\in(M,g)\) if there is a smooth
embedding \(\phi:B_2(0)\subset\mathbb R^4\longrightarrow M\), with
\(\phi(0)=x\), such that
\[
 \widehat g=\rho^{-2}\phi^*g,\qquad
 Q^{-1}I\le\widehat g\le QI,\qquad
 \|\widehat g\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B_2)}\le Q.                \tag{1}
\]
Changing the harmless numerical convention in the coordinate norm changes
only the constants below. Write \(U_{x,\rho}=\phi(B_2)\), and denote the
supremum of these scales by \(r_{\alpha,Q}(x)\). Smoothness supplies a
positive scale at every point, after increasing the fixed \(Q\) if needed.
The scale is capped at 1 because the target Einstein normalization has
already fixed an ambient length unit. Harmonic coordinates satisfying (1)
can be used, but their coordinate functions need not be harmonic.

Thus (1) is a condition solely on the metric, independent of the harmonic
form or its zeros. It is not a lower bound on the injectivity radius of
the entire manifold, and no uniform positive lower bound for this scale
will be imposed on a sequence.

**Theorem 1 (a harmonic zero costs normalized amplitude energy).** There
is \(c=c(\alpha,Q)>0\) with the following property. Let \(\Omega\) be a
smooth two-form satisfying
\[
                         d\Omega=d_g^*\Omega=0
\]
on \(U_{x,\rho}\), where (1) holds. If \(\Omega(x)=0\), then
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
  \int_{U_{x,\rho}}\bigl(|\Omega|_g-\sqrt2\bigr)^2\,d\mu_g
       \ge c\rho^4.}                                      \tag{2}
\]
Self-duality is unnecessary for this local assertion. The normalization
\(\sqrt2\) is the one used by the global monopole construction.

**Proof.** Pull back and rescale both objects by
\[
          \widehat g=\rho^{-2}\phi^*g,\qquad
          \widehat\Omega=\rho^{-2}\phi^*\Omega.
\]
Then \(|\widehat\Omega|_{\widehat g}=|\Omega|_g\circ\phi\), the
Hodge equations remain homogeneous, and the integral in (2) is
\(\rho^4\) times the corresponding integral on \(B_2\). Work at this
unit scale and call that integral \(A\). If \(A\ge1\), the conclusion
holds with any \(c\le1\). Otherwise
\[
 \int_{B_2}|\widehat\Omega|^2\,d\mu_{\widehat g}
 \le 2A+4\operatorname{Vol}_{\widehat g}(B_2)\le C(Q).       \tag{3}
\]

Here is the regularity input, including its coefficient requirements.
View \(D=d+d_{\widehat g}^*\) on the full exterior-algebra bundle. Its
principal symbol is Clifford multiplication and is uniformly invertible
away from the zero covector. In coordinates its principal coefficients
are uniformly \(C^{1,\alpha}\), and its zero-order coefficients are
bounded, by (1). The local first-order elliptic estimate on nested balls is
\[
 \|u\|_{W^{1,p}(B_a)}
 \le C(p,Q,\alpha,a,b)
       \bigl(\|Du\|_{L^p(B_b)}+\|u\|_{L^p(B_b)}\bigr),
 \qquad 0<a<b<2.                                         \tag{4}
\]
One obtains (4) by freezing the principal coefficients on uniformly small
coordinate balls, applying the constant-coefficient Fourier-multiplier
estimate, absorbing their oscillation, and using a cutoff; the bounded
zero-order term is retained on the right. A finite covering gives the
displayed constants. This argument needs no bound on second metric
derivatives and takes no derivative of Ricci curvature.

Apply (4) to the pure-degree form \(\widehat\Omega\), viewed as an
inhomogeneous form. Its full Hodge--Dirac image is zero. Starting from
(3), successively use \(W^{1,2}\hookrightarrow L^4\), the interior
\(W^{1,4}\) estimate, the critical embedding into each finite \(L^p\),
and the \(W^{1,8}\) estimate. Morrey's inequality gives, on \(B_1\),
\[
       \|\widehat\Omega\|_{C^{0,1/2}(B_1)}\le C(\alpha,Q).
                                                               \tag{5}
\]
Since \(\widehat\Omega(0)=0\), uniform ellipticity and (5) imply
\(|\widehat\Omega|_{\widehat g}\le1/2\) throughout \(B_\theta\)
for a fixed \(\theta=\theta(\alpha,Q)>0\). Consequently
\[
 A\ge(\sqrt2-1/2)^2\operatorname{Vol}_{\widehat g}(B_\theta)
   \ge(\sqrt2-1/2)^2Q^{-2}|B_\theta|>0.
\]
Taking the minimum of this lower bound and 1 and rescaling proves (2).
\(\square\)

In particular, the theorem is not a new bounded-Ricci regularity theorem:
it uses a declared coordinate scale. Its content for this project is that
the actual Hodge equation turns the supplied mean amplitude into a
quantitative obstruction to zeros at that scale.

## 2. Eliminating the auxiliary form from the condition

Use the input and conventions of
[Actual-form composition](../mathematics/current/line1_composed_application.md).
Thus \(M\) is closed, connected, oriented and four-dimensional, and
\(a\) is the first Chern class of a Spin\(^c\) structure whose
unperturbed Seiberg--Witten equations are solvable for every metric in
\([g]\). The structure remains fixed as the positive weight varies.
Let
\[
 \begin{split}
 V&=\operatorname{Vol}(M,g),\qquad p=(a_g^+)^2>0,\\
 r&=\fint_M|\operatorname{Ric}_g+g|^2,\\
 q&=V^{-1}\left[\frac32\int_M(s^2+12|W^+|^2)
                                      -72\pi^2p\right],
 \qquad \kappa=r+q\le1/2880.
 \end{split}                                             \tag{6}
\]
The curvature functional gives \(q\ge0\). Let \(H\) denote the
harmonic self-dual representative of \(a_g^+\), not the harmonic
representative of the whole class when \(a_g^-\ne0\), and put
\(\Omega=-2\pi H\).

**Lemma 2 (actual harmonic amplitude).** The inherited supply theorem
implies directly
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
  \int_M\bigl(|\Omega|-\sqrt2\bigr)^2\,d\mu
                          \le818\kappa V.}             \tag{7}
\]
There is no remaining auxiliary weight or negative-square budget in (7).

**Proof.** For every \(\eta>0\) sufficiently small, the same actual
class supplies a smooth form \(\omega\) satisfying
\[
 \int|\omega-\Omega|^2\le(49\kappa+\eta)V,\qquad
 \int(|\omega|^2-2)^2\le(720\kappa+\eta)V.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality and
\[
       (t-\sqrt2)^2=\frac{(t^2-2)^2}{(t+\sqrt2)^2}
                      \le\tfrac12(t^2-2)^2,\qquad t\ge0,
\]
give
\[
 \int(|\Omega|-\sqrt2)^2
 \le2\int|\Omega-\omega|^2+\int(|\omega|^2-2)^2
 \le(818\kappa+3\eta)V.
\]
The left side is independent of the weight and of \(\eta\). Let
\(\eta\downarrow0\). No convergence of the auxiliary forms is
asserted or needed. \(\square\)

Combining (2) and (7) yields a purely raw-input consequence:
\[
 \boxed{H(x)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       r_{\alpha,Q}(x)^4\le C(\alpha,Q)\kappa V.}        \tag{8}
\]
Indeed apply (2) at every admissible scale and then take their supremum.
This also handles the case where that supremum is not attained.

## 3. Whole-core matching and exact realization

Retain the raw compactification hypotheses
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(M_j,g_j)\le D,\qquad
 (a_j^+)^2\ge A_0>0,\qquad
 \delta_j+q_j\longrightarrow0,
 \quad \delta_j=\|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}^{\sharp}+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}.
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
Each \(a_j\) is supplied by an actual conformally solvable Spin\(^c\)
structure, with the fixed-structure/variable-weight quantifier of Section 2.
By [Orbifold smoothing bridge](20260924_orbifold_smoothing_bridge.md),
Theorem A, a subsequence has a compact negative KE orbifold limit
\((X,h,J)\). Its regular exterior, with fixed small collars around the
singular links, embeds in \(M_j\). Let \(B_{\nu,j}\) be the
complementary **whole** connected fillings, with their actual markings.
As in that theorem, each chosen link bounds its indicated filling. All
nested necks and bubbles are included in \(B_{\nu,j}\).

For each \(j\), suppose metric data supply a number \(\rho_j>0\)
such that every point of \(\bigcup_\nu B_{\nu,j}\) has an admissible
coordinate scale at least \(\rho_j\), with fixed \(\alpha,Q\).
The coordinate neighborhoods lie in \(M_j\) and may cross the fixed
boundary collars. This avoids any boundary estimate for the Hodge equation.

**Theorem 3 (defect-to-core-scale realization).** There is
\(c_0=c_0(\alpha,Q)>0\) such that the following conditions, for all
sufficiently large indices in the extracted subsequence, force marked
Milnor matching:

1. \((r_j+q_j)V_j<c_0\rho_j^4\);
2. each singular filling satisfies either
   - its limiting singularity is \(A_1\) and
     \(b_2(B_{\nu,j};\mathbb Q)=1\), or
   - \(B_{\nu,j}\) is a rational homology four-ball.

The second alternative forces the limiting singularity to be Wahl,
\(\frac1{n^2}(1,na-1)\), with \(n\ge2\), \(0<a<n\), and
\((a,n)=1\). Every actual marked filling is diffeomorphic to the
corresponding local Q-Gorenstein Milnor fiber.

If, in addition, the complex orbifold \(X\) admits a projective
Q-Gorenstein smoothing, then each such \(M_j\), on its original smooth
structure, admits a negative Kähler--Einstein metric. A sufficient global
smoothing test is \(H^2(Z,T_Z(-\log E))=0\), with the resolution and
divisor as in Lee--Park's criterion used in the inherited bridge.

The first condition is implied by either of the more directly recorded
conditions
\[
           \frac{(r_j+q_j)V_j}{\rho_j^4}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
           \frac{(4\delta_j^2+q_j)V_j}{\rho_j^4}
                                     \longrightarrow0.          \tag{10}
\]

**Proof.** Since \(r_j\le4\delta_j^2\), (9) puts the sequence within
the range of Lemma 2. If \(H_j\) vanished at a point of a whole
filling, (2) and (7), applied at the supplied scale, would imply
\(c\rho_j^4\le818(r_j+q_j)V_j\). Choose \(c_0<c/818\).
Thus every \(H_j\) is zero-free on the entire filling.

The inherited harmonic/contact theorem now applies, with no change to
its remaining hypotheses. For clarity, its implications are as follows.
The actual \(\Omega_j=-2\pi H_j\) converges to the limiting Kähler
form in \(C^{1,\beta}\) on each fixed regular collar. Therefore it
gives the canonical strong convex contact boundary. Self-duality and
closedness, together with the newly proved nonvanishing throughout the
core, make \((B_{\nu,j},\Omega_j)\) a strong symplectic filling.
Classical filling classification gives the \(A_1\) Milnor filling in
the first case, and a Wahl rational ball in the second. The boundary
mapping-class extension argument in that theorem gives the actual
relative marking, not merely an unmarked diffeomorphism.

If a global smoothing exists, its exterior trivialization and these
relative diffeomorphisms glue to an oriented diffeomorphism from \(M_j\)
to a smooth fiber. The canonical divisor is ample, by the negative KE
metric on \(X\) and openness of ampleness in the Q-Gorenstein family.
Aubin--Yau gives a negative KE metric on the smooth fiber; pull it back.
This proves existence on \(M_j\), without an Einstein inverse or a
claim that the new metric is close to \(g_j\). \(\square\)

There is no positive lower bound on \(\rho_j\): the theorem permits
concentration at arbitrarily small scales if the original total defect
decays faster than the fourth power of the smallest supplied core scale.
The metric scale, the Betti/rational-ball condition, and the global
smoothing test are separate verifiable inputs; none is declared to
follow from (9) alone. The result includes non-Einstein inputs: sufficiently
small smooth perturbations of any negative KE metric with its compatible
actual class satisfy the criterion on prescribed cores, by continuity of
the Hodge projection and the functionals. On a degenerating KE sequence,
one may choose the perturbations separately at each positive \(\rho_j\).
This observation establishes nonemptiness, not a new construction of KE
metrics or a new smoothing example.

**Corollary 4 (non-Wahl rational cores have a quantitative cost).** In
(9), suppose a whole filling is a rational homology ball but its limiting
singularity is not Wahl. Then for large \(j\) it contains an interior
zero \(x_j\) of \(H_j\), and
\[
               r_{\alpha,Q}(x_j)^4
                    \le C(\alpha,Q)(r_j+q_j)V_j.         \tag{11}
\]
If all its points have a supplied scale at least \(\rho_j\), then
\((r_j+q_j)V_j\ge c_0\rho_j^4\), after reducing \(c_0\).

**Proof.** The inherited contact-filling obstruction forces a zero, and
its regular collar contains none. Apply (8). \(\square\)

## 4. Unchanged periods and harmonicity do not remove the scale issue

The following construction is classical at its source. Its quantitative
rescaling identifies a genuine limitation of replacing the new condition
by averaged form information alone.

**Proposition 5 (small-support harmonic zero circles).** Let
\((S,g_0,J,\Omega_0)\) be a fixed closed Kähler surface, with
\(|\Omega_0|_{g_0}=\sqrt2\). There are smooth metrics \(g_\rho\)
and closed self-dual forms \(\Omega_\rho\), for \(\rho\downarrow0\),
such that:

1. \(g_\rho=g_0\) and \(\Omega_\rho=\Omega_0\) outside a fixed
   Darboux ball of radius \(C\rho\);
2. \([\Omega_\rho]=[\Omega_0]\), and \(\Omega_\rho\) is its
   **actual harmonic representative** for \(g_\rho\);
3. \(\Omega_\rho\) has a transverse zero circle in that ball;
4. \(g_\rho\) is uniformly bilipschitz to \(g_0\),
   \(\|\Omega_\rho\|_\infty\le2\sqrt3\), and
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_S(|\Omega_\rho|^2-2)^2\,d\mu_{g_\rho}&=O(\rho^4),\\
 \int_S|\Omega_\rho-\Omega_0|_{g_\rho}^2\,d\mu_{g_\rho}
                                                    &=O(\rho^4),\\
 \int_S|\nabla^{g_\rho}\Omega_\rho|^2\,d\mu_{g_\rho}
                                                    &=O(\rho^2),\\
 \int_S|\nabla^{2,g_\rho}\Omega_\rho|^2\,d\mu_{g_\rho}
                                                    &=O(1).
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{12}
\]
In particular the first three integrals tend to zero, although zeros
persist. The volumes stay bounded above and below, so the same conclusions
hold for averaged integrals. The Hodge Laplacian of each form is exactly
zero.

**Proof.** Taubes [T06], Proposition 2.1 and its proof in Section 2,
construct a near-symplectic form on a standard symplectic ball which
equals the standard form outside a compact interior set and has one
transverse zero circle. Its compactly supported difference from the
standard form is exact: \(H_c^2(B^4;\mathbb R)=0\). Fix one such
model, denoted \(\widetilde\Omega\), on a ball of fixed radius.

Auroux--Donaldson--Katzarkov [ADK05], Proposition 1, supply a smooth
metric making this closed form self-dual and hence harmonic. The metric
may be prescribed to be Euclidean near the boundary, where the form is
standard. To see the relative assertion directly from their Section 7.2,
positive three-plane bundles containing a given positive two-form form a
convex set in the graph parametrization of the positive Grassmannian.
Extend the positive plane determined near the zero circle, and splice it
to the Euclidean positive plane on the exterior annulus, where the form
is nonzero. This constructs a conformal class agreeing with the prescribed
one there. Choose a smooth conformal factor agreeing with 1 there and
large enough on the interior to arrange
\(|\widetilde\Omega|\le2\sqrt3\). This rescaling preserves
self-duality of two-forms in dimension four. We may also arrange that its
Ricci tensor is nonzero at some interior point: a further smooth conformal
perturbation supported away from the zero circle can prescribe a nonzero
Hessian at a point, and the conformal Ricci formula then ensures this.
Take the perturbation small enough to retain the amplitude bound, first
leaving a strict margin there. Fix the resulting smooth model metric.

Take Darboux coordinates for \(\Omega_0\) at a point of \(S\), with
\(g_0\) equal to the Euclidean metric at the origin. Transplant the
model by dilation \(x=\rho y\): its coordinate coefficients are
\(\widetilde g(x/\rho)\) and
\(\widetilde\Omega_{ab}(x/\rho)\). Equivalently, the pullbacks
under dilation are \(\rho^2\widetilde g\) and
\(\rho^2\widetilde\Omega\). The form equals \(\Omega_0\)
near the boundary and glues exactly. On a surrounding annulus keep that
form fixed and interpolate the compatible positive planes from the model
metric to those of \(g_0\). Since \(g_0(\rho y)\) is uniformly
smooth and tends to its value at the origin, this relative interpolation
has uniformly bounded derivatives in the \(y\) coordinates. Its
conformal factor can again enforce the stated amplitude bound and match
\(g_0\). Both objects are unchanged outside the enlarged ball.

The compactly supported exactness proves equality of classes. Closedness
and self-duality give \(d^*_{g_\rho}\Omega_\rho=0\); on the closed
manifold Hodge uniqueness makes it the actual harmonic representative.
The inserted transverse zero circle is unaffected by the outer annulus.

In rescaled coordinates the metrics are uniformly smooth and elliptic,
the form is uniformly smooth, and the changed region has volume
\(O(\rho^4)\). One and two covariant derivatives of the form are
respectively \(O(\rho^{-1})\) and \(O(\rho^{-2})\) there. Outside,
the original Kähler form is parallel for the original metric. These facts
prove all four estimates (12). \(\square\)

This applies with an actual class, rather than merely a real cohomology
class. For example take the negative KE product of two closed hyperbolic
curves of genus at least two. Put \(a=c_1(S,J)\), so
\([\Omega_0]=-2\pi a\), and \(a^2>0\). Its canonical Spin\(^c\)
class and its negative are ordinary monopole classes, by the classical
Seiberg--Witten input recalled in LeBrun [L06], Section 3. In particular
the same structure is solvable for all metrics, hence for all conformal
weights for each \(g_\rho\). Proposition 5 gives
\[
       a^+_{g_\rho}=a,\qquad a^-_{g_\rho}=0,\qquad
       -2\pi H_{g_\rho}=\Omega_\rho,
\]
with \(p=a^2\) fixed and positive. All periods, the positive projection
budget, and the negative-square cost are unchanged; there are nevertheless
harmonic zero circles and arbitrarily small averaged structure errors.

The construction is also relative on any prescribed symplectic Milnor
filling: perform it in an interior Darboux ball of an \(A_1\) or Wahl
fiber, away from its contact collar. The filling, its boundary marking,
its boundary form and **every period on every closed two-cycle** are
unchanged. It creates interior zeros of a closed self-dual harmonic form.
On a filling this means a solution of the interior Hodge equations; no
uniqueness for an unspecified boundary value problem is claimed. On a
closed ambient manifold the uniqueness assertion is the one in
Proposition 5. No zero-free hypothesis can be deduced from these period
and collar data alone.

At a zero of the inserted model, (1) holds with scale at least
\(c_1\rho\), for fixed \(\alpha,Q\). Theorem 1 supplies the reverse
order of magnitude for the amplitude error, while (12) supplies the
upper bound. Thus the power four in (2) is the correct scaling for the
local statement: a hypothesis \(A=o(\rho^\gamma)\) with
\(\gamma<4\) would allow these zero-circle examples. This is not a
claim of sharp constants or sharpness within pointwise almost-Einstein
sequences.

## 5. Exact scope of the new bridge

The conclusion added to the previous theorem is
\[
 \boxed{\text{actual raw defect below the fourth power of the whole-core
 scale}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{marked Milnor matching}}
\]
in its stated \(A_1\)/rational-ball topological regimes. After the
separate global Q-Gorenstein test this gives an Einstein metric on the
original smooth manifold, as required by Yau26 for the covered inputs.

The zero-circle construction explains why keeping only global averages
would lose relevant information. It does **not** satisfy the claimed
small pointwise Ricci hypothesis (9): the fixed nonzero model Ricci tensor
in the preceding proof gives
\(\delta_-(g_\rho)\ge c\rho^{-2}-1\) at its transplanted point.
Thus the pointwise Ricci defect actually diverges. Nor do (12) establish
that the raw functional \(r_\rho+q_\rho\) tends to zero. Therefore
it neither disproves automatic matching under the complete original
input nor supplies a Yau26 counterexample. What it disproves is inference
of nonvanishing from the listed harmonic, period and average-form
properties alone.

The remaining mathematical question is consequently specific: does the
pointwise Ricci equation plus actual near-saturation force the ratio in
(10), or provide a different zero-removal mechanism on the concentration
cores? That implication has not been proved here. The present theorem
allows the ratio to be checked directly and quantifies its failure when
contact topology forces a zero; it does not assume that all relevant ALE
information has already classified the whole core.
