## Deep-Reasoning

This step moved the restricted four-dimensional negative Ishida--LeBrun route from tensor algebra toward the actual analytic platform needed for a smoothing theorem. The important structural insight is the separation of roles created by the Step 35 Hessian-cutoff contractions: the first smoothing estimates no longer require pointwise control of the evolving cutoff Hessian, because Hessian-a terms in the curvature and defect energies are paid by signed tensor integration by parts using the derivative structures of the contraction tensors. That separation changes the nature of the next obstacle. The route is no longer blocked mainly by local algebra in the power estimates; it is blocked by whether the degenerate fixed-cutoff flow is a legitimate smooth platform up to the selected time and whether the sequence-uniform quantities needed by terminal calibration are propagated independently.

The correct analytic picture now has three different quantifier levels. First, short-time smooth existence is per datum: for each smooth closed input metric and fixed smooth cutoff, the flow \(\partial_tg=-2\chi^2(\operatorname{Ric}+g)\) only needs to start for some time depending on that individual input, including exterior geometry. This prevents a false demand for sequence-uniform initial existence and permits coefficient regularization only if one proves regularization-parameter-uniform convergence for that fixed datum. Yang's source supplies the right model: a transported-coefficient DeTurck system, not a fixed-coefficient gauge and not ordinary Schauder estimates whose constants degenerate with ellipticity. Second, extension to the chosen time \(T=\theta r^2\) is a maximal-time problem: higher derivative constants may depend on the individual metric and a positive starting time, but must remain finite as the maximal endpoint is approached. Third, quantities used downstream in the geometric contradiction must be sequence-uniform: metric drift, terminal plateau curvature, retained plateau mass, moving Sobolev comparison, and especially the unweighted global defect \(Q=\int |E|^2\). Confusing these levels was the source of earlier instability in the route.

The transported DeTurck formulation is now clear. The hatted system must use \(\widehat a=a\circ\varphi_t^{-1}\) and the full gauge \(\mathcal L_{\widehat aW}\widehat g\), because \(\mathcal L_{\widehat aW}\widehat g=\widehat a\mathcal L_W\widehat g+d\widehat a\otimes W^\flat+W^\flat\otimes d\widehat a\). With \(\partial_t\varphi_t\circ\varphi_t^{-1}=-\widehat aW\), pullback gives the target fixed-cutoff normalized flow exactly. The only genuinely new normalization term relative to Yang is \(\delta(-2\widehat a\widehat g)=-2\widehat a h-2(\delta\widehat a)\widehat g\); the \((\delta\widehat a)\operatorname{Ric}\) part is already present in the transported Ricci term. TTCS sharpened that \(\delta\widehat a\) generally has a \(\widehat\chi d\widehat\chi\) factor rather than a full \(\widehat a\) factor, but this remains lower order for Yang's tame construction. Thus per-datum short-time existence is acceptable when explicitly inherited from Yang's tame estimates with this lower-order perturbation, while a generic strictly parabolic regularization argument alone is not enough.

The separate global \(Q\) mechanism is a real advance because terminal Chern--Weil/monopole recalibration uses the unweighted integral \(\int |E|^2\); neither \(J_6=\int\eta^2a^4|E|^6\) nor \(\|aE\|_\infty\) controls it near the zero set of \(a\). Under the smooth first-exit geometry, the homogeneous defect equation gives \(Q_U'+cX_U\le C(1+r^{-2})Q_U+C\int F(\chi E)^2\), where the \(\nabla a\) and \(\nabla^2a\) terms are handled by signed integration by parts and \(|da|^2/a=4|d\chi|^2\). Buffered curvature energy \(K_2^\eta=\int\eta^2F^2\), with \(\eta=1\) on \(\operatorname{supp}\chi\), gives \(\int F(\chi E)^2\le C\mathcal S(K_2^\eta)^{1/2}(X_U+r^{-2}Q_U)\). For small \(\gamma=\mathcal S(K_2^\eta)^{1/2}\), this absorbs \(X_U\). Summing over macro-disjoint supports and using the frozen complement yields \(Q(t)\le e^{C(1+r^{-2})t}Q(0)\), hence at \(t=\theta r^2\) a sequence-uniform factor \(e^{C\theta(1+r^2)}\). This preserves \(Q(T)/A\to0\) whenever \(Q(0)/A\to0\), which is exactly what the terminal Derdzinski contradiction route needs.

The shifted-\(U\) continuation interface remains the hard analytic object. The right variable is \(U=a(\operatorname{Rm}+G)\), because \(\operatorname{Ric}(U)=aE\) and \(\partial_t\Gamma=g^{-1}*\nabla\operatorname{Ric}(U)\). This avoids inserting uncontrolled split coefficients such as \(da*F+a\nabla\operatorname{Rm}\), and it makes the base pair \((\nabla U,\nabla^2\chi)\) the natural continuation object. A03's later Step 38 repair, read in this step, is important: signed terms \(\int\chi\nabla_i\nabla_j\chi\,B^{ij}(V)\) with \(|B|\le C_p|V|^p\), \(|\nabla B|\le C_p|V|^{p-1}|\nabla V|\) are controlled by one integration by parts and Young, not by an evolving \(\|\nabla^2\chi\|\) coefficient. This repairs the finite-\(L^p\) base-pair layer and the apparent single-Hessian term at \(m=2\). But it does not yet prove continuation: the normalized unweighted \(L^p\) propagation for \(T=\operatorname{Rm}+G\), the full index-level \(U\) and \(W=\nabla U\) equations with all \(G\), connection, normalization, and cutoff terms, and the higher staggered induction remain to be written.

The restricted negative route is therefore substantially stronger, but still far from the full Yau Problem 26 resolution. If the analytic platform is completed, it could rule out the near-critical Ishida--LeBrun negative laboratory by preserving terminal \(Q/A\to0\), obtaining bounded-curvature plateaux, reapplying calibration, using exponential-pullback selection, and reaching the forbidden calibrated Einstein germ. Even that would not decide the positive branch, arbitrary four-manifold topology, dimensions \(n\ge5\), dimension-only constants, or Lean formalization. A02's positive-branch period-transport update reinforces this global judgment: the collapsed toric front is separate and still unresolved, with compact-polygon monodromy for \(m=6,8\) now the meaningful question rather than the earlier rod/corner accounting shortcut.
