## Reframing the central asymmetry: scalar curvature is flexible, the two‑sided Ricci relation is rigid — and the ARTT paper shows exactly why

The Aliouane–Rifford–Theillière construction is the sharpest available lens on route (ii), and it argues *against* flexibility as a counterexample engine rather than for it. Their mixed convex integration prescribes $\mathrm{Scal}^{g_\epsilon}\approx \mathrm{Scal}^{g_0}-k$ with $\|g_\epsilon-g_0\|_{C^0}\to0$, using the diagonal ansatz $g=\mathrm{diag}(1,e^{2h_2},e^{2h_3},1,\dots)$ and corrugations with frequency $N$. The mechanism is that the second‑order corrugation forces $\partial_1^2 h_i \sim N\dot\gamma_i$, i.e. each individual second derivative grows like $N$, while the loop constraint $(\gamma_x)_1=-(\gamma_x)_2$ makes the *scalar* combination $\partial_1^2h_2+\partial_1^2h_3=O(1)$ through exact cancellation. Now compute the Ricci eigenvalue in the warped direction directly: for this diagonal metric the dominant term of $R_{22}$ is $-\tfrac12\partial_1^2(e^{2h_2})=-e^{2h_2}\big(\partial_1^2h_2+2(\partial_1h_2)^2\big)$, so the mixed eigenvalue $(g^{-1}\mathrm{Ric})_{22}=e^{-2h_2}R_{22}\sim -\partial_1^2h_2\sim -N$, while $(g^{-1}\mathrm{Ric})_{33}\sim +N$. The trace (scalar) sees the cancellation; the *operator norm* of $g^{-1}\mathrm{Ric}$ satisfies $\|A_g\|_{\mathrm{op}}\gtrsim N\to\infty$. Hence along the very family that makes scalar curvature flexible, $\delta_\sigma(g_\epsilon)\to\infty$. 

This is decisive for the whole flexibility program: convex integration achieves scalar prescription precisely by driving the trace‑free Ricci to oscillate with unbounded amplitude. The two‑sided pinched relation $c_n g\le\mathrm{Ric}\le C_n g$ is a *closed, non‑ample* second‑order relation whose defining set constrains every eigenvalue of $A_g$ simultaneously; the affine‑in‑second‑derivatives structure that ARTT exploit for the single scalar $L\cdot\sigma^2_{11}$ collapses when one demands control of all $n$ eigenvalues at once. Any attempt to refute $P_n^\sigma$ by an h‑principle must therefore be an h‑principle for the matrix relation itself, and the trace‑cancellation computation above is the concrete obstacle it would have to defeat. Lohkamp‑type flexibility for $\mathrm{Ric}<0$ is an *open one‑sided* relation and controls only the upper eigenvalue; it says nothing about the lower bound that pinching demands. I would record as a clean, verifiable statement: **any construction of $\delta_\sigma$-small metrics on a fixed $M$ must keep $\|\mathrm{Ric}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ bounded, which the corrugation frequency forbids** — so route (ii) does not produce counterexamples, and its real value is negative information about where new constructions cannot come from.

## Strategic geometry of the quantifiers, and where a refutation could even live

The logical structure $\forall n\,(P_n^+\vee P_n^-)$ has an underused consequence. To *establish* $Q_n$ one only needs **one** branch per dimension; the branches can be proved by entirely different mechanisms (in dimension 3 both hold, positive by Hamilton, minus by geometrization). To *refute* the whole problem one must fix a single dimension and defeat **both** branches there. These two tasks have very different geographies, and mapping them changes research priorities.

A refutation is essentially blocked above dimension four by ignorance rather than by difficulty: no simply‑connected closed manifold of dimension $\ge5$ is known to carry no Einstein metric at all. A branch counterexample in dimension $\ge5$ would first require exhibiting such a manifold with the additional feature of Ricci‑pinchability — solving a famous open problem as a sub‑step. The realistic arena for any refutation is dimension four, where Hitchin–Thorpe and the Seiberg–Witten/LeBrun obstructions exclude Einstein metrics *of every sign* (they set $E=0$, independent of the Einstein constant's sign, which is exactly the "every sign" requirement). Everything below concentrates the refutation analysis in dimension four and treats the existence side as the more plausible route for general $n$.

## Route (i): a positive‑branch gap theorem modulo degeneration in dimension four

The positive branch in dimension four is far more constrained than the abstract statement suggests, and the constraints assemble into a compactness dichotomy. Take $g_j$ with $\delta_+(g_j)\to0$, i.e. $\mathrm{Ric}_{g_j}\to g_j$ in operator norm with positive normalization. Then:

- Myers gives $\mathrm{diam}(M,g_j)\le\pi\sqrt{3/(1-\varepsilon_j)}$ and finite $\pi_1$; Bochner gives $b_1=0$; so a counterexample manifold is essentially simply connected with $\chi>0$.
- Gauss–Bonnet and signature with $E\to0$, $s\to4$ force $\int|W_{g_j}|^2\le 8\pi^2\chi(M)+o(1)$ and $\int|Rm_{g_j}|^2\le C(\chi,\tau)$, since $\int s^2\sim16\,\mathrm{Vol}$ and $\int|E|^2\to0$.

So the family sits in the classical four‑dimensional compactness class: bounded diameter, two‑sided Ricci, bounded $L^2$ curvature — exactly the Anderson/Bando–Kasue–Nakajima/Tian and Cheeger–Naber setting. This yields a genuine dichotomy for a hypothetical counterexample on a fixed $M$ with no Einstein metric of any sign:

**(a) Non‑collapse.** If $\mathrm{Vol}(M,g_j)\ge v>0$, subsequential Gromov–Hausdorff limits are Einstein orbifolds $X_\infty$ with $\mathrm{Ric}=g_\infty>0$ and finitely many Ricci‑flat ALE bubbles; the desingularization/gluing analysis (Biquard, Ozuch, LeBrun–Ozuch, Yao) then tries to reconstruct an Einstein metric on $M$. If it succeeds, "no Einstein" is contradicted. So a non‑collapsed counterexample requires a *provable obstruction to desingularizing the specific positive Einstein orbifold limit back to $M$* while $M$ nonetheless carries the pinched sequence — this is exactly the substance route (iii) must supply.

**(b) Collapse.** Otherwise $\mathrm{Vol}(M,g_j)\to0$. Then $\int s^2, \int|E|^2\to0$, so $\chi(M)=\tfrac1{8\pi^2}\int|W|^2+o(1)$: *all* of the Euler characteristic must survive as concentrated Weyl energy while volume vanishes. This is the pivotal candidate lemma.

**Candidate Lemma (collapse with $\chi\ne0$ under pinched positive Ricci).** *A fixed closed simply‑connected $M^4$ with $\chi>0$ admits no sequence $g_j$ with $\delta_+(g_j)\to0$ and $\mathrm{Vol}(M,g_j)\to0$.* Under bounded sectional curvature this is essentially the Cheeger–Fukaya–Gromov statement that polarized collapse forces $\chi=0$; the open content is to run it under only two‑sided Ricci with bounded $\int|Rm|^2$, using the fact that $\chi$ localizes entirely onto the bubbling Weyl energy and that Ricci‑flat ALE bubbles carry quantized energy tied to their orbifold groups. If this lemma holds, case (b) is empty, the positive branch reduces to case (a), and $P_4^+$ becomes a corollary of the desingularization program. If it fails, the failure mode *is* the shape of the only possible positive‑branch counterexample and tells the constructor exactly what to build (a $\chi>0$ manifold collapsing with Weyl concentration). Either outcome is high value. The cleanest first computation is the energy‑quantization bookkeeping: match $8\pi^2\chi(M)$ against the sum of ALE bubble energies permitted under collapse, and see whether $\chi(\mathbb{CP}^2\#k\overline{\mathbb{CP}^2})=3+k$ is realizable.

## The neck obstruction: why the known no‑Einstein four‑manifolds may resist positive pinching

All four‑manifolds currently known to carry no Einstein metric are connected sums / iterated blow‑ups ($\mathbb{CP}^2\#k\overline{\mathbb{CP}^2}$ and relatives). Positive Ricci *lower* bounds survive some connected sums (Sha–Yang, Perelman), but those metrics have wide Ricci spread. Two‑sided pinching is qualitatively different.

**Candidate meta‑lemma (pinching resists surgery).** *Pinched positive Ricci, $\delta_+(g)<\varepsilon_n$ with dimension‑only $\varepsilon_n$, forces almost‑maximal volume and hence GH‑closeness to a spherical space form, which a nontrivial connected sum cannot approximate.* The mechanism to test: a separating essential hypersurface in a connected sum, made minimal (or a $\mu$‑bubble), inherits a width bound from the Ricci lower bound through the stability/second‑variation estimates of Schoen–Yau–Gromov; a two‑sided pinch then also bounds the volume from below via Cheeger–Colding almost‑rigidity, contradicting the small waist of a neck. If provable, this excludes every current no‑Einstein four‑manifold from being a positive‑branch counterexample, strongly supporting $P_4^+$. The delicate point, and the reason it is not automatic, is that the volume lower bound is not handed over by the lower Ricci bound alone; it must be extracted from the two‑sided pinch, which is precisely the almost‑rigidity input. This lemma also predicts where the constructor could push back: find a no‑Einstein four‑manifold that is *not* a connected sum, or a pinched positive Ricci metric on a connected sum with genuinely small waist.

## Route (iii): orbifold/ALE smoothing counterexamples, and the two precise gaps

The desingularization obstruction literature gives "no Einstein metric near this orbifold degeneration," but a refutation needs "no Einstein metric on $M$ at all" together with "$\delta_\sigma\to0$ in the unweighted $C^0$ sense." Two gaps must be crossed, and naming them precisely is the useful output.

*Gap 1 (weighted versus unweighted).* Ozuch/Biquard/Yao control residuals in weighted Hölder norms adapted to the neck scale; the problem's $\delta_\sigma$ is the unweighted supremum of $\|A_g-\sigma\mathrm{Id}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. A weight that suppresses the neck region can hide an unweighted eigenvalue spike exactly where curvature concentrates. A candidate sub‑lemma worth isolating: *on a gluing neck of a positive Einstein orbifold with an ALE bubble, is $\sup_{\mathrm{neck}}\|A_g-\mathrm{Id}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ bounded below by a dimension‑only constant?* If yes, orbifold smoothing cannot drive $\delta_\sigma\to0$ and route (iii) cannot supply the pinched inputs — again supporting the existence side.

*Gap 2 (local obstruction versus global exclusion).* The desingularization obstruction only blocks Einstein metrics that are GH‑close to the chosen degeneration. A refutation must combine it with a global, sign‑independent exclusion — this is where LeBrun's Seiberg–Witten obstruction is the correct partner, because it excludes *all* Einstein metrics on $M$ (all signs) via the $\int(\tfrac{s^2}{24}+2|W^+|^2)$ lower bound forced by a nonzero SW basic class. The clean joint statement to aim at: a fixed SW‑obstructed $M^4$ whose only compactness limits under $\delta_\sigma\to0$ are orbifold degenerations blocked by Gap‑1 lower bounds. If the neck lower bound holds, this program cannot close, which is itself a strong structural conclusion.

## Route (iv): products and topological transfer within one dimension

The requirement to defeat both branches in one fixed dimension makes transfer operations attractive, but the scaling structure forbids the obvious ones. A product of a positively pinched factor ($A_g$ eigenvalues near $+1$) with a sub‑zero pinched factor (eigenvalues near $-1$) has $A_{g_1\oplus g_2}$ eigenvalues straddling zero; by the dossier's own scaling reduction, any interval containing zero in its interior is automatically satisfiable after rescaling and conveys no Einstein information. So opposite‑sign products are dead as transfer devices.

Same‑sign products preserve a branch but cannot manufacture the *other* branch in the same dimension. This exposes a genuine structural obstacle to any refutation: the positive branch is governed by Myers/Bochner (finiteness, $b_1=0$, sphere‑like almost‑rigidity), the minus branch by aspherical/simplicial‑volume rigidity (Gromov's $\|M\|\le(n-1)^n n!\,\mathrm{Vol}$ from $\mathrm{Ric}\ge-(n-1)g$, and Besson–Courtois–Gallot entropy). A single fixed manifold pinchable near $+g$ tends to be sphere‑like ($\chi>0$, $b_1=0$), while one pinchable near $-g$ tends to be aspherical with positive simplicial volume — these are nearly disjoint topological worlds. The high‑value conjecture is therefore that **in every dimension at least one branch is a theorem**, i.e. the "$\vee$" is never vacuous, precisely because no fixed manifold can be both sphere‑like and aspherical. Making "the two branches live on disjoint manifold classes" into a proof would establish $\forall n\,Q_n$ without ever exhibiting the dimension‑only constants explicitly on a single manifold.

## Cross tests against the standard obstructions

*Chern–Weil.* Already used above: $\delta_\sigma<1/(1+\sqrt3)$ forces $2\chi>3|\tau|$ (both signs), and pinning $s,E$ turns Gauss–Bonnet into the collapse‑versus‑bubbling accounting that drives route (i). A four‑manifold on or beyond Hitchin–Thorpe cannot host any tight $\pm1$ pinch, so such manifolds are excluded from both branches simultaneously — a self‑consistency test any candidate must pass.

*Myers/Bochner.* Positive branch: finite $\pi_1$, $b_1=0$, diameter bound — a positive‑branch counterexample is simply connected with $\chi>0$. Minus branch: $\mathrm{Ric}<0$ kills Killing fields (finite isometry group), so the minus‑branch existence route cannot lean on symmetry and must be genuinely PDE‑theoretic.

*Simplicial volume / entropy.* For the minus branch, $\mathrm{Ric}\approx-g$ gives $\|M\|\le c_n\mathrm{Vol}(M)$ and connects to BCG minimal‑volume‑entropy rigidity. Hamenstädt–Jäckel's Gromov–Thurston examples show non‑locally‑symmetric sub‑zero pinched manifolds still carry Einstein metrics, so entropy rigidity is a construction tool, not an exclusion — pushing toward the possibility that $P_n^-$ is *true* broadly.

*Spin / index / Seiberg–Witten.* These are the only sign‑independent exclusions available and hence the indispensable partner for any refutation, but they also test the positive‑branch counterexample from the other side: on spin manifolds positive scalar curvature (which pinched $+g$ implies) forces $\hat A=0$ and constrains SW invariants; where a nonzero SW class survives, LeBrun's inequality excludes Einstein but does *not* by itself exclude a merely pinched metric (which may carry large $W^+$). So SW gives the "no Einstein" half cleanly while leaving the "pinchable" half to be settled by the neck/collapse lemmas — confirming that the real content lives there.

## The most promising existence direction, stated as a missing analytic input

The single most valuable target is the minus branch as a global theorem: extend Jäckel's effective stability by removing the sectional‑below‑zero, thin‑part, and derivative hypotheses, keeping only $\delta_-(g)\le\varepsilon_n$. The concrete missing input is a uniform invertibility estimate for the linearized Einstein operator (Einstein operator plus gauge, the Lichnerowicz‑type Laplacian) that depends only on the dimension and on $\delta_-$, without an injectivity‑radius or thin‑part input — the place every current proof spends its extra assumptions. Two sub‑questions decide feasibility: (1) does $\mathrm{Ric}\approx-g$ alone give a dimension‑only lower bound on the bottom of the relevant Laplacian spectrum modulo gauge, or can near‑kernel modes appear under collapse; (2) can the near‑kernel modes be absorbed as moduli (as in Anderson–Bamler Dehn filling) rather than becoming genuine obstructions. If (1) holds in the non‑collapsed regime and (2) handles collapse, $P_n^-$ becomes a theorem for all $n$, which alone settles $\forall n\,Q_n$ regardless of the positive branch. The corresponding minus‑branch counterexample search, by contrast, is blocked above dimension four by the absence of any known Einstein obstruction, and in dimension four by the neck obstruction to sub‑zero pinching on blow‑ups — so the evidence currently tilts toward existence via the minus branch, with the positive branch as an independent second route through the collapse lemma.
