The full Yau 26 problem remains unresolved: no dimension-only pinching interval has been proved, no fixed dimension has been shown to defeat both normalized signs, and no Lean formalization of a final mathematical resolution exists. This Step nevertheless changed the negative graph-neck program substantially by replacing an unsupported fixed-length nonlinear core picture with two exact structures: unavoidable contraction at a reflected torus center and compulsory anisotropy flux at every viable finite exit.

The Step 74 mode equation was correct, but its interpretation was not. For ψ''+3ψ'−k²e^(−2t)ψ=0, the two standard radial basis functions are bounded in t for each fixed k, yet the solution normalized by ψ(0)=1 and ψ'(0)=0 satisfies lim ψ=sinh(k)/k. The transfer factors 1.175, 1.813, 3.339, 14.841, and 186.310 for k=1,2,3,5,8 show exponential frequency amplification. Fixed-mode Neumann–Dirichlet uniqueness therefore supplies neither frequency-uniform control nor invertibility of the nonlinear Einstein map. The prescribed CMC ordinary differential model also did not evolve its curvature source from an intrinsic metric satisfying the Hamilton and momentum equations. Moreover, the normal identity e^(2R)(3−H(R))=3+∫e^(2r)(|S−I|²+E_rr)dr shows that physical Gaussian length must diverge when both the full defect and 3−H(R) vanish. These facts remove the former premise that a core interval of length about five is geometrically sufficient.

A new exact theorem identifies the local burden at every T²-cohomogeneity-one reflected center. For q=ds²+G(s) on T³, with flat T² orbits, A=(1/2)G^(−1)G' and h=tr A, one has R_q=−2h'−|A|²−h². Since h=(log sqrt(det G))' is periodic with mean zero, either h is identically zero or it has a downward zero. At such a zero, if R_q=−6, then the orbit-plane sectional curvature is K_orb=−det A=|A|²/2=3−h'≥3. The orbit-normal direction is an eigenvector of Ric_q+3I with eigenvalue −K_orb≤−3. At an exact reflected four-dimensional Einstein center, S'(0)=Ric_q+3I, so some principal direction initially contracts with acceleration at most −3. This is not peculiar to the sinusoidal seed and includes non-diagonal periodic 2×2 orbit metrics.

The small-defect version was strengthened by independent verification after the main note was written. If E=Ric_4+3g and ||E||_op≤δ, then e=R_q+6=tr_T E−E_nn. Combining the downward-zero equation directly with S'=Ric_q+3I−E_T avoids counting E_vv twice and gives λ_min S'(0)≤−3+3δ. The workspace note and symbolic JSON currently contain the weaker valid constant −3+5δ and must be amended; group messages #537–#538 also stopped at the weaker constant. The exact equivalence is between K_orb≥3 and the selected orbit-normal eigenvalue being at most −3, not between K_orb≥3 and the existence of an unspecified small eigenvalue.

The anisotropy equation supplies the complementary outer-face structure. In conformal coordinates for the diagonal orthogonally-transitive class, d=b−c satisfies div(e^(b+c) grad d)=0 for an exact Einstein metric. Reflection makes its normal derivative zero at the inner face. If the two orbit principal curvatures are also equal at a finite outer face, the weighted energy identity forces d to be constant everywhere. The center metric is then conformal to a flat torus metric, which cannot have scalar curvature −6 by the integrated Yamabe equation. Thus a successful finite interface cannot become exactly umbilic in the orbit directions. It must retain nonzero d_n at every finite stage, even if its unweighted size tends to zero; exponential geometric weights may allow that small derivative to carry a nonvanishing flux into the Step 73 adiabatic tail.

The center theorem explains the center-shape computations. In the reciprocal-warp Yamabe family, the sampled maximum positive sectional curvature fell from 5.323 at amplitude one to 3.126 at amplitude eight, 3.090 at amplitude ten, and 3.051 at amplitude fifteen. A three-mode shape search reached 3.03297. The analytic theorem proves the value three is a lower bound throughout the whole T²-cohomogeneity-one center class, so these high-amplitude seeds approach the sharp local obstruction rather than eliminating initial shear. Attempts to search a broader independent two-warp family timed out because each shape evaluation solved a nonlinear periodic Yamabe problem; their incomplete console samples did not produce a durable result and are not used.

Several full-five-component Gaussian searches were then used to pressure the corrected formulation. Runs that fixed equal orbit derivatives at the outer face were recognized as solving an already impossible exact problem. The most relevant free-flux run fixed the Fourier-projected Step 69 center and its projected Koiso second jet, left the outer metric and anisotropy flux free, and minimized E_rr, E_rz, E_zz, E_x, and E_y together. Its sampled operator suprema remained approximately 16.03, 16.03, and 15.96 for R=2,4,6, concentrated near physical radius 0.19. A continuation from a numerical germ at t=0.15 similarly retained sampled suprema 23.52, 22.12, and 21.60 near the inner interface. Independent reading confirmed the Ricci formulas and orthonormal normalization, but also found that all iterations ended at the function-evaluation cap, optimized an averaged Frobenius residual rather than the operator supremum, lacked off-grid testing, and used only a Fourier projection of the continuous center. The germ continuation matched only metric and first normal derivative, while its global radial basis coupled outer value and derivative. These computations therefore establish only that the tested finite spectral parameterizations did not resolve the analytic jet cascade; they do not supply a numerical lower bound or geometric nonexistence result.

The negative construction problem is now more precise. A parameter-dependent core must transform an order-three initial contraction into an outer state with small full Einstein defect, small S−I, small scaled intrinsic curvature, and a small but nonzero anisotropy derivative carrying the required weighted flux. Exact finite umbilicity is impossible, while the Step 73 long tail shows that approximate flattening after such an entry is inexpensive. The decisive mathematical question is whether the center contraction, anisotropy flux, and normal weighted balance combine into a global positive cost, or whether a multiscale core can redistribute them over increasing physical length. Either answer would materially advance the negative branch. Even success there would leave the positive branch and the all-dimensional quantifiers; peer messages #535–#539 also show that the current positive-orbifold local obstruction calculations remain nondecisive for the original problem.
