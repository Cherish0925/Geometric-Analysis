## Deep-Reasoning

Step 97 produced a structural breakthrough in understanding the free-T² connector problem for the negative branch of Yau 26. The central discovery is the **orbit-shape conformal dilution mechanism**: for a trial metric of the form q(n,s)=cosh²(n)·q₀(s) on the quotient cylinder, the Raychaudhuri equation H'+|W|²=3 is satisfied exactly (H=3tanh(n)), while the tangential Ricci residual E_T=Ric(q₀)+2sech²(n)·q decays at rate e^{-2n}. This was verified numerically with fitted decay rate 1.9745≈2.0 and perfect agreement between the analytic formula and the full numerical Ricci computation.

The mechanism operates through a simple but powerful principle: in an expanding geometry where the metric grows as cosh²(n), the s-derivatives of the orbit shape α₀(s) are diluted by the conformal factor. The 3D Ricci contribution Ric(q₀) is constant in n, but its operator norm q⁻¹Ric(q₀) decays as cosh⁻²(n)≈4e⁻²ⁿ because the inverse metric shrinks. The additional 2sech²(n)·q term from the Raychaudhuri-compatible Weingarten also decays at rate e⁻²ⁿ. Together, the total operator-norm defect drops from 9.25 at n=0 to 0.012 at n=4 and 0.0002 at n=6.

This discovery has profound implications for the connector construction. Previous steps (85-96) established that exact OT connectors are obstructed by the Bochner/resolvent mechanism, and that charged connectors face exact cancellations in the twist tension. The present finding shows that the orbit-shape transition—previously thought to require forced adiabatic decay at cost O(|α₀|/K) per unit length—is actually handled for free by the expanding geometry. The orbit shape can be maintained at full amplitude throughout the connector, with its Ricci contribution naturally suppressed by the conformal dilution.

The step also identified the Hamiltonian constraint as the key structural connection between the Koiso center and the expanding regime. At a minimal center (H=0) with Weingarten energy |W|²=S, the Hamiltonian requires R(q₀)=-6-S, which demands a curved T³ separator. The backbone ODE (σ''=6-σ'²-uσ', u'=3-u²-σ'u) with flat-T³ initial conditions gives σ''(0)=6 and u'(0)=3, yielding H'(0)=9—but Raychaudhuri demands H'(0)=3-S. This 6+S mismatch is precisely the orbit-shape curvature contribution to the tangential Einstein equations. The cosh²(n) trial resolves this by satisfying Raychaudhuri exactly while leaving the orbit curvature as a controlled exponentially-decaying tangential residual.

The remaining gap is the center-to-expansion connection. The cosh²(n)·q₀ trial has H(0)=0 and |W|²(0)=0, but the Koiso center has |W|²=S with off-diagonal Weingarten from the charge. The Hamiltonian deficit at n=0 is 5.1 (R(q₀)=-0.9 vs required -6). This mismatch reflects the difference between the pure diagonal orbit metric q₀ and the full Koiso separator metric with connection and curved-orbit-path curvature. To close this gap, either: (1) the Koiso analytic germ must be continued from the center past the Gaussian conjugate point (n≈0.47 for the base-circle collapse) into the expanding regime, where it hands off to the cosh² decay; or (2) a modified trial in harmonic/conformal gauge must interpolate between the exact Koiso center and the cosh² regime without coordinate singularities.

For the full Yau 26 problem, this mechanism is specific to the free-T² negative branch. The positive branch requires different manifold families and Einstein obstructions. Higher dimensions lack the specific four-dimensional topology. And even completing the negative-branch free-T² connector would need to be embedded in a closed graph manifold with certified all-sign Einstein nonexistence. The orbit-shape dilution mechanism is a significant structural advance, but it addresses only one component of a multi-component problem.
