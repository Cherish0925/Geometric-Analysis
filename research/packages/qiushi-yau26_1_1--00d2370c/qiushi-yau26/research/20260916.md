# Ricci Pinching and Einstein Metrics: Three Connected Research Problems

Does pointwise Ricci pinching ensure that the underlying smooth manifold admits an Einstein metric? A bound on Ricci curvature does not by itself control full curvature, noncollapse, an inverse operator, or compactness. Constructing a metric with small Einstein defect and proving the existence of an exact Einstein metric are therefore distinct problems.

The papers develop three connected lines: rigidity near a topological equality, pointwise approximation on fixed orbifold resolutions and surface bundles, and global Einstein existence on the same smooth manifold. They include arguments, calculations, and the hypotheses under which these apply. New constructions, representations, and analytic methods remain part of the research scope.

The [research map](../references/research-map.md) locates statements, proofs, computations, and open problems from the three research Sessions and the accumulated web manuscripts. The [source index](../references/source-coverage.json) records their correspondence with the retained material.

## 1. Problem and normalization

Work with closed, connected, smooth real manifolds. The working formulation asks whether, for each $n\ge2$, there are real constants $c_n<C_n$, depending only on dimension, such that every smooth Riemannian metric satisfying

$$
c_ng\le\operatorname{Ric}_g\le C_ng
$$

lies on a smooth manifold admitting some positive-definite smooth metric $h$ with $\operatorname{Ric}_h=\lambda h$ for a constant $\lambda\in\mathbb R$. The inequalities hold as quadratic forms at every point. Neither the signs of $c_n,C_n$ nor the sign of $\lambda$ are prescribed. The output need not be close to $g$ or preserve its symmetries.

Set

$$
\delta_\sigma(g)=\left\|g^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}_g-\sigma I\right\|_{L^\infty,\mathrm{op}},
\qquad e_\sigma(M)=\inf_{g\in\operatorname{Met}(M)}\delta_\sigma(g),
\quad \sigma=\pm1.
$$

In a fixed dimension, the interval statement is equivalent to the existence of a dimension-uniform sufficient defect threshold for at least one sign. Indeed, for $\widehat g=ag$, the covariant Ricci tensor is unchanged and the Ricci endomorphism is divided by $a$. Choose a nonzero interior point $\mu$ of the interval and multiply a metric normalized near $\operatorname{sign}\mu$ by $1/|\mu|$; sufficiently small defect then places its Ricci eigenvalues inside the interval. Conversely, a small interval centered at $1$ or $-1$ gives the corresponding interval statement.

A positive answer must cover every admissible smooth manifold. A complete disproof requires failure of both normalized branches in one fixed dimension; the two branches may use different manifolds. A fixed manifold with no Einstein metric and $e_\sigma(M)=0$ disproves that branch. A sequence of such manifolds with defects tending to zero also suffices. Throughout, the excluded output must include Einstein metrics of every sign.

The absence of a higher-dimensional mechanism limits an all-dimensional affirmative proof. It does not prevent a complete disproof by two branches in dimension four or another fixed dimension.

See [foundations](20260910.tex) for the classical hypotheses, scaling, and literature. The stated interval problem is the working formulation; historical problem text and later commentary are distinct sources.

## 2. The three scientific lines

The lines address three transfers: from global near-equality information to controlled local geometry; from a small residual to a solution of the actual equation; and from local or restricted structure to Einstein existence on a fixed smooth manifold. They share foundations and computations. They are neither an exhaustive classification nor a priority order, and do not prescribe fixed Session roles. A new argument may connect these questions or introduce a different route to the original problem.

### 2.1 Rigidity: can exact Einstein geometry approach the topological boundary?

The [uniform-gap paper](20260916_uniform_gap.tex) develops a general mechanism. Let a closed oriented four-manifold satisfy $\mathrm{MP}(A)$: $b_+(M)>1$, and for every metric there is an applicable monopole class $a_g$ with $(a_g^+)^2\ge A>0$. Suppose also that $\chi(M)\le LA$. Its exact-Einstein theorem states

$$
M\text{ admits an Einstein metric}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{2\chi(M)+3\tau(M)}{A}\ge\frac23+\eta(L),
\qquad\eta(L)>0.
$$

Uniformity is controlled by $L$, not by compactness on one fixed manifold. After normalizing to $\operatorname{Ric}=-g$, the monopole and Chern–Weil estimates give

$$
\operatorname{Vol}(M)\ge2\pi^2A,
\qquad\fint_M|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\le16L,
$$

using the full four-index curvature norm. Approach to the critical ratio also makes the average of a nonnegative calibration density tend to zero. Fixed-scale selection, collapse-tolerant regularity for exact Einstein metrics, and a local unfolding with controlled multiplicity carry this small density to one controlled chart. The limiting Weyl spectrum is incompatible with local Einstein geometry. Only one chart retaining the contradictory information is required; a smooth compact limit of the entire sequence is not.

Blowups provide an application. For a closed minimal complex surface of general type $X$ with $b_+(X)>1$, put

$$
A=c_1^2(X)>0,\qquad M=X\#k\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}.
$$

The classical Einstein obstruction first gives $k\le A/3$. Noether's inequality then gives $\chi(M)/A<42$, so the same $\eta(42)$ applies. Consequently, for some $\gamma>0$ independent of $X,k$,

$$
k\ge\left(\frac13-\gamma\right)A
$$

excludes every Einstein metric on $M$. The classical bound establishes the budget before the new separation is proved; the desired gap is not assumed.

The [rigidity paper](20260916_rigidity.tex) develops the near-equality density, local selection and transfer, necessary collapse, and diameter escape, then studies extension from exact to approximate equations. An exact-Einstein topological gap is not a uniform Ricci-defect gap for all near-Einstein inputs.

For $d=(1/3-k/A)_+$, a near-critical sequence with $\delta_-\to0$ and $d\to0$ has a vanishing normalized volume fraction of fixed-scale noncollapsed centers. The bounded-diameter conclusion is $\delta_-^2+d\ge c(D)>0$; the unrestricted target is $\delta_-^2+d\ge c_*>0$. Spinor estimates, Walsh phase separation, weak pairings, and degenerate local flows investigate whether near-equality information survives this degeneration. A construction at one fixed ratio $k/A<1/3$ inside the forbidden band does not satisfy $d\to0$ and requires a separate argument.

### 2.2 Construction: how can pointwise Ricci defect vanish on fixed smooth topology?

#### Fixed orbifold resolutions: Weyl freedom and logarithmic scales

The [construction paper](20260916_construction.tex) treats ALE cores, Eguchi–Hanson corrections, logarithmic Weyl matching, angular modes, and interactions between nodes. For a Weyl quadratic tensor $K$ satisfying the stated trace, divergence, and radial conditions,

$$
D\operatorname{Ric}_{e}[\eta(\log r)K]
=-\frac{\eta''+6\eta'}{2r^2}K.
$$

A bare Ricci-flat EH core has order-one endomorphism defect relative to $\operatorname{Ric}\approx\sigma g$; small volume cannot hide it. The nonzero Einstein source must first be corrected in the core. Core and exterior quadratic terms then have the same Ricci contraction and can be compared in a common radial gauge. Since $K=O(r^2)$, spreading a finite Weyl mismatch over a logarithmic annulus of length $L$ costs $O(L^{-1})$ in the linearized Ricci equation. Second-order tails, nonlinear estimates in all three regions, and global matching yield, under the specified fixed-orbifold and resolution hypotheses,

$$
\delta_\sigma(g_a)\le\frac{C}{|\log a|}\longrightarrow0.
$$

This establishes $e_\sigma(M)=0$ for the specified smooth resolution, not the absence of another Einstein metric there. Chirality cancellation, projected flux, and obstruction computations retain their own gauge, mode, and remainder hypotheses.

The spherical example makes a further distinction precise. For the specified $A_1$ fillings of $Q=S^4/\mathbb Z_2$, the construction gives $\|g_i^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}_{g_i}-3I\|_\infty\to0$ and Gromov–Hausdorff convergence to $Q$. Ozuch's exact-Einstein obstruction excludes a sequence of smooth metrics with $\operatorname{Ric}=3g$ converging to this orbifold. Thus this limit can be attained by pointwise approximations but not by exact Einstein metrics; this still says nothing about exact metrics away from that degeneration. The construction paper's `sec:football-comparison` supplies the involution, normalization, fixed-resolution hypotheses, and proof. Its `sec:exact-desingularization` compares the pointwise endpoint with the earlier finite-$L^p$ constructions and logarithmic cutoffs.

#### Fixed surface bundles: Dehn twists and balanced double collars

Chapter six of the [cumulative manuscript](20260912.tex) gives a different construction on a fixed Dehn-twist surface bundle $E_D$. A path of hyperbolic fiber metrics satisfies $h_1=D^*h_0$. Its parameter changes slowly along a long collar in a degenerating base, and the ends are identified by the same mapping class. The full Ricci-defect matrix gives

$$
\delta_-(G_\ell)=O(L^{-1})\longrightarrow0,
\qquad\operatorname{Vol}(E_D,G_\ell)=V_0.
$$

For this fixed-fiber, fixed-path family, every fixed order also satisfies $\|\nabla^m\operatorname{Rm}\|_\infty\le C_m$ and $\|\nabla^m(\operatorname{Ric}+G_\ell)\|_\infty\le C_m/L$. The short base circle creates no inverse-length loss: coefficients are independent of its angular coordinate, and the orthonormal connection contains bounded $\tanh t$ terms. Degeneration may therefore occur in injectivity radius, collar length, and global gluing without curvature blowup. These bounds describe this explicit family, not arbitrary Ricci-pinched metrics.

Degenerating both fiber and base collars and balancing their profiles cancels the two mixed linear terms in the core:

$$
\delta_-(G_\varepsilon)=\frac{2}{\pi^4}\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4).
$$

Three scalar correction equations, tail estimates, and global cutoffs then reduce the residual to $O(\varepsilon^3)$. Global TT test tensors and the conformal Ricci equation give the corresponding lower bounds within the specified corrected family and conformal classes. This explains an obstruction to a particular correction, not to Einstein metrics in every conformal class. The [global paper](20260916_global.tex) relates these bounds to the smooth-manifold question.

Three families must be distinguished: fixed-fiber slow interpolation, simultaneous fiber/base degeneration, and tensor-corrected metrics. The corrected family need not preserve the original exact volume or submersion structure. The [construction paper](20260916_construction.tex) puts both mechanisms, their sources, tails, global matching, and exact-repair questions in one argument.

These are not two names for one trick. Surface bundles use monodromy and double-collar balance; log-Weyl matching uses directions invisible to the local Ricci linearization. Both produce pointwise inputs of the required kind. Their global Einstein questions remain separate.

### 2.3 Global existence: can approximation and exact Einstein geometry separate on the same manifold?

The [global paper](20260916_global.tex) treats surface bundles, graph manifolds, long-neck connectors, algebraic smoothing, and global topology.

Write $\mathsf P_\sigma(M)$ for $e_\sigma(M)=0$ and $\mathsf N(M)$ for the absence of every Einstein metric. A fixed-object branch counterexample requires both on the same $M$. Constructing any exact Einstein metric disproves $\mathsf N(M)$. The property $\mathsf P_-$ on a surface bundle and $\mathsf N$ on an unrelated graph manifold cannot be combined into a counterexample.

**Global invariants and attainment on surface bundles.** For a connected degree-$d$ cover of $E_D$, set $V_*=dV_0$. The fixed-topology construction supplies upper bounds, and the canonical monopole classes supply matching lower bounds over all metrics. The resulting Yamabe invariant is $-4\sqrt{V_*}$; the minimal volumes under $s\ge-4$ and $\operatorname{Ric}\ge-g$ are both $V_*$. The infima of scalar, Ricci, and full-curvature squared energies are $16V_*,4V_*,8V_*$. These are global conclusions even though general Einstein existence is unresolved. Equality in the first two energies forces an excluded Kähler structure. The last instead satisfies

$$
\int|\operatorname{Rm}|^2=8V_*
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda g.
$$

Its attainment is therefore precisely the unresolved Einstein-existence question. A hypothetical normalized Einstein metric has negative Einstein constant and $V_*<\operatorname{Vol}<3V_*$. Submersion, harmonic-representative, optimal-target, and Weyl-eigenline arguments still require actual geometric structure; a bundle projection does not force an unknown metric to be adapted to it.

**The cost of gluing graph manifolds.** The specified nontrivial extended-graph class has an all-sign Einstein obstruction but lacks $\mathsf P_-$. Hypothetical approximating metrics must have mean Weyl energy tending to zero. A hyperbolic local limit is nevertheless possible, so the forbidden-local-spectrum argument from the rigidity line does not apply unchanged. The question is whether nonhyperbolic gluing topology can be confined to degenerating regions while the full Ricci defect vanishes.

Charged local Einstein germs, Jacobi–Maxwell estimates, Noether identities, and two-ended matching equations provide analytic structure. A local germ does not solve the global boundary-value problem; failure of one symmetric model does not exclude other constructions. Relative Chern–Gauss–Bonnet identities and BCG-type volume bounds must be compared with the input construction on the same object.

A separate completed comparison concerns $N\#N$, where $N$ is a closed oriented hyperbolic four-manifold. The global paper's `sec:hyperbolic-double` combines the degree-two volume bound and Chern–Gauss–Bonnet to give the all-metrics estimate

$$
e_-(N\#N)\ge
\frac{1-\sqrt{1-1/\chi(N)}}{1+\sqrt{1-1/\chi(N)}}>0.
$$

Its dependence on $\chi(N)$ is essential. Letting this lower bound tend to zero across different $N$ does not construct a sequence of metrics with small defect.

**Existence through algebraic smoothing.** The [construction paper](20260916_construction.tex) retains the BCGP product quotient and the seven-line Campedelli cover as distinct examples, with their equations, nodes, and smoothing arguments. Pulling a negative Kähler–Einstein metric back from a smooth fiber proves Einstein existence on the underlying smooth manifold. It can change the original resolution's complex structure. Non-ampleness of its original canonical bundle is not an all-metrics Einstein obstruction.

**Positive pinching and toric topology.** The [global paper](20260916_global.tex) derives rod equations, corner expansions, separated Einstein germs, and multi-rod parameter families. Integral rod labels determine smooth topology; the full Ricci system determines metric matching. The fifth-order mismatch along a common constant-curvature rod and the incompatible end cone angles of a separated family identify concrete failures, while preserving the local constructions and the remaining global filling problem.

## 3. Connections between the methods

Scaling and curvature-norm conventions come first. Near-equality in integral inequalities can then be resolved into local defect quantities. One set of methods uses regularity to obtain an impossible limiting geometry; another uses freedoms not directly controlled by Ricci contraction to construct multiscale approximate solutions.

Local-flow analysis develops selected supports, critical Sobolev cancellation, weighted energies, and Moser iteration. Highest-cutoff weights and a common existence time require actual estimates; finite-order identities do not prove continuation. Construction analysis distinguishes the full tensor kernel from the TT kernel, finite-mode projections from the global Green operator, and a leading obstruction from changes allowed across large scale ratios.

The [foundations paper](20260910.tex) gives a contraction argument with explicit constants. For a gauge-fixed equation $F(u)=r+Lu+N(u)$ with inverse bound $B$ and quadratic nonlinear constant $K$, take $R=2B\|r\|$. The ball of radius $R$ must consist of admissible metrics and satisfy $8B^2K\|r\|\le1$. Residual, inverse, and nonlinearity must be measured in the same spaces and parameter scales. With a kernel, solve the complement equation and then the complete finite-dimensional obstruction equation. Approximate construction, complement correction, and exact existence are three distinct steps.

The surface-bundle spectral analysis gives a concrete warning. On an exact Einstein closed product, after removing the actual kernel, one can have $\|h_\ell\|_\infty\gtrsim\log(1/\ell)$ while $\|Jh_\ell\|_{C^m}$ stays bounded. Under the constructed spectral conditions, the complementary $L^2$ gap remains positive. An $L^2$ inverse bound does not imply a maximum-norm inverse bound.

The papers also retain computations that changed the research direction. The construction paper writes out the EH three-function fitting problem, endpoint conditions, residual, and boundary-discretization failure. The rigidity paper develops spinor defects, Walsh phase separation, and terminal transfer. The global paper derives every Gaussian Ricci block, noncommutative matrix derivatives, six endpoint conditions, and matching with shared parameters. The mathematical objects can be reconstructed from the text rather than guessed from old scripts.

Four-dimensional Weyl chirality, Chern–Weil identities, and monopole theory are special tools. Higher-dimensional research can examine critical $L^{n/2}$ curvature budgets, ALE bundles, spectral effects, and collapse, but does not inherit four-dimensional topological conclusions. Products may propagate approximate inputs without forcing an arbitrary Einstein metric to split.

The [cumulative manuscript](20260912.tex) retains broader developments: ADE/type-T/Wahl resolutions, scalar repair, algebraic geometry and smoothing obstructions, surface bundles, Weyl source terms, harmonic methods, and adapted Einstein equations. It complements the three papers and is not merely background.

The organization of the research and the division into publishable results are different questions. The A1 and surface-bundle constructions have distinct proof chains; the latter also supplies the upper bounds for its global variational theory. Those shared arguments should remain connected rather than counted twice. A paper may join connected results or isolate an independently useful theorem, without having to match one of the three lines exactly.

## 5. Continuing the research

The rigidity question is how near-equality information can retain local force when small Ricci defect permits collapse. The construction question is which fixed smooth objects admit pointwise approximation, and whether residual, inverse, and complete obstruction can be controlled on a common scale. The global question is object-specific: attainment and general Einstein structure on surface bundles, complete connectors on graph manifolds, existence through algebraic smoothing, positive-branch integral topology, and higher-dimensional mechanisms.

These questions can supply one another with constructions, obstructions, and conditional theorems. No single method is prescribed. Existing arguments may be strengthened, combined, or corrected by counterexamples. New invariants, function spaces, decompositions, constructions, and analytic mechanisms are admissible when their conclusions return rigorously to the original problem.
