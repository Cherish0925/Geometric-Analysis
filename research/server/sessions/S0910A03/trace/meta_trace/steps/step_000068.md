Step 68 absorbed the newly supplied 20260912.tex manuscript (6558 lines) and extracted its central structural insight: two complementary difficulties organize the negative-branch counterexample problem. The surface bundle E_D achieves δ→0 at rate Θ(ε³) with exact coefficient 2/π⁴ for the balanced double-collar and a matched lower bound cε³ from a global TT obstruction, but Einstein nonexistence on E_D is genuinely open — every standard exclusion mechanism fails (Hitchin–Thorpe satisfied, b₊>1, no complex structure on any cover, not virtually Kähler, no sec≤0; the full curvature energy inf∫|Rm|²=8V₀ is attained iff Einstein exists, and non-attainment of scalar/Ricci energy does not transfer). Extended graph manifolds have Di Cerbo Einstein nonexistence but no δ→0 construction, with Weyl concentration ||W||≥(1−3δ)/√3 and logarithmic diameter growth. These two advantages cannot simply be spliced.

The manuscript's Chapter 3 confirmed and extended our Step 67 local Koiso theorem with explicit seed metrics, calibration of the central torus, and necessary anisotropy bounds for S³ initial data. The Berger core analysis (explicit biaxial extension with curvature singularity at finite distance) shows that every specified symmetric extension hits a singularity — the local reversal cannot be continued in a symmetric ODE class. Chapter 6 provides the complete surface-bundle construction: the Ricci matrix (eq 3.66) has error exclusively in shape-operator terms, the balanced profiles give exact second-order coefficient, the linearized correction reduces to three solvable scalar equations with explicit Green operators, and the global TT tensor from concentrating holomorphic quadratic differentials produces the nonlinear lower bound cε³ ≤ Cδ+Cε²a+Ca². The non-zero flux (B-solution boundary term) prevents a Fredholm obstruction at second order. Chapter 8 provides two rigidity theorems for fibered Einstein metrics (integrable horizontal → product; basic mean curvature → product via Euler bundle + Wood + LeBrun), both only for metrics adapted to the fiber structure; the manuscript explicitly warns that a smooth manifold's fiber-bundle structure does not constrain a general Einstein metric. Chapter 10 (last chapter) gives volume-window, Weyl-concentration, and diameter-growth constraints for approximately pinched extended graph manifolds, the WP length descent excluding non-forced effective models, and Jäckel's effective stability theorem with its sectional-curvature hypotheses.

The boundary mean curvature threshold (lines 1658–1725) was verified computationally: h₀+h₁ ≤ 6k tanh(kL/2) for the bridge gives interface cost δ > csch²(R/√3), hence R > (√3/2)log(1/δ). This makes the graph manifold neck logarithmically long but with exponentially shrinking cross-section, so all neck components (Koiso half-core, adiabatic layers, transitions) have total volume → 0 — no volume obstruction exists. The ASD obstruction was verified: reflection across a codimension-1 hypersurface reverses 4D orientation, exchanging W⁺↔W⁻; if W⁺=0, both chiralities vanish, forcing constant curvature K=−1, and T³ cannot carry this. Equation (E) trace factor corrected to 1/2 (the Koiso conclusion is unaffected since ġ₀=0). The entropy approach fails for E_D because its simplicial-volume/entropy structure is too product-like (A > B always).

For the harmonic-map approach to Einstein nonexistence on E_D: an energy-minimizing harmonic map in the fiber-projection homotopy class exists (target negatively curved), and if it were a harmonic morphism, Wood + LeBrun would force a complex structure, contradicting Kotschick. The gap: harmonicity does not imply harmonic-morphism status (the horizontally conformal condition is independent); and the map may have critical points. This approach requires new analysis of the interaction between the Einstein equation, the critical set, and the aspherical topology. It remains speculative but is the only identified candidate mechanism.

The research has reached a genuinely new understanding of the problem's structure. The previous 66 steps of machinery development (cutoff flow, weighted estimates, all-order continuation, terminal density, graph-manifold barriers) are reusable tools, but they do not by themselves resolve either complementary difficulty. The next valuable work is to develop one of the blocking questions: Einstein nonexistence on E_D, or δ→0 construction on graph manifolds. The unread manuscript chapters (4: algebraic surfaces, 5: gauge theory saturation, 7: Weyl eigenlines, 9: harmonic representatives/optimal targets) may contain additional mechanisms relevant to these questions and should be read before committing to a large computation.
