The central mathematical discovery is that the orthogonally transitive T²-invariant Einstein system with Ric = −3g reduces to a **weighted harmonic map to H² = SL(2,ℝ)/SO(2)**, coupled to area and base conformal-factor equations. This reduction unifies all prior connector work—the Step 73 expanding tail, the Step 74/75 linearized mode analysis, the Step 76 center energy identities, and the Step 78 Bianchi VII₀ barrier—into a single coherent mathematical framework.

The structural equations are:
1. Area: Δ(eσ) = 6e^{2λ+σ} (Liouville-type, driven by Λ = −3)
2. Orbit: div(eσ M⁻¹dM) = 0 (weighted harmonic map to H²)
3. Conformal: Δλ = (|∇σ|² − e_{H²})/4
4. Constraints: one cross and one difference equation

The orbit equation in H² polar coordinates (d, ψ) yields:
- d-equation: div(eσ∇d) = eσ sinh(d)cosh(d)|∇ψ|² [positive source drives d outward]
- ψ-conservation: div(eσ sinh²(d)∇ψ) = 0 [angular momentum conservation]

The mechanism analysis reveals WHY the VII₀ barrier holds and what escapes it. In VII₀, the orbit map is helicoidal: (d(r), kz) in H². The angular velocity ψ_z = k is frozen, making the d-equation source sinh(d)cosh(d)k² permanently positive and d monotonically increasing. In the general two-variable case, ψ(r,z) evolves via the conservation law and is NOT frozen. The angular gradient |∇ψ| can become small, removing the d-equation source. The growing weight eσ (driven by Λ = −3) enables this redistribution.

Critically, the TTCS verification exposed three important constraints beyond the structural escape:

(a) **Iwasawa sign correction**: The α-equation in Iwasawa coordinates has a NEGATIVE sign: div(eσ∇α) = −e^{σ−2α}|∇β|². This is consistent with the POSITIVE sign in polar coordinates because the Christoffel symbols differ. The diagonal case (β=0) is unaffected.

(b) **Convex distance maximum principle**: For EXACT Einstein metrics on a reflected cylinder with constant outer boundary, the orbit map must be constant. Proof: F(U) = ½d²_{H²}(U,P) satisfies Hess F ≥ g_{H²}, so div(ρ∇(F∘U)) ≥ ρ|dU|². Neumann center + Dirichlet exit = 0 forces U ≡ P. This is a genuine obstruction to exact connectors.

(c) **Matrix Noether flow**: The full sl(2,ℝ)-valued conservation Q(r) = ∫ ρ M⁻¹M_r dz ≡ 0 is stronger than the scalar angular flux.

The maximum principle means the connector MUST be approximate (||E|| → 0, not E = 0). For approximate connectors, the forced orbit equation is div(ρ M⁻¹dM) = −2e^{2λ}ρ E°_orbit. Even as ||E||_∞ → 0, the weighted forcing e^{2λ}ρ||E|| ∼ e^{4r}δ can remain O(1) on long expanding connectors, potentially overcoming the maximum principle.

A zero-mode estimate gives the needed connector length: for a diagonal transition with d(r) = d₀(1−χ(r/R)), the orbit defect is O(d₀/R). Setting δ = d₀/R gives R = d₀/δ. For graph lattice parameter N with d₀ = 2 log N, the connector length R ∼ (2 log N)/δ grows logarithmically in N and inversely in δ.

The decisive open question is now precisely stated: can a sequence of approximate long connectors with ||E||_op → 0 transfer the center's O(1) orbit energy to the Step 73 tail interface while satisfying all constraints (Hamilton, momentum, base)? The answer determines the negative branch of Yau 26 for extended graph four-manifolds.

For the positive branch: A01's Green-response computation for the mixed-chirality desingularization and the corrected automorphism removal are the active analytical tools. The #₃(S²×S²) case cannot be positive Kähler–Einstein (b₊ = 3), so any result requires a non-Kähler mechanism.
