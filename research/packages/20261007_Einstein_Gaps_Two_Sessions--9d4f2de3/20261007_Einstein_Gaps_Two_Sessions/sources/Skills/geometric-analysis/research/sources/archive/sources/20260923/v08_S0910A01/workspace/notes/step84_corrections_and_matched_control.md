## 2. T-symmetry specialization (Strategist proposal)

**Pencil data.** Take $c=1/2$, $\kappa=-1$ in the family:
$$Q_0=\sum x_i^2,\qquad Q_{1,\varepsilon}=\varepsilon x_0^2-\varepsilon x_1^2+(1-\varepsilon)x_2^2
+(1+\varepsilon)x_3^2+\tfrac12 x_4^2.$$

The holomorphic involution $T:(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\mapsto(x_2,x_3,x_0,x_1,x_4)$
satisfies $T^*Q_0=Q_0$ and $T^*Q_{1,\varepsilon}=Q_0-Q_{1,\varepsilon}$, so $T$ preserves
every fiber $X_\varepsilon=V(Q_0)\cap V(Q_{1,\varepsilon})$.

**Node transitivity.** The group $G=\langle T, S_0, S_2\rangle$ with
$$S_0:(x_0,x_1,...)\mapsto(-x_0,x_1,...),\qquad
S_2:(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\mapsto(x_0,x_1,-x_2,x_3,x_4)$$
acts transitively on all four nodes:
- $T$: A-pair $\leftrightarrow$ B-pair
- $S_0$: exchanges within A-pair
- $S_2$: exchanges within B-pair

**Holomorphic vector field removal.** The continuous automorphism group of $X_0$ is
$\operatorname{Aut}^0\cong\mathbb G_m^2$, generated by rotations $V_1=x_0\partial_{x_1}-x_1\partial_{x_0}$
and $V_2=x_2\partial_{x_3}-x_3\partial_{x_2}$. Under $G$:
- $T_*V_1=V_2$, $T_*V_2=V_1$
- $S_{0*}V_1=-V_1$, $S_{0*}V_2=V_2$
- $S_{2*}V_1=V_1$, $S_{2*}V_2=-V_2$

Hence the $G$-invariant subspace of $\operatorname{span}(V_1,V_2)$ is $\{0\}$.
This removes the entire holomorphic-vector-field kernel in the $G$-equivariant category.

**Consequence for Spotti's theorem.** Spotti (arXiv:1211.5344) proves KE desingularization of
nodal del Pezzo orbifolds under a finite automorphism group assumption. $X_4^T$ has infinite
$\operatorname{Aut}^0$, so the stated theorem does not apply. However, in the $G$-equivariant
category, the invariant automorphisms are finite. This suggests an equivariant adaptation of
Spotti's analytic argument may succeed, where the substantive task is establishing the
$G$-invariant inverse function theorem estimate and nonlinear remainder control.

## 3. Matched-control reduction

**Setup.** Consider the $G$-equivariant KE family $\{g_\varepsilon\}$ on smooth fibers $X_\varepsilon$,
degenerating to $g_0$ on $X_0$. By $G$-transitivity on nodes, the four EH bubble scales are equal.
By Bando--Mabuchi uniqueness (up to $\operatorname{Aut}_0$) and $G$-equivariance of the
metric, the degeneration is a $G$-invariant curve in the Biquard--Ozuch parameter space.

**All-same chirality path.** The algebraic smoothing of $X_4^T$ gives the all-same chirality
configuration with topology $\operatorname{Bl}_5\mathbb P^2$ (Fano, $\tau=-4$). By Tian/OSS,
KE exists on every smooth fiber. This path gives $F_{\rm same}(t_\varepsilon)=0$ in matched
coordinates. At equal scale $t$:
$$F_{\rm same}(t)=t\cdot[s+d_{\rm same}+2c_{\rm same}]\cdot t^2+O(t^{7/2}),$$
so $s+d_{\rm same}+2c_{\rm same}=0$ at the leading $t^3$ layer (assuming no log terms at
this order and correct normalization matching).

**Mixed chirality coefficient.** The diagonal mixed pattern ($+$ at NN,SS; $-$ at NS,SN) gives
$\#_3(S^2\times S^2)$ ($\tau=0$). At equal scale:
$$F_{\rm mixed}(t)=t\cdot[s+d_{\rm same}+2c_{\rm opp}]\cdot t^2+O(t^{7/2}),$$
where $c_{\rm opp}$ is the adjacent opposite-chirality response. Using the control relation:
$$F_{\rm mixed}(t)=2(c_{\rm opp}-c_{\rm same})\cdot t^3+O(t^{7/2}).$$

**The decisive quantity is therefore $c_{\rm opp}-c_{\rm same}$.**

## 4. Chirality-response angular analysis

**Ozuch source formulas.** For $\zeta=(0,1,0)$, insertion direction $\omega_2$:
$$H^4(\zeta^+)=-\frac{\rho(\zeta)^-\circ\zeta^+}{2r^4},\qquad
H^4(\zeta^-)=-\frac{\zeta^-\circ\rho(\zeta)^+}{2r^4}$$
where $\rho_x(\zeta)_j=x^T(\zeta^+\circ\omega_j^-)x/|x|^2$.

The linearized curvature:
$$d_eR^-(H^4(\zeta^+))=\frac{12\pi_{\rm tr}(\rho(\zeta)^-\otimes\rho(\zeta)^-)}{r^6}
\quad\in\operatorname{End}(\Omega^-),$$
$$d_eR^+(H^4(\zeta^-))=\frac{12\pi_{\rm tr}(\rho(\zeta)^+\otimes\rho(\zeta)^+)}{r^6}
\quad\in\operatorname{End}(\Omega^+).$$

**Key angular identities** (to be verified computationally):
1. $|\rho_x(\omega_2^+)|^2=|\rho_x(\omega_2^-)|^2$ for all $x\in S^3$ (scalar H⁴ chirality-independent)
2. $\rho^+\otimes\rho^+-\rho^-\otimes\rho^-$ is traceless and in $\ell=2,4$ harmonics on $S^3$
3. The chirality-dependent curvature involves $\operatorname{End}(\Omega^+)$ vs $\operatorname{End}(\Omega^-)$,
   coupling differently to the receiver's obstruction sector

The crucial distinction: same-chirality response ($-$-sector source → $-$-sector obstruction)
vs cross-chirality response ($+$-sector source → $-$-sector obstruction). These couple through
different parts of the Lichnerowicz Green kernel on the product.
