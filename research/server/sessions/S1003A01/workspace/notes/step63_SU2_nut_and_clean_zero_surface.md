## 1. Compact clean torsion-zero surfaces [P]

Let `(M^4,g,J,Omega)` be Einstein almost-Kähler with scalar curvature `s != 0`, and let `T=nabla Omega`. There is no closed smooth embedded surface `C` contained in `{T=0}` on which `nabla T` is everywhere nonzero.

At a torsion zero the Step-62 first jet is

`f(z)=c z+(S+A) zbar`, with `||A||^2=8|c|^2+||S||^2`

and

`det_R Df=9|c|^4+|det(S+A)|^2`.

Because `TC` lies in `ker DT`, the determinant vanishes. Hence `c=0` and `det(S+A)=0`. If `DT` is nonzero, it has complex-antilinear complex rank one and real rank two; its kernel is exactly the `J`-complex line `TC`. The cone forces `A != 0`. The exact Step-62 curvature dictionary therefore gives `eta=0` and `W_3^+ != 0` everywhere on `C`.

AAD identifies `W_3^+` with a section of `K_J^{-2}`. Its nonvanishing restriction trivializes that line over `C`, so `2 c1(M,J)[C]=0`, hence `c1(M,J)[C]=0` as an integer pairing. On the other hand the actual canonical Chern form is

`gamma_J=R(Omega)-a wedge Ja`, representing `2 pi c1(M,J)`.

On `C`, `a=0`; Einsteinity, the zero Rayleigh value, and `eta=0` imply `R(Omega)=(s/4)Omega`. With the `J`-orientation on `C`,

`2 pi c1(M,J)[C]=(s/4) Area(C) != 0`,

a contradiction. This is sensitive to nonzero scalar curvature even though the pointwise cone is scalar-curvature independent. It does not exclude isolated zeros or a surface with exceptional points where `DT=0`. For a closed smooth zero surface with dense rank-two locus, `W_3^+|_C` is nonzero there and vanishes exactly where `DT=0`; its degree is

`deg(K_J^{-2}|_C)=s Area(C)/(4 pi)=2(chi(C)+C.C)`,

which is negative for `s<0`. If the exceptional zeros are isolated, their signed windings sum to this degree, so at least one is negative. Since this is a smooth, not known holomorphic, section, the missing information is whether the differentiated AAD system permits such negative winding. For nonisolated zeros one retains the localized first Chern class.

## 2. Analytic regular-nut equations [P]

For diagonal Bianchi IX use a geodesic radial variable `t`, `u=t^2`, and

`a_i=t f_i(u)`, `H_i=t a_i'/a_i=1+2u f_i'/f_i`, `Z_i=t a_i/(a_j a_k)=f_i/(f_j f_k)`.

For `lambda=-3`, the Einstein system is analytic at the nut:

`2u H_i' - H_i + H_i sum H - 2[Z_i^2-(Z_j-Z_k)^2] - 3u=0`,

with Hamiltonian constraint

`sum_{i<j}H_iH_j + sum Z_i^2 -2sum_{i<j}Z_iZ_j -3u=0`.

The general smooth diagonal Einstein nut has

`f_i=1+A_i u+...`, `A_i=1/6+d_i`, `d_1+d_2+d_3=0`.

Thus the two trace-free cubic coefficients are the local self-dual Weyl data. The exact recurrence in `step63_bianchiIX_nut_series.py` uniquely solved coefficients through `u^5`; the Einstein and Hamiltonian residuals vanish at every solved order.

For an invariant closed self-dual form `Omega=sum x_i omega_i`, put `X_i=x_i^2`. Closure is

`u X_i'=M_i X_i`, `M_i=2Z_i-H_j-H_k`.

Smoothness at the nut gives leading weights `R_i=X_i(0)>=0`, `sum R_i=1`. Constant length requires every positive coefficient `L_n=[u^n]sum X_i` to vanish. The script computed `L_1,...,L_5` exactly.

## 3. Exact exclusion of an interior three-direction nut [E]

The ideal generated by `L_1,...,L_5`, after `R_3=1-R_1-R_2`, has dimension one in the four geometric variables (the Singular output reports dimension two only because its auxiliary Rabinowitsch variable `z` is unused in the full ideal). A Rabinowitsch localization away from

`R_1 R_2 R_3 (d_1-d_2)(2d_1+d_2)(d_1+2d_2)`

has standard basis `1` (`bix_nut_component.out`). Therefore no solution through this order has all three leading form weights nonzero and pairwise-distinct metric cubic eigenvalues. More strongly, the minimal associated primes of the full ideal support three affine lines, the permutations of

`(d_1,d_2,d_3)=(1/6,-1/12,-1/12)`, `R=(1,r,-r)`.

The algebraic lines include signed/complexified weights. For real almost-Kähler data `R_i=x_i(0)^2>=0`, they reduce to the three vertices `R=(1,0,0)` and permutations. The apparent many least-squares outputs cluster near these positive-dimensional algebraic boundary components; the exact algebra rules out their interpretation as distinct feasible branches.

Substitution into the exact recurrence identifies the surviving branch. With axis 1 selected,

`f_1=1+u/3+u^2/30+u^3/630+u^4/22680+u^5/1247400+...`,

`f_2=f_3=1+u/12+u^2/480+u^3/40320+u^4/5806080+u^5/1277337600+...`.

These are precisely

`a_1(t)=sinh(sqrt(2)t)/sqrt(2)`, `a_2(t)=a_3(t)=sqrt(2)sinh(t/sqrt(2))`,

and direct substitution gives `mu_1=b_2=b_3=0`, hence `q=0`. This is the standard complex-hyperbolic Kähler metric in polar/Bianchi-IX form (up to the chosen structure-constant normalization), with `Omega=omega_1`; the other associated primes are its permutations. The formal series and the closed form agree through all five computed orders.

The finite algebra already forces any formal solution onto the biaxial Kähler initial data. This extends to all orders: if a coefficient `b_i u^n` (`n>=2`) is added to the metric series, the linearized order-`u^n` Einstein recurrence has eigenvalue `4(n+2)(n-1)` on the trace-free subspace and `2(n+2)(2n+1)` on the trace direction, hence is invertible. Closure transport is likewise unique and any component with `R_i=0` remains zero. The explicit hyperbolic functions supply the convergent solution with those data. The Hamiltonian constraint propagates by the contracted Bianchi identity, so the spatial recurrence is sufficient. Therefore, after axis permutation, every real formal diagonal invariant regular-nut solution is exactly the Kähler branch above; analytic germs, and smooth Einstein germs by analytic regularity together with elliptic analyticity of the harmonic form, obey the same local rigidity.

Consequently the proposed diagonal SU(2)-invariant regular-nut construction does **not** realize a strict negative-Einstein almost-Kähler isolated cone zero. This is a genuine invariant obstruction, not a solver failure. It does not exclude non-diagonal SU(2) metrics, forms not invariant under the full SU(2), or unrestricted analytic germs represented by the Step-62 numerical jets.

## 4. Interpretation

The Step-62 cone remains the unrestricted local theorem: every nondegenerate torsion zero has index +1, and finite general jets on the cone survive through D=5/selected D=6 tests. The present exact computation says that cohomogeneity-one regularity is much more rigid than the unrestricted equations. At a nut the orbit isotropy and the singular closure transport force the leading form direction to be the simple Kähler curvature eigenline, with the repeated eigenspace orthogonal to it; propagation then yields the Kähler CH metric. Thus the suggested diagonal nut was not a faithful generic realization of the cone.

The clean-surface theorem supplies a complementary global assembly obstruction. Locally admissible rank-two zero geometry cannot persist around a closed smooth component when `s != 0`; any compact surface component must contain higher-order exceptional points that carry the negative `K_J^{-2}` degree. The next sharp calculation is therefore the differentiated AAD system at such exceptional points, aimed at the winding of `W_3^+`, while a genuine isolated strict germ should be sought either without full cohomogeneity-one symmetry or by a convergence/formal-integrability argument for the general cone jets.
