## Deep-Reasoning

This step produced three substantive structural findings for the collapsed positive toric route, each changing how the route should be understood and pursued.

The most important finding is the diagonal-sector identification. Using Liu's Theorem 3.1 (Einstein + T² on simply connected closed 4-manifold implies geometrically polar) and Proposition 5 (diagonal action + Einstein implies diagonal metric), combined with the algebraic observation that h(v,v) = a²p² + b²q² vanishes on a codimension-2 set only for labels with a=0 or b=0, the diagonal twist-free framework is shown to be the COMPLETE sector for alternating-label actions. This has immediate consequences: Page (F₁), CLW (dP₂), and toric KE dP₃ have 3+ circle classes and are outside this sector, so Step 39's calibration against them was asking the wrong question within this framework. The only known diagonal T²-Einstein 4-manifolds are S⁴ (m=2) and S²×S² (m=4, product). The research question sharpens to: does the diagonal sector contain any m≥6 solution? Equivalently: does #_k(S²×S²) for k≥2 carry a diagonal T²-invariant Einstein metric?

The polygon/rod gap analysis quantifies the geometric obstruction for constant-curvature orbit spaces. In H²(-A), a right-angled regular m-gon has side s_reg(m) = 2 arccosh(√2 cos(π/m))/√A, while the rod ODE gives L_rod = π/√(1+2A). The asymptotic ratio L_rod/s_reg approaches (π/√2)/s(m), giving a 68.7% gap at m=6, 45.3% at m=8, and 26.0% as m→∞. Even incorporating the Faber-Krahn eigenvalue constraint (λ₁ = 2 requires A ≤ 0.346 for m=6), a residual 7.9% gap persists. This means constant-curvature orbit spaces cannot support m=6 diagonal Einstein at large A, and even at the eigenvalue boundary the rod exceeds the polygon side. The gap grows with smaller m, making the hexagonal case harder than the asymptotic regime.

The linearized deformation analysis at the product metric confirms expected rigidity. The operator L = -(Δ₀+1) with mixed BCs (Dirichlet in x, Neumann in y on [0,π]²) has eigenfunctions sin(nx)cos(my) with eigenvalue n²+m²-1. The kernel is {(n,m)=(1,0)} only — trivial Λ-rescaling. The full linearized Einstein system (eigenfunction solvability + Neumann rod correction + traceless horizontal equation) imposes 3 constraint families on 1 free function (the conformal factor of the orbit-space metric), yielding an overdetermined system. This is a linearized/counting argument, not a rigorous proof; the vulnerability is whether the constraints could be algebraically dependent. Liu's Lemma 5.2 (uniqueness of g^Ω given g_Σ in the even-extension class) is the strongest available uniqueness tool.

The b₊ question identifies a potential shortcut that could resolve the entire diagonal sector. All known simply connected positive Einstein 4-manifolds have b₊ ≤ 1: S⁴(0), CP²(1), S²×S²(1), Page(1), CLW(1), dP₃..₈(1). The stress family #_k(S²×S²) has b₊ = k ≥ 2 for k ≥ 2. If b₊ ≤ 1 is necessary for positive Einstein with T² action, all m ≥ 6 diagonal Einstein metrics are excluded. Liu proved this under sec ≥ 0; the general case is open. A targeted knowledge search was inconclusive.

Peer status: A01 (#349) withdrew their continuation/first-exit closure, repairing the weighted shifted U equation and support normalization. A03 (#350) withdrew the all-order continuation but proved a valid m=2 coupled continuation mechanism. Neither the positive nor negative branch of Yau-26 is closed.

The toric route has been clarified but not resolved. The gap analysis and rigidity argument are circumstantial evidence against m≥6 diagonal Einstein, but neither the polygon gap (which applies only to constant-K_B models), the Faber-Krahn bound (which is not tight), nor the linearized count (which is not a proof of independence) gives a complete obstruction. The strongest next work within this route would be either (a) a rigorous uniqueness theorem for the product metric without sec ≥ 0, or (b) a resolution of the b₊ question from the literature or from the structure of the diagonal system, or (c) a numerical eigenfunction/PDE computation on the hexagonal orbit space to check feasibility directly. Outside the toric route, the noncollapsed positive Route A (harmonic-radius + bubble-frame convergence) and the negative branch (A01/A03's continuation work) remain the other fronts toward Yau-26.
