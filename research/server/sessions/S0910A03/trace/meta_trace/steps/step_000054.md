## Deep-Reasoning

Step 54 corrected two consequential misreadings and rebuilt the active analytic direction. First, the previously asserted A^{-1/2} deterioration of the Ishida–LeBrun route is false. The terminal construction retains disjoint eligible cores P with Vol(P) at least a fixed fraction of Vol(M), while the global density satisfies ∫𝔇 ≤ Cδ²Vol(M). Volume-weighted selection therefore gives one core with mean density at most C′δ², with no residual topological parameter A. Likewise, the geography-uniform curvature-to-volume ratio permits a fixed small physical support radius. On maximal-function good supports, K₂(0) ≤ (1+κ²)Vol(B) and the local Sobolev constant satisfies S ≤ Cr²Vol(B)^{-1/2}, hence γ = S K₂(0)^{1/2} ≤ Cr²√(1+κ²); collapse volume cancels. A02’s contrary argument chose r proportional to A^{1/4} unnecessarily and combined an upper energy bound with a Sobolev lower bound in the wrong direction. Even a sufficient threshold proportional to A^{-1/2} would not bound the true optimal gap from above. Peer message #386 transmitted this correction, and A01 independently confirmed the proportional-volume cancellation.

Second, Step 53’s Banach contraction is not valid. Its transport velocity depends on the frozen input metric U, so the transport difference contains one derivative of U₁−U₂, whereas the available weighted parabolic dissipation belongs to the output difference V₁−V₂. A bounded high-order ball gives only Hölder-1/2 dependence through interpolation, not the required Lipschitz dependence. Theorem 5.1 and the obsolete scaling section were marked as superseded.

A synchronized-output construction was then developed. Given a frozen metric path U, solve jointly for an output metric V and transported cutoff β, using β²+ε as the metric diffusion coefficient and β²W(V) as the transport velocity. At a fixed point U=V, the ε→0 system is exactly the transported-cutoff DeTurck formulation whose pullback solves ∂ₜg = −2χ²(Ric+g). The single-solution high-order energy has the weighted dissipation ∑_{k≤s}∫(β²+ε)|D^{k+1}V|². Top derivatives of β in the Lie term are integrated onto V and acquire β; top derivatives of V in the transport equation already carry β². Independent TTCS reconstruction supports this ε-uniform finite-order estimate.

For differences, the decisive correction is to work two Sobolev orders below the invariant ball, r=s−2, and orient both equations relative to the same branch. The metric principal difference is decomposed with branch-2 operator (β₂²+ε)U₂^{-1}D²Z. Its coefficient-difference sources are controlled directly by the individual H^s bounds, so no interpolation of a difference is used. The transport top term is β₂²D^{r+1}Z and is absorbed by the same branch-2 metric dissipation. The exact identity d(β₁²)−d(β₂²)=2β₂dω+2ωdβ₁ supplies the matching β₂ factor for the Lie cross term. The TTCS tensor reading corrected one formula: under the chosen two-term Lie decomposition the top coefficient is W(U₁), not W(U₂). After that correction, it found no surviving unweighted D^{r+1}(U₁−U₂), D^{r+1}ω, or D^{r+1}Z term and no inverse power of ε at finite order. These results are recorded in `workspace/notes/step54_exact_synchronized_output_ledger.md`; the broader route comparison and normalization repair are in `workspace/notes/step54_route_reassessment_and_joint_weighted_iteration.md`.

This still does not establish the start theorem. The remaining analytic work is to construct the fixed-ε coupled parabolic–transport inner solution, run the outer Xu contraction, and derive an all-order ε-uniform tame hierarchy with time-derivative bounds on one common interval. Finite H^s compactness alone does not yield a smooth limit across the moving zero set of β. The needed hierarchy has the form E_m′+cD_m ≤ C(E_s)E_m+C_m(E_{m−1}) for every m, followed by diagonal compactness and passage of εU^{-1}D²V to zero. This is now the precise unresolved interface rather than a vague appeal to Yang or an invalid critical-curvature estimate.

The strategic comparison also incorporated A01’s new exact result for the BCGP Z₄², K²=2, six-A₁ product-quotient resolution. Its proof note derives T_X^0 ≅ (π_*T_Y)^G, computes H²(X,T_X^0)=0 by equivariant Serre duality, Künneth, and characters, globalizes the node smoothings, applies Aubin–Yau on the ample-canonical smooth fiber, and uses ADE simultaneous resolution plus Ehresmann to transfer the negative Einstein metric to the minimal resolution. This is strong route control: a serious near-Einstein candidate supports an unrelated exact Einstein metric, illustrating why local gluing behavior cannot decide the output-metric quantifier. It does not address the mixed positive fillings, arbitrary four-manifold topology, dimensions n≥5, or dimension-only constants.

The restricted negative route remains scientifically valuable because its volume-weighted smoothing and forbidden-germ mechanism may be reusable for other global densities, and its family normalization is still viable. Its completion would nevertheless cover only a specified four-dimensional negative geography. The full Yau 26 problem remains open: the mixed positive product-quotient fillings require topology and Einstein-existence analysis, higher dimensions lack an analogous mechanism, and Lean formalization cannot begin at the requested full level until a genuine proof or counterexample exists.
