# Topological local regularity and a diameter-free Einstein repair criterion

24 September 2026. This note separates a local geometric extension from the
classical global elliptic solver it can support. It does not infer a uniform
inverse from a curvature bound or from absence of a known obstruction.

## 1. The geometric statement needs neither diameter nor a Weyl bound

Write \(V_{-K/3}(r)\) for the four-dimensional model-ball volume,
\(\omega_4=\pi^2/2\), and \(\theta=v/V_{-K/3}(1)\).
For a nonempty class below \(0<\theta\le1\).

**Theorem 1 (diameter-free local regularity).** Fix \(K,v>0\) and
\(0<\alpha<1\). Closed connected oriented smooth four-manifolds with
\[
 \|\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}\le K,
 \qquad \inf_{x\in M}\operatorname{Vol}B_1(x)\ge v,                \tag{2}
\]
and \(\pi_2(M)=0\)
have a uniform positive \(C^{1,\alpha}\) harmonic radius. Its bound depends
only on \(K,v,\alpha\), not on diameter, total volume or topology.
No Weyl bound or condition on \(\pi_1(M)\) is required.
No finite number of smooth types
or global compactness is asserted without a diameter bound.

**Proof.** Relative Bishop comparison gives
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}B_r(x)\ge
 v\frac{V_{-K/3}(r)}{V_{-K/3}(1)}\ge\theta\omega_4r^4,
 \qquad 0<r\le1.                                             \tag{4}
\]
The local Jiang--Naber curvature estimate, after a fixed rescaling, gives
\[
 \int_{B_a(x)}|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\le C(K,v)                  \tag{5}
\]
for a fixed \(a=a(K)>0\). Only a ball around the eventual blowup centre is
used; a global covering and a total-energy bound are unnecessary.

Suppose the minimum harmonic radius \(\rho_j\) tends to zero, at \(x_j\).
Rescale by \(\rho_j^{-2}\). Every rescaled harmonic radius is at least one,
Ricci tends uniformly to zero, and (4) gives Euclidean lower volume growth
on every fixed rescaled ball. The harmonic-coordinate argument in
[the compact realization proof](20260923_compact_realization.md), section 4,
therefore gives a complete nonflat smooth Ricci-flat pointed limit \(X\).
In particular, the harmonic metric equation gives strong local \(W^{2,p}\)
convergence, for each finite \(p\), without differentiating the Ricci error.
The harmonic-radius normalization excludes a flat limit.

For each fixed \(R\), the rescaled radius-\(R\) ball lies inside the original
\(B_a(x_j)\) for large \(j\). Passing (5) to the limit and exhausting in
\(R\) gives finite **total** curvature energy on \(X\), with bound
\(C(K,v)\). The lower volume ratio is at least \(\theta\). The Ricci-flat
asymptotic theorem then gives an ALE end of positive order. Optimal decay
order is not needed.

A compact core \(B\) of \(X\) embeds in \(M_j\). The strengthened
topological input is
[Bamler--Chen, Lemma 4.35](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.03154v3): no nontrivial
Ricci-flat ALE core embeds in a smooth four-manifold with integer
\(H_2=0\) and torsion-free integer \(H_1\). The proof cites Zhang's
Theorem 6.1 and the earlier Anderson argument; it does not assume
half-flatness.

For completeness, applying that lemma here does not require the original
fundamental group to be torsion-free. A Ricci-flat ALE space has finite
fundamental group. A connected component of the preimage of \(B\) in
\(\widetilde M_j\) is therefore a finite cover \(\widehat B\) of \(B\),
embedded in \(\widetilde M_j\). Its interior carries the lifted complete
nonflat Ricci-flat ALE metric: the cover is finite and the end remains an
ALE end. Hurewicz and \(\pi_2(M_j)=0\) give
\(H_1(\widetilde M_j;\mathbb Z)=H_2(\widetilde M_j;\mathbb Z)=0\).
Lemma 4.35 now excludes \(\widehat B\), the required contradiction.
This proves the harmonic-radius bound. \(\square\)

The finite-cover use of this lemma is developed separately in
[the topological continuation](20260924_ale_boundary_compatibility.md).

The external curvature input in (5) is
[Jiang--Naber, Theorem 1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.05583).
It controls local full curvature energy from bounded Ricci and noncollapse;
it is not an Einstein regularity theorem imposed on the input metrics.
The local use of that theorem is the reason diameter disappears from this
part of the argument.

This stronger classical topological input supersedes the need for chiral
concentration control in this local regularity theorem. The older
half-flat argument remains valid but is not the strongest available one.

## 2. What replaces compactness in a global existence argument

Theorem 1 supplies local geometry. A global inverse is a different input.
We give a sufficient condition that yields an exact metric, rather than
another necessary condition on a hypothetical output.

For a smooth background metric \(g\), let
\[
 B_gu=\delta_gu+\tfrac12d\operatorname{tr}_gu,
 \qquad
 \Phi_g(h)=\operatorname{Ric}_h+h+\delta_h^*(B_gh).               \tag{9}
\]
Here divergence is minus covariant contraction and \(\delta_h^*\) is its
formal adjoint on one-forms. The derivative at \(g\) is the self-adjoint
elliptic operator
\[
 L_g=\tfrac12\Delta_{L,g}+I,
 \quad
 \Delta_{L,g}u=\nabla^*\nabla u+\operatorname{Ric}\circ u
             +u\circ\operatorname{Ric}-2\mathring R u.          \tag{10}
\]
An **absolute spectral gap** means
\[
 \|L_gu\|_{L^2}\ge\lambda\|u\|_{L^2}
       \quad\text{for every smooth symmetric two-tensor }u.     \tag{11}
\]
This permits negative eigenvalues but excludes both a kernel and small
eigenvalues of either sign. It is not a scalar Laplacian gap.

**Theorem 2 (topological local geometry plus a global inverse).** Fix
\(v,K_1,\lambda>0\) and \(0<\alpha<1\).
There exist \(\varepsilon,C>0\), depending only on these data, such that
the following holds. Let a closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold
with \(\pi_2(M)=0\) carry a smooth metric satisfying
\[
 \inf_x\operatorname{Vol}B_1(x)\ge v,\qquad
 \|\nabla\operatorname{Ric}\|_\infty\le K_1,
 \qquad (11).                                                \tag{12}
\]
Write \(E=\operatorname{Ric}_g+g\) and
\(\delta=\|g^{-1}E\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}\). If
\[
 \delta^{1-\alpha}+\|E\|_{L^2(M,g)}<\varepsilon,                \tag{13}
\]
then there is a smooth metric \(h\) on the same smooth manifold with
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_h=-h,\qquad
 \|h-g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}+\|h-g\|_{H^2}
       \le C\bigl(\delta^{1-\alpha}+\|E\|_2\bigr).             \tag{14}
\]
The Hölder norm is measured in uniformly controlled small harmonic charts;
an equivalent intrinsic bounded-geometry norm gives the same statement.
There is no diameter or total-volume bound. Neither a Kähler output nor
negative sectional curvature is required. Condition (13) uses the
**unnormalized** integral residual, not its mean.

**Proof.** Take \(\varepsilon\) small enough that \(\delta<1/2\). Then the
Ricci bound needed in Theorem 1 holds with \(K=2\). First use its harmonic
radius conclusion with an exponent strictly larger than \(\alpha\).
In these coordinates the bound on \(\nabla\operatorname{Ric}\), the
controlled Christoffel symbols and the Ricci bound control the coordinate
\(C^{0,\alpha}\) norm of Ricci. Schauder estimates in the harmonic metric
equation therefore give uniform \(C^{2,\alpha}\) bounds for the metric on
smaller charts, and a uniform curvature bound. These local constants have
no diameter or volume dependence.

Use the Banach spaces
\[
 X=C^{2,\alpha}\cap H^2,\qquad Y=C^{0,\alpha}\cap L^2,
 \quad \|u\|_X=\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}+\|u\|_{H^2},
 \quad \|f\|_Y=\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}+\|f\|_2.                   \tag{15}
\]
For each individual closed manifold, (11) and self-adjoint elliptic
Fredholm theory give a unique inverse and
\(\|L_g^{-1}f\|_2\le\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_2\).
Uniform local elliptic estimates, summed in a bounded-overlap atlas, give
\[
 \|u\|_{H^2}\le C(\|L_gu\|_2+\|u\|_2).                      \tag{16}
\]
The atlas may have arbitrarily many members; its overlap bound, rather
than its cardinality, enters this inequality. Local Moser estimates for a
connection Laplacian with bounded zero-order coefficients, followed by
Schauder estimates, give
\[
 \|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}
       \le C(\|L_gu\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}+\|u\|_2).                \tag{17}
\]
Thus
\[
                         \|L_g^{-1}f\|_X\le C_I\|f\|_Y       \tag{18}
\]
uniformly across (12). Local bounded-geometry estimates and the explicit
global hypothesis (11), not compactness of the whole manifold, give (18).

Expand
\[
 \Phi_g(g+u)=E+L_gu+N_g(u).
\]
For \(\|u\|_X,\|z\|_X\le\rho\), with a fixed small \(\rho\), the standard
coordinate formula for Ricci and (9) gives
\[
 \|N_g(u)-N_g(z)\|_Y
     \le C_N(\|u\|_X+\|z\|_X)\|u-z\|_X.                    \tag{19}
\]
For clarity, its second-order terms have the form
\(u*\nabla^2u\), \(\nabla u*\nabla u\), and bounded curvature times
\(u*u\), with smooth coefficients for positive-definite \(g+u\).
Hölder product estimates bound their first norm in (15); their \(L^2\)
norm is bounded by one \(C^{2,\alpha}\) factor and one \(H^2\) factor.
There is no multiplication by \(\operatorname{Vol}(M)^{1/2}\).

Tensor interpolation, using \(\nabla E=\nabla\operatorname{Ric}\), yields
\[
 \|E\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\le C\delta^{1-\alpha},\qquad \delta\le1,
                                                                  \tag{20}
\]
where \(C\) can depend on \(K_1\) and the chart data. On the ball of
radius \(R=2C_I\|E\|_Y\) in \(X\), the map
\(u\mapsto-L_g^{-1}(E+N_g(u))\) is a contraction when
\(R\le\rho\) and \(2C_IC_NR<1\). This gives a positive-definite solution
\(h=g+u\) of \(\Phi_g(h)=0\) and (14).

Smallness in \(C^{2,\alpha}\) makes \(\operatorname{Ric}_h\) negative
definite. Put \(b=B_gh\). Applying the contracted Bianchi operator for
\(h\) gives
\[
 0=2B_h\delta_h^*b=\nabla_h^*\nabla_h b-\operatorname{Ric}_h(b).
\]
Integration on the closed manifold forces \(b=0\). Hence
\(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\). Elliptic bootstrapping of the gauged equation
with smooth background makes \(h\) smooth. \(\square\)

The Bianchi-gauge operator, local Moser estimate and hybrid-norm repair
method are classical. For primary statements see
[Jäckel, sections 2 and 4--5](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16692v2) and
[Premoselli, section 4.1](https://numdam.org/item/10.5802/tsg.367.pdf).
The contribution here is the geometric input to this solver: topological
core exclusion and local noncollapse supply controlled charts without
global diameter. The spectral gap remains an independent hypothesis.

## 3. A pointwise-input application with localized error

**Corollary 3.** In Theorem 2, suppose \(E\) vanishes outside a measurable
set \(\Omega\) of volume at most \(V_0\). A sufficiently small pointwise
defect \(\delta<\delta_0(v,K_1,\lambda,V_0,\alpha)\) then gives an
exact negative Einstein metric on the same smooth manifold, with no bound
on the total volume or diameter.

Indeed, in four dimensions \(|E|\le2\delta\), so
\[
 \|E\|_2\le2\delta\sqrt{V_0}.                               \tag{21}
\]
Take \(\delta_0\) to satisfy (13). Exact Einstein regions of arbitrarily
large volume do not increase the residual bound. Their possible effect on
small global eigenvalues must still be tested through (11).

This gives a sufficient same-smooth-type realization statement, not a
claim that every original Yau26 input has localized error. The repaired
metric is not forced to respect an input symmetry or complex structure.

## 4. Which information cannot be discarded

1. **Averaging does not provide (13).** If
   \(r=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2\), then
   \(\|E\|_2=\sqrt{r\operatorname{Vol}(M)}\). Small \(r\) with growing
   volume is insufficient. For an explicit normalization test, take covers
   \((M_j,h_j)\) of degree \(N_j\to\infty\) of a fixed closed hyperbolic
   four-manifold, scaled to \(\operatorname{Ric}_{h_j}=-h_j\), and put
   \(g_j=(1+t_j)h_j\), \(t_j=N_j^{-1/4}\). Then
   \[
    \delta_-(g_j)=\frac{t_j}{1+t_j}\to0,
    \quad \|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}+g_j\|_2
       =2t_j\sqrt{N_j\operatorname{Vol}(h_1)}\to\infty.        \tag{22}
   \]
   These manifolds already have Einstein metrics; (22) disproves an
   implication between norms, not an existence assertion.
2. **A local regularity theorem does not eliminate global kernels.** On
   \(\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h\) with the standard negative Einstein product,
   a Teichmüller deformation of one factor, in transverse-traceless gauge,
   gives a nonzero tensor in \(\ker L_g\). Thus Theorem 2 deliberately does
   not replace the previous compact KE realization theorem: that theorem
   can accommodate such moduli without inverting a full operator. Deleting
   these modes requires an obstruction equation and an integrability
   argument, not merely a choice of notation.
3. **Neither gauge saturation nor a Weyl estimate is needed in Theorem 2.**
   The stronger topological input now handles curvature concentration.
   It does not supply unit-ball noncollapse, the linear gap, or the small
   unnormalized residual. The previous actual-form estimates can still
   provide geometric structure, but they do not replace these hypotheses.
4. **This is not a replacement of Jäckel's theorem.** His Theorem 1.1 has
   strict negative sectional curvature, an exactly hyperbolic thin part,
   bounded Ricci derivative and small unnormalized residual; it allows
   arbitrarily small injectivity radius. Here local noncollapse and a
   topological regularity mechanism replace that thin-part analysis,
   while the global linear gap is explicit rather than obtained from
   negative sectional curvature. Neither statement subsumes the other.

## 5. Relation to the first scientific line

The previous compact realization mechanism was
\[
 \text{gauge saturation plus bounded diameter}
 \Longrightarrow\text{smooth compact limit}
 \Longrightarrow\text{same-type KE existence}.
\]
The present sufficient mechanism is different:
\[
 \text{vanishing }\pi_2\text{ plus local noncollapse and bounded Ricci}
 \Longrightarrow\text{uniform local geometry},
\]
followed by a separately verified global inverse and small mixed residual,
which imply a genuine solution on the original manifold. This obtains
Einstein existence without diameter, but not from unrestricted Yau26 input.
It identifies two distinct missing pieces in a large-scale degeneration:
local concentration control and global low-mode solvability. Replacing
diameter by either one alone would omit the other.

Theorem 1 is the local scope extension obtained from the stronger classical
ALE exclusion. Theorem 2 and Corollary 3 are its explicit composition with a
classical solver, not a newly invented inverse-function method or a claim
of global priority. They supply a usable sufficient theorem with all
additional input stated, rather than assigning another round of work to
an unspecified global matching problem.

# Finite-cover gauge supply and Einstein realization on the original manifold

24 September 2026. This note separates the location of the gauge-theoretic
input from the location of the exact Einstein output. The cover may have
arbitrarily large degree and diameter. We compactify the **base**, not the
cover, and do not assume that its monopole class or auxiliary form descends.
The result is a new composition in this project, not a claim of publication
priority or a solution of unrestricted Yau26.

## 1. Statements

All manifolds are closed, connected, smooth and four-dimensional. Ricci uses
tensor norms and Weyl curvature uses Hilbert–Schmidt endomorphism norms.
Write
\[
 \delta=\|\operatorname{Ric}_g^\sharp+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}},
 \qquad r=\fint_M|\operatorname{Ric}_g+g|^2\le4\delta^2.
 \tag{1}
\]
For a finite covering \(\pi:N\to M\) of degree \(d\), set
\(\widetilde g=\pi^*g\), \(\widetilde V=dV\). An **actual conformal
input** on an oriented \(N\) is a specified Spin\(^c\) structure whose
ordinary unperturbed monopole equations are solvable for every metric in
\([\widetilde g]\). The structure is fixed throughout this conformal class.
For such an input in orientation \(\sigma\), define the positive harmonic
projection energy \(p_\sigma\) and
\[
 q_\sigma=\frac{1}{\widetilde V}
 \left\{\frac32\int_N(s^2+12|W^\sigma|^2)
                       -72\pi^2p_\sigma\right\}\ge0.
 \tag{2}
\]
An arbitrary cohomology class, or solvability only at one metric, is not
substituted for this input.

When the base is oriented, the cover is always given the pullback
orientation. In particular this convention fixes the chirality in
Theorem C, not just the signs in a signature formula.

**Theorem A (virtual bilateral realization).** Given \(D,A_0>0\), there
is \(\varepsilon(D,A_0)>0\) such that the following holds. Let \(M\) be
oriented, \(\operatorname{diam}(M,g)\le D\), and let an oriented finite
cover \(N\to M\) have actual conformal inputs in both orientations, with
\[
 p_+,p_->0,\qquad \frac{\max(p_+,p_-)}d\ge A_0,
 \qquad \delta+q_++q_-<\varepsilon(D,A_0).
 \tag{3}
\]
Then the **same smooth base \(M\)** admits an exact metric \(h\) with
\(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\), whose universal Riemannian cover is
\(\mathbb H^2_{-1}\times\mathbb H^2_{-1}\).

**Theorem B (one input above a nonorientable base).** The same conclusion
holds when \(M\) is nonorientable and an oriented finite cover has just
one actual input satisfying
\[
                    p/d\ge A_0,\qquad \delta+q<\varepsilon(D,A_0).
 \tag{4}
\]
No regularity of the covering is required in either theorem. Neither its
degree nor its diameter appears in the threshold. There is no homotopy,
class-square-cost, Ricci-derivative, or separate curvature-energy hypothesis.
Global Kähler structures on the base are **not** asserted. The exact
Riemannian output is sufficient for the adopted Yau26 existence question.

**Theorem C (virtual gauge at the Chern boundary).** Let \(M\) be oriented,
\(\operatorname{diam}(M,g)\le D\), and
\[
       A:=2\chi(M)+3\tau(M)=3(2\chi(M)-3\tau(M))>0.
 \tag{5}
\]
Suppose an oriented finite cover of any degree has one actual conformal
input with \(p\ge dA\). There is \(\varepsilon(D)>0\) such that
\(\delta<\varepsilon(D)\) implies that the same smooth base admits a
metric locally isometric to complex hyperbolic two-space, normalized by
\(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\) and holomorphic sectional curvature \(-2/3\).
This is a Riemannian quotient conclusion: a global complex structure on
\(M\) need not descend.

Thus gauge supply may live on a finite cover even when the base does not
admit an almost-complex structure. This does not say that Einstein existence
on an arbitrary cover automatically descends; the proofs instead construct
an exact limit on the original base.

## 2. Eliminate the auxiliary form before descending

Let \(\mathcal K\) be the orthogonal orbit in
\(\operatorname{Sym}_0(\mathbb R^3)\) with eigenvalues
\[
                          (-2/3,1/3,1/3).
 \tag{6}
\]
For a chiral Weyl operator put \(e_\sigma=\operatorname{dist}(W^\sigma,
\mathcal K)^2\). This is a scalar curvature invariant; unlike a chosen
eigenform, it is unchanged by a change of orthonormal frame.

The retained [actual-form theorem](../mathematics/current/line1_composed_application.md),
Section 2, supplies \(\omega\) with, for every arbitrarily small
\(\eta>0\),
\[
 \fint_N\bigl(|W^\sigma-A_\omega|^2+(|\omega|^2-2)^2\bigr)
       \le720\kappa_\sigma+\eta,
 \quad \kappa_\sigma=r+q_\sigma,
 \quad A_\omega=\frac12\left(\frac{|\omega|^2}{3}I-\omega\otimes\omega\right).
 \tag{7}
\]
We shrink the thresholds in Theorems A--C so that each invoked
\(\kappa_\sigma\le1/2880\), as required by that theorem. In a
contradiction sequence this holds for every sufficiently late term.
This zeroth-order statement needs no square-cost or Hessian bound.
For \(u=|\omega|^2\), the eigenvalues of \(A_\omega\) are
\((-u/3,u/6,u/6)\), including at \(u=0\). Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(A_\omega,\mathcal K)^2=(u-2)^2/6,
 \qquad e_\sigma\le2|W^\sigma-A_\omega|^2+(u-2)^2/3.
\]
Letting the smoothing allowance tend to zero gives
\[
                         \fint_N e_\sigma\le1440\kappa_\sigma.
 \tag{8}
\]
There is no need to descend \(\omega\), choose a global eigenline, or
compare classes on different covers. The only inherited analytic input
here is the actual-form theorem; (8) is its finite-dimensional consequence.

The completed-square scalar–Weyl estimate also gives
\[
 \fint_N(\sqrt6|W^\sigma|-2)^2\le2\kappa_\sigma.
 \tag{9}
\]
For any measurable \(E\subset N\),
\[
 \int_E|W^\sigma|^2\le\frac43\operatorname{Vol}(E)
                                      +\frac23\kappa_\sigma\widetilde V.
 \tag{10}
\]
The factor \(\widetilde V\) cannot be dropped on the cover. It disappears
only after the correct division by the covering degree.

## 3. Descent of energy and spectral error, with no degree loss

For an oriented base and orientation-preserving cover, natural curvature
invariants pull back. Therefore (8) and (10) give on the base
\[
 \fint_M e_\sigma\le1440\kappa_\sigma,\qquad
 \int_E|W^\sigma|^2\le\frac43\operatorname{Vol}(E)
                                      +\frac23\kappa_\sigma V.
 \tag{11}
\]
Here one applies (10) to \(\pi^{-1}E\) and divides both sides by \(d\).
Nothing about a monopole structure being deck-invariant was used.

For nonorientable \(M\), factor any connected oriented covering through
the orientation double cover \(M^{\rm or}\). Its intermediate degree is
\(d/2\). The orientation-reversing involution on \(M^{\rm or}\) is an
isometry and exchanges the two Weyl halves. Thus, with local orientations
used on the base,
\[
 \int_N e_+=\frac d2\int_M(e_++e_-),\qquad
 \int_{\pi^{-1}E}|W^+|^2=\frac d2\int_E|W|^2.
 \tag{12}
\]
The sums in these formulas are globally defined even though their labels
are not. It follows that one input on \(N\) gives
\[
 \fint_M(e_++e_-)\le2880\kappa,\qquad
 \int_E|W|^2\le\frac83\operatorname{Vol}(E)+\frac43\kappa V.
 \tag{13}
\]
This is why Theorem B needs only one gauge input. It is not an assumption
that the original class descends to \(M^{\rm or}\) or to \(M\).

The scalar monopole inequality, divided by \(d\), gives in both cases
\[
 32\pi^2A_0\le\frac1d\int_Ns_-^2
                  =\int_Ms_-^2\le16(1+\delta)^2V,
 \qquad V\ge\frac{2\pi^2A_0}{(1+\delta)^2}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Merely \(p>0\) on covers of unbounded degree would not give this lower
bound. The per-sheet budget in (3)–(4) is essential to this proof.

## 4. Compactness occurs on the base

Suppose Theorem A or B fails. Take counterexamples with fixed \(D,A_0\)
and the relevant defect tending to zero. Equation (14), bounded Ricci,
diameter, and Bishop–Gromov comparison give uniform upper and lower total
volume bounds and a uniform local lower volume ratio on the base.
Equations (11) or (13) imply
\[
 \lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{\rho_j}(x_j)}|W_{g_j}|^2=0
                         \quad\hbox{whenever }\rho_j\to0.
 \tag{15}
\]
All constants are independent of the finite covers.

The harmonic-radius argument in
[bilateral realization, Section 3](20260924_bilateral_realization.md)
now applies directly to \((M_j,g_j)\), including an unoriented base:
if the minimum harmonic radius tends to zero, rescale there. Harmonic
coordinates, noncollapse and the metric equation give a complete smooth
Ricci-flat limit, with strong local \(W^{2,p}\) convergence for finite
\(p\). Equation (15) makes its Weyl curvature vanish. A complete flat
limit with Euclidean lower volume growth is Euclidean, contradicting the
normalization of the harmonic radius. This local argument uses no choice
of global orientation and no ALE classification.

There is therefore a uniform harmonic radius. A finite atlas and
subsequence give diffeomorphisms with one fixed smooth **base** \(M_\infty\)
and convergence in \(C^{1,\alpha}\), and strongly in finite \(W^{2,p}\),
to a smooth metric \(h\) with \(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\).
This standard argument does not assert convergence of all derivatives of
the approximate metrics. Most importantly, no compactness, bounded degree,
or equivariance theorem for the varying covers has been invoked.

## 5. Exact local geometry, without descending forms

Strong curvature convergence and (11) or (13) imply pointwise on the
smooth limit that both \(W^+\) and \(W^-\) have spectrum (6).
For an Einstein four-metric the chiral Weyl Weitzenböck identity, with
\(\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla\), is
\[
 \frac12\Delta|W^\sigma|^2
 =|\nabla W^\sigma|^2+\frac s2|W^\sigma|^2-18\det W^\sigma.
 \tag{16}
\]
Here \(s=-4\), \(|W^\sigma|^2=2/3\) and
\(\det W^\sigma=-2/27\). The last two terms cancel, while the left
side vanishes. Thus both Weyl halves are parallel. Equivalently one may
use Derdziński's local conformal-Kähler theorem with constant Weyl norm.

Their simple eigenlines have local parallel generators of norm \(\sqrt2\),
defining opposite-orientation parallel orthogonal complex structures
\(J_+,J_-\). They commute. The parallel symmetric involution
\(-J_+J_-\) has two rank-two eigenspaces. On the simply connected
universal cover these structures are global; completeness and de Rham
splitting give two complete simply connected surface factors. Each has
Ricci curvature \(-g\), hence Gaussian curvature \(-1\). This proves
the universal-cover conclusion in A and B. Pulling \(h\) back through
the base diffeomorphisms contradicts the chosen counterexamples and proves
the uniform thresholds.

The eigenlines can have sign monodromy on the base. Ignoring it would
incorrectly strengthen the statement to global Kähler existence.

## 6. Proof at the Chern boundary

For Theorem C, covering multiplicativity gives \(T_\pm(N)=dT_\pm(M)\).
Apply the exact balances from
[the Chern-boundary proof](20260924_chern_boundary_realization.md) upstairs:
\[
 \mathfrak D_2+72\pi^2(p-dA)+54\int_N|W^-|^2
                                  =18\int_N|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2.
 \tag{17}
\]
Consequently \(q\le72\delta^2\) and
\(\fint_M|W^-|^2\le\delta^2\). Also \(p/d\ge A\ge1\), so (14)
gives a base volume floor independent of \(d\). The \(W^+\) estimate
(11) and the vanishing total \(W^-\) energy give base compactness just
as above. The limit has \(W^-=0\), \(\operatorname{Ric}=-h\), and
\(W^+\) spectrum (6). Equation (16) makes \(W^+\) parallel, so the
universal cover is Kähler–Einstein and Bochner-flat. It is
\(\mathbb{CH}^2\), with holomorphic sectional curvature \(-2/3\).
Again the exact metric is obtained on the base itself.

## 7. A real quotient showing why the stronger input scope matters

Let \(g,h\ge2\) be even. The oriented hyperbolic surfaces \(\Sigma_g\)
and \(\Sigma_h\) can be realized as orientation double covers of closed
nonorientable hyperbolic surfaces. Their free orientation-reversing
isometric involutions are \(\iota_g,\iota_h\). The diagonal action
\((\iota_g,\iota_h)\) on \(N=\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h\) is free and
orientation-preserving. Put
\[
                M=N/\langle(\iota_g,\iota_h)\rangle.
 \tag{18}
\]
The product metric already descends to an exact negative Einstein metric.
Both orientations on \(N\) have compatible negative KE structures and
their canonical actual monopole inputs. Here
\[
 \chi(M)=2(g-1)(h-1),\quad \tau(M)=0,\quad
 d=2,\quad p_+=p_-=8(g-1)(h-1),\quad q_+=q_-=0.
 \tag{19}
\]
For even \(g,h\), \(\chi(M)+\tau(M)\equiv2\pmod4\). An oriented
almost-complex four-manifold has integral Todd genus
\((\chi+\tau)/4\), so this base admits **no almost-complex structure
in either orientation**. In particular the global KE conclusion of the
base-input bilateral theorem is impossible here, while Theorem A applies.
Small smooth perturbations of the base metric retain the same actual
cover inputs and have small deficits, so the input regime is not confined
to exact equality metrics. The product-cover canonical SW invariants
provide solvability for all metrics, not only the displayed product.

This quotient is a calibration example, not a newly discovered Einstein
manifold. It demonstrates the strict difference between actual gauge
supply on the base and supply on a cover, and why the output must retain
possible holonomy monodromy. A nonorientable example for B is simply the
product of a closed nonorientable hyperbolic surface and an oriented one.

## 8. Relation to the research programme and sources

The added bridge is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{actual gauge supply on a finite cover}\\
 \Downarrow\\
 \text{degree-normalized curvature information downstairs}\\
 \Downarrow\\
 \text{exact Einstein geometry on the original base}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The unbounded degree causes no loss because the proof discards the
auxiliary form before descending and uses spectral invariants instead.
It does not assume a gauge class on the base, or silently pull an arbitrary
Einstein metric down a finite covering.

The diameter and small saturation requirements remain genuine. These
statements do not handle the large-diameter shear escape, arbitrary
collapsed input, or arbitrary gauge-free Yau26 input. Nor is the finite
cover allowed to carry an unrelated metric: it must carry \(\pi^*g\).

Primary external inputs are LeBrun's
[monopole curvature inequalities](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0003068)
and Derdziński's
[harmonic-Weyl structure and identities](https://www.numdam.org/article/CM_1983__49_3_405_0.pdf).
The compactness step and actual-form PDE are the explicitly cited retained
proof dependencies. The finite checker validates orbit distances, degree
normalizations, the Weyl cancellation, and the quotient's characteristic
numbers; it does not certify these PDE inputs or worldwide originality.

# From weighted coercivity to uniformly local Einstein repair

24 September 2026. This continuation removes the unnormalized global
\(L^2\) residual from the preceding
[diameter-free repair criterion](20260924_local_to_global_scope.md), on a
specified growth--coercivity regime. The additional information is a
quantitative bound on propagation through the whole manifold, not another
smallness condition on the residual's support.

## 1. Statement

For a smooth metric on a closed four-manifold use the Bianchi-gauged operator
\[
 \Phi_g(h)=\operatorname{Ric}_h+h+\delta_h^*(B_gh),\qquad
 B_gu=\delta_gu+\tfrac12d\operatorname{tr}_gu.
\]
Its derivative at \(g\) is
\[
 L_g=\tfrac12\Delta_{L,g}+I
     =\tfrac12\nabla^*\nabla+V_g,                            \tag{1}
\]
where \(V_g\) is the symmetric curvature endomorphism. Unlike the earlier
absolute spectral gap, the condition used here is **positive coercivity**:
\[
 \int_M\langle L_gu,u\rangle\,d\mu_g
              \ge\lambda\int_M|u|^2\,d\mu_g
 \quad\text{for every smooth symmetric two-tensor }u.         \tag{2}
\]
No kernel or low mode has been projected out.

**Theorem A (pointwise repair with controlled volume growth).** Fix
\(v,K_1,C_G,\lambda>0\), \(d,H\ge0\), and \(0<\alpha<1\), with
\[
                              H^2<8\lambda.                 \tag{3}
\]
There are \(\varepsilon,C>0\), depending only on these data, such that
the following holds. Suppose a closed connected oriented smooth
four-manifold \(M\), with \(\pi_2(M)=0\), carries a smooth metric \(g\)
satisfying
\[
 \inf_x\operatorname{Vol}_gB_1(x)\ge v,\qquad
 \|\nabla\operatorname{Ric}_g\|_\infty\le K_1,                 \tag{4}
\]
the positive coercivity (2), and the actual-manifold growth bound
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}_g B_R(x)\le C_G(1+R)^d e^{HR}
       \quad(x\in M,\ R\ge0).                               \tag{5}
\]
If
\[
 \delta_-(g)=\|g^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}_g+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}}
                    <\varepsilon,                          \tag{6}
\]
then the same smooth manifold has a smooth metric \(h\) with
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_h=-h,\qquad
 \|h-g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\mathrm{uloc}}}
                       \le C\delta_-(g)^{1-\alpha}.           \tag{7}
\]
Here the uniformly local Hölder norm is the supremum of coefficient norms
in a uniformly controlled small harmonic atlas, not a sum over that atlas.
There is no diameter, total-volume, total-residual, or support-volume bound.
The theorem does not require a gauge class or a prescribed complex structure.

The topological assumption is one supplier of local bounded geometry. If
uniform \(C^{2,\alpha}\) harmonic charts are provided directly, the same
theorem holds without \(\pi_2(M)=0\) and without using (4) to obtain them;
in that formulation the residual hypothesis is
\(\|\operatorname{Ric}+g\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\mathrm{uloc}}}<\varepsilon\).
Thus the main analytic conclusion is a genuine uniformly local
\(C^{0,\alpha}\to C^{2,\alpha}\) inverse and repair estimate.

## 2. The inverse estimate: both decay and growth are retained

Choose once and for all
\[
 H/2<a<\sqrt{2\lambda},\qquad c_a=\lambda-a^2/2>0.             \tag{8}
\]
For a fixed centre \(x\), put \(\phi_x(y)=e^{-a d_g(x,y)}\).
This positive Lipschitz function satisfies
\(|d\phi_x|\le a\phi_x\) almost everywhere. No differentiability of the
distance function at its cut locus is assumed.

For any smooth tensor \(u\), integration by parts gives the exact identity
\[
 \int\langle L_g(\phi_xu),\phi_xu\rangle
 =\int\phi_x^2\langle L_gu,u\rangle
       +\tfrac12\int|d\phi_x|^2|u|^2.                       \tag{9}
\]
For Lipschitz \(\phi_x\), use density in \(H^1\) in the quadratic-form
identity. The zeroth-order terms agree on both sides; the factor
\(1/2\) is the leading coefficient in (1).

If \(L_gu=f\), (2), (8), Cauchy--Schwarz, and (9) give
\[
 c_a\|\phi_xu\|_2^2
 \le\int\phi_x^2\langle f,u\rangle
 \le\|\phi_xf\|_2\|\phi_xu\|_2,
\quad
 \|\phi_xu\|_2\le c_a^{-1}\|\phi_xf\|_2.                    \tag{10}
\]
The right side can now be bounded by a **local supremum**, rather than the
global \(L^2\) norm. Splitting into unit annuli and using (5),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_M e^{-2a d_g(x,y)}\,d\mu_g(y)
 &\le C_G e^H\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
            (m+2)^d e^{-(2a-H)m}\ =:J_a<\infty.
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{11}
\]
The constant is independent of \(x\), diameter, and total volume. Therefore
\[
 \|u\|_{L^2(B_1(x))}
        \le e^a c_a^{-1}\sqrt{J_a}\,\|f\|_\infty.             \tag{12}
\]

In the regime of Theorem A, \(\delta_-<1/2\) bounds Ricci uniformly.
The local regularity theorem in the preceding continuation, using
\(\pi_2=0\) and the unit-ball lower bound, supplies uniform harmonic
radii. The Ricci derivative bound and the harmonic metric equation then
supply uniform \(C^{2,\alpha}\) metric bounds on smaller charts. In
particular the coefficients of (1), including \(V_g\), have uniform
\(C^{0,\alpha}\) bounds.

Local Moser estimates for the connection Laplacian with bounded potential
give, for a fixed \(0<\rho\le1\),
\[
 |u|(x)\le C_{\rm loc}
       \bigl(\|u\|_{L^2(B_\rho(x))}+\|f\|_{C^0(B_\rho(x))}\bigr).
\]
Combining this with (12), then applying local Schauder estimates, proves
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}
     \le C_I\|L_gu\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}.
 \quad}                                                     \tag{13}
\]
On each closed manifold, positive coercivity and elliptic Fredholm theory
give existence and uniqueness of \(u=L_g^{-1}f\). Equation (13) makes the
inverse uniform across the family. We have not deduced (13) from an
unweighted \(L^2\) gap alone: (11) is the additional global step.

For polynomial growth, \(H=0\), every \(\lambda>0\) permits a choice in
(8). For exponential growth, the sufficient condition (3) is the precise
comparison needed by this argument. It is not claimed optimal. In
particular failure of (3) does not imply failure of an Einstein inverse.

## 3. Nonlinear repair

In the uniformly local Hölder spaces put
\[
 E=\operatorname{Ric}_g+g,\qquad
 \Phi_g(g+u)=E+L_gu+N_g(u).
\]
Uniform \(C^{2,\alpha}\) bounds give, in a fixed sufficiently small ball,
\[
 \|N_g(u)-N_g(z)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}
 \le C_N\bigl(\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}
             +\|z\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}\bigr)
                \|u-z\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}.             \tag{14}
\]
This is a local Hölder product estimate: neither summation over all charts
nor a volume factor appears. By (4) and tensor interpolation,
\[
 \|E\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}\le C\delta_-^{1-\alpha}.       \tag{15}
\]
Equations (13)--(15) make
\(u\mapsto-L_g^{-1}(E+N_g(u))\) a contraction in the ball of radius
\(2C_I\|E\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}\) when (6) is sufficiently small.
The resulting \(h=g+u\) is positive definite and has negative definite
Ricci tensor. Applying \(B_h\) to \(\Phi_g(h)=0\) gives, for \(b=B_gh\),
\[
              \nabla_h^*\nabla_h b-\operatorname{Ric}_h(b)=0.
\]
Integration on the closed manifold gives \(b=0\), so \(h\) is Einstein.
Elliptic bootstrapping gives smoothness. This proves Theorem A.

## 4. A nonempty large-diameter family with distributed residual

Fix an oriented closed hyperbolic four-manifold \((N,h)\) with
\(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\) and an epimorphism
\(\varphi:\pi_1(N)\to\mathbb Z\). Such compact examples exist by
[Millson's construction](https://math.umd.edu/~jjm/papers/bettinumber.pdf),
which gives nonzero first Betti number in every dimension at least three.
Let \((\widehat N,\widehat h)\) be the cyclic infinite cover and
\((N_m,h_m)\) its quotient by \(m\mathbb Z\).

**Lemma B (the correct cover-growth bound).** There is \(C_N<\infty\),
depending only on this fixed cyclic cover, such that
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}_{h_m}B_R(x)\le C_N(1+R)
                 \quad(m\ge1,\ R\ge0).                      \tag{16}
\]

**Proof.** The deck group \(\mathbb Z\) acts properly and cocompactly on
\(\widehat N\). Choose a bounded fundamental region of finite volume.
The orbit map of this action is a quasi-isometry: equivalently, only
\(O(1+R)\) translates of the region can meet a radius-\(R\) ball, with
constants depending on the fixed region and deck generator. Counting their
volumes proves the linear bound on \(\widehat N\), uniformly in the centre.
Every radius-\(R\) ball in \(N_m\) is the image of a radius-\(R\) ball in
\(\widehat N\), by lifting shortest paths from its centre. Its volume is
at most the volume upstairs, proving (16). \(\square\)

The cover used in this argument is **not** the universal hyperbolic cover,
and (16) is not asserted for arbitrary finite covers. Since
\(\operatorname{Vol}(N_m)=m\operatorname{Vol}(N)\), (16) also shows
\(\operatorname{diam}(N_m)\to\infty\).

Choose \(\eta>0\) small and consider any smooth metrics \(g_m\) with
\[
                  \|g_m-h_m\|_{C^3_{\rm uloc}(h_m)}\le\eta.   \tag{17}
\]
The constants below are uniform in \(m\). The metrics are uniformly
bilipschitz to \(h_m\), so (16) remains a polynomial bound with adjusted
constant, and unit balls stay uniformly noncollapsed. They have
\(\pi_2=0\), bounded Ricci derivative, and sectional curvature uniformly
close to \(-1/3\).

For the exact \(h_m\), Koiso's estimate gives
\[
                \int\langle L_{h_m}u,u\rangle
                              \ge\tfrac13\|u\|_2^2.          \tag{18}
\]
Its almost-Einstein version gives, for \(\eta\) sufficiently small,
positive coercivity with \(\lambda=1/4\) for every \(g_m\) in (17).
Thus \(H=0,d=1,\lambda=1/4,a=1/2\) work simultaneously, with
\[
 c_a=\tfrac18,\qquad
 J_a\le C_N\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+2)e^{-j}
        =C_N\frac{2-e^{-1}}{(1-e^{-1})^2}.                    \tag{19}
\]
Theorem A applies to every metric in (17) whose pointwise Ricci residual
is below its fixed threshold. The required threshold is independent of
the number of cyclic cells, and the metrics need not be deck-invariant.

This family contains residuals which are small everywhere but not small
in global \(L^2\). For an explicit example, choose a ball in one fixed
fundamental cell and a smooth bump \(\beta\) supported there and equal to
one on a smaller ball. On its \(m\) disjoint translates choose arbitrary
coefficients \(c_j\in[1,2]\), and put
\[
 f_m=t\sum_{j=1}^m c_j\beta_j,\qquad g_m=e^{2f_m}h_m.          \tag{20}
\]
For small fixed \(t>0\), (17), all uniform constants and
\(\delta_-(g_m)\le C t\) hold. On each smaller ball the conformal factor
is constant, so \(\operatorname{Ric}_{g_m}+g_m
=(e^{2tc_j}-1)h_m\). Consequently
\[
 \|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_m}+g_m\|_{L^2(g_m)}
                           \ge c t\sqrt m.                  \tag{21}
\]
For sufficiently small fixed \(t\), Theorem A repairs every \(g_m\),
although (21) diverges. This is a real gain over the earlier mixed-norm
criterion, not merely a change from one notation for the residual to
another. The underlying covers already admit hyperbolic metrics; this
application establishes a **uniform repair neighbourhood for distributed
perturbations**, not previously unknown Einstein topologies.

## 5. Scope relative to classical analysis and the Yau26 program

The weighted quadratic-form identity is classical; see
[Jäckel, Remark 2.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16692v2).
His Lemma 2.4 supplies the almost-Einstein Koiso estimate used in (18),
after scaling from \(\operatorname{Ric}=-(n-1)g\) to \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\).
The local Moser estimate is his Lemma 2.2. The new composition here uses
the **actual quotient's slow volume growth** to convert weighted residual
norms into uniformly local norms, and then uses the preceding topological
regularity result as a source of controlled charts.

Jäckel's Theorem 1.1 permits collapse, under its negative-sectional and
hyperbolic-thin-part hypotheses, but retains a small global \(L^2\)
residual. The present argument requires local noncollapse and (3),(5),
but permits errors spread throughout arbitrarily large volume.
[Premoselli's repair analysis](https://numdam.org/item/10.5802/tsg.367.pdf)
uses a different weighted inverse tailored to its gluing regions and
source support. None of these results is an unrestricted replacement for
the others. No global-priority claim is made for the weighted principle.

There are two important mathematical exclusions from the present theorem.
First, the earlier assumption
\(\|L_gu\|_2\ge\lambda\|u\|_2\) is not interchangeable with (2);
negative eigenvalues invalidate the weighted coercivity step. Second,
products of hyperbolic curves have genuine Teichmüller kernels, so they
do not satisfy (2). An obstruction equation cannot be bypassed by deleting
those tensors. The growth comparison also concerns all balls on the actual
manifold; a global entropy number without uniform finite-radius bounds
does not by itself supply (11).

The scientific increment is therefore specific: the prior no-diameter
criterion required \(\delta^{1-\alpha}+\|E\|_2\) to be small; here a
verified growth--coercivity condition makes **pointwise small Ricci error**
sufficient in a family of unbounded diameter and volume. It remains a
restricted sufficient answer to the original same-smooth-manifold
existence question, not a dimension-only Yau26 threshold.

threshold and constants in (19), and the four-dimensional scaling in
(21). These are finite algebraic checks. They do not certify the elliptic,
covering-space or nonlinear arguments above.

# A dimension-independent Einstein repair corollary

24 September 2026. This is a short extension of
[the uniformly local repair theorem](20260924_uniformly_local_repair.md),
not a new proof of its weighted inverse. That proof is dimension-independent
once its local harmonic charts are supplied. The four-dimensional
topological mechanism for producing those charts is not used here.

## 1. Uniformly local repair in dimension \(n\ge3\)

Fix \(n\ge3\), \(0<\alpha<1\), \(\rho>0\), \(\Lambda,C_G,\lambda>0\),
and \(d,H\ge0\), with
\[
                              H^2<8\lambda.                 \tag{1}
\]
A uniformly controlled harmonic atlas means harmonic coordinate charts
centred at every point, with nested coordinate balls of radii \(\rho\)
and \(2\rho\), uniform ellipticity
\(\Lambda^{-1}I\le(g_{ij})\le\Lambda I\), and coefficient
\(C^{2,\alpha}\) bounds at most \(\Lambda\) on the larger balls.
Uniformly local norms are suprema over the smaller charts, not sums.
The constants below depend on these atlas data.

**Corollary 1.** There exist \(\varepsilon,C>0\), depending only on the
fixed data above, with the following property. Let \((M^n,g)\) be a
closed connected smooth Riemannian manifold with such an atlas and
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}_g B_R(x)\le C_G(1+R)^d e^{HR}
       \quad\text{for all }x\in M,\ R\ge0.                  \tag{2}
\]
For the full Bianchi-gauged linearization
\[
 L_g=\tfrac12\Delta_{L,g}+I,
 \qquad
 \Phi_g(h)=\operatorname{Ric}_h+h+\delta_h^*(B_gh),
 \quad B_gu=\delta_gu+\tfrac12d\operatorname{tr}_gu,
\]
assume positive quadratic coercivity on **all** symmetric two-tensors:
\[
 Q_g(u):=\int_M\langle L_gu,u\rangle\,d\mu_g
                       \ge\lambda\|u\|_{L^2(g)}^2.          \tag{3}
\]
If \(E=\operatorname{Ric}_g+g\) satisfies
\[
                   \|E\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}<\varepsilon,
\]
then there is a smooth metric \(h\) on the same smooth manifold with
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_h=-h,
 \qquad
 \|h-g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}
                   \le C\|E\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}.     \tag{4}
\]
Neither orientation, \(\pi_2\), gauge-theoretic classes, nor a diameter,
total-volume or total-\(L^2\)-residual bound is assumed.

**Proof.** The Lichnerowicz operator has principal part
\(\tfrac12\nabla^*\nabla\) in every dimension. Choose
\(H/2<a<\sqrt{2\lambda}\) and put
\(\phi_x=e^{-a\operatorname{dist}_g(x,\cdot)}\). The weighted identity
\[
 Q_g(\phi_xu)
 =\int_M\phi_x^2\langle L_gu,u\rangle
             +\tfrac12\int_M|d\phi_x|^2|u|^2
\]
and (2)--(3) give, exactly as in the preceding theorem,
\[
 \|u\|_{L^2(B_1(x))}
 \le \frac{e^a\sqrt{J_a}}{\lambda-a^2/2}\|L_gu\|_\infty,
 \qquad
 J_a=C_Ge^H\sum_{j\ge0}(j+2)^d e^{-(2a-H)j}<\infty.        \tag{5}
\]
Local Moser and Schauder estimates in the supplied atlas therefore yield
\[
 \|L_g^{-1}f\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}
                   \le C_I\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}.
\]
Fredholm theory and (3) give this inverse on each closed \(M\).
Only the local constants change with \(n\); no dimension enters the
absorption coefficient \(\lambda-a^2/2\) or the growth condition (1).
The uniformly local Hölder quadratic estimate for
\(\Phi_g(g+u)-E-L_gu\) gives the same contraction proof. Smallness makes
\(h=g+u\) positive definite and \(\operatorname{Ric}_h\) negative
definite. Finally, \(b=B_gh\) satisfies
\(\nabla_h^*\nabla_hb-\operatorname{Ric}_h(b)=0\); integration forces
\(b=0\). Elliptic bootstrapping gives smoothness. \(\square\)

If additionally \(\|\nabla\operatorname{Ric}_g\|_\infty\le K_1\),
the atlas gives
\(\|E\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}
\le C\delta_-^{1-\alpha}\) for \(\delta_-\le1\).
Thus small pointwise Ricci defect alone suffices with these same fixed
geometric and operator data. An absolute spectral gap is not (3), and
genuine kernels have not been removed.

## 2. Actual large-diameter examples in every dimension \(n\ge3\)

Fix a closed hyperbolic \((N^n,h)\), normalized by
\(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\), and an epimorphism
\(\pi_1(N)\to\mathbb Z\).
[Millson's compact examples](https://math.umd.edu/~jjm/papers/bettinumber.pdf)
provide such a choice in every \(n\ge3\). Let \((N_m,h_m)\) be the finite
cyclic covers. The fixed infinite cyclic cover has a proper cocompact
\(\mathbb Z\)-action, so counting its bounded fundamental regions gives
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}_{h_m}B_R(x)\le C_N(1+R),
 \qquad \operatorname{Vol}(N_m,h_m)=m\operatorname{Vol}(N,h).
\]
Consequently their diameters tend to infinity. This bound concerns the
actual cyclic quotients, not their universal covers or arbitrary covers.

For sectional curvature \(-1/(n-1)\), the classical Koiso estimate gives
\[
 Q_{h_m}(u)\ge\lambda_n\|u\|_2^2,
 \qquad \lambda_n=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}>0.                     \tag{6}
\]
This is [Jäckel's Lemma 2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16692v2),
rescaled from \(\operatorname{Ric}=-(n-1)g\); its quadratic estimate,
unlike his principal theorem's stated dimensional scope, also applies
in dimension three.

All sufficiently small \(C^3_{\rm uloc}(h_m)\) perturbations \(g_m\)
have uniform harmonic atlas and Ricci-derivative bounds, satisfy (2)
with \(H=0,d=1\), and satisfy (3) with \(\lambda=\lambda_n/2\).
The last assertion follows from the almost-Einstein Koiso estimate and
uniform negative sectional curvature; it is not spectral continuity
with a constant depending on \(m\). Corollary 1 supplies a uniform
repair threshold independent of \(m\).

The residual can genuinely be distributed over all cells. For disjoint
lifts \(\beta_j\) of a fixed bump, equal to one on a smaller ball, take
\(f_m=t\sum_{j=1}^m c_j\beta_j\), \(1\le c_j\le2\), and
\(g_m=e^{2f_m}h_m\). On the smaller balls,
\[
 |\operatorname{Ric}_{g_m}+g_m|_{g_m}^2\,d\mu_{g_m}
 =n(e^{2tc_j}-1)^2e^{(n-4)tc_j}\,d\mu_{h_m}.
\]
Hence \(\|E_m\|_2\ge c t\sqrt m\) while
\(\|E_m\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}\le Ct\), uniformly in \(m\).
Small fixed \(t\) is allowed despite the divergent total residual.

This corollary makes one transferable analytic module explicit. It gives
uniform correction on known hyperbolic smooth types and a general
conditional solver, not new Einstein topologies or a dimension-only
Yau26 threshold. In higher dimensions, the controlled atlas remains an
input; the four-dimensional topological regularity theorem has not been
extended by this argument.

# Bilateral gauge saturation on genuinely collapsed local covers

24 September 2026. This note connects the actual-form estimates to a
collapsed regularity theorem. It does not replace base noncollapse by a
renamed base-volume hypothesis. Instead, maximal local nilpotency rank
supplies harmonic charts **after local rewinding**, and a covering-average
estimate transfers the gauge information to those charts. The output is
local product geometry outside a quantitatively small-volume set, not a
global Einstein repair or a classification of all collapsed regions.

## 1. A collapse-tolerant structure statement

Use the bilateral actual conformal Spin\(^c\) inputs and conventions of
[bilateral realization](20260924_bilateral_realization.md), Section 1.
Thus \(a_+\) is actual in the given orientation and \(a_-\) in the
opposite orientation; their positive projected energies satisfy
\(p_+,p_->0\). Define
\[
 \delta=\|\operatorname{Ric}^\sharp+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}},
 \quad r=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}+g|^2,
 \quad q_\pm=\frac{\frac32\int(s^2+12|W^\pm|^2)-72\pi^2p_\pm}{V},
 \quad K=2r+q_++q_-.
 \tag{1}
\]
The \(q_\pm\) are nonnegative; Weyl norms are endomorphism norms.
There is **no** lower bound on \(p_\pm\), total volume, or the volume of
base balls in the result below.

Fix a scale \(0<\rho\le1\). Let \(\epsilon_N,h_N>0\) be the
four-dimensional Euclidean-model constants in Naber–Zhang's Proposition
6.4, reduced if needed so that \(h_N<1\). Define \(\mathcal G_\rho\)
to consist of points \(x\) for which, for some \(k\in\{1,2,3,4\}\),
\[
 d_{GH}(B_{2\rho}(x),B_{2\rho}(0^k))<\epsilon_N\rho,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Image}
 [\pi_1(B_{\epsilon_N\rho}(x))\to\pi_1(B_{2\rho}(x))]=4-k.
 \tag{2}
\]
The rank is the Hirsch rank of a finite-index nilpotent subgroup, not a
Betti number or the rank of \(\pi_1(M)\). The small group in (2) is
almost nilpotent under these hypotheses. Closedness supplies the local
completeness required by the classical theorem. A fixed measurable
subset of the points satisfying (2) can be used if desired.

Let \(\pi_x:U_x\to B_\rho(x)\) be the **local universal cover** with
its pulled-back metric, and choose a lift \(\widetilde x\). The metric
space \(U_x\) is generally incomplete at the lifted outer boundary.
All balls used below stay a definite distance from that boundary. We do
not identify \(U_x\) with a subset of the universal cover of \(M\).

**Theorem A (rewound product structure on the bulk).** Fix
\(\rho\in(0,1]\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). There are \(a>0\),
\(C<\infty\), and a modulus \(\Psi(t)\to0\) as \(t\downarrow0\),
depending only on \(\rho,\alpha\) and the fixed classical constants,
with the following property. Suppose \(M\) is a closed connected oriented smooth
four-manifold with the actual inputs above,
\(\delta\le1/2\), and \(0<K\le1/2880\). Then there is a measurable
set \(\mathcal E\subset M\) satisfying
\[
                  \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal E)}{V}
                         \le C K^{1/4},                 \tag{3}
\]
such that, for each \(x\in\mathcal G_\rho\setminus\mathcal E\), a
neighbourhood of \(\widetilde x\) containing its radius-\(a\) ball is
\(\Psi(\delta+K^{1/4})\)-close in pointed geometric \(C^{1,\alpha}\)
to a neighbourhood in
\[
                         \mathbb H^2_{-1}\times\mathbb H^2_{-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
One may define this closeness using a pointed embedding of a fixed
slightly larger model ball, whose image contains \(B_a(\widetilde x)\),
and the scaled \(C^{1,\alpha}\) norm of the difference of the pulled-back
metrics. Shrinking \(a\) by a fixed factor makes these equivalent usual
local definitions interchangeable.

Equivalently, along any sequence with \(\delta_j\to0\),
\(K_j\to0\), there are exceptional sets of relative volume tending to
zero, and all the indicated rewound balls converge uniformly in this
local geometric sense to product balls. For \(K=0\), the equality case
already makes \(g\) Einstein and Kähler in both orientations, so the
product statement is exact.

This theorem does not assume bounded diameter, \(\pi_2=0\), a
class-square cost, or base injectivity radius. It allows genuine rank-one
or rank-two collapse; Section 6 gives an exact rank-two example. The
classical rank/model test (2) is not proved to hold everywhere. The
exceptional-volume estimate is relative to the whole manifold, not to
each arbitrarily small thin component.

## 2. The covering-average lemma: multiplicity is not free

**Lemma 1.** Suppose \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge-3g\),
\(\pi:U\to B_R(x)\) is a connected normal Riemannian covering, and
\(4b<R\), \(b\le1\). For any nonnegative measurable function \(F\)
on \(B_R(x)\),
\[
 \fint_{B_b(\widetilde x)}\pi^*F
       \le C_1\fint_{B_b(x)}F,
 \qquad C_1\le\frac{V_{-1}(3b)}{V_{-1}(b)}\le C(4).
 \tag{5}
\]
The constant does not depend on the number of sheets, a base-ball volume,
or an injectivity radius. Both sides are **averages**.

**Proof.** Choose a Dirichlet fundamental domain based at
\(\widetilde x\). Its part \(D_b\) projecting to \(B_b(x)\) has
volume \(\operatorname{Vol}B_b(x)\) and lies in
\(B_b(\widetilde x)\), apart from measure-zero choices on its boundary.
Indeed a minimizing base geodesic from \(x\) to any point of \(B_b(x)\)
lifts inside that ball, and the Dirichlet representative is no farther.

If a point of \(B_b(x)\) has \(m\) lifts in
\(B_b(\widetilde x)\), each is a translate of its representative in
\(D_b\). Its deck transformation moves \(\widetilde x\) by less than
\(2b\). Denote the number of such deck transformations by \(N\).
The sets \(\gamma D_b\) for these transformations are disjoint up to
measure zero and contained in \(B_{3b}(\widetilde x)\). Therefore
\[
 m\le N\le
 \frac{\operatorname{Vol}B_{3b}(\widetilde x)}
      {\operatorname{Vol}B_b(x)}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The balls have compact closure away from the boundary of the cover;
proper discontinuity makes the relevant orbit count finite. The area
formula and (6) bound the lifted integral by
\(\operatorname{Vol}B_{3b}(\widetilde x)\fint_{B_b(x)}F\).
Divide by \(\operatorname{Vol}B_b(\widetilde x)\) and apply the local
Bishop–Gromov comparison, which is valid since \(3b<R\). This proves
(5). \(\square\)

In contrast, an estimate for an unnormalized integral lifts with the
multiplicity in (6), which can diverge. A global small average does not
say that every local base average is small. The next step quantifies
exactly this second issue rather than suppressing it.

## 3. Global gauge error gives small local averages off a small set

The inherited actual-form theorem gives global forms
\(\omega_\pm\in\Omega^\pm(M)\), with
\(\|\omega_\pm\|_\infty\le2\sqrt3\). Put
\[
 F=\sum_{\sigma=\pm}
     \bigl[(|\omega_\sigma|^2-2)^2+|\nabla\omega_\sigma|^2\bigr].
 \tag{7}
\]
Use the smoothing tolerance \(K/4\) for each form and write
\(\kappa_\sigma=r+q_\sigma\), so \(\kappa_++\kappa_-=K\).
The actual estimates give
\[
 \fint F\le734K+8\sqrt K\le750\sqrt K.
 \tag{8}
\]
The first-order bound and the uniform amplitude, not the second-order
Hodge estimate, are used. There is no hidden negative-square cost.

For any fixed \(b\le1\), let
\(\mathcal A_bF(x)=\fint_{B_b(x)}F\). Local volume doubling under the
Ricci lower bound implies
\[
 \int_M\mathcal A_bF(x)\,d\mu(x)\le C_2\int_MF.
 \tag{9}
\]
To see this explicitly, if \(d(x,y)<b\), then
\(B_b(y)\subset B_{2b}(x)\), hence
\(\operatorname{Vol}B_b(y)\le C_2\operatorname{Vol}B_b(x)\).
Fubini applied to the ball-averaging kernel now proves (9).
Consequently, for every \(t>0\),
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Vol}\{x:\mathcal A_bF(x)>t\}}{V}
              \le\frac{750C_2\sqrt K}{t}.              \tag{10}
\]
Take \(t=K^{1/4}\). This proves (3). At every remaining point,
Lemma 1 transfers the small base average to its local universal cover:
\[
               \fint_{B_b(\widetilde x)}\pi_x^*F
                         \le C_1K^{1/4}.               \tag{11}
\]
The potentially enormous covering multiplicity has cancelled against
the correct local volume normalization. No inequality asserting
\(\int\pi_x^*F\le C\int_MF\) is used.

## 4. Maximal rank supplies regularity; the forms identify the limit

For \(x\in\mathcal G_\rho\), apply Naber–Zhang, Proposition 6.4,
to \(\rho^{-2}g\). Since \(\delta\le1/2\), the Ricci tensor norm of
\(g\) is at most three, and rescaling preserves the required bound.
The conclusion supplies a uniform harmonic radius at the lift of \(x\)
in the universal cover of \(B_\rho(x)\), at least \(h_N\rho\).
This is a classical theorem used with its rank condition, not a new
claim that Ricci bounds alone give regular local covers.

Choose \(b\) to be a fixed sufficiently small multiple of
\(h_N\rho\), with \(4b<\rho\). The covering metric has controlled
harmonic coordinates on a neighbourhood larger than \(B_b(\widetilde x)\).
The harmonic metric equation and bounded Ricci give uniform finite
\(W^{2,p}\) bounds on smaller charts and hence \(C^{1,\beta}\)
precompactness for every \(\beta<1\). No derivative of Ricci is needed.

We give the argument for the qualitative uniform modulus in Theorem A.
If it failed, there would be good points with \(\delta_j\to0\),
\(K_j^{1/4}\to0\), and controlled cover charts that stay a fixed
distance from product geometry. The charts have a convergent subsequence.
In harmonic coordinates
\[
 g^{ab}\partial_a\partial_b g_{ij}
            =-2\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}+Q_{ij}(g,\partial g).
 \tag{12}
\]
Pointwise \(\operatorname{Ric}+g\to0\) makes the limit a smooth exact
negative Einstein metric. Subtracting its equation improves convergence
to strong finite \(W^{2,p}\), and therefore to the prescribed
\(C^{1,\alpha}\) on still smaller charts.

Lift the actual forms to these charts. Their uniform bounds and (11)
give fixed-chart \(H^1\) bounds. Rellich compactness and convergence of
connections yield two smooth parallel limiting forms, one self-dual and
one anti-self-dual, both of norm \(\sqrt2\). The associated parallel
complex structures commute. Their product is a symmetric trace-zero
involution, with two rank-two parallel eigenspaces. Local de Rham
splitting and \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\) make both factors hyperbolic
surfaces of curvature \(-1\). On a fixed smaller ball this is exactly
(4), contradicting the assumed failure of uniform closeness.
This proves Theorem A. \(\square\)

The limit assertion uses neither global compactness nor an ALE theorem.
It also does not identify the limiting local deck transformations across
different points or across the exceptional set. That missing global
information is precisely why Theorem A is not a global realization result.

## 5. Why this changes the scope, and where it stops

The earlier bounded-diameter bilateral theorem obtains one compact
smooth limit and hence an exact metric on the original manifold. The
present theorem removes diameter and **base noncollapse** at the cost of
a different conclusion: quantitative product geometry in local covers
on the maximal-rank bulk. The two conclusions are not interchangeable.

There are two distinct failure mechanisms left visible:

1. A point can fail the rank/model condition (2). Small global gauge
   deficit does not prove maximal nilpotent rank or a smooth collapsed
   base. Naber–Zhang's singular-fibre examples explain why that topology
   cannot simply be omitted from their theorem.
2. A point can belong to \(\mathcal E\). The global bounds control its
   volume, not its topology, its monodromy, or the tensor modes needed to
   solve the global Einstein equation.

Even if (2) holds everywhere and the exceptional-volume bound tends to
zero, no assertion here controls a full global tensor inverse or glues
local exact product limits into a single metric on \(M\). The
[diameter-free repair criterion](20260924_local_to_global_scope.md)
cannot be invoked by replacing its base-ball Moser estimate with an
unexplained cover estimate: the inverse and the global residual norm
need their own covering-compatible control. Theorem A supplies genuine
collapsed local geometry, not that missing global solver.

## 6. Actual collapse tests and a sharp failure of unnormalized transfer

### 6.1 Exact bilateral saturation allows rank-two thin geometry

Take two closed hyperbolic surfaces of fixed genera at least two, each
with a separating geodesic of length \(\ell\to0\), and their product
metric \(g_\ell\). The canonical classes of the two product complex
structures are actual in both orientations, and
\[
                    \delta_\ell=q_{+,\ell}=q_{-,\ell}=0.
 \tag{13}
\]
Near the two short geodesics the metric is exactly
\[
 dt_1^2+\ell^2\cosh^2(t_1)d\theta_1^2
 +dt_2^2+\ell^2\cosh^2(t_2)d\theta_2^2,
 \tag{14}
\]
where each \(\theta_i\) has period one. Collar widths tend to infinity.
At their common waist, fixed-radius base-ball volumes are \(O(\ell^2)\).
The balls converge to Euclidean two-dimensional balls, and the fibre
group in (2) is \(\mathbb Z^2\), of maximal rank \(4-2\). Unwrapping
the two circles gives exact hyperbolic-product geometry. These are real
negative Einstein and gauge-saturated examples excluded by any uniform
base-ball volume lower bound, but covered by Theorem A.

This is not a new Einstein construction. It is an exact test showing
that the theorem's changed input regime includes genuine collapse, not
merely a reformulated noncollapsed condition.

### 6.2 Absolute integral errors need not stay small after rewinding

On the same actual metrics choose a fixed nonzero nonnegative smooth
function \(f(t_1,t_2)\), supported in \(|t_i|<b/10\), independent of
\(\theta_1,\theta_2\), and extend it by zero. Then
\[
 \int_M f\,d\mu_{g_\ell}
 =\ell^2\int f\cosh t_1\cosh t_2\,dt_1dt_2\to0.
 \tag{15}
\]
On the local universal cover use \(u_i=\ell\theta_i\). The lifted
metric is
\(dt_1^2+\cosh^2(t_1)du_1^2+dt_2^2+\cosh^2(t_2)du_2^2\),
independent of \(\ell\). Its lifted function is the same nonzero
\(f(t_1,t_2)\). Its integral over a fixed suitably chosen lifted ball
is bounded below by a positive constant. Thus no universal estimate
of the form
\[
            \int_{B_b(\widetilde x)}\pi^*f\le C\int_M f
 \tag{16}
\]
can hold, even when the underlying metrics have exact Einstein and
bilateral gauge equality. This counterexample concerns an inference
between integral norms. The function \(f\) is not asserted to be an
actual monopole defect on the equality metrics, where those defects
are zero. It pinpoints why a proof based only on their global integral
bounds cannot freely lift such bounds without a local-normalization step.

### 6.3 Local and global universal covers are different objects

For example, a Hopf circle in \(S^3\) is contractible in \(S^3\),
but represents the infinite cyclic fibre group over a small disk in
the Hopf base. A local cover of that tube unwraps the circle; the global
universal cover does not. Taking a product with \(S^1\) gives a
four-dimensional example with \(\pi_2=0\) exhibiting this distinction.
This elementary example is not claimed to satisfy the negative
Einstein/gauge hypotheses. It shows why even \(\pi_2(M)=0\) does not
justify embedding every rewound local ball into \(\widetilde M\).
Theorem A works entirely on the local cover and never makes that inference.

## 7. Source and status

- Naber–Zhang, [*Topology and epsilon-regularity Theorems on Collapsed Manifolds with Ricci Curvature Bounds*](https://arxiv.org/html/1412.1326),
  Proposition 6.4, supplies the maximal-rank harmonic-radius theorem on
  the local universal cover; its Proposition 6.5 requires the exact
  Einstein equation or Ricci-derivative control for curvature bounds.
  Only Proposition 6.4 is used here. We specialize its model to Euclidean
  space so its constants do not hide a varying limit-space dependence.
- The [actual-form theorem](../mathematics/current/line1_composed_application.md)
  supplies (7)–(8); [bilateral realization](20260924_bilateral_realization.md)
  gives the opposite-handed form algebra. The present theorem does not
  require the second-order form estimate.
- The [preserved collar construction](../mathematics/current/round11_closed_gluing.md),
  Section 2, records the classical hyperbolic collar formula and its
  primary reference. The product tests above use these classical metrics,
  not a new gluing construction.

The new project bridge is the combination of actual gauge supply,
covering averages, and maximal-rank collapsed regularity. Its bad-set
bound is proved by a direct averaging argument; its local geometric
modulus is qualitative compactness. It neither proves maximal rank from
saturation nor settles global Einstein existence in the collapsing regime.

# Gauge saturation, orbifold compactification, and marked smoothing realization

24 September 2026. A continuation of the
[actual-form composition](../mathematics/current/line1_composed_application.md)
and [compact KE realization](20260923_gauge_saturation_ke.md).
This note removes the assumption that the second homotopy group vanishes
from the **compactification** conclusion. Smooth realization is then obtained
under a separate, explicit algebraic and relative-diffeomorphism condition.
It does not assert that these latter conditions follow from Ricci pinching.

The contribution here is a composition of the inherited actual-form estimates
with classical bounded-Ricci orbifold compactness and algebraic smoothing.
The compactness, ALE classifications, deformation criterion and negative KE
existence theorem retain their classical attributions. No priority claim is
made for the composition.

## 1. Original input and compactification theorem

All manifolds are connected, closed, smooth and oriented of real dimension
four. Ricci uses the tensor norm except in the definition of the pointwise
operator defect. Two-forms use exterior-algebra norms and Weyl curvature
uses the Hilbert--Schmidt norm as an endomorphism of two-forms. Write
\[
 \delta_j=\|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}^{\sharp}+I\|_{\infty,\mathrm{op}},
 \quad V_j=\operatorname{Vol}(M_j,g_j),\quad
 p_j=(a_j^+)^2,
\]
\[
 q_j=\frac{\mathfrak D_2(g_j,a_j)}{V_j},\qquad
 \mathfrak D_2(g,a)=\frac32\int_M(s^2+12|W^+|^2)-72\pi^2(a^+)^2.
 \tag{1}
\]
An **actual conformally solvable class** means the Chern class of a
Spin\(^c\) structure whose unperturbed monopole equations are solvable for
every metric in the conformal class of the specified metric. The structure
may vary with \(j\), but is fixed when its auxiliary positive weights vary.

**Theorem A (negative KE orbifold compactification).** Suppose that, for
fixed \(D,A_0>0\),
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(M_j,g_j)\le D,\qquad p_j\ge A_0,
 \qquad \delta_j+q_j\longrightarrow0,                 \tag{2}
\]
and each \(a_j\) is an actual conformally solvable class. No hypothesis is
made on \(\pi_1(M_j)\) or \(\pi_2(M_j)\). A subsequence converges in the
Gromov--Hausdorff topology to a compact oriented orbifold \((X,h)\) with
finitely many isolated quotient singularities, and
\[
              \operatorname{Ric}_h=-h.
\]
There is a **global orbifold Kähler structure** \(J\) on \(X\), with its
given orientation. On every compact subset of \(X_{\mathrm{reg}}\), the
convergence is realized by smooth embeddings and is \(C^{1,\alpha}\) for
every \(\alpha<1\) and strong \(W^{2,p}\) for every finite \(p\).
The limiting metric and complex structure are smooth in orbifold charts.

No covering space is required to define \(J\). No negative-square cost,
Ricci-derivative bound, curvature-energy assumption, or initially given
complex structure on \(M_j\) is added to (2). This is not smooth compactness
on all of \(M_j\): topology may be carried by the neighborhoods that shrink
to the singular points.

### Proof: the bounded-Ricci compactness input really applies

Put \(r_j=\fint|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}+g_j|^2\) and
\(\kappa_j=r_j+q_j\). For large \(j\), \(\delta_j\le1/2\), so
\[
 |\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}|\le3,\qquad
 -6\le s_j\le-2,\qquad r_j\le4\delta_j^2.
\]
The actual monopole scalar inequality gives
\[
 32\pi^2A_0\le\int s_{j,-}^2
       \le16(1+\delta_j)^2V_j,\qquad
 V_j\ge\frac{8\pi^2A_0}{9}=:v_0.                     \tag{3}
\]
Bishop--Gromov comparison using the diameter bound provides an upper
volume bound \(V_j\le V_0(D)\) and, at every point, lower ball-volume
bounds \(\operatorname{Vol}B_\rho\ge c(D,A_0)\rho^4\) for
\(0<\rho\le1\). Applying the local Jiang--Naber \(L^2\)-curvature
bound on a uniformly finite fixed-scale cover yields
\[
                    \int_{M_j}|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\le E(D,A_0).
 \tag{4}
\]

Use **bounded-Ricci orbifold compactness**, not an Einstein-only bubble
theorem: two-sided Ricci bounds, noncollapse, bounded diameter and bounded
critical curvature energy give an orbifold subsequential limit with
\(C^{1,\alpha}\), weak \(W^{2,p}\) orbifold metric and regular-locus
convergence. Anderson explicitly distinguishes this result from the
earlier Einstein theorem in the introduction of [A04], referring to [A90].
The varying-manifold version uses (4). The same bounded-Ricci conclusion is
used explicitly in Honda--Mondino [HM25], proof of Theorem 2, p. 452.
The Ricci lower bound gives ordinary, irreducible quotient neighborhoods,
not a union of several cones at one vertex; see [A04], Remark 2.1(i).

On regular harmonic charts the metric equation is
\[
 g_j^{ab}\partial_a\partial_b(g_j)_{uv}
    =-2(\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j})_{uv}
                +Q_{uv}(g_j,\partial g_j).             \tag{5}
\]
The Ricci error itself tends uniformly to zero. The limiting equation is
\(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\); elliptic bootstrapping makes \(h\) smooth
on the regular locus. Applying interior estimates to the difference of
(5) and its limiting equation gives strong \(W^{2,p}\) convergence on
smaller charts. The right-hand side converges strongly; no derivative of
the Ricci error is needed. The limit already has \(W^{2,p}\) regularity
in local orbifold covers by the compactness theorem. Its Einstein equation
holds almost everywhere there (a point has zero measure); harmonic-coordinate
regularity and elliptic bootstrapping therefore make \(h\) a smooth
Einstein orbifold metric at the singular points as well.

### Proof: saturation selects a global complex structure

The inherited actual-form theorem supplies smooth self-dual \(\omega_j\)
and choices \(\eta_j\downarrow0\) with
\[
 \|\omega_j\|_\infty\le2\sqrt3,\qquad
 \fint(|\omega_j|^2-2)^2\le720\kappa_j+\eta_j,
\]
\[
 \fint|\nabla\omega_j|^2
       \le13\kappa_j+4\sqrt{2\kappa_j}+\eta_j.          \tag{6}
\]
Only the first-order part of that theorem is used. In particular its
additional second-order estimate and square-cost hypothesis are not used.
Because \(V_j\le V_0\), the unnormalized derivative and amplitude errors
in (6) tend to zero. Pull back to the same regular-locus embeddings as the
metrics. Rellich compactness on an exhaustion and a diagonal subsequence
give a global weak limit \(\omega_\infty\). It satisfies
\[
 \nabla^h\omega_\infty=0,\qquad
 *_h\omega_\infty=\omega_\infty,\qquad
 |\omega_\infty|^2=2.                                  \tag{7}
\]
The norm assertion follows from strong local \(L^2\) convergence, the
uniform amplitude bound and (6); it is not inferred merely from nonzero
total mass. The regular locus is connected. The form is one global limit
of global forms, rather than locally chosen eigenvectors.

In a uniformizing ball at a singular point, lift (7) to the punctured ball.
The smooth extended metric has bounded connection coefficients. The parallel
equation, constant norm and short paths imply that the lift has a unique
continuous limit at the origin. Its first-order parallel equation then
gives smooth extension. The extension is invariant under the finite chart
group because the form was invariant on the punctured ball. Thus (7) holds
on the whole orbifold. The relation
\(\omega_\infty(u,v)=h(Ju,v)\) defines a parallel orthogonal complex
structure: a self-dual two-form of norm \(\sqrt2\) in dimension four
squares to \(-I\) in this relation. Parallelism gives integrability and
closedness. This proves Theorem A. \(\square\)

## 2. What the small-scale fillings do, and do not, inherit

There is a useful conclusion stronger than an unconstrained Ricci-flat
blow-up. Set \(w_j=\sqrt6|W^+_{g_j}|\). The classical completed-square
estimate used in the actual-form proof gives
\[
 \fint(w_j-2)^2\le2\kappa_j,\qquad
 \int_E|W^+_{g_j}|^2\le\frac43\operatorname{Vol}(E)
                          +\frac23\kappa_jV_j         \tag{8}
\]
for every measurable subset \(E\subset M_j\).

**Proposition B (chirality of smooth ALE bubbles).** Any smooth complete
nonflat pointed limit obtained by harmonic-radius blow-up at scales
\(\rho_j\to0\), with convergence on compact subsets, is Ricci-flat and
anti-self-dual in the inherited orientation. With the Euclidean growth and
finite energy supplied by (3)--(4), it is ALE.

Indeed \(|\operatorname{Ric}_{\rho_j^{-2}g_j}|\le3\rho_j^2\).
On each rescaled ball of fixed radius \(R\), (8), scale invariance of
four-dimensional Weyl energy and the unscaled upper ball-volume bound give
\[
 \int_{B_R}|W^+_{\rho_j^{-2}g_j}|^2
       \le C\rho_j^4R^4+\frac23\kappa_jV_0\longrightarrow0.
\]
Harmonic-coordinate convergence as in (5) is strong enough to pass curvature
in \(L^2\). The limit has \(W^+=0\). Bando--Kasue--Nakajima's asymptotic
theorem applies to this **exact smooth Ricci-flat limit**, not to the input
metrics. This proves the proposition.

Wright's classification [W11], Theorem A, now applies legitimately: a
non-simply-connected nonflat bubble is a finite cyclic quotient of a
Gibbons--Hawking space and is Kähler; simply connected bubbles are the
hyperkähler ALE spaces. Şuvaina's [S11], Theorem B, relates the resulting
Ricci-flat Kähler complex surface to a **minimal resolution of a fiber**
of a local Q-Gorenstein deformation. The theorem does not say that the
particular complex structure of every bubble is already a smooth Milnor
fiber. Nor has the chosen bubble complex structure been identified with
the limiting complex marking at the outer orbifold link.

In particular, Proposition B is not a classification of the entire compact
region removed from \(M_j\) at a singular point. Such a region can involve
several scales and intermediate orbifold levels. No bubble-tree energy
identity or assertion that its topology equals one terminal bubble is used
below. The realization condition is imposed on the **whole actual filling**.

A concrete distinction is the quotient singularity
\(\mathbb C^2/\frac14(1,1)\). Its minimal resolution has a single
\((-4)\)-sphere, so a compact resolution neighborhood has \(b_2=1\).
Its Q-Gorenstein Milnor fiber is instead a rational ball with
\(b_2=0\) and \(\pi_1=\mathbb Z_2\). They fill the same oriented
quotient link but are not diffeomorphic. This classical example already
prevents identifying a smoothing filling from the link alone. It is not
claimed that both fillings arise from sequences satisfying (2).

## 3. A marked smoothing criterion returning to the original smooth type

Let \((X,h,J)\) be the limit in Theorem A, with singular points
\(x_1,\ldots,x_N\). Choose small disjoint quotient neighborhoods \(U_\nu\)
with smooth links \(Y_\nu\), and put
\[
                    K=X\setminus\bigcup_\nu\operatorname{int}U_\nu.
\]
Regular-locus convergence gives oriented smooth embeddings
\(\iota_j:K\hookrightarrow M_j\), including collars of the boundary.
Let \(B_j\) be the closure of their complement. The condition below
includes that \(B_j\) has exactly one connected filling \(B_{\nu,j}\)
at each boundary component, so no unproved topological separation statement
is hidden in the notation.

A **projective Q-Gorenstein smoothing** is a proper flat projective family
\(\pi:\mathcal X\to\Delta\) with central fiber \(X\), smooth fibers
\(X_t\) for sufficiently small \(t\ne0\), and relative canonical divisor
\(K_{\mathcal X/\Delta}\) Q-Cartier. Use the complex structure \(J\)
just produced, not an unrelated complex structure on the underlying space.
After shrinking \(\Delta\), the exterior is differentiably trivial by
Ehresmann's theorem with boundary. Its identification with \(K\) equips
each local Milnor fiber \(F_{\nu,t}\) with a specified boundary marking
by \(Y_\nu\).

**Theorem C (marked Q-Gorenstein realization).** Suppose \(X\) admits
such a smoothing. For an index \(j\), assume that for some small regular
parameter \(t_j\ne0\) each whole filling admits an orientation-preserving
diffeomorphism
\[
                    f_{\nu,j}:B_{\nu,j}\longrightarrow F_{\nu,t_j}
 \tag{9}
\]
whose boundary restriction agrees, up to isotopy, with the marking induced
by \(\iota_j\) and the exterior trivialization. Then the same oriented
smooth manifold \(M_j\) admits a negative KE metric. If (9) holds for all
sufficiently large indices of the extracted subsequence, the conclusion
holds for all of those indices.

**Proof.** An isotopy on a boundary extends across its collar. Modify the
maps (9) there so that they agree exactly with the exterior identification.
Collar-compatible gluing then gives an oriented diffeomorphism
\[
                           M_j\cong X_{t_j}.           \tag{10}
\]
The KE orbifold has positive canonical orbifold bundle: the curvature form
of its canonical Hermitian metric is \(-\rho_h=\omega_h>0\). A divisible
power of the canonical bundle descends to the quotient space, and the
orbifold Kodaira embedding theorem makes \(K_X\) ample as a Q-divisor.
Equivalently this is the usual canonical polarization of a negative KE
orbifold with quotient singularities.

Choose an index \(m\) such that \(mK_{\mathcal X/\Delta}\) is Cartier.
Its restriction to \(X\) is ample. Openness of relative ampleness, after
shrinking the base, makes \(K_{X_t}\) ample on each nearby smooth fiber.
The Aubin--Yau theorem gives a smooth Kähler metric \(h_t\) with
\(\operatorname{Ric}_{h_t}=-h_t\) on \(X_t\). Pulling it back through
(10) proves the claim. \(\square\)

Thus the output meets the Yau26 existence requirement on the covered smooth
types. It need not be close to \(g_j\), and no invertibility or integrability
assumption on a tensor Einstein gluing operator is needed. The analytic
compactification and the marked smooth-type matching remain different steps.

The relative condition (9) is finite local topology plus gluing data. It is
not replaceable by equality of fundamental groups, Betti numbers, links or
intersection forms. It is also not inferred from the ALE classification.
Once the marked data are known, however, no global diffeomorphism or complex
structure on \(M_j\) has to be assumed: (10) constructs the former and
pulls back the latter.

## 4. An algebraically checkable smoothing input

For quotient surface singularities, the local Q-Gorenstein smoothable
types are the rational double points and the cyclic singularities
\[
                  \frac1{dn^2}(1,dna-1),\qquad
                 d>0,\quad n\ge2,\quad (a,n)=1.       \tag{11}
\]
These are the class T singularities of Kollár--Shepherd-Barron. A local
smoothing at each singularity does not itself supply a global smoothing.

**Corollary D (cohomological realization test).** In Theorem C the global
smoothing assumption is satisfied if the normal projective surface \(X\)
has only class T singularities and its minimal resolution
\(r:Z\to X\), with reduced exceptional divisor \(E\), satisfies
\[
                         H^2(Z,T_Z(-\log E))=0.        \tag{12}
\]
Consequently (11), (12) and the marked filling condition (9) imply negative
KE existence on the original \(M_j\).

This is exactly the globalization criterion of Lee--Park [LP07], Theorem
2.1; their proof passes from (12) to vanishing of the global obstruction
sheaf cohomology, then globalizes the local Q-Gorenstein deformations.
We use its projective family, not an arbitrary first-order deformation.
Condition (12) is sufficient, not necessary, and is not claimed to follow
from the Ricci or gauge estimates.

For rational double points the standard Milnor filling is diffeomorphic to
the ADE resolution plumbing. For the cyclic types the Milnor filling,
not generally the full exceptional plumbing, is the relevant object; the
case \(d=1\) is the usual rational-ball replacement. Răsdeaconu--Şuvaina
[RS09], Proposition 2.6 and Remark 2.9, give the classical algebraic
rational-blowdown interpretation. To apply this interpretation to an
arbitrary \(M_j\), its actual exterior and boundary marking must still
match the algebraic surgery. Theorem C expresses precisely that requirement.

**Concrete ADE specialization.** Suppose the limit complex surface is a
degree \(d\ge5\) hypersurface in \(\mathbb{CP}^3\) with only nodes.
A generic pencil \(F_0+tG=0\), after restricting to a small disk, is a
global Gorenstein smoothing; adjunction gives
\(K_{X_t}=\mathcal O_{X_t}(d-4)\), which is ample. Each local Milnor
fiber has the oriented smooth type of the \((-2)\)-disk bundle over
\(S^2\). If the actual complementary pieces are these bundles with the
Milnor boundary markings, Theorem C applies. This specialization needs no
verification of (12), because the global smoothing is written explicitly.
It is a classical family illustrating a nonempty, checkable matching test,
not a newly constructed family of Einstein manifolds. In particular a
vanishing \(\pi_2\) condition is absent, not reintroduced under another name.

## 5. Precise scope and the remaining implication

The conclusions established from the raw data alone are
\[
 \boxed{\text{(2)}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{negative KE orbifold limit}}
\]
and the anti-self-duality of its smooth Ricci-flat ALE blow-ups. With the
additional global smoothing and marked filling inputs, they become
\[
 \boxed{\begin{gathered}
       \text{negative KE orbifold} +\text{global QG smoothing}\\
       +\text{marked Milnor fillings}\\
       \Downarrow\\
       \text{negative KE on the original smooth type}.
       \end{gathered}}
\]
These statements handle a genuine alternative to excluding all bubbles:
allow singular limits and recover the smooth type by algebraic realization.

There is no proof here that (2) forces all singularities to be class T in
their limiting complex representation, that their local smoothings
globalize, or that each entire geometric filling has the correct relative
Milnor type. Classifying terminal ALE spaces does not prove those three
claims. Resolving them, or supplying a counterexample to one, is the actual
remaining issue for unconditional realization without the topological
no-bubble hypothesis. Neither a full energy identity nor a new numerical
gap constant substitutes for this missing information.
