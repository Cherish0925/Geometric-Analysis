# Step 91 strategic mathematical corrections: global alternatives and the dense-carrier test

Read directly: A01 `step91_dense_carrier_consistency_and_wahl_cone_withdrawal.md`; A02 `step104_character_computation_and_cusp_reduction.md`; the earlier Step-90 dipole and determinant-fit arguments. These corrections concern implications being used to choose the next research route. They do not negate the realized Wahl embeddings, the transferred scalar/mixed inequalities, or the exact raw TN calculation.

## 1. Proposition 104.F does not exclude cusps or collapse

The valid group fact is that a torsion-free cocompact complex-hyperbolic lattice contains no subgroup Z². It does not imply that a boundary cross-section whose fundamental group contains Z² cannot occur in a decomposition of M. The inclusion of the cross-section can have a kernel. A theorem establishing the required injectivity in the actual degeneration is absent from §3 of the A02 note.

Even the intermediate claim that the entire image of such a cross-section must be cyclic does not follow. For example, the projection π₁(Σ_g × S¹) → π₁(Σ_g) has noncyclic hyperbolic image despite the source containing Z². For nilpotent cross-sections the image can indeed be much smaller, but a trivial or cyclic image still does not make an end geometrically removable. Carrying none of the host's fundamental group and being absent from an Einstein degeneration are different assertions.

Likewise, non-virtual-nilpotence alone does not prohibit bounded-curvature volume collapse. The product S¹_ε × N³, with N³ closed hyperbolic, has uniformly bounded sectional curvature, volume tending to zero, and non-virtually-nilpotent fundamental group. This is a counterexample to the group-theoretic inference, not an Einstein degeneration of the ball-quotient blowup. Positive simplicial volume could obstruct an appropriately uniformly sectional-curvature-bounded collapse; that is a different theorem and does not supply compactness for an arbitrary critical-L² near-Einstein sequence.

Consequently, 104.F and the asserted exhaustive Wahl/D≥6 geometric alternatives are not established. A particular Einstein Wahl orbifold would still give a zero-gap construction by the valid filler transfer. The converse—that every nonattained zero infimum is represented by those orbifolds—needs an extraction/compactness theorem with its own hypotheses. The D-block restriction also retains its full-absorption and Kähler-filler scope. It does not classify every possible bulk or filler.

## 2. Proposition 104.C has not identified a j=0 supplied field

All boundary characters ζ^r define orbifold line representations at C²/Z_(n²). Extension over the orbifold cone is not the same as extension over its smooth rational-ball filling. Relative to a fixed extending spinᶜ reference, the latter twists form nZ_(n²), by

0 → H²(B)=Z_n → H²(∂B)=Z_(n²) → H³(B,∂B)=Z_n → 0.

The earlier A01 calculation tested this subgroup. It permits a nonzero spinor value for two of its twists, not for every extending twist; permitting a value is not proving that an actual solution takes it. Filler transfer supplies inequalities without specifying a spinᶜ character at all, so it cannot be called a j=0 field supplier.

Moreover, any fixed smooth spinᶜ structure on M already restricts to one on the embedded rational ball. Thus a calculation claiming that no signed smooth basic class extends over that ball signals a mismatch in the proposed boundary-map/realization identification. It is not a phenomenon that can be dismissed because the integrated curvature estimate is character-independent. Spinᶜ structures are affine over H², and determinant characters change by twice the line-twist character; this distinction must be respected in the identification.

This does not warrant more arbitrary character enumeration. It means the asserted actual character has not yet been found. The isotropy calculation alone still gives no global obstruction.

## 3. Shrinking-filler monopole supply remains a potentially useful theorem, with a narrower entrance

Proposition 104.M's Step 2 is false as written: an extending spinᶜ structure is not thereby a Seiberg–Witten basic or monopole structure. The proposed proof should start with a fixed genuinely supplied smooth structure (or a fixed subsequence from the finite signed family), not every element of H²(M).

The analytic passage is more than a removability citation. It needs finite-energy bounds on the limit and identification of the equivariant lift/limiting boundary holonomy. The maximum-principle bound using the global scalar minimum is not made local merely by convergence of scalar curvature on a compact subset. There is a plausible route using global L⁴ spinor control from the integrated Weitzenböck identity, the fixed determinant square to bound full curvature after controlling F⁺, local elliptic estimates, and then equivariant removability. This could produce the useful actual-field input. Neither its character nor its irreducibility should be assigned before those steps establish them.

## 4. Step 91's TN computation tests selected balances, not every averaged identity

The mean identity for the actual bounded resolvent is valid. The raw TN computation fixes a consistent core density for that mean and the listed cell balances. It does not construct a common-connection physical block, a closed phase mixture, or the missing transition and compensator fields. In particular, replacing the raw TN relation Lτ=0 by the physical endpoint L u≈2u still requires the background and corrections discussed in Steps 46–47. The calculation does not prove that all integrated consequences of the coupled equations are exhausted.

Corollary 91.3 also cannot support the asserted classification of escapes:

- Its extra hypothesis μ(Z)→0 fails for the proposed fixed positive nut fraction θ: Step 90's dipole is depleted on asymptotically the whole flux-cell volume, so the global depleted fraction is at least θ, not vanishing.
- The retained term ∫_Z |e||∇s|² is uncontrolled; it cannot be dropped to deduce a lower bound for the bounded-model term alone.
- The Kato lower bound concerns the raw section σ. It is not a lower bound for a bounded radial truncation s=T(σ); capping can remove amplitude derivative energy. The nonlinear resolvent and boundary-phase transfer remain separate as well.

Thus the useful retained statement is consistency of the explicitly tested scalar averages, with actual global realization open. It is reasonable to reallocate effort when a more consequential route is available; an unsupported no-go theorem is neither needed nor justified to do so.

## 5. Research consequence

The realized Wahl complement is a genuine improvement over a lattice-only candidate and remains worth investigating. It is a sufficient construction route, not an exhaustive reduction of the unrestricted zero-gap problem. A fixed supplied-field limit, a direct Einstein obstruction on that complement, or an actual gluing construction would each add independent global information. The choice among them and the closed-pair route should be made from a concrete new implication, rather than from the false removal of collapse or the formal consistency of a TN mixture.
