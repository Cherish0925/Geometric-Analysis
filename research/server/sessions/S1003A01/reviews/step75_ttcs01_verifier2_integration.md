**The scalar-curvature supply is valid for every compact complex-hyperbolic surface, including fake projective planes. ## 1. What the resolution calculation proves

Let \(N\) be the orientation-matched resolution of
\[
O=S^4/\langle(t,w)\mapsto(t,-w)\rangle.
\]
Removing the two cone neighborhoods leaves
\[
C\cong\mathbb{RP}^3\times I.
\]
Each truncated Eguchi–Hanson cap is the disk bundle \(D_{-2}\) of \(\mathcal O(-2)\), with its complex orientation. Thus
\[
N=D_{-2}^{\,N}\cup C\cup D_{-2}^{\,S}.
\]

The elementary topology is sound, with the following details made explicit:

* \(\pi_1(C)=\mathbb Z/2\). Each boundary inclusion into \(C\) induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups, while each cap is simply connected. Van Kampen therefore gives \(\pi_1(N)=0\).
* Mayer–Vietoris gives
  \[
  0\longrightarrow \mathbb Z[C_N]\oplus\mathbb Z[C_S]
  \longrightarrow H_2(N;\mathbb Z)
  \longrightarrow
  \ker\!\left((\mathbb Z/2)^2\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\right)
  \longrightarrow0.
  \]
  The final kernel is \(\mathbb Z/2\). The two exceptional spheres therefore generate an index-two sublattice.
* Orientation matching makes both spheres have self-intersection \(-2\), and they are disjoint. Their lattice is \(\langle-2\rangle\oplus\langle-2\rangle\).
* Since \(N\) is closed and simply connected, its intersection form is unimodular on torsion-free \(H_2\). It has rank two and is negative definite, hence
  \[
  Q_N\cong-I_2.
  \]

These facts identify the **oriented homeomorphism type** as
\[
N\approx \overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}.
\]
They do not, by themselves, identify the smooth structure.

The supplied *Torus Actions on Alexandrov 4-Spaces*, Theorem 2.10(1), likewise explicitly concludes “equivariantly homeomorphic.” Its introductory discussion of smoothing continuous actions does not establish that a homeomorphism between the two already-given smooth manifolds is a diffeomorphism. Consequently, **the citation available in the supplied files does not close Step 74’s smooth recognition argument.**

This distinction is load-bearing: the resolution metrics initially live on \(X\#mN\). Seiberg–Witten estimates on the standard smooth blow-up \(X\#2m\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}\) cannot be transferred merely through a homeomorphism.

## 2. Explicit orbit data and the orientation issue

The effective torus is
\[
T'=T^2/\langle(-1,-1)\rangle,
\qquad
\Lambda=\mathbb Z^2+\mathbb Z\,\tfrac12(1,1)
\]
for its cocharacter lattice. Write \(e_1=(1,0)\), \(e_2=(0,1)\).

The two original boundary arcs of the orbit bigon have isotropy circles generated by \(e_1,e_2\). The determinant of these vectors in \(\Lambda\) has absolute value two, encoding the \(A_1\) vertices.

Choose the global orientation so that \((w_1,w_2)\) is orientation-preserving near the north pole. The same \(w\)-coordinates are orientation-reversing near the south pole. An orientation-preserving complex chart there is
\[
(w_1,\overline w_2).
\]
Accordingly, the inserted resolution circles are
\[
v_N=\tfrac12(e_1+e_2),
\qquad
v_S=\tfrac12(e_1-e_2).
\]
The cyclic isotropy generators are therefore
\[
e_1,\ v_N,\ e_2,\ v_S.
\]
In the lattice basis \(p=e_1,\ q=v_N\), these become
\[
\boxed{(1,0),\ (0,1),\ (-1,2),\ (1,-1).}
\]
Their consecutive determinants are \(1,1,-1,1\), so every corner is smooth. These are cocharacter generators of isotropy circles, not the character equations \(G(m,n)\) used elsewhere in the torus-action paper.

This supplies the missing local orientation information in the phrase “complex-oriented Eguchi–Hanson at both poles.” At the south pole the \(U(2)\)-invariance is understood in \((w_1,\overline w_2)\), which still contains the original coordinate-rotation torus. The action therefore extends smoothly and effectively across both caps. It has a disk orbit space, four boundary arcs, and no exceptional finite-isotropy orbits.

The weight computation also gives an explicit candidate match to the standard connected sum. The lattice automorphism
\[
P(x,y)=(x+y,x)
\]
takes the cyclic list to
\[
(1,1),\ (1,0),\ (1,-1),\ (0,1).
\]
This is the square obtained by joining two standard projective-plane triangles along the circles \((1,0),(0,1)\), with a sign reversal in one summand. The oriented intersection form \(-I_2\) selects the two reverse-oriented projective planes.

Thus the geometric identification is now explicit. What remains is the **smooth uniqueness theorem for this weighted orbit data**, or a direct smooth proof.

Two valid ways to complete it are:

1. **Smooth Orlik–Raymond classification.** Supply the precise result that a closed simply connected smooth four-manifold with an effective smooth \(T^2\)-action is diffeomorphic to an equivariant connected sum of the standard smooth blocks. Then \(Q_N=-I_2\) alone selects
   \[
   N\cong^{+}\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}.
   \]
   Under this broad smooth decomposition statement, comparing all weights is unnecessary, although the weights above verify the construction and its orientation.

2. **Smooth weighted-orbit classification.** Supply the statement that the appropriate oriented weighted disk determines a locally standard smooth action up to equivariant diffeomorphism, and use the explicit square match above.

The supplied modern paper identifies the original source as Orlik–Raymond, *Actions of the torus on 4-manifolds. I*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. **152** (1970), 531–559. Its original smooth statement is not reproduced in the bounded input. The remaining issue is therefore an exact smooth theorem citation or its proof—not a demonstrated obstruction to the proposed diffeomorphism.

### Is a Kirby/lattice shortcut valid?

**A lattice shortcut is not valid.** Neither unimodularity, \(Q_N=-I_2\), Freedman’s recognition, nor Donaldson diagonalization promotes homeomorphism to diffeomorphism. Expressing the exceptional classes as \(E_1\pm E_2\) does not automatically produce embedded blow-down spheres.

**A genuine Kirby proof is valid**, but must encode the boundary gluing and establish the standard smooth handlebody by actual handle moves.

There is also a shorter smooth gluing route, proposed in the supplied branch work:
\[
N=D_{-2}\cup_\varphi D_{-2},
\]
with \(\varphi\) orientation-reversing on the oriented boundaries. If one supplies
\[
\pi_0\operatorname{Diff}^{+}(\mathbb{RP}^3)=1,
\]
then any two orientation-reversing gluings differ by a map isotopic to the identity, so yield diffeomorphic glued manifolds. One must additionally exhibit the standard \(\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}\) as the same two-cap decomposition; the weighted square provides the relevant model. This route is mathematically legitimate, but **the mapping-class assertion and the smooth decomposition are not proved or sourced in the supplied files**. It is not an already-completed Kirby calculation.

## 3. The metricwise supply holds when \(b^+=1\)

The correct supply statement is polarized:
\[
\boxed{\text{For every }g\text{ on }M_k,\text{ there is a monopole for }(M_k,H_g^+)
\text{ with }(a_g^+)^2\ge A.}
\]
It need not be a single all-metric monopole class, nor must all signed blow-up classes be simultaneous all-metric monopoles.

This follows directly from LeBrun’s supplied paper, Definition 1 and the proof of Proposition 3.2, with the chamber matching explained in `round13_chamber.md`.

Let \(c=c_1(X)\), pulled back to \(M_k\), so \(c^2=A>0\). Fix an arbitrary metric \(g\) and polarization \(H=H_g^+\). Choose the signs of the exceptional generators so that
\[
c_H^+\cdot E_j\le0.
\]
The chamber-compatible blow-up formula supplies a nonzero small-perturbation invariant at \(H\) for
\[
a_H=c-\sum_{j=1}^kE_j.
\]
Hence \(a_H\) is a monopole for every \(H\)-adapted metric. Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(a_H^+)^2
&=(c_H^+)^2-2\sum_jc_H^+\cdot E_j
  +\left(\sum_jE_{j,H}^+\right)^2\\
&\ge(c_H^+)^2\ge c^2=A.
\end{aligned}
\]
The minus sign in this expansion is important; the displayed expansion in the converted LeBrun text contains an inconsistent sign in its preceding line.

When \(b^+=1\), orient a unit generator \(\omega\) of \(H\) so that \(c\cdot\omega>0\). The selected signs give
\[
a_H\cdot\omega
=c\cdot\omega-\sum_jE_j\cdot\omega
\ge c\cdot\omega>0.
\]
Thus the selected blow-up invariant lies in the actual small-perturbation chamber for the prescribed polarization. On the base, \(c^2>0\) prevents \(c_H^+=0\), so the nonzero small-perturbation invariant persists as the polarization varies. This is **not** independence from the other, large-perturbation chamber.

The usual scalar Weitzenböck estimate now yields, metric by metric,
\[
\boxed{S(g)\ge32\pi^2(a_H^+)^2\ge32\pi^2A.}
\]
LeBrun’s Theorem 2.3 also yields
\[
\boxed{\int(s-\sqrt6|W^+|)^2\,d\mu\ge72\pi^2A,}
\]
because multiplying its integrand by \(9/4\) converts
\(\frac23s-2\sqrt{2/3}|W^+|\) into \(s-\sqrt6|W^+|\).

Every metric conformal to \(g\) has the same polarization. Thus the selected structure stays fixed throughout the conformal argument. Across the resolution sequence \(g_\varepsilon\), the structures may change freely; neither convergence nor continuity of their selection is needed.

**Correction to Step 74:** replace the convexity-based explanation of (SW) by this polarized selection argument. It works for every \(k\), including \(k>A\), where treating all signed classes as simultaneous all-metric monopoles would be unjustified.

## 4. No fake-projective-plane exception remains

For a compact ball quotient,
\[
b^+=1+2p_g,\qquad \chi(\mathcal O_X)=A/9.
\]
If \(b^+=1\), then \(p_g=0\), and
\[
A/9=1-q>0.
\]
Hence \(q=0\), \(A=9\), giving the fake-projective-plane case. These are minimal general-type surfaces and fall within the polarized argument above.

Thus fake projective planes are the subfamily requiring explicit chamber bookkeeping, **not an excluded or unresolved scalar-supply subfamily**.
