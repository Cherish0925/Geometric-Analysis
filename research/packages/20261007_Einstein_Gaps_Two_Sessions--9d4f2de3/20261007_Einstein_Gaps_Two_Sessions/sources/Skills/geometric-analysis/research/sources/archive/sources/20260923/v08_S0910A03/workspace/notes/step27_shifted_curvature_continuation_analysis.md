# Step 27: shifted-curvature analysis of normalized local-flow continuation

## 0. Question and correction to Step 26

Consider on a closed smooth manifold

\[
\partial_tg=-2aE,\qquad a=\chi^2,\qquad E=\operatorname{Ric}_g+g,
\tag{0.1}
\]

where \(\chi\) is a fixed spatial cutoff.  The desired extension statement is:
if \(T<\infty\) and

\[
\sup_{M\times[0,T)}|a\operatorname{Rm}|<\infty,
\tag{0.2}
\]

then the solution extends smoothly through \(T\).  Constants in this theorem
may depend on the individual smooth pair \((g_0,\chi)\); no sequence-uniform
cutoff derivative estimate is required.

The Step 26 continuation specification is not a proof.  It omitted the
highest cutoff derivatives generated by the normalization.  If one splits
\(-2aE=-2a\operatorname{Ric}-2ag\), the all-covariant curvature variation of
\(-2ag\) includes

\[
-2a\operatorname{Rm}-(\nabla^2a)\owedge g,
\tag{0.3}
\]

up to the fixed curvature convention.  Therefore \(\nabla^m\operatorname{Rm}\)
has a term \((\nabla^{m+2}a)*g\), and \(\nabla^m(a\operatorname{Rm})\) has
derivatives of \(a\nabla^2a\).  Metric comparison and a zeroth-order bound on
\(a\operatorname{Rm}\) do not bound these terms.  They must enter Wang's
triangular induction, not be declared harmless.

The full-velocity tensor memo avoids the apparent source by recombining the
Ricci and normalization parts:

\[
\partial_t\operatorname{Rm}
 =a\Delta\operatorname{Rm}+a\mathcal A(\operatorname{Rm})
 +(da)*\nabla E+(\nabla^2a)*E,
\tag{0.4}
\]

with algebraic terms absorbed in \(a\mathcal A\).  Formula (0.4) is good for
low-order energy estimates because \(E\) and \(\nabla E\) are contractions of
curvature plus the parallel metric.  For high derivatives, it still contains
\(\nabla^{m+2}a*E\).  The central question is whether Wang's mutual induction
between cutoff and curvature derivatives survives when the contraction is
\(E\) rather than \(\operatorname{Ric}\).

## 1. The shifted curvature tensor

Fix the Kulkarni--Nomizu convention

\[
(g\owedge g)_{ijkl}=2(g_{ik}g_{jl}-g_{il}g_{jk}),
\]

and define

\[
G_{ijkl}=\frac1{2(n-1)}(g\owedge g)_{ijkl}
        =\frac1{n-1}(g_{ik}g_{jl}-g_{il}g_{jk}),
\qquad
\widetilde R=\operatorname{Rm}+G.
\tag{1.1}
\]

With contraction in the first and third slots,

\[
g^{ik}G_{ijkl}=g_{jl},
\qquad
\operatorname{Ric}(\widetilde R)=\operatorname{Ric}+g=E.
\tag{1.2}
\]

At every fixed time, metric compatibility gives

\[
\nabla G=0,
\qquad
\nabla^k\widetilde R=\nabla^k\operatorname{Rm}\quad(k\ge1).
\tag{1.3}
\]

The shift therefore changes only zero-order curvature.  Moreover, after the
standard normalization \(0\le\chi\le1\), hence \(0\le a\le1\), and \(|G|\) is
dimension-controlled,

\[
|a\widetilde R|\le |a\operatorname{Rm}|+C_n.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Thus (0.2) is equivalent, up to a fixed dimensional additive constant, to a
bound for \(a\widetilde R\).

The time derivative of \(G\) is also algebraic.  For
\(h=\partial_tg=-2aE\),

\[
\partial_tG=\frac1{n-1}
(h_{ik}g_{jl}+g_{ik}h_{jl}-h_{il}g_{jk}-g_{il}h_{jk})
          =a\,E*g.
\tag{1.5}
\]

There are no derivatives of \(a\) or \(E\) in (1.5).  This is the principal
advantage of the shift.

## 2. Uncommuted shifted-curvature evolution

Substituting \(E=\operatorname{Ric}(\widetilde R)\) into the exact
full-velocity curvature formula gives schematically

\[
\boxed{
\partial_t\widetilde R
 =a\Delta\widetilde R
  +a\,\mathcal Q_G(\widetilde R)
  +(da)*\nabla\operatorname{Ric}(\widetilde R)
  +(\nabla^2a)*\operatorname{Ric}(\widetilde R),}
\tag{2.1}
\]

where \(\mathcal Q_G\) is algebraic and polynomial of degree at most two in
\(\widetilde R\), with dimension-controlled constant and linear terms arising
from \(G\) and from (1.5).  The exact reaction vanishes at \(\widetilde R=0\), because then
\(E=0\) and the flow is stationary.  Thus it may be organized so that

\[
|\mathcal Q_G(\widetilde R)|\le C_n(|\widetilde R|+|\widetilde R|^2).
\tag{2.2}
\]

(A coarser bound with an added constant is harmless for energy estimates but
hides this cancellation.)

Equation (2.1) is a schematic equality of universal contractions rather than
an assertion that each displayed coefficient is one.  Its load-bearing feature
is exact derivative order: the cutoff Hessian multiplies the Ricci contraction
of the *same shifted tensor*, and the cutoff gradient multiplies one derivative
of that contraction.

Commuting \(m\) covariant derivatives gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t\nabla^m\widetilde R
={}&a\Delta\nabla^m\widetilde R\\
&+\sum_{i+j+k=m+2,\ k\le m+1}
   \nabla^i\chi*\nabla^j\chi*\nabla^k\widetilde R\\
&+\sum_{i+j+k=m}
   \nabla^i a*\nabla^j\widetilde R*\nabla^k\widetilde R\\
&+\text{terms with one shifted-curvature factor replaced by }G.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The first sum includes the true highest cutoff term

\[
\chi\,\nabla^{m+2}\chi*\widetilde R
\quad\text{(equivalently }\nabla^{m+2}a*\operatorname{Ric}(\widetilde R)
\text{ plus lower product terms).}
\tag{2.4}
\]

The \(G\)-terms are not differentiated curvature sources: \(\nabla G=0\), so
they either reduce the number of curvature factors or become pure cutoff
terms of the same derivative order already present in (2.3).  Hence the shift
does **not** remove \(\nabla^{m+2}\chi\); it restores the same structural
placement as Wang's unnormalized formula.

## 3. Weighted shifted curvature and the dependency triangle

Set

\[
U=a\widetilde R.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Since \(a\) is fixed as a scalar function, \(\partial_ta=0\).  Rewriting
\(a\partial_t\widetilde R\) as \(a\Delta U\) plus product-commutator terms
gives the analogue of Wang Lemma 4.5:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t\nabla^m U
={}&a\Delta\nabla^mU\\
&+\sum_{i+j+k=m+2,\ k\le m+1}
 \nabla^i\chi*\nabla^j\chi*\nabla^kU\\
&+\sum_{i+j+k=m,\ k\le m-1}
 \nabla^ia*\nabla^jU*\nabla^k\widetilde R\\
&+U*\nabla^mU+\mathcal L_{G,m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Here \(\mathcal L_{G,m}\) consists of universal terms with the same or lower
derivative orders and dimension-controlled coefficients.  The dangerous
highest-cutoff pieces remain

\[
\chi\nabla^{m+2}\chi*U,
\qquad
\nabla\chi*\nabla^{m+1}\chi*U,
\qquad
\chi\nabla^{m+1}\chi*\nabla U.
\tag{3.3}
\]

They cannot be bounded pointwise from the induction hypotheses.  Wang handles
them in the integrated \(L^p\) estimate by one integration by parts; the
derivative transfers either to \(\nabla^mU\), where the diffusion controls it,
or to \(U\)/lower factors, and leaves a product involving
\(\nabla^{m+1}\chi\).  Thus the same cyclic highest-order dependencies are
expected:

1. \(\|\nabla^mU\|_{L^p}\) depends on
   \(\|\nabla^{m-1}\widetilde R\|_{L^{\dot p}}\) and
   \(\|\nabla^{m+1}\chi\|_{L^{\ddot p}}\), after the top term is integrated
   by parts;
2. the prior unweighted quantity
   \(\|\nabla^{m-1}\widetilde R\|_{L^{\dot p}}\) requires cutoff derivatives
   only through order \(m\), because its raw \(\nabla^{m+1}\chi\) source loses
   one derivative under the same energy integration by parts;
3. \(\|\nabla^{m+1}\chi\|\) depends on
   \(\|\nabla^m(aE)\|\), and

\[
aE=a\operatorname{Ric}(\widetilde R)=\operatorname{Ric}(U),
\tag{3.4}
\]

so the latter is a contraction of \(\nabla^mU\) plus lower terms from the
evolving contraction convention.

More precisely, because \(\chi\) is a fixed scalar,

\[
\partial_t\nabla^r\chi
 =\sum_{q=0}^{r-2}\nabla^q(\partial_t\Gamma)*\nabla^{r-1-q}\chi,
\qquad
\partial_t\Gamma=\nabla(aE)=\nabla\operatorname{Ric}(U),
\tag{3.5}
\]

schematically.  Therefore

\[
\partial_t|\nabla^r\chi|
\le C_r\sum_{q\le r-1}|\nabla^q\operatorname{Ric}(U)|
                                |\nabla^{r-q}\chi|,
\tag{3.6}
\]

which is exactly Wang Lemma 4.6 with \(a\operatorname{Ric}\) replaced by
\(aE=\operatorname{Ric}(U)\).  No separate term \(\nabla^q(ag)\) remains.
This is the strongest reason the shift is useful: it preserves the contraction
relation governing the cutoff derivative evolution.

## 4. Base estimates and what is already sufficient

Assume \(\sup|U|<\infty\), hence \(\sup|a\widetilde R|<\infty\).  The flow
velocity satisfies

\[
|aE|=|\operatorname{Ric}(U)|\le C_n|U|,
\tag{4.1}
\]

so the metrics are uniformly bilipschitz on \([0,T)\).  Consequently
\(|\nabla\chi|\) and a Sobolev constant are uniformly bounded, just as in
Wang Proposition 4.9.

The unweighted \(L^p\) base estimate for \(\widetilde R\) follows by testing
(2.1) against \(|\widetilde R|^{p-2}\widetilde R\).  The
\((\nabla^2a)*\operatorname{Ric}(\widetilde R)\) term is integrated by parts
once.  The resulting terms contain \(|da|\,|\nabla\widetilde R|\) and are
absorbed into the weighted diffusion plus \(C\int|\widetilde R|^p+C\), using
bounded \(|\nabla\chi|\), compactness, and \(|a\widetilde R|\le C\).  The
algebraic \(G\)-terms contribute at most \(C(1+\|\widetilde R\|_p^p)\).  Thus,
for each fixed sufficiently large \(p\),

\[
\frac d{dt}\int|\widetilde R|^p\le C_p\left(1+\int|\widetilde R|^p\right),
\tag{4.2}
\]

with constants depending on \((g_0,\chi,T,p)\).  This is the normalized
analogue of Wang Lemma 4.10.  It supplies the unweighted curvature norms used
in the higher induction.

The shift also makes Hamilton's terminal reconstruction natural.  Bounds for
all \(\nabla^mU\) imply bounds for all derivatives of
\(aE=\operatorname{Ric}(U)\), hence for all time derivatives/spatial jets of
the compactly supported velocity.  Outside \(\operatorname{supp}\chi\) the
metric is stationary.  This is the normalized analogue of Wang Proposition
4.3; the algebraic shift does not alter the argument.

## 5. What is and is not established in this step

The shifted tensor repairs the structural omission in Step 26:

- it packages \(E\) as the Ricci contraction of one curvature-type tensor;
- its covariant derivatives equal those of ordinary curvature;
- its time variation adds only algebraic terms;
- the highest \(\nabla^{m+2}\chi\) terms remain visible and occupy exactly the
  slot treated by Wang's integration-by-parts induction;
- the cutoff evolution is driven by derivatives of
  \(aE=\operatorname{Ric}(a\widetilde R)\), closing the same dependency cycle.

This establishes a credible transfer mechanism but, at the present level of
writing, not yet a complete extension theorem.  A complete proof must carry
\(\mathcal L_{G,m}\) through the detailed estimates of Wang Lemma 4.11 and
Propositions 4.13--4.14, showing explicitly that each term is bounded by the
same three induction families.  In particular it must not suppress
\(\chi\nabla^{m+2}\chi*U\) before the integration by parts.

## 6. Normalized DeTurck construction

Yang's hatted system transports the coefficient.  For the normalized flow the
hatted metric equation must be

\[
\partial_t\widehat g
=-2\widehat a\bigl(\operatorname{Ric}(\widehat g)+\widehat g\bigr)
 +\mathcal L_V\widehat g,
\qquad
\widehat a=(\chi\circ\varphi_t^{-1})^2,
\tag{6.1}
\]

with the same DeTurck vector field/diffeomorphism equation as Yang.  The added
term is zeroth order in \(\widehat g\), so the regularized principal operator
is unchanged.  Naturality gives

\[
\varphi_t^*\widehat a=\chi^2,
\quad
\varphi_t^*\operatorname{Ric}(\widehat g)=\operatorname{Ric}(g),
\quad
\varphi_t^*\widehat g=g,
\tag{6.2}
\]

and the Lie derivative cancels the pullback derivative, yielding (0.1).
Thus short-time existence for each smooth input transfers directly; the issue
addressed above is terminal extension under only (0.2).

## 7. Consequence for the active four-dimensional route

If the detailed shifted-curvature induction closes, the Step 26 first-exit
argument can use the already obtained whole-support bound
\(|a\operatorname{Rm}(t)|\lesssim\bar\beta/t\) to continue each smooth flow to
the uniform target time.  Higher derivative bounds uniform across the pinching
sequence are then needed only on a smaller \(a=1\) plateau at positive time,
where ordinary normalized Ricci-flow interior estimates apply.  The geometric
endpoint remains the Step 6 projector/Einstein--Weyl contradiction.

Even that result would concern a critical four-dimensional negative
Ishida--LeBrun family, not the unrestricted Yau 26 statement.  The positive
branch, arbitrary topology, dimensions at least five, and Lean formalization
remain open.
