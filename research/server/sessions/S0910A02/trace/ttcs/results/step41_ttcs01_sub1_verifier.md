## 1. What Liu actually gives for diagonal actions

The source separation is clean:

- **Theorem 3.1:** every isometric \(T^2\)-action on a closed simply connected Einstein \(4\)-manifold is geometrically polar.
- **Definition 2:** a diagonal action is one whose codimension-two isotropy circles are globally of the two classes \(S(1,0)\) and \(S(0,1)\).
- **Proposition 5:** for such an action, the off-diagonal fiber coefficient \(g^\Omega_{12}\) solves Liu’s linear equation with zero Dirichlet data on every edge. Lemma 5.2 forces \(g^\Omega_{12}=0\).

Neither result uses \(\sec\ge0\). That assumption enters Liu’s main classification by forcing the totally geodesic quotient surface to be flat and by enabling the later curvature arguments.

For a diagonal fiber metric
\[
g^\Omega=a^2\,d\varphi_1^2+b^2\,d\varphi_2^2,
\]
a circle generated by \(v=(p,q)\) has squared length
\[
|v|^2=p^2a^2+q^2b^2.
\]
Away from vertices, precisely one of \(a,b\) vanishes on a rod. Therefore a collapsing circle there must be one of the two coordinate classes. This elementary observation agrees with Liu’s definition.

Two qualifications matter:

- The labels are intrinsically **unoriented**: \(S(p,q)=S(-p,-q)\). The sequence
  \[
  e_1,e_2,-e_1,-e_2,\ldots
  \]
  is therefore a choice of primitive representatives, not invariant data.
- Adjacent labels must have determinant \(\pm1\), so the two unoriented classes alternate. A cyclic alternating polygon has an **even** number of edges. Consequently, the \(m=5\) row in the Step-40 and Step-41 data is not a closed smooth diagonal toric case. The JSON identification of \(m=5\) with \(\#_1(S^2\times S^2)\) is incorrect; for the alternating family,
  \[
  m=2k+2,\qquad M\cong\#_k(S^2\times S^2).
  \]

The Page, CLW, and \(dP_3\) fan labels recorded in the JSON have three circle classes, so those displayed toric actions are not diagonal. The supplied Liu text does not itself provide those fan lists, so a source for the lists is still needed. Also, “irrelevant” is too broad: these metrics cannot calibrate the alternating rod ansatz, but they can still test the general polar equations, determinant normalization, and edge conventions.

Finally, Liu’s introductory sentence that six toric Einstein examples were known is time-stamped and does not amount to a literature-wide classification of all diagonal Einstein metrics. Product uniqueness without \(\sec\ge0\) is not proved in the supplied source.

## 2. The reduced orbit problem: source results and unresolved mathematics

### 2.1 Source-grade content

Liu supplies the following exact structure.

Let
\[
\phi=\sqrt{\det g^\Omega}.
\]
For an Einstein metric with \(\operatorname{Ric}=\Lambda g\), Proposition 2 gives
\[
-\Delta_\Sigma\phi=2\Lambda\phi,
\]
with \(\phi>0\) in the interior and \(\phi=0\) on \(\partial\Sigma\). Hence
\[
2\Lambda=\lambda_1(\Sigma,g_\Sigma).
\]

For every entry \(u=g^\Omega_{ij}\), Proposition 3 derives
\[
-\frac12\Delta_\Sigma u
+\frac1{2\phi}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla u\rangle
+2K_\Sigma u
=\Lambda u. \tag{2.1}
\]
Lemma 5.2 supplies uniqueness among the smooth-even solutions once the Dirichlet edge values are fixed. Thus the exact source formulation is:

> For a fixed torus action and fixed quotient metric \(g_\Sigma\), there is at most one invariant Einstein completion.

“At most one” is essential. For an arbitrary \(g_\Sigma\), even the edge data may be incompatible.

Along a rod, if \(u\) denotes the length of the surviving circle, Liu’s computation gives
\[
-\frac{u''}{u}+2K_\Sigma=\Lambda, \tag{2.2}
\]
together with
\[
u(0)=u(\ell)=0,\qquad u'(0)=1,\qquad u'(\ell)=-1
\]
after fixing the circle periods and orientation along the edge. For \(K_\Sigma=-A\) and \(\Lambda=1\), this yields
\[
\ell=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{1+2A}}.
\]

### 2.2 What existence still requires

Solving the source equations requires all of the following to agree:

1. The principal eigenvalue must satisfy \(\lambda_1=2\Lambda\), with the eigenfunction normalized consistently with rod smoothness.
2. Every edge ODE (2.2) must meet both endpoint values and both endpoint derivatives.
3. Equation (2.1) must admit smooth-even solutions with the action-prescribed edge values.
4. The resulting matrix \(g^\Omega\) must be positive definite in the interior.
5. It must satisfy
   \[
   \det g^\Omega=\phi^2.
   \]
   Liu’s Proposition 4 demonstrates that this identity can break even when the individual linear equations have unique solutions.
6. The horizontal Einstein equation must hold. For the diagonal ansatz it is
   \[
   \operatorname{Ric}_\Sigma
   -a^{-1}\operatorname{Hess}_\Sigma a
   -b^{-1}\operatorname{Hess}_\Sigma b
   =\Lambda g_\Sigma. \tag{2.3}
   \]
   The two vertical equations are
   \[
   -\frac{\Delta a}{a}
   -\frac{\langle\nabla a,\nabla b\rangle}{ab}=\Lambda,
   \qquad
   -\frac{\Delta b}{b}
   -\frac{\langle\nabla a,\nabla b\rangle}{ab}=\Lambda. \tag{2.4}
   \]

Liu does not prove a converse saying that the eigenfunction equation, the entrywise linear equations, and the determinant identity automatically imply (2.3).

### 2.3 Is “overdetermined” mathematically justified?

It is justified only in the following global sense: fixing \(g_\Sigma\) slaves \(g^\Omega\) to at most one candidate, while smooth rod data, determinant normalization, positivity, and (2.3) impose further compatibility on \(g_\Sigma\).

It is not yet a formal symbol count. Before fixing gauge, the unknowns are \(g_\Sigma,a,b\). Locally, a two-dimensional metric modulo diffeomorphism contributes one scalar function, so there are effectively three functional unknowns. The Einstein equations have contracted-Bianchi dependencies. Thus the reduced system can be elliptically determined at the principal-symbol level while still having highly restrictive global rod data.

Accordingly, “three constraint families for one function” does not prove product rigidity. One must write the complete gauge-fixed linearized operator, include the edge jets and polygon moduli, quotient diffeomorphism directions, and compute its kernel and cokernel.

### 2.4 Concrete defect in the Step-40 product linearization

At the product solution
\[
g_{\Sigma,0}=dx^2+dy^2,\qquad
a_0=\sin x,\qquad b_0=\sin y,
\]
write
\[
g_\Sigma=e^{2\varepsilon s}g_{\Sigma,0},\quad
a=a_0+\varepsilon\alpha,\quad
b=b_0+\varepsilon\beta.
\]
Then
\[
\psi:=\delta(ab)=\alpha\sin y+\beta\sin x.
\]
Directly linearizing
\[
-\Delta_{g_\Sigma}(ab)=2ab
\]
gives
\[
-(\Delta_0+2)\psi
=4s\sin x\sin y. \tag{2.5}
\]
There is no \(\Delta s\) term in this direct eigenfunction linearization, because in dimension two
\[
\Delta_{e^{2\varepsilon s}g_0}
=e^{-2\varepsilon s}\Delta_0.
\]

Therefore the Step-40 equation
\[
-(\Delta_0+1)\alpha
=(\Delta_0s-2s)\sin x
\]
is not the linearization of Proposition 2 as labeled. It might arise only after a further elimination using parts of (2.3), but that derivation is absent.

The kernel interpretation also conflicts with the stated rod data. The mode \(\alpha=\sin x\), \(s=0\), obeys the Dirichlet data but
\[
\alpha_x(0,y)=1,\qquad \alpha_x(\pi,y)=-1,
\]
whereas the smooth rod correction requires both to be zero when \(s=0\). Thus this mode is not an admissible infinitesimal deformation under the full edge jets, and it is not simply a \(\Lambda\)-rescaling at fixed \(\Lambda=1\).

The linear analysis also omits:

- the corresponding equation for \(\beta\);
- the full linearization of (2.3);
- geodesic-edge conditions, which in a fixed conformal realization give \(\partial_\nu s=0\);
- diffeomorphism directions and the quadrilateral conformal modulus;
- corner compatibility.

Product isolation remains plausible, but it is not established by the current files.

## 3. What the corrected FEM does and does not show

### 3.1 The corrected conformal weight is right

In the Poincaré disk,
\[
g=\rho(x,y)(dx^2+dy^2),
\qquad
\rho=\frac{4}{(1-r^2)^2}.
\]
In two dimensions,
\[
\int |\nabla u|_g^2\,dV_g
=\int |\nabla u|_{\mathrm{Euc}}^2\,dx\,dy,
\qquad
\int u^2\,dV_g
=\int \rho\,u^2\,dx\,dy.
\]
Thus the corrected mass matrix uses \(\rho\), not \(\rho^2\). The superseded run cannot be used.

### 3.2 Exact reduction for the regular constant-curvature model

Let \(P_m\subset\mathbb H^2(-1)\) be the regular right-angled \(m\)-gon and let
\[
\mu_m=\lambda_1(P_m).
\]
Under scaling to curvature \(-A\),
\[
\lambda_1(P_m,-A)=A\mu_m.
\]
For \(\Lambda=1\), the eigenfunction requirement gives
\[
A_{\rm eig}=\frac{2}{\mu_m}.
\]
The rod and polygon side lengths agree only at the explicit \(A_{\rm side}\) recorded in the JSON. Hence the regular constant-\(K_\Sigma\) model requires
\[
A_{\rm eig}=A_{\rm side},
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad
\mu_m=\frac{2}{A_{\rm side}}. \tag{3.1}
\]

For \(m=6\), the finest supplied run gives
\[
\mu_6\approx6.97315,\qquad
A_{\rm eig}\approx0.286815,\qquad
A_{\rm side}\approx0.270961.
\]
At the side-matching curvature,
\[
\lambda_1\approx1.88945,
\]
which is about \(0.11055\) below \(2\). Equivalently, the required unit-curvature eigenvalue is approximately \(7.381\), around \(0.408\) above the computed value. At \(A_{\rm eig}\), the rod is approximately \(1.842\%\) longer than the polygon side.

The mesh sequence changes \(\mu_6\) from \(6.98158\) to \(6.97315\), while the spectral separation in (3.1) is much larger. This is good internal numerical evidence. It is not a certified eigenvalue enclosure: the Step-41 script, exact domain construction, quadrature details, and interval bounds were not supplied.

### 3.3 Strengthened and weakened aspects

The corrected computation **strengthens** the regular constant-curvature picture because Step 40’s Faber–Krahn estimate did not exclude equality. The inequality only placed \(A\) below approximately \(0.346\), while \(A_{\rm side}\approx0.271\) remained allowed. Step 41 directly estimates the actual eigenvalue and separates the two required curvatures.

It **weakens the “large gap” rhetoric** near the geometrically relevant parameter. The \(68.7\%\) number is the \(A\to\infty\) side-length ratio; the eigenvalue-selected curvature is near \(0.287\), where the rod/side mismatch is only about \(1.84\%\). The spectral residual \(2-1.88945\) is the more meaningful numerical quantity.

The data support the tested cases \(m=6,8,10,12\). They do not establish the statement for every even \(m\ge6\). The \(m=5\) run is useful only as a numerical geometry test, since an odd cyclic polygon cannot carry the alternating diagonal labels.

Nothing in these runs addresses variable \(K_\Sigma\), a nonregular quotient geometry, the full equations (2.3)–(2.4), or an Einstein metric without the chosen torus symmetry.

A short rigorous theorem is visible here: a conforming trial function with Rayleigh quotient
\[
\mathcal R(u)<\frac{2}{A_{\rm side}}
\]
would prove the \(m=6\) regular constant-curvature incompatibility. An exactly represented inner polygon and interval evaluation of the finite-element integrals could turn the present numerical separation into such a proof.

## 4. Scientific leverage for Yau-26

### Diagonal toric front

The durable result is the unconditional diagonal reduction under the Einstein and isometric-action hypotheses. Its direct connection to Yau-26 remains weak for two independent reasons:

1. Liu’s introduction supplies positive-Ricci metrics on diagonal connected sums, but the files do not supply a sequence with
   \[
   \|\operatorname{Ric}-\Lambda g\|_{L^\infty}\to0.
   \]
2. Excluding diagonal \(T^2\)-invariant Einstein metrics would not exclude Einstein metrics with another symmetry group or no continuous symmetry.

The \(b_+\ge2\) observation does not bridge the second point. No supplied theorem rules out positive Einstein metrics with \(b_+\ge2\), and Hitchin–Thorpe permits \(\#_k(S^2\times S^2)\). Likewise, the sentence that all known examples have \(b_+\le1\) is a literature observation, not a theorem in Liu’s paper.

Thus the toric work is a strong symmetry-reduced laboratory, especially for a collapsed approximate construction, but a theorem confined to regular constant-\(K_\Sigma\) polygons would remain several logical steps from the original proposition.

### Route A noncollapsed front

Step 33 contains a much more direct object for four-dimensional positive noncollapsed sequences:

- an actual Lichnerowicz–Bianchi indicial decomposition on
  \[
  C(S^3/\mathbb Z_2),
  \]
  including parity, trace/Bianchi gauge, volume mode, and pure-gauge quotient;
- an annular harmonic-radius theorem excluding intermediate \(L^2\)-curvature loss;
- the balanced-annulus expansion at \(r\asymp t^{2/3}\), with the error pairing \(o(t^4)\).

This would connect the original \(C^0\) Ricci-endomorphism input to the \(H_2\)–\(H^{-4}\) Wronskian obstruction. Among the supplied positive-sign objects, it has much greater direct reach than regular toric polygons, although it still treats only dimension four and the noncollapsed alternative.

### The \(E=\operatorname{Ric}+g\) cutoff-flow front

The Step-40 staggered induction is conditional derivative regularity. It assumes:

- a smooth solution already exists on a positive-time slab;
- \(\sup|U|<\infty\), where \(U=\chi^2(\operatorname{Rm}+G)\);
- finite per-datum Sobolev and metric-equivalence constants.

It does not provide the transported-coefficient start/restart, nor a quantitative passage from merely small \(\|\operatorname{Ric}+g\|_\infty\) to the assumed \(U\)-control. Those are precisely the pieces connecting the energy induction to the Yau antecedent. This front has broader dimensional potential than Route A, because a dimension-only theorem centered at \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\) would directly furnish admissible constants \(c_n<C_n<0\).

## Most valuable concrete objects

- **For the toric sector:** the complete gauge-fixed linearized Einstein edge problem at \(S^2\times S^2\), not the present equation count; and a certified Rayleigh quotient for the regular right-angled hexagon.
- **For the positive noncollapsed sector:** the balanced-annulus transfer theorem built on the full \(C(S^3/\mathbb Z_2)\) indicial inventory and harmonic-radius control. This is the sharpest theorem-sized bridge already formulated in the supplied files.

The main target drift in Steps 40–41 is therefore not the diagonal identification—it is valid—but the movement from “at most one symmetric completion” and “regular constant-curvature numerical mismatch” toward statements about absence of arbitrary Einstein metrics or resolution of the original pinching proposition.
