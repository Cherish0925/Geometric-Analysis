## Deep-Reasoning

Step 16 overturns the Step 15 conclusion that a fixed critical Ishida–LeBrun manifold has a proved negative Ricci-pinching gap. The failure is mathematical rather than bibliographic. Li’s curvature-energy argument propagates a supremum over every center in its working domain. Centers near the boundary require neighboring balls outside that domain; restricting the supremum to an inner plateau merely restores the uncontrolled inner/outer energy inequality. Ricci flow also has no finite propagation speed, so a buffer much larger than √t does not make transition data irrelevant. A maximal-function estimate at one center controls radius-r balls only through a fixed multiple of r, not a much larger plateau. The fixed-topology gap is therefore withdrawn.

The metric-by-metric inequality
∫(|W⁺|−1/(2√6))² ≤ (7/24)∫|Ric+g|²
remains sound. It converts terminal global L² Einstein-defect control into the calibrated Weyl modulus on any smooth metric on the same critical topology, but supplies no evolution estimate. The actual parabolic problem must include the full support of a=χ². The defect energy has no E-independent source and no intrinsic 1/χ loss because |da|²/a=4|dχ|². Curvature evolution adds ∇²a·E·Rm and ∇a·∇E·Rm. The latter can only be handled by coupling curvature energy to the weighted ∫a|∇E|² dissipation. This leads to local energies Rα and Pα with a whole-support finite-neighbor system; a large combination Rα+κPα is the natural quantity. Yet this system is not a theorem from the present initial data because metric equivalence, evolving relative Sobolev inequalities, the covariant Hessian of χ, and an integrable pointwise estimate for aE depend on one another. Direct reading of Wang confirms this circle: evolving cutoff Hessians and ∇(χ²Rm) are coupled, while the sourced pointwise-curvature result assumes global Sobolev and full-support critical energy. A promising way to break the circle is a Yang-type Moser estimate ||aE(t)||∞ ≤ C_p δ(r²/t)^(2/p), p>2. Its singularity is integrable, and the relative-Sobolev volume factors have the correct four-dimensional cancellation under collapse, but this estimate still must be derived for the normalized cutoff equation on the entire active support.

A substantial downstream simplification was proved. If a positive-volume center set P lies in a uniformly buffered region with |sec|≤K and r<π/(2√K), exponential pullbacks h_p=exp_p* g give noncollapsed radius-r balls even when exp_p is highly noninjective. Rauch comparison and invariance of Liouville measure yield
∫_P avg_{B_r(0),h_p}(F∘exp_p) dp ≤ C(n,K,r)∫F,
where C is the ratio of the hyperbolic and spherical radial model-volume integrals. Sheet number and injectivity radius never enter because geodesic flow retains terminal direction. Thus terminal global nonnegative deficits can select a fixed-radius regular pullback chart without a theorem about CFG rank strata or normal-cover accessibility. Positive-time curvature-derivative estimates, or another sufficiently strong compactness mechanism, are still needed to pass curvature smoothly.

The harmonic-form passage was also repaired. Unique continuation has no quantitative local-mass content and cannot stop normalized harmonic forms from vanishing on selected charts. At the evolved metric one must reconstruct its sign monopole class, harmonic representative, spinor, and rescaled monopole. The scalar–Weyl weight q=√6|W⁺|−s may change sign when only terminal L² defect is known. Setting r=q⁺ repairs this: smooth positive weights with f² approximating r+ε satisfy inf L⁺(f)=∫r², and the exact nonnegative deficit decomposition acquires the favorable term ∫q⁻u²; no derivatives of f occur. Consequently q⁻, harmonic-amplitude error, and lowest-eigenline error are controlled by the terminal defect. A joint density containing |E|², (q⁺−9/2)², harmonic-amplitude error, and ⟨(W⁺+√(2/3)|W⁺|I)H,H⟩ can be fed into the exponential-chart averaging lemma. On a smoothly convergent selected chart it produces a nonzero limiting harmonic form in the extremal lowest eigenline, hence the calibrated spectrum (−1/6,1/12,1/12) needed by the established Step 6 local contradiction.

This reorganizes the fixed-topology program. Collapse classification and unique continuation are no longer necessary downstream prerequisites; the difficult center is now concentrated in one transition-inclusive local-flow theorem that must simultaneously produce a positive-volume bounded-curvature buffer, terminal global L² defect control, metric equivalence, and derivative estimates. Even success there would settle only the fixed critical four-dimensional negative laboratory. Dimension-only constants across changing topology, the unrestricted positive branch, dimensions at least five, and Lean formalization remain far from the full Yau Problem 26 goal. The peer toric positive work supplies useful invariant identities but presently has no reduction from arbitrary metrics to invariant ones.
