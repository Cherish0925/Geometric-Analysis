[Part 2/2 of: The topology of the Eisenstein-Picard modular surface]

Consider the red path and the green path in Figure 5, which give two isotopic curves in $P_{6}$. Moreover, the red path in Figure 5 is the intersection $H_{u} \cap\left[z_{0}, z_{3}, q_{\infty}\right]$, and the green path in Figure 5 is the intersection $N \cap\left[z_{0}, z_{3}, q_{\infty}\right]$. Then we consider neighborhoods $N_{M_{\leqslant u}}\left(P_{6}\right)$ and $N_{N}\left(P_{6}\right)$ of $P_{6}$ in $M_{\leqslant u}$ and $N$ respectively. So $N_{M_{\leqslant u}}\left(P_{6}\right) \cap P_{6}$ and $N_{N}\left(P_{6}\right) \cap P_{6}$ are homeomorphic by homeomorphism between $M_{\leqslant u}$ and $N$. In other words, consider the homeomorphism between $M_{\leqslant u}$ and $N$, we delete the region co-bound by the red and green curves in $P_{6}$. So what we shall do is gluing the two disks in the left sub-figure of Figure 5 co-bounded by the green and cyan arcs (and orange, purple arcs). But in fact, these two disks are glued together into a bigger disk, say $E$, as in the right sub-figure of Figure 5.

The complement of $\operatorname{int}\left(h\left(w_{3}\right) \cup h\left(w_{4}\right) \cup h\left(w_{12}\right)\right.$ ) in a closed neighborhood of $P_{6}$ in $M$ is an orbifold 1-handle $h\left(\left[w_{12}, z_{0}\right]\right)$. That is, gluing a copy of $\mathbb{D}_{1}^{1} \times \mathbb{D}_{2}^{1} \times \mathcal{F}_{6}$ to the 4-orbifold we have got. Where the disk $E$ corresponds to $\mathbb{D}_{1}^{1} \times \mathbb{D}_{2}^{1} \times\left\{c_{6}\right\}$, and $c_{6}$ is the coned point of $\mathcal{F}_{6}$. Moreover, the two orange and purple arcs indicate $\partial \mathbb{D}_{1}^{1} \times \mathbb{D}_{2}^{1}$.

We remark that attaching the orbifold 1-handle $h\left(\left[z_{0}, w_{12}\right]\right)$ needing more carefully, by this we mean that the $\mathbb{Z}_{6}$-coned singular set of $h\left(\left[z_{0}, w_{12}\right]\right)$ must be glued to $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$-coned singular sets of $h\left(w_{4}\right)$ and $h\left(w_{12}\right)$.

Step 3. Attach the product of a pair of pants and $\mathcal{F}_{2}$, namely $h\left(\left[z_{0}, w_{4}, w_{12}\right]\right)$, to get a 4-orbifold with 0-dimensional and 2-dimensional singular sets.

We denote by $\mathbb{C}_{2}$ the $\mathbb{C}$-line fixed by $R$. The intersection of $\mathbb{C}_{2}$ and the fundamental domain $D$ is the green triangle $\left[z_{0}, z_{2}, w_{12}\right]$ in Figure 3 with vertices $z_{1}, z_{2}$ and $w_{12}$. Note that the vertex $P J\left(w_{4}\right)$ lies in the interior of the edge $\left[z_{1}, z_{2}\right]$. Moreover, $P Q^{-1}$ identifies $\left[z_{1}, w_{12}\right]$ and $\left[z_{2}, w_{12}\right]$.

So the quotient space of
$$\left[z_{1}, z_{2}, w_{12}\right]-\left(z_{1} \cup z_{2} \cup w_{12} \cup P J\left(w_{4}\right)\right.$$
in $M$ is a pair of totally geodesic pants $P_{2}$, which is a $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$-coned singular set. Which is similar to the pair of pants $P_{6}$.

The complement of $\operatorname{int}\left(h\left(w_{4}\right) \cup h\left(w_{12}\right) \cup h\left(z_{0}\right)\right)$ in a closed neighborhood of $p_{6}$ in $M$ is the product of a pair of pants and $\mathcal{F}_{2}$, we denote it by $h\left(\left[z_{0}, w_{4}, w_{12}\right]\right)$. Where $P_{2}-\left(h\left(w_{4}\right) \cup h\left(w_{12}\right) \cup h\left(z_{0}\right)\right)$ is the pair of pants here.

We remark that $h\left(\left[z_{0}, w_{4}, w_{12}\right]\right)$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$-coned orbifold. So the singular set of it must be glued to $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$-coned singular sets of $h\left(w_{4}\right), h\left(w_{12}\right)$ and $h\left(z_{0}\right)$.

In other words, we view the union of closed neighborhoods of $\left[z_{0}, w_{4}\right]$, $\left[z_{1}, w_{12}\right]$ and $\left[z_{0}, w_{4}, w_{12}\right]$ as a whole object $h\left(\left[z_{0}, w_{4}, w_{12}\right]\right)$. So $\left[z_{0}, w_{4}\right]$ and $\left[z_{1}, w_{12}\right]$ do not contribute new orbifold 1-handles anymore.

Step 4. Attach two 2-handles
$$h\left(\left[z_{0}, w_{4}, w_{3}, J\left(w_{4}\right)\right]\right) \quad \text { and } \quad h\left(\left[z_{0}, J\left(w_{4}\right), z_{1}, w_{12}\right]\right) .$$

The complement of $\operatorname{int}\left(h\left(w_{4}\right) \cup h\left(w_{12}\right) \cup h\left(z_{0}\right)\right)$ in a closed neighborhood of $\left[z_{0}, w_{4}, w_{3}, J\left(w_{4}\right)\right]$ in $N$ is the product of two 2-disks. So it gives a 2-handle in $N$. Where
$$\left[z_{0}, w_{4}, w_{3}, J\left(w_{4}\right)\right]-\operatorname{int}\left(h\left(w_{4}\right) \cup h\left(w_{12}\right) \cup h\left(z_{0}\right)\right)$$
is the disk $\mathbb{D}_{1}^{2}$ in the 2-handle $\mathbb{D}_{1}^{2} \times \mathbb{D}_{2}^{2}$.
Similarly, the complement of $\operatorname{int}\left(h\left(w_{4}\right) \cup h\left(w_{12}\right) \cup h\left(z_{0}\right)\right)$ in a closed neighborhood of $\left[z_{0}, J\left(w_{4}\right), z_{1}, w_{12}\right]$ in $N$ is the product of two 2-disks, and it gives a 2-handle in $N$. Where
$$\left[z_{0}, J\left(w_{4}\right), z_{1}, w_{12}\right]-\operatorname{int}\left(h\left(w_{4}\right) \cup h\left(w_{12}\right) \cup h\left(z_{0}\right)\right)$$
is the disk $\mathbb{D}_{1}^{2}$ in the 2-handle $\mathbb{D}_{1}^{2} \times \mathbb{D}_{2}^{2}$.
We remark that attaching 2-handles
$$h\left(\left[z_{0}, J\left(w_{4}\right), z_{1}, w_{12}\right) \quad \text { and } \quad h\left(\left[z_{0}, w_{4}, w_{3}, w_{12}\right]\right)\right.$$
must be along two well-chosen curves on the 4-orbifold obtained after Step 3. These curves are hidden in the side-pairing pattern of the fundamental domain $D^{*}$ of $\mathbf{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2} / \mathrm{PU}\left(2,1 ; \mathcal{O}_{3}\right)$.

Step 5. At last we attach a 3-handle
$$h\left(\left[w_{12}, z_{0}, z_{1}, z_{2},\left(w_{3}, w_{4}, J\left(w_{4}\right), P J\left(w_{4}\right)\right)\right]\right) .$$

The complement of $\operatorname{int}\left(h\left(w_{3}\right) \cup h\left(w_{4}\right) \cup h\left(w_{12}\right) \cup h\left(z_{0}\right)\right.$ ) in a closed neighborhood of $\left[w_{12}, z_{0}, z_{1}, z_{2},\left(w_{3}, w_{4}, J\left(w_{4}\right), P J\left(w_{4}\right)\right)\right]$ in $N$ is the product of $\mathbb{D}^{3} \times \mathbb{D}^{1}$. So it gives a 3-handle in $N$. Where
$$\left[w_{12}, z_{0}, z_{1}, z_{2},\left(w_{3}, w_{4}, J\left(w_{4}\right), P J\left(w_{4}\right)\right)\right]-\left(h\left(w_{3}\right) \cup h\left(w_{4}\right) \cup h\left(w_{12}\right) \cup h\left(z_{0}\right)\right)$$
is the disk $\mathbb{D}^{3}$ in the 3-handle.
