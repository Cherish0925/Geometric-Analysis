# Research Report

## Task and source reconstruction

This project asked whether near equality in LeBrun's genuine scalar--Weyl
monopole estimate, on the exact Ishida--LeBrun no-Einstein boundary, forces a
forbidden symplectic/almost-Kähler structure or can be lost through
degeneration. I reconstructed the rescaled Seiberg--Witten proof, the older
conformal proof, the convex-hull equality argument, the Bauer--Furuta sign
construction, and the final Blair/Chern--Weil rigidity step from the four
listed primary sources. No pure \(W_+\) estimate was used.

The detailed result is
`Sessions/S0910A03/workspace/codex_loop/work/quantitative_deficit_analysis.md`.
It contains proofs, exact constants, the Walsh-polarization argument, a
bounded-geometry theorem, and a detailed audit of the Step 3 conclusions.

## Main quantitative result

Let

\[
M=\Bigl(\#_{j=1}^mX_j\Bigr)\#N,\qquad m=2\ \text{or}\ 3,
\]

put \(A=\sum_jc_1^2(X_j)\),
\(D=4m-(2\chi+3\tau)(N)\), and assume the exact boundary \(D=A/3\).
For a metric satisfying

\[
\delta=\|g^{-1}(\operatorname{Ric}+g)\|_\infty,
\]

write

\[
R=\frac1{8\pi^2}\int|\operatorname{Ric}^{\circ}|^2,
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{3\delta^2}{(1-\delta)^2}.
\]

For \(\kappa<1\), the curvature algebra gives

\[
R\le \frac{2A}{3}\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa}.
\]

Among the full Bauer--Furuta sign cube there is a sign class \(a\) with
\((a^+)^2\ge A\). If

\[
\Delta_a=\int(s-\sqrt6|W_+|)^2-72\pi^2(a^+)^2,
\]

then the new explicit bound is

\[
\boxed{\Delta_a\le108\pi^2R
\le72\pi^2A\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa}.}
\]

The same \(108\pi^2R\) bound holds for the average of \(\Delta_a\) over all
signs.

For the optimally rescaled monopole, let

\[
q=-(s-\sqrt6|W_+|),\qquad f=\sqrt q,
\]

let \(H_a\) be the harmonic self-dual representative of \(a^+\), and put
\(\rho=|\Phi|^2\), \(\psi=2\sqrt2\sigma(\Phi)\), and
\(\varphi=3\sqrt2\sigma(\Phi)\). The scalar--Weyl deficit has an exact
decomposition into nonnegative terms. In particular, if \(H\) denotes the
common Hölder gap, \(J_A=\|iF_A^+-2\pi H_a\|_2^2\), and \(P\) is the retained
integrated Weitzenböck slack, then

\[
\Delta_a=18J_A+H+(P+H)\frac{1+\theta}{\theta^2},
\qquad 0<\theta\le1.
\]

Consequently

\[
J_A\le\frac{\Delta_a}{18},\qquad
P\le\frac{\Delta_a}{2},\qquad
H\le\frac{\Delta_a}{3}.
\]

The retained spinor-amplitude, quadratic-map/Kato, Hodge-Weitzenböck, and
lowest-Weyl-eigenvalue deficits are individually bounded by
\(4\Delta_a/27\). In particular,

\[
\int|d\psi|^2,\ \int|d\rho|^2\le\frac{2\Delta_a}{27},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|iF_A^+-2\pi H_a\|_2^2\le\frac{\Delta_a}{18},\qquad
\|f\varphi+6\sqrt2\pi H_a\|_2^2\le\Delta_a.}
\]

At the optimal weight, the two published Hölder applications have the same
numerical gap. A quantitative moment argument additionally gives

\[
\int f^2(|\varphi|-f)^2\le\Delta_a.
\]

Because \(|W_+|\) need not be smooth at its zero set, these assertions are
implemented rigorously using smooth positive weights converging uniformly to
\(\sqrt q\). The bounds hold with an arbitrarily small additional error; they
do not assume a canonical convergent choice of monopoles.

## Algebraic and conformal deficits

The completion square admits a sharper decomposition than the usual prose
invocation of triangle and finite-dimensional Cauchy--Schwarz. If
\(x=\|s\|_2\), \(y=\||W_+|\|_2\), and
\(z=\|s-\sqrt6|W_+|\|_2\), define

\[
D_{\rm tri}=(x+\sqrt6y)^2-z^2,
\qquad
D_{\rm CS}=(x-8\sqrt6y)^2.
\]

Then

\[
\int(s+8\sqrt6|W_+|)^2=D_{\rm CS}+8D_{\rm tri},
\]

and the boundary equations give

\[
D_{\rm CS}\le864\pi^2R,
\qquad D_{\rm tri}\le108\pi^2R.
\]

The conformal-minimization value gap is at most \(\Delta_a\). This does not
control the conformal Euler--Lagrange residual or distance to a minimizer
without an additional coercivity theorem. The detailed note also gives an
additive three-term deficit decomposition for LeBrun's older generalized
Yamabe proof; every term there is at most \(4\Delta_a/9\).

## Consequences for curvature and harmonic forms

The exact completion and pointwise scalar pinching imply

\[
\left\|q-\frac92\right\|_2
\le\frac{3\sqrt6}{2}\pi\sqrt R
+\frac92\delta\sqrt{\operatorname{Vol}(g)}.
\]

Together with the curvature-projection estimate, this proves that
\(|H_a|\) approaches the nonzero constant

\[
c_0=\frac{3}{4\sqrt2\pi}
\]

in \(L^2\). A uniform scale-invariant Sobolev inequality upgrades the spinor
amplitude from \(L^2\) to \(L^4\), but cannot produce a pointwise lower bound
in the critical dimension four.

An exact nonvanishing criterion was proved: if the harmonic representatives
have a uniform Hölder modulus and balls have a uniform \(vr^4\) lower-volume
bound, then \(L^2\) convergence of \(|H_a|\) to \(c_0\) implies
\(\inf|H_a|\ge c_0/2\). To identify the resulting symplectic form's first
Chern class with \(a\), one additionally needs a \(C^0\) upgrade of the
spinor-quadratic-form/harmonic-form comparison. These hypotheses are stated
explicitly in the detailed note; a Sobolev bound alone is not silently used
as compactness.

## Simultaneous sign saturation and Walsh polarization

Let \(x_r\) be the harmonic self-dual representatives of the signed
coordinates \(c_j^+\) and \(E_\ell^+\), and
\(H_\epsilon=\sum_r\epsilon_rx_r\). With

\[
\mathcal L=\frac{q}{6\sqrt2\pi},\qquad
K=\int\mathcal L^2,
\]

the averaged analytic estimates and exact Walsh identities give

\[
\boxed{
\left\|\sum_r|x_r|^2-\mathcal L^2\right\|_1
\le2\sqrt{6KR}+o(1),}
\]

and, for \(p\ne q\),

\[
\boxed{
\|\langle x_p,x_q\rangle\|_1\le\sqrt{6KR}+o(1).}
\]

Thus all-sign near saturation forces spatial \(L^1\) orthogonality, not
collinearity. Collinearity follows only if the lowest eigenspace of \(W_+\)
is a uniformly separated line. Under a gap
\(\lambda_2-\lambda_1\ge\gamma>0\) and \(f\le F\), the total component of
the coordinate forms perpendicular to this line is at most

\[
3R+\frac{F^2}{2\gamma}R+o(1).
\]

Orthogonality plus collinearity forces phase separation: on the good region,
at most one canonical summand carries the harmonic mass at a point, while the
Walsh sum identity requires one summand to carry it. This becomes a
contradiction under \(C^0\) precompactness and harmonic unique continuation,
but not under mere \(L^2\) control. Shrinking transition sets remain a real
degeneration channel.

On a fixed manifold the sign cube is finite, so vanishing average deficit
does force every sign deficit to vanish. For varying topology or growing
\(b_2(N)\), only the average and the selected maximizing sign are uniformly
controlled; a sparse set of bad signs may persist.

## Sharp conditional theorem and degeneration alternative

I proved the following bounded-geometry exclusion statement. On a fixed
Ishida--LeBrun boundary manifold with \(m=2\) or \(3\), no sequence with
\(\delta_i\to0\) can have both a uniform positive
\(C^{2,\alpha}\) harmonic radius and a uniform diameter bound. Under those
explicit hypotheses, Cheeger compactness produces a \(C^{2,\beta}\) limit
with \(\operatorname{Ric}=-g\). The boundary Chern--Gauss--Bonnet identity
and a maximizing sign class force exact mixed-monopole equality. The
published equality theorem then yields a negative Kähler--Einstein metric and
the forbidden symplectic structure, contradicting the connected-sum
topology.

Accordingly, every hypothetical boundary sequence must lose harmonic-radius
or diameter control. At the monopole level it must also defeat at least one of
uniform Hölder/noncollapse control, a lowest-eigenline gap, or elliptic
\(C^0\) control of the quadratic forms. The counterexample-compatible
alternative is phase localization on different macroscopic regions, with a
transition/topology core undergoing anisotropic collapse, Weyl concentration
or eigenvalue degeneracy, or escape down a long end. The standard scalar-flat
ASD neck is incompatible with pointwise \(\delta\to0\), and every smooth
noncollapsed blow-up is Ricci-flat, but these facts do not exclude collapse or
cusp-like escape.

## Audit of the Step 3 note

The Step 3 numerical conclusions were independently rederived and are
correct:

\[
S\to\frac{16A}{27},\qquad W\to\frac{2A}{27},\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{Vol}}{A}\to\frac{32\pi^2}{9},
\]

\[
\frac1{\pi^2A}\int|W_+|^2\to\frac4{27},\qquad
\frac1{\pi^2A}\int|\operatorname{Ric}^\circ|^2\to0,
\]

and

\[
\int|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\to\frac{128\pi^2A}{9}.
\]

Under Ishida--LeBrun's additional exact-\(\mathcal I_r\) hypotheses, the
comparison value \(\mathcal I_r=32\pi^2A/3\) is also correct.

Three qualifications should be absorbed into future use of Step 3. First,
individual sign saturation is uniform only for a fixed finite sign cube.
Second, if \(A\) varies, period-plane and volume statements are normalized,
not absolute. Third, only the analytic proof deficits for a selected sign or
on average have been shown small. The Blair/Chern--Weil quantities are not
defined until compactness supplies a genuine nonvanishing almost-Kähler form,
so that final equality stage cannot be described as quantitatively saturated
without additional assumptions.

## Equality case and the remaining obstruction

Once a genuine equality form exists, the source proof is complete. It is a
constant-length closed self-dual form in the lowest Weyl eigenline; convex
equality aligns the active monopole vertices; and the Blair curvature becomes
harmonic. If

\[
D_{\rm Ric,J}=\int\left(|\operatorname{Ric}^\circ|^2-
\frac{(s^*-s)^2}{16}\right)\ge0,
\]

then Chern--Gauss--Bonnet and Chern--Weil give

\[
\int s^*(s^*-s)=4D_{\rm Ric,J}.
\]

Saturation has \(s^*<0\) and
\(s^*-s=|\nabla\omega|^2\ge0\), so both sides vanish and the metric is
negative Kähler--Einstein. The unresolved issue is therefore not the exact
equality classification but the critical \(L^2\)-to-pointwise compactness
upgrade.

## How Qiushi should use the result

Future work should use the exact deficit identity rather than the assertion
that “all inequalities nearly saturate.” Equations (30)--(37) of the detailed
note are the rigorous analytic input; equations (46)--(48) isolate the
finite-dimensional sign mechanism. The next decisive theorem would show that
the explicit \(L^2\) estimates imply the Hölder and \(C^0\) hypotheses under
weaker geometric assumptions, or else characterize the collapse set where
they fail.

For Yau Problem 26, this rules out the exact Ishida--LeBrun boundary as a
bounded-geometry source of negatively pinched counterexamples. It does not
rule out a collapsing or cusp-like sequence, and it does not address the
positive branch or dimensions above four. Its main advance is to turn the
remaining negative-branch loophole into a precise compactness-versus-phase-
separation problem with explicit quantitative errors.
