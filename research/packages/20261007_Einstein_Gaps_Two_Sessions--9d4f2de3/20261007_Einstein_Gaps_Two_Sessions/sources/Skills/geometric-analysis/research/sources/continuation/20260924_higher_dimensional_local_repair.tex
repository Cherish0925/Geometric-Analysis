\hypertarget{a-dimension-independent-einstein-repair-corollary}{%
\section{A dimension-independent Einstein repair
corollary}\label{a-dimension-independent-einstein-repair-corollary}}

24 September 2026. This is a short extension of
\href{20260924_uniformly_local_repair.md}{the uniformly local repair
theorem}, not a new proof of its weighted inverse. That proof is
dimension-independent once its local harmonic charts are supplied. The
four-dimensional topological mechanism for producing those charts is not
used here.

\hypertarget{uniformly-local-repair-in-dimension-nge3}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{1. Uniformly local repair in dimension
\(n\ge3\)}{1. Uniformly local repair in dimension n\textbackslash ge3}}\label{uniformly-local-repair-in-dimension-nge3}}

Fix \(n\ge3\), \(0<\alpha<1\), \(\rho>0\), \(\Lambda,C_G,\lambda>0\),
and \(d,H\ge0\), with \[
                              H^2<8\lambda.                 \tag{1}
\] A uniformly controlled harmonic atlas means harmonic coordinate
charts centred at every point, with nested coordinate balls of radii
\(\rho\) and \(2\rho\), uniform ellipticity
\(\Lambda^{-1}I\le(g_{ij})\le\Lambda I\), and coefficient
\(C^{2,\alpha}\) bounds at most \(\Lambda\) on the larger balls.
Uniformly local norms are suprema over the smaller charts, not sums. The
constants below depend on these atlas data.

\textbf{Corollary 1.} There exist \(\varepsilon,C>0\), depending only on
the fixed data above, with the following property. Let \((M^n,g)\) be a
closed connected smooth Riemannian manifold with such an atlas and \[
 \operatorname{Vol}_g B_R(x)\le C_G(1+R)^d e^{HR}
       \quad\text{for all }x\in M,\ R\ge0.                  \tag{2}
\] For the full Bianchi-gauged linearization \[
 L_g=\tfrac12\Delta_{L,g}+I,
 \qquad
 \Phi_g(h)=\operatorname{Ric}_h+h+\delta_h^*(B_gh),
 \quad B_gu=\delta_gu+\tfrac12d\operatorname{tr}_gu,
\] assume positive quadratic coercivity on \textbf{all} symmetric
two-tensors: \[
 Q_g(u):=\int_M\langle L_gu,u\rangle\,d\mu_g
                       \ge\lambda\|u\|_{L^2(g)}^2.          \tag{3}
\] If \(E=\operatorname{Ric}_g+g\) satisfies \[
                   \|E\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}<\varepsilon,
\] then there is a smooth metric \(h\) on the same smooth manifold with
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_h=-h,
 \qquad
 \|h-g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}
                   \le C\|E\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}.     \tag{4}
\] Neither orientation, \(\pi_2\), gauge-theoretic classes, nor a
diameter, total-volume or total-\(L^2\)-residual bound is assumed.

\textbf{Proof.} The Lichnerowicz operator has principal part
\(\tfrac12\nabla^*\nabla\) in every dimension. Choose
\(H/2<a<\sqrt{2\lambda}\) and put
\(\phi_x=e^{-a\operatorname{dist}_g(x,\cdot)}\). The weighted identity
\[
 Q_g(\phi_xu)
 =\int_M\phi_x^2\langle L_gu,u\rangle
             +\tfrac12\int_M|d\phi_x|^2|u|^2
\] and (2)--(3) give, exactly as in the preceding theorem, \[
 \|u\|_{L^2(B_1(x))}
 \le \frac{e^a\sqrt{J_a}}{\lambda-a^2/2}\|L_gu\|_\infty,
 \qquad
 J_a=C_Ge^H\sum_{j\ge0}(j+2)^d e^{-(2a-H)j}<\infty.        \tag{5}
\] Local Moser and Schauder estimates in the supplied atlas therefore
yield \[
 \|L_g^{-1}f\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}
                   \le C_I\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}.
\] Fredholm theory and (3) give this inverse on each closed \(M\). Only
the local constants change with \(n\); no dimension enters the
absorption coefficient \(\lambda-a^2/2\) or the growth condition (1).
The uniformly local Hölder quadratic estimate for \(\Phi_g(g+u)-E-L_gu\)
gives the same contraction proof. Smallness makes \(h=g+u\) positive
definite and \(\operatorname{Ric}_h\) negative definite. Finally,
\(b=B_gh\) satisfies \(\nabla_h^*\nabla_hb-\operatorname{Ric}_h(b)=0\);
integration forces \(b=0\). Elliptic bootstrapping gives smoothness.
\(\square\)

If additionally \(\|\nabla\operatorname{Ric}_g\|_\infty\le K_1\), the
atlas gives \(\|E\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}} \le C\delta_-^{1-\alpha}\)
for \(\delta_-\le1\). Thus small pointwise Ricci defect alone suffices
with these same fixed geometric and operator data. An absolute spectral
gap is not (3), and genuine kernels have not been removed.

\hypertarget{actual-large-diameter-examples-in-every-dimension-nge3}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{2. Actual large-diameter examples in every
dimension
\(n\ge3\)}{2. Actual large-diameter examples in every dimension n\textbackslash ge3}}\label{actual-large-diameter-examples-in-every-dimension-nge3}}

Fix a closed hyperbolic \((N^n,h)\), normalized by
\(\operatorname{Ric}_h=-h\), and an epimorphism
\(\pi_1(N)\to\mathbb Z\).
\href{https://math.umd.edu/~jjm/papers/bettinumber.pdf}{Millson's
compact examples} provide such a choice in every \(n\ge3\). Let
\((N_m,h_m)\) be the finite cyclic covers. The fixed infinite cyclic
cover has a proper cocompact \(\mathbb Z\)-action, so counting its
bounded fundamental regions gives \[
 \operatorname{Vol}_{h_m}B_R(x)\le C_N(1+R),
 \qquad \operatorname{Vol}(N_m,h_m)=m\operatorname{Vol}(N,h).
\] Consequently their diameters tend to infinity. This bound concerns
the actual cyclic quotients, not their universal covers or arbitrary
covers.

For sectional curvature \(-1/(n-1)\), the classical Koiso estimate gives
\[
 Q_{h_m}(u)\ge\lambda_n\|u\|_2^2,
 \qquad \lambda_n=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}>0.                     \tag{6}
\] This is \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16692v2}{Jäckel's Lemma
2.4}, rescaled from \(\operatorname{Ric}=-(n-1)g\); its quadratic
estimate, unlike his principal theorem's stated dimensional scope, also
applies in dimension three.

All sufficiently small \(C^3_{\rm uloc}(h_m)\) perturbations \(g_m\)
have uniform harmonic atlas and Ricci-derivative bounds, satisfy (2)
with \(H=0,d=1\), and satisfy (3) with \(\lambda=\lambda_n/2\). The last
assertion follows from the almost-Einstein Koiso estimate and uniform
negative sectional curvature; it is not spectral continuity with a
constant depending on \(m\). Corollary 1 supplies a uniform repair
threshold independent of \(m\).

The residual can genuinely be distributed over all cells. For disjoint
lifts \(\beta_j\) of a fixed bump, equal to one on a smaller ball, take
\(f_m=t\sum_{j=1}^m c_j\beta_j\), \(1\le c_j\le2\), and
\(g_m=e^{2f_m}h_m\). On the smaller balls, \[
 |\operatorname{Ric}_{g_m}+g_m|_{g_m}^2\,d\mu_{g_m}
 =n(e^{2tc_j}-1)^2e^{(n-4)tc_j}\,d\mu_{h_m}.
\] Hence \(\|E_m\|_2\ge c t\sqrt m\) while
\(\|E_m\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm uloc}}\le Ct\), uniformly in \(m\). Small
fixed \(t\) is allowed despite the divergent total residual.

This corollary makes one transferable analytic module explicit. It gives
uniform correction on known hyperbolic smooth types and a general
conditional solver, not new Einstein topologies or a dimension-only
Yau26 threshold. In higher dimensions, the controlled atlas remains an
input; the four-dimensional topological regularity theorem has not been
extended by this argument.
