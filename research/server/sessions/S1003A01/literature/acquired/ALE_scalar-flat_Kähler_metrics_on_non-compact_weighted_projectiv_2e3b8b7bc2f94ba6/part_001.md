\title{
ALE SCALAR-FLAT KÄHLER METRICS ON NON-COMPACT WEIGHTED PROJECTIVE SPACES
}

\author{
VESTISLAV APOSTOLOV AND YANN ROLLIN
}

\begin{abstract}
We construct new explicit toric scalar-flat Kähler ALE metrics with orbifold singularities, which we use to obtain smooth extremal Kähler metrics on appropriate resolutions of orbifolds.
\end{abstract}

\section*{1. Introduction}

In this paper we apply the formalism from [5, 6] in order to generalize the construction of scalar-flat Kähler asymptotically locally euclidean (ALE) metrics, defined on the total space of the vector bundle $\mathcal{O}(-r) \rightarrow \mathbb{C} P^{n}$ (see e.g. [23, 31, 27, 29, 22]) to a family of scalar-flat Kähler non-compact ALE orbifolds, called non-compact weighted projective spaces.

Using the Arezzo-Pacard gluing theory, non-compact weighted projective spaces are then used as building blocks for the construction of new extremal Kähler metrics (in the sense of Calabi) on certain resolutions of a large class of extremal orbifolds. More precisely, we show that extremal Kähler orbifolds with singularities of type $\mathfrak{I}$ admit resolutions endowed with extremal Kähler metrics as well, in suitable Kähler classes (cf. §1.2).
1.1. New scalar-flat ALE Kähler metrics on non-compact weighted projective spaces. Let
$$\mathbf{a}=\left(a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m}\right), \text { with } m \geq 2 \text { and } a_{j}>0,$$
be a $(m+1)$-tuple of positive integers. In the sequel, a shall be referred to as a weight-vector. Non-compact weighted projective spaces are then defined as follows:

Definition A. The non-compact weighted projective space $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m}}^{m}$ associated to the weight-vector $\mathbf{a}$ is the complex orbifold obtained as the quotient of the open set
$$\left\{\left(z_{0}, z_{1}, \ldots, z_{m}\right):\left(z_{1}, \ldots, z_{m}\right) \neq(0, \ldots, 0)\right\} \subset \mathbb{C}^{m+1}$$
by the $\mathbb{C}^{*}$-action
$$\rho_{\mathbf{a}}(\lambda)\left(z_{0}, z_{1}, \ldots, z_{m}\right)=\left(\lambda^{-a_{0}} z_{0}, \lambda^{a_{1}} z_{1}, \ldots, \lambda^{a_{m}} z_{m}\right) .$$

To avoid dealing with orbifold quotients, we shall also assume
$$\operatorname{gcd}\left(a_{0}, \ldots, a_{m}\right)=1$$
which insures that $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m}}^{m}$ is a simply connected orbifold, see e.g. [6, Rem. 2].
Example 1. Note that the total space of the line bundle $\mathcal{O}(-r) \rightarrow \mathbb{C} P^{m-1}$ is isomorphic to the weighted projective space $\mathbb{C} P_{-r, 1, \ldots, 1}^{m}$. The bundle projection $\mathcal{O}(-r) \simeq \mathbb{C} P_{-r, 1, \ldots, 1}^{m} \rightarrow \mathbb{C} P^{m-1}$ is given by
$$\left[z_{0}: z_{1}: \ldots: z_{m}\right] \mapsto\left[z_{1}: \ldots: z_{m}\right]$$
in homogeneous coordinates. Weighted projective spaces have in general orbifold singularities. However, in the particular case when $\mathbf{a}=(r, 1, \ldots, 1)$, it is a smooth complex manifold.

We can now state one of our main results:
Theorem A. For any $m \geq 2$, the orbifold $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m}}^{m}$ admits a scalar-flat Kähler ALE metric $g_{\mathbf{a}}$ with quotient singularity at infinity $\mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{a}}$, where $\Gamma_{\mathbf{a}}$ is the finite cyclic sub-group of $\mathrm{U}(m)$ generated by
$$\gamma=\operatorname{diag}\left(\xi^{a_{1}}, \ldots, \xi^{a_{m}}\right) \subset \mathrm{U}(m),$$
where $\xi$ is a primitive $a_{0}$-th rooth of unity. The metric admits a hamiltonian 2-form of order $\ell$, where $\ell$ is the number of different integers $\left\{a_{j}, j=1, \ldots, m\right\}$, and is Ricci-flat if and only if $a_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{i}$.

In the special case $\mathcal{O}(-r) \simeq \mathbb{C} P_{-r, 1, \ldots, 1}^{m}$, the ALE metrics we get coincide with the well-known Burns-Eguchi-Hanson-LeBrun-Pedersen-Poon-Simanca metrics on the total space of $\mathcal{O}(-r) \rightarrow \mathbb{C} P^{m-1}$, see e.g. [23, 31, 27, 29]. ${ }^{1}$ We review this known construction in Section 2 and present the general case in Section 3.

Another interesting special case is when the complex dimension is 2 (i.e. $m=2$ ). It has been then brought to our attention by J. Viaclovsky [30] that the conformal geometry of the ALE metric on $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, a_{2}}^{2}$ in Theorem A should be related to the Bochner-flat (or anti-self-dual) orbi-metrics on the corresponding compact orbisurface $\mathbb{C} P_{a_{0}, a_{1}, a_{2}}^{2}$, see [15] for the case $a_{0}=r, a_{1}=a_{2}=1$. We show in Section 4 that this is indeed the case:

Theorem B. Let $\mathbb{C} P_{a_{0}, a_{1}, a_{2}}^{2}$ be the compact weighted projective plane of weight $\mathbf{a}=\left(a_{0}, a_{1}, a_{2}\right)$, i.e. the quotient of $\mathbb{C}^{3} \backslash\{0\}$ by the $\mathbb{C}^{*}$-action $\varphi_{\mathbf{a}}(\lambda)\left(z_{0}, z_{1}, z_{2}\right)=$ $\left(\lambda^{a_{0}} z_{0}, \lambda^{a_{1}} z_{1}, \lambda^{a_{2}} z_{2}\right)$, endowed with its Bochner-flat Kähler orbi-metric $\tilde{g}_{\mathbf{a}}$, see [13, 33]. Then, on $\mathbb{C} P_{a_{0}, a_{1}, a_{2}}^{2} \backslash[1,0,0]$, the metric $\tilde{g}_{\mathbf{a}}$ is conformally isometric to the ALE scalar-flat Kähler metric $g_{\mathbf{a}}$ on $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, a_{2}}^{2}$, given by Theorem $A$.
1.2. Resolution of isolated orbifold singularities. ALE spaces appear naturally as 'bubbles' at the boundary of the moduli spaces involved in the Donaldson- Tian-Yau conjecture. Such 'bubbles', as the metrics constructed in Theorem A, can also be used in conjunction with the gluing theory of [12, 11, 10] as an input for constructing new extremal Kähler metrics.

The action of a finite cyclic subgroup $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{U}(m)$ of order $b_{0}>1$ on $\mathbb{C}^{m}$ is generated, up to a conjugation, by
$$\gamma\left(z_{1}, \ldots, z_{m}\right):=\left(\xi^{b_{1}} z_{1}, \ldots, \xi^{b_{m}} z_{m}\right),$$

\footnotetext{
${ }^{1}$ The case $m=2, r=1$ is due to Burns, $m=2, r=2$ to Eguchi-Hanson, $m=2, r>2$ to LeBrun, $m>1, r=1$ to Simanca, and $m>2, r>2$ to Pedersen-Poon and Rollin-Singer.
}
for a $b_{0}$-th primitive root of unity $\xi$. We shall encode the relevant information by introducing the $(m+1)$-tuple of (not necessarily positive) integers $\mathbf{b}=\left(b_{0}, \ldots, b_{m}\right)$ and denoting $\Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ the cyclic group generated by (1.1). The complex orbifold $\mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ has an isolated singularity at the origin if and only if $b_{0}$ is coprime with $b_{j}$ for every $j>0$, i.e.
$$\text { g.c.d }\left(b_{0}, b_{j}\right)=1, \quad \forall j \in\{1, \ldots, m\} .$$
We say that two $(m+1)$-tuples of integers $\mathbf{c}=\left(c_{0}, \ldots, c_{m}\right)$ and $\mathbf{b}=\left(b_{0}, \ldots, b_{m}\right)$ are congruent (and write $\mathbf{b} \sim \mathbf{c}$ ) if
$$b_{0}=c_{0} \text { and } b_{i} \equiv c_{i} \bmod b_{0}, \forall i>0 .$$
The congruence does not change the group $\Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$.
To state our next result, observe that $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m}}^{m}$ has only isolated orbifold singularities if and only if the weight vector $\mathbf{a}=\left(a_{0}, \ldots, a_{m}\right)$ has pairwise coprime components, i.e.
$$\text { g.c.d }\left(a_{i}, a_{j}\right)=1, \quad \forall i \neq j \in\{0, \ldots, m\} .$$
The above condition is obviously stronger than (1.2), i.e. it implies that $\mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{a}}$ has an isolated orbifold singularity as well. Finally, we should point out that the weighted projective space $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m}}^{m}$ is non-singular if and only if $a_{j}=1, \forall j>0$ (see Example 1).

We are now going to introduce the groups $\Gamma_{\mathbf{b}} \subset \mathrm{U}(m)$ of type $\mathfrak{I}$.
Definition B. Let $\mathbf{b}=\left(b_{0}, \ldots, b_{m}\right)$ be a $(m+1)$-tuple of positive integers with $b_{0}>1$, such that $\mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ is an isolated orbifold singularity, i.e. the condition (1.2) holds. We say that $\Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ is of type $\mathfrak{I}$ if either:
\begin{itemize}
\item[-] b is congruent to $\mathbf{a}=\left(b_{0}, 1, \ldots, 1\right)$,
\item[-] or b is congruent to a weight vector a $=\left(a_{0}, \ldots, a_{m}\right)$ such that a satisfies the condition (1.3), and all the isolated orbifold singularities of $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots a_{m}}^{m}$ are of type $\mathfrak{I}$, in the sense that they are all modelled on quotients of $\mathbb{C}^{m}$ by a group of type $\mathfrak{I}$.
\end{itemize}

In practice, in order to check whether $\mathbf{b}$ is of type $\mathfrak{I}$ one can use an inductive process in which, at each stage either (1.3) will fail, or we have to check the above definition for weight vectors $\tilde{\mathbf{b}}=\left(\tilde{b}_{0}, \ldots, \tilde{b}_{m}\right)$ such that $\tilde{\mathbf{b}} \neq \mathbf{b}$ and $\left|\tilde{b}_{i}\right| \leq\left|b_{i}\right|$. We illustrate this by the following examples.

Example 2. Beside the obvious case $\mathbf{b}=\left(b_{0}, 1, \ldots, 1\right)$, one can easily check that the following weight vectors
\begin{itemize}
\item[] $\mathbf{- ~ b}=(5,3,2,1)$;
\item[-] $\mathbf{b}=(q, p, 1, \ldots, 1)$, where $q$ and $p$ are two positive coprime integers
\end{itemize}
define cyclic groups $\Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ of type $\mathfrak{I}$.
In the first case, $\mathbb{C} P_{-5,3,2,1}^{3}$ has 2 isolated orbifold singularities, modelled on the groups $\Gamma_{\mathbf{c}}$ with c being one of the 4-ples (2, -5, 3, 1) ~ (2, 1, 1, 1) or (3, -5, 2, 1) ~ $(3,1,2,1)$. The first one is obviously of type $\mathfrak{I}$, whereas $\mathbf{a}=(3,1,2,1)$ satisfies condition (1.3) and the only isolated orbifold singularity of $\mathbb{C} P_{-3,2,1,1}^{3}$ is modelled on the cyclic group $\Gamma_{\mathbf{c}}$ with $\mathbf{c}=(2,-3,1,1) \sim(2,1,1,1)$ so we conclude $\Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ is of type $\mathfrak{I}$.

A similar inductive procedure shows that the weight vector $\mathbf{b}=(q, p, 1, \ldots, 1)$, where $q$ and $p$ are two positive coprime integers is of type $\mathfrak{I}$. Indeed, at each $n$ th stage we have to consider an isolated singularity modelled on a weight vector ( $q_{n}, p_{n}, 1, \ldots, 1$ ) with $q_{n}, p_{n}$ being coprime positive integers (so that (1.3) holds) obtained by performing the Euclide algorithm with overestimated remainder, starting with $q_{0}=q, p_{0}=p$. Thus, $p_{n}<p_{n-1}$ so we will eventually obtain a weight vector of the form $\left(q_{n}, 1, \ldots, 1\right)$.

The quotients $\mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma$ by a group of type $\mathfrak{I}$ are called singularities of type $\mathfrak{I}$. Similarly, a complex orbifold $\mathcal{X}$ with singularities isomorphic to a neighbourhood of singularities of type $\mathfrak{I}$, will be called an orbifold with singularities of type $\mathfrak{I}$. Singularities of type $\mathfrak{I}$ always admit particular resolutions, as suggested by the above definition. Indeed, if $\Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ is of type $\mathfrak{I}$, then there exists a congruent to $\mathbf{b}$, such that $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m}}$ has only singularities of type $\mathfrak{I}$. In addition, there is a canonical projection (see Section 5)
$$q: \mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m}}^{m} \rightarrow \mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{a}} .$$
Thus, $\mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{a}}=\mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ and the above projection can be used to glue a non-compact weighted projective space onto the singularity. By definition, the orbifold singularities of $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m}}^{m}$ are all of type $\mathfrak{I}$. So we can glue further weighted projective spaces of non-compact type onto these singularities. After a finite number of steps, this procedure stops and we have to glue a smooth non-compact weighted projective space $\mathbb{C} P_{-r, 1, \ldots, 1}^{m}$. This construction provides a smooth complex resolution
$$\mathcal{Y} \rightarrow \mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{b}} .$$
referred to as a resolution of type $\mathfrak{I}$ (cf. §5.1 for more details). Notice that resolutions of type $\mathfrak{I}$ need not to be unique.

The above construction of local resolutions readily extends to provide a (generally non unique) resolution
$$\widehat{\mathcal{X}} \rightarrow \mathcal{X}$$
for any orbifold $\mathcal{X}$ with singularities of type $\mathfrak{I}$. We shall refer to such a resolution as a resolution of type $\mathfrak{I}$. This leads to one of our main results:

Theorem C. Suppose $(\mathcal{X}, \omega)$ is a compact orbifold with only isolated singularities of type $\mathfrak{I}$, endowed with an extremal Kähler metric.

Then every complex resolution $\widehat{\mathcal{X}} \rightarrow \mathcal{X}$ of type $\mathfrak{I}$ admits Kähler extremal metrics in suitable Kähler classes.

\subsection*{1.3. Remarks and Applications.}
1.3.1. Characterization of singularities of type $\mathfrak{I}$. Definition B does not provide an immediate list of all $\mathfrak{I}$ singularities. Thus, it would be nice to obtain a criterion, perhaps in terms of toric geometry, to determine which singularities $\mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ are of type $\mathfrak{I}$. We did not succeed solving this arithmetic problem so far, but it looks like an interesting problem motivated by Theorem C.

In order to check that a singularity is of type $\mathfrak{I}$, we proceed by finding an explicit resolution like in (1.4). In dimension 2 , the quotients of the form $\mathbb{C}^{2} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ with $\mathbf{b}=(q, p, 1)$ where $p$ and $q$ are two coprime integers are all of type $\mathfrak{I}$. Their resolution is the well-known Hirzebruch-Jung resolution (as noticed in Example 2).

Remark 3. In the two dimensional case, Calderbank-Singer [14] constructed directly ALE scalar-flat Kähler metrics on the Hirzebruch-Jung resolution of $\mathbb{C}^{2} / \Gamma_{q, p, 1}$. The Calderbank-Singer metrics together with the relevant gluing theory were used in [29, 32] in order to prove a version of Theorem C for complex surfaces. Thus, Theorem C provides a generalization of these results in higher dimensions. \(\square\)
1.3.2. New examples of extremal Kähler manifolds. Weighted projective spaces of compact type $\mathbb{C} P_{a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m}}^{m}$ admit extremal Kähler metrics by [13, 33]. Hence, Theorem C applies to every weighted projective space of compact type with singularities of type $\mathfrak{I}$. In view of Example 2,
\begin{itemize}
\item[-] $\mathbb{C} P_{5,3,2,1}^{3}$;
\item[-] $\mathbb{C} P_{q, p, 1, \ldots, 1}^{m}$, where $q$ and $p$ are two positive coprime integers
\end{itemize}
are such compact orbifolds, thus they admit resolutions of type $\mathfrak{I}$, endowed with extremal Kähler metrics.

Quotients are another common way to introduce various families of extremal Kähler orbifold with isolated singularities. Unfortunately, it is generally not easy to tell whether the singularities are of type $\mathfrak{I}$. For instance one can consider the action of $\Gamma=\Gamma_{q, p, 1,1}$ on $\mathbb{C}^{3}$ for $q>p>1$ coprime. This action extends as a holomorphic action to the compactification $\mathbb{C} P^{1} \times \mathbb{C} P^{1} \times \mathbb{C} P^{1}$. The quotient $\left(\mathbb{C} P^{1}\right)^{3} / \Gamma$ has 8 isolated singularities. Many of which are of type $\mathfrak{I}$. Yet, one of the singularities is modelled on $\mathbb{C}^{3} / \Gamma_{q, p, 1, q-1}$. It is not clear whether this singularity is of type $\mathfrak{I}$ in general, which brings us back to the question raised in §1.3.1.
1.3.3. Toward smooth scalar-flat ALE metrics. It seems reasonable to expects a version of Theorem C adapted to ALE spaces endowed with scalar-flat Kähler metrics. This should be straightforward when the work in progress of Arezzo-Lena [10] is completed. Their idea is to generalize the Arezzo-Pacard gluing theory to the case of (non-compact) scalar-flat ALE orbifolds.

Once such a gluing theory is available, we could prove that the total space $\mathcal{Y}$ of any resolution $\mathcal{Y} \rightarrow \mathbb{C}^{m} / \Gamma_{\mathbf{b}}$ of type $\mathfrak{I}$, introduced at (1.4), carries a scalar-flat Kähler ALE metric.

\section*{2. ALE SCALAR-FLAT KäHLER METRICS ON $\mathcal{O}(-r)$}

We recall in this section the higher dimensional extension, found by Simanca [31], Pedersen-Poon [27], and Rollin-Singer [29], of the Burns-Eguchi-Hanson-LeBrun Kähler scalar-flat ALE metrics [21,23] on the total space of $\mathcal{O}(-r) \rightarrow \mathbb{C} P^{1}$ (for $r=2$ the metric is hyper-Kähler and therefore Ricci-flat). The general setting is described in [22], but we follow the notation of [5] as warm-up for the general case which we present in the next section. The Kähler metric is taken of the following form, known as the Calabi anstaz:
$$\begin{aligned}
g & =\xi \check{g}_{F S}+\frac{d \xi^{2}}{\Theta(\xi)}+\Theta(\xi) \theta^{2} \\
\omega & =\xi \check{\omega}_{F S}+d \xi \wedge \theta, \quad d \theta=\check{\omega}_{F S}
\end{aligned}$$
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item[-] $\check{\omega}_{F S}$ is the Fubini-Study metric on $\mathbb{C} P^{n}$ of scalar curvature $\check{s}_{F S}=\frac{2 n(n+1)}{r}$ and, by slightly abusing the notation, we denote with the same symbols its pull-back to the total space of $\mathcal{O}(-r)$ with $r \in \mathbb{N}$,
\end{itemize}
\begin{itemize}
\item[-] $\Theta(\xi)$ is a positive smooth function (called profile function), defined on the interval $(\alpha, \infty)$ for some $\alpha>0$, and satisfying
$$\Theta(\alpha)=0, \quad \Theta^{\prime}(\alpha)=2 .$$
For any such profile function $\Theta(\xi)$, the corresponding metric is well-defined on the total space $\stackrel{\circ}{M}$ of a principal $\mathbb{C}^{*}$-bundle of degree $r$ over $\mathbb{C} P^{n}$ (note that our normalization is that $\left[\check{\omega}_{F S}\right]=r \gamma$ where $\gamma$ is the generator of $\left.H^{2}\left(\mathbb{C} P^{n}, \mathbb{Z}\right) \cong \mathbb{Z}\right)$, and the boundary conditions (3.16) imply that the metric extends smoothly at infinity (see e.g. [22, 6]), or, equivalently, that the metric is defined on the total space $M$ of the vector bundle $\mathcal{O}(-r) \rightarrow \mathbb{C} P^{n}$. The completeness of the metric is studied in [22], and it is shown that it is equivalent to the growth of $\Theta(\xi)$ at $\infty$ being at most quadratic in $\xi$. It follows from (2.1) that
$$J d \xi=\Theta(\xi) \theta$$
a relation we shall use below. Note also that a geometric meaning of the variable $\xi$ will be given in (2.8).
The formula for the scalar curvature is [5,22]
$$S c a l_{g}=\frac{\check{s}_{F S}}{\xi}-\frac{1}{\xi^{n}}\left(\xi^{n} \Theta(\xi)\right)^{\prime \prime},$$
where, we recall, our normalization is $\check{s}_{F S}=\frac{2 n(n+1)}{r}$. Putting
$$\Theta(\xi):=\frac{1}{\xi^{n}}\left(\frac{2}{r} \xi^{n+1}+a_{1} \xi+a_{0}\right),$$
we obtain a two-parameter family of formal scalar-flat solutions. The boundary conditions (3.16) imply
$$a_{0}=\frac{2(n-r) \alpha^{n+1}}{r}, \quad a_{1}=\frac{2(r-n-1) \alpha^{n}}{r},$$
for some positive parameter $\alpha>0$. This determines a one-parameter family of tensors $g_{\alpha}$ on $\mathcal{O}(-r)$ but we note that they are all homothetic: indeed, the tensor $\lambda g_{\alpha}$ can be written in the form (2.1) by substituting $\xi$ with $\lambda \xi$, and $\Theta(\xi)$ with $\Theta(\lambda \xi) / \lambda$, respectively (this introduces the change of the parameter $\alpha$ with $\alpha / \lambda$ ). We also note that by the results in [5], for $\alpha=0$ we get a flat Kähler metric $g_{0}$ defined on the total space of the principal $\mathbb{C}^{*}$ bundle of degree $-r$ over $\mathbb{C} P^{n}$.
As the growth of $\Theta(\xi)$ at infinity is linear in $\xi, g_{\alpha}$ are complete at infinity [22], so the only verification to carry out is that the defining polynomial
$$P_{\alpha}(x):=\frac{r}{2} x^{n} \Theta(x)=x^{n+1}+(r-n-1) \alpha^{n} x+(n-r) \alpha^{n+1}$$
has no real roots on the interval $(\alpha, \infty)$, which in turn assures that $g_{\alpha}$ is positivedefinite. Factoring out $(x-\alpha)$, we have that
$$P_{\alpha}(x) /(x-\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} x^{n-i} \alpha^{i}+(r-n) \alpha^{n}$$
is strictly positive on $(\alpha, \infty)$ for $\alpha>0$ and $r \in \mathbb{N}$, thus showing that the $g_{\alpha}$ defines a scalar-flat, complete Kähler metric on $M=\mathcal{O}(-r)$.
We should next determine the asymptotic of $g_{\alpha}$ at $\infty$. To this end we need to express the metrics (2.1) in a suitable complex chart at infinity. We first notice that the description (2.1) is adapted, at each fibre, to the symplectic point of view of
\end{itemize}
[20], where the symplectic form $\omega$ is fixed, and the equivariant compatible complex structures are parametrized by the profile functions $\Theta(\xi)$. Making a fibre-wise Legendre transform allows one to recover the usual parametrization in terms of relative Kähler potentials as follows: For a given profile function $\Theta(\xi)$, let $u(\xi)$ be a function satisfying $u^{\prime \prime}(\xi)=1 / \Theta(\xi)$ and put $y:=u^{\prime}(\xi)$. Then, by (2.3),
$$d^{c} y=J d y=u^{\prime \prime}(\xi) J d \xi=\frac{J d \xi}{\Theta(\xi)}=\theta$$
showing that $d d^{c} y=\check{\omega}_{F S}$ (see (2.1)), where the $d^{c}$ operator is taken with respect to the complex structure corresponding to $\Theta(\xi)$. This shows that if $\beta$ is (the pullback to $M$ of) a local 1-form on $\mathbb{C} P^{n}$ such that $\theta=d t+\beta$, where $t: \stackrel{\circ}{M} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is locally defined (up to an additive constant) on each fibre, then $\exp (y+i t)$ gives a $\mathbb{C}^{*}$-equivariant holomorphic coordinate on the fibres of $\stackrel{\circ}{M}$.

Furthermore, letting $F(y):=-u(\xi)+y \xi$, it is easily checked (using again (2.3)) that
$$\begin{aligned}
d d^{c} F(y) & =d J\left(-u^{\prime}(\xi) d \xi+y d \xi+\xi d y\right) \\
& =d\left(\xi u^{\prime \prime}(\xi) J d \xi\right)=d\left(\frac{\xi J d \xi}{\Theta(\xi)}\right) \\
& =d(\xi \theta)=d \xi \wedge \theta+\xi d \theta=\omega
\end{aligned}$$
This calculation shows that a Kähler potential for the metric (2.1) is given by $F(y)=H(\xi)$, where
$$\begin{aligned}
H(\xi) & =\int^{\xi} \frac{x}{\Theta(x)} d x \\
& =\frac{r}{2} \int^{\xi} \frac{x^{n+1} d x}{x^{n+1}+(r-n-1) \alpha^{n} x+(n-r) \alpha^{n+1}}
\end{aligned}$$

These local observations can be adapted to our geometric situation as follows: Let $h$ be the natural hermitian metric on the tautological line bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1) \rightarrow \mathbb{C} P^{n}$, induced by the Fubini-Study metric $\check{g}_{F S}$ of the base. We denote also by $h$ the corresponding hermitian metric on the line bundle $M=\mathcal{O}(-r) \rightarrow \mathbb{C} P^{n}$ and let $y_{0}=\log |\cdot|_{h}$ be the positive smooth function defined on
$$\stackrel{\circ}{M}=\mathcal{O}(-r) \backslash S_{0},$$
where $S_{0}$ stands for the zero section. One thus has
$$d d^{c} y_{0}=\check{\omega}_{F S},$$
where $d^{c}$ is taken with respect to the standard complex structure $J_{0}$ on the total space $M=\mathcal{O}(-r)$. Sending $y$ to $y_{0}$ introduces a $\mathbb{C}^{*}$-equivariant bundle preserving diffeomorphism on $\stackrel{\circ}{M}$, identifying the complex structure $J$ corresponding to $\Theta$ with the standard complex structure $J_{0}$ on $M \subset M$. This diffeomorphism does not preserve the symplectic form $\omega$. Instead, according to (2.6), it sends $\omega$ to a Kähler form in the same Kähler class on $\left(\stackrel{\circ}{M}, J_{0}\right)$, written as $d d^{c} F\left(y_{0}\right)$, where $d^{c}$ is taken with respect to $J_{0}$ and $F(y)=H(\xi)$ is the function determined in terms of the profile function $\Theta$ by (2.7).

Consider a Kähler metric $g_{0}$ of the form (2.1) with profile function $\Theta_{0}(\xi):=\frac{2}{r} \xi$. This corresponds to letting $\alpha=0$ in our construction, and thus the metric $g_{0}$ is
flat as already observed. The corresponding function $y_{0}(\xi)$ is then defined by the equation $y_{0}^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\Theta_{0}(\xi)}=\frac{r}{2 \xi}$, so that we can take
$$y_{0}(\xi):=\frac{r}{2} \log (\xi) .$$
This shows that
$$F_{0}\left(y_{0}\right)=H_{0}(\xi)=\frac{r}{2} \xi=\frac{r}{2} \exp \left(\frac{2}{r} y_{0}\right),$$
or, equivalently, $F_{0}\left(y_{0}\right)=\frac{r}{2} \exp \left(\frac{2}{r} y_{0}\right)$ is a Kähler potential of a flat Kähler metric defined on $\left(\stackrel{\circ}{M}, J_{0}\right)$.

There is an orbifolds holomorphic map
$$q: M=\mathcal{O}(-r) \rightarrow \mathbb{C}^{n+1} / \Gamma_{r},$$
where $\Gamma_{r} \subset \mathrm{U}(n+1)$ is the cyclic group of order $r$, generated by $\varepsilon_{r} \mathrm{Id}$ for a primitive $r$-th root of unity $\varepsilon_{r}$, defined as follows. Let $\left[x_{0}: \cdots: x_{n}\right]$ be a point on $\mathbb{C} P^{n}$ written in homogeneous coordinates and denote by $\left(x_{0}, \ldots, x_{n}\right)^{\otimes r}$ the corresponding generator of the fibre of $\mathcal{O}(-r) \rightarrow \mathbb{C} P^{n}$. Then, for $x=\left(\left[x_{0}: \cdots: x_{n}\right] ; \lambda\left(x_{0}, \ldots, x_{n}\right)^{\otimes r}\right) \in$ $M$,
$$q(x):=\lambda^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(x_{0}, \ldots, x_{n}\right) .$$
The map $q$ provides a biholomorphism between $\stackrel{\circ}{M}=\mathcal{O}(-r) \backslash S_{0}$ and $\left(\mathbb{C}^{n+1} \backslash\{0\}\right) / \Gamma_{r}$, with inverse
$$q^{-1}\left(z_{0}, \ldots z_{n}\right)=\left(\left[z_{0}: \cdots: z_{n}\right] ;\left(z_{0}, \ldots, z_{n}\right)^{\otimes r}\right) .$$
Note that $q$ contracts the zero section $S_{0}$ of $M$ to the origin of $\mathbb{C}^{n+1} / \Gamma_{r}$, so it can be thought as an orbifold blow-down map. Under the identification of $\stackrel{\circ}{M}$ with $\left(\mathbb{C}^{n+1} \backslash\{0\}\right) / \mathbb{Z}_{r}$ via $q$, the function $y_{0}:=\log |\cdot|_{h}$ becomes
$$y_{0} \circ q^{-1}=\frac{r}{2} \log \left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left|z_{i}\right|^{2}\right),$$
so that
$$\frac{r}{2} \xi \circ q^{-1}=F_{0}\left(y_{0} \circ q^{-1}\right)=\frac{r}{2} \sum_{i=0}^{n}\left|z_{i}\right|^{2}=\frac{r}{2}\|z\|^{2}$$
showing that $g_{0}$ pulls back to $r$-times the standard flat metric on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$. We can therefore use $q^{-1}$ to examine the asymptotic behaviour of the metric $g_{\alpha}$ for any $\alpha>0$. Recall that, up to homothety, we can assume $0<\alpha<1$. From the above discussion and [5, Thm. 1], the function $y_{\alpha}(\xi)$ and the Kähler potential $F_{\alpha}\left(y_{\alpha}\right)$ corresponding to the $\Theta(\xi)$ can be expressed as functions of $\xi$ as follows
$$\begin{aligned}
y_{\alpha}(\xi) & =\int_{1}^{\xi} \frac{d x}{\Theta(x)}=\frac{r}{2} \int_{1}^{\xi} \frac{x^{n} d x}{x^{n+1}+(r-n-1) \alpha^{n} x+(n-r) \alpha^{n+1}} \\
F_{\alpha}\left(y_{\alpha}\right) & =\int_{1}^{\xi} \frac{x}{\Theta(x)} d x=\frac{r}{2} \int_{1}^{\xi} \frac{x^{n+1} d x}{x^{n+1}+(r-n-1) \alpha^{n} x+(n-r) \alpha^{n+1}} .
\end{aligned}$$
As $y_{\alpha}(\xi)$ is strictly monotone on $(\alpha, \infty)$, we can define a sooth function
$$\varphi(\xi):=y_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(y_{0}(\xi)\right)=M \xi+O(1),$$
where $M$ is a positive real constant. Using
$$\begin{aligned}
y_{0}(\xi) & =\frac{r}{2} \log \xi=\frac{r}{2} \int_{1}^{\xi} \frac{x^{n} d x}{x^{n+1}} \\
& =y_{\alpha}(\varphi(\xi))=\frac{r}{2} \int_{1}^{\varphi(\xi)} \frac{x^{n} d x}{x^{n+1}+(r-n-1) \alpha^{n} x+(n-r) \alpha^{n+1}} \\
& =\frac{r}{2} \log \varphi(\xi)-\frac{r}{2} \int_{1}^{\varphi(\xi)} \frac{(r-n-1) \alpha^{n} x+(n-r) \alpha^{n+1}}{x\left(x^{n+1}+(r-n-1) \alpha^{n} x+(n-r) \alpha^{n+1}\right)} d x
\end{aligned}$$
back in (2.10), one finds
$$\begin{aligned}
\varphi(\xi) & =\xi \exp \left(\int_{1}^{\varphi(\xi)} \frac{(r-n-1) \alpha^{n} x+(n-r) \alpha^{n+1}}{x\left(x^{n+1}+(r-n-1) \alpha^{n} x+(n-r) \alpha^{n+1}\right)} d x\right) \\
& =\xi-\frac{(r-n-1)}{n} \alpha^{n} \xi^{1-n}+O\left(\xi^{-n}\right)
\end{aligned}$$
Note that, by (2.8) and (2.9), $F_{\alpha}(\xi)-F_{0}(\xi)=\frac{r(r-n-1)}{2(n-1)} \alpha^{n} \xi^{1-n}+O\left(\xi^{-n}\right)$ when $n>1$ whereas $F_{\alpha}(\xi)-F_{0}(\xi)=\frac{r(2-r)}{2} \alpha^{2} \log \xi+O\left(\xi^{-1}\right)$ when $n=1$. It then follows (using (2.11)) that
$$\begin{aligned}
\left(q^{-1}\right)^{*} \omega_{\alpha} & =d d^{c} F_{\alpha}\left(y_{0} \circ q^{-1}\right)=d d^{c} F_{\alpha}\left(y_{\alpha}\left(\varphi\left(\xi \circ q^{-1}\right)\right)\right. \\
& =d d^{c}\left(\left(F_{\alpha}(\varphi(\xi))-F_{0}(\varphi(\xi))+F_{0}(\varphi(\xi))\right) \circ q^{-1}\right) \\
& =d d^{c}\left(\frac{r}{2}\|z\|^{2}+A\|z\|^{2-2 n}+O\left(\|z\|^{1-2 n}\right)\right)
\end{aligned}$$
when $n \geq 2$, and
$$\left(q^{-1}\right)^{*} \omega_{\alpha}=d d^{c}\left(\frac{r}{2}\|z\|^{2}+A \log \|z\|+O\left(\|z\|^{-1}\right)\right),$$
when $n=1$, which are precisely the estimates required to apply the theory of Arezzo-Pacard [12] (recall, $m=n+1$ in our notation). Our argument also shows that the constant $A$ vanishes iff $r=m$, which is constant with the condition that the metric is Ricci-flat.

\section*{3. The general case: Proof of Theorem A}

We shall prove Theorem A in three main steps, roughly corresponding to Sections 3.1, 3.2 and 3.3 below. In Sect. 3.1, we define a family of scalar-flat Kähler metrics $\left(g_{\mathbf{a}}, \omega_{\mathbf{a}}\right)$, depending on $(\ell+1)$ positive integers $\mathbf{a}=\left(a_{0}, \ldots, a_{\ell}\right)(\ell \geq 1)$ with $a_{1}<\cdots<a_{\ell}$, and $\ell$ non-negative integers $n_{1}, \ldots, n_{\ell}$. We follow closely the theory of hamiltonian 2-forms from [5, 6] in order to define the metric on a (non-compact) $2 m$-dimensional manifold $M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}$, which is a principal $\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{\ell}$-bundle over $S=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell} \mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}$ (we allow some or all of the factors of $S$ to be points). In Sect. 3.2, we use the toric formalism of [1] in order to study how the metric closes on a bigger manifold, $M_{\mathbf{a}}$, which contains $M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}$ as a dense subset. In fact, we show that $M_{\mathbf{a}}=\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{\ell}, \ldots, a_{\ell}}^{m}$, where each $a_{j}(j=1, \ldots, \ell)$ is counted $\left(n_{j}+1\right)$-times. In Sect. 3.3, we return again to the setting of Sect. 3.1 in order to construct a complex chart at infinity, and obtain in this chart a suitable asymptotic expansion for a Kähler potential of the metric, in terms of the Euclidean norm. This will allow us to conclude that the scalar-flat Kähler metrics we construct are ALE.
3.1. Definition and local properties of the metric. We introduce in this subsection an ansatz from [5] which describes a family of scalar-flat Kähler metrics of complex dimension $m$, admitting a Hamiltonian 2-form of order $\ell$ with $1 \leq \ell \leq m$. The case $\ell=1$ is the the Calabi ansatz that has been already introduced for the construction in the previous section. The specific choice of constants in the construction, which may appear somewhat mysterious to the Reader, is made in order to achieve the conclusion of Proposition 6 below.

We first start by introducing the smooth manifold on which the metric will be defined.

Definition 4. Let $\ell \geq 1$ be a positive integer, $\left(n_{1}, \ldots, n_{\ell}\right)$ an $\ell$-plet of non-negative integers, and $\mathbf{a}=\left(a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{\ell}\right)$ an $(\ell+1)$-plet of positive integers satisfying $a_{1}<$ $\cdots<a_{\ell}$. Consider the manifold $S=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell} \mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}$. In the case when some (or all) $n_{j}=0$, the corresponding factor is taken to be a point, so that $S$ is a smooth complex manifold of dimension $\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} n_{j} \geq 0$. Let $\pi: P_{\mathbf{a}} \rightarrow S$ denote the total space of the principal $T^{\ell}$-bundle over $S$ with Chern class
$$c_{1}\left(P_{\mathbf{a}}\right)=\left(\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{1}}\right) \gamma_{1}, \ldots,\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{\ell}}\right) \gamma_{\ell}\right) \in H^{2}(S, \mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathbb{R}^{\ell},$$
where $\gamma_{i}$ is the ample generator of $H^{2}\left(\mathbb{C} P_{j}^{n}, \mathbb{Z}\right)$, viewed also as a homology class on $S$ by the obvious pull-back. When $n_{j}=0$, we take $\gamma_{j}=0$.

For each $n_{j}>0$, we equip $\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}$ with a Fubini-Study metric ( $g_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}, \omega_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}$ ) of scalar curvature $\frac{2 n_{j}\left(n_{j}+1\right)}{r_{j}}$, where the positive real constants $r_{j}, j=1, \ldots, \ell$ are defined by (a choice that will be explained later)
$$r_{j}=(-1)^{\ell-j}\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{j}}\right)^{\ell-2} a_{0} \prod_{k \neq j}\left(a_{j}-a_{k}\right),$$
and where the product in the r.h.s. is over indices $k, j \in\{1, \ldots, \ell\}$. We then consider on $P_{\mathbf{a}}$ the $\mathbb{R}^{\ell}$-valued connection 1-form $\tilde{\theta}=\left(\tilde{\theta}_{1}, \ldots, \tilde{\theta}_{\ell}\right)$ defined by
$$d \tilde{\theta}_{j}=\frac{1}{r_{j}}\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{j}}\right) \pi^{*} \omega_{\mathbb{C} P^{n} j}$$
for any $j$ with $n_{j} \geq 1$, and $d \tilde{\theta}_{j}=0$ when $n_{j}=0$. To simplify notation, in what follows we shall tacitly assume $g_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}$ and $\omega_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}$ being zero for each $n_{j}=0$, so that (3.26) holds for each $j=1, \ldots, \ell$.

Let $\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\cdots<\alpha_{\ell}<0$ be the real numbers defined in terms of $\mathbf{a}=$ $\left(a_{0}, \ldots, a_{\ell}\right)$ by setting
$$\alpha_{j}:=-\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{j}}, \quad j=1, \ldots, \ell$$
It is then easily seen that in therms of the $\alpha_{j}$ 's,
$$r_{j}=\frac{(-1)^{\ell-j}}{\prod_{k \neq j}\left(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}\right)},$$
where the product is over indices $k, j \in\{1, \ldots, \ell\}$.
The general ansatz from [5] produces a scalar-flat Kähler metric $\left(g_{\mathbf{a}}, \omega_{\mathbf{a}}\right)$ on the smooth $2 m$-dimensional manifold
$$M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}:=\stackrel{\circ}{\Xi}_{\ell} \times P_{\mathbf{a}},$$
where $m=\ell+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} n_{j}, P_{\mathbf{a}}$ is the principal $T^{\ell}$ bundle over $S$ introduced in Definition 4, and
$$\stackrel{\circ}{\Xi}_{\ell}:=\left(\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}\right) \times \cdots \times\left(\alpha_{\ell-1}, \alpha_{\ell}\right) \times(0, \infty) .$$
We shall still denote by $\pi: M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0} \rightarrow S$ the projection from $D_{\ell} \times P_{\mathbf{a}}$ to $S$, which acts trivially on the first factor and equals to the bundle projection $\pi: P_{\mathbf{a}} \rightarrow S$ on the second.

The Kähler metric is defined in terms of the degree $m$ polynomial
$$p(x)=2 \prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(x-\alpha_{j}\right)^{1+n_{j}}$$
which we shall refer to as the characteristic polynomial. Denote by
$$\Theta(x)=2 \prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(x-\alpha_{j}\right)$$
the minimal polynomial of $p(x)$. We then consider the Kähler metric $\left(g_{\mathbf{a}}, \omega_{\mathbf{a}}\right)$ on $M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}$ defined as follows:
$$\begin{aligned}
g_{\mathbf{a}}= & \sum_{r=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{\ell-r+1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\alpha_{r}-\xi_{j}\right)\right) \pi^{*} g_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{r}}} \\
& +\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{p\left(\xi_{j}\right) \Delta_{j}}{\Theta\left(\xi_{j}\right) F_{j}\left(\xi_{j}\right)} d \xi_{j}^{2}+\frac{\Theta\left(\xi_{j}\right) F_{j}\left(\xi_{j}\right)}{p\left(\xi_{j}\right) \Delta_{j}}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\ell} \sigma_{r-1}\left(\hat{\xi}_{j}\right) \theta_{r}\right)^{2}\right) \\
\omega_{\mathbf{a}}= & \sum_{r=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{\ell-r+1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\alpha_{r}-\xi_{j}\right)\right) \pi^{*} \omega_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{r}}}+\sum_{r=1}^{\ell} d \sigma_{r} \wedge \theta_{r} \\
d \theta_{r}= & (-1)^{r} \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\alpha_{j}^{\ell-r} \pi^{*} \omega_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}\right), \\
J d \xi_{j}= & \frac{\Theta\left(\xi_{j}\right) F_{j}\left(\xi_{j}\right)}{\Delta_{j} p\left(\xi_{j}\right)} \sum_{r=1}^{\ell} \sigma_{r-1}\left(\hat{\xi}_{j}\right) \theta_{r}, \quad J \theta_{r}=(-1)^{r} \sum_{j=1}^{\ell} \frac{p\left(\xi_{j}\right)}{\Theta\left(\xi_{j}\right) F_{j}\left(\xi_{j}\right)^{2}} \xi_{j}^{\ell-r} d \xi_{j},
\end{aligned}$$
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item[-] $\left(\xi_{1}, \ldots, \xi_{\ell}\right)$ are independent coordinates defined over the product of intervals
$$\stackrel{\circ}{\Xi}_{\ell}=\left(\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}\right) \times \cdots \times\left(\alpha_{\ell-1}, \alpha_{\ell}\right) \times(0, \infty),$$
\item[-] $\left(\theta_{1}, \cdots, \theta_{\ell}\right)$ are 1-forms on $P_{\mathbf{a}}$ expressed in terms of the connection 1-forms $\left(\tilde{\theta}_{1}, \ldots, \tilde{\theta}_{\ell}\right)$ introduced in (3.2) by
\end{itemize}
$$\theta_{r}=(-1)^{r-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} r_{j} \alpha_{j}^{\ell-r-1} \tilde{\theta}_{j}\right) .$$
\begin{itemize}
\item[-] $\Delta_{j}=\prod_{k \neq j}\left(\xi_{j}-\xi_{k}\right), \sigma_{r}$ denotes the $r$-th elementary symmetric function of the variables $\left(\xi_{1}, \ldots, \xi_{\ell}\right)$, whereas $\sigma_{r-1}\left(\hat{\xi}_{j}\right)$ stands for the $(r-1)$-th elementary symmetric function of $\xi_{i}$ 's with $\xi_{j}$ omitted (and we set $\sigma_{0}:=1$ );
\end{itemize}
\begin{itemize}
\item[-] $F_{1}(x), \ldots, F_{\ell}(x)$ are degree $m$ polynomials defined by
$$\begin{aligned}
& F_{1}(x)=\cdots=F_{\ell-1}(x)=p(x)=2 \prod_{k=1}^{\ell}\left(x-\alpha_{k}\right)^{\left(n_{k}+1\right)} \\
& F_{\ell}(x)=2 x\left(\sum_{r=1}^{m} b_{m-r} x^{m-r}\right)
\end{aligned}$$
with
$$F_{\ell}^{\prime \prime}(x)=p^{\prime \prime}(x), \quad F_{\ell}^{\prime}(0)>0 .$$
\end{itemize}
In order to see that $g_{\mathbf{a}}$ is positive-definite on $M_{\mathbf{a}}$, notice that $(-1)^{j} F_{j}(x)>0$ on $\left(\alpha_{j}, \alpha_{j+1}\right)$ for $j=1, \ldots, \ell-1$ by construction, whereas (3.10) implies that $F_{\ell}(x)>0$ on ( $0, \infty$ ) (by the first condition in (3.10), $F_{\ell}^{\prime \prime}(x)=p^{\prime \prime}(x)>0$ on that interval). Furthermore, according to [5], (3.7) defines a Kähler metric which has zero scalar curvature. It is Ricci-flat iff $F_{\ell}^{\prime}(x)=p^{\prime}(x)$. It will be useful to notice (again by the classification results in [5]) that (3.7) defines a flat Kähler metric if, moreover, we have have $F_{\ell}(x)=p(x)$.

Remark 5. Note that (3.7) yields a $g_{\mathbf{a}}$-orthogonal decomposition of the tangent space of $M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}$ (defined in (3.5))
$$T M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}=\mathcal{H} \oplus \mathcal{V}$$
where $\mathcal{H}$ is defined to be the common annihilator of $\left(\theta_{1}, \ldots, \theta_{\ell}\right)$ and $\left(d \xi_{1}, \ldots, d \xi_{\ell}\right)$, whereas $\mathcal{V}$ is the tangent space to the fibres of the projection $\pi: M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0} \rightarrow S$. We shall accordingly refer to elements of $\mathcal{H}$ and $\mathcal{V}$ as horizontal and vertical vectors. \(\square\)

The condition (3.10) allows one to determine the coefficients $b_{m-1}, \ldots, b_{1}$ in terms of the $\alpha_{i}$ 's (or, equivalently, in terms of a) whereas $b_{0}$ is a free constant (which we will determine later, see (3.22)) satisfying
$$b_{0}=\frac{1}{2} p^{\prime}(0)=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(1+n_{j}\right) \frac{1}{\alpha_{j}}\right) \prod_{k=1}^{\ell} \alpha_{k}^{n_{k}+1}$$
iff the metric is Ricci-flat (the latter being equivalent to $F_{\ell}^{\prime}(x)=p^{\prime}(x)$ because of (3.10)).

In what follows, we shall re-write (3.7) by using the symplectic toric formalism of [1] and [6].

Let $K_{j}, j=1, \ldots, \ell$ denote the vertical vector fields on $P_{\mathbf{a}}$, defined by $\theta_{r}\left(K_{j}\right)=$ $\delta_{j r}$. These are commuting Killing vector fields for the Kähler structure (3.7), corresponding to angular coordinates on each fibre $T^{\ell}$ of $P_{\mathbf{a}}$. It follows from the explicit form (3.7) of the metric that the elementary symmetric functions $\sigma_{j}, j=1, \ldots, \ell$ of $\xi_{1}, \ldots, \xi_{\ell}$ are the corresponding momenta for the Kähler form $\omega_{\mathbf{a}}$. In terms of the coordinates ( $\sigma_{1}, \ldots, \sigma_{\ell}$ ), the metric (3.7) is defined on $M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}=\stackrel{\circ}{\Sigma}_{\ell} \times P_{\mathbf{a}}$ (see e.g. [6, Prop. 7]), where
$$\begin{aligned}
\stackrel{\circ}{\Sigma}_{\ell}=\left\{\left(\sigma_{1}, \ldots, \sigma_{\ell}\right):\right. & (-1)^{\ell-j+1} \sum_{r=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{r} \sigma_{r} \alpha_{j}^{\ell-r}>0, j=1, \ldots, \ell, \\
& \left.(-1)^{\ell-1} \sigma_{\ell}>0\right\},
\end{aligned}$$
and we have put $\sigma_{0}:=1$. The metric (3.7) is then written on $\stackrel{\circ}{\Sigma}_{\ell} \times P_{\mathbf{a}}$, see [5, Prop. 6 \& 11] as
$$\begin{aligned}
g_{\mathbf{a}}= & \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left((-1)^{\ell-j+1} \sum_{r=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{r} \sigma_{r} \alpha_{j}^{\ell-r}\right) \pi^{*} g_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}} \\
& +\sum_{i, j=1}^{\ell}\left(\left(\operatorname{Hess}\left(U^{0}\right)\right)_{i j} d \sigma_{i} d \sigma_{j}+\left(\operatorname{Hess}\left(U^{0}\right)\right)_{i j}^{-1} \theta_{i} \theta_{j}\right) \\
\omega_{\mathbf{a}}= & \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left((-1)^{\ell-j+1} \sum_{r=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{r} \sigma_{r} \alpha_{j}^{\ell-r}\right) \pi^{*} \omega_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell} d \sigma_{i} \wedge \theta_{i},
\end{aligned}$$
where
$$U^{0}\left(\sigma_{1}, \ldots, \sigma_{\ell}\right):=-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} \int^{\xi_{j}} \frac{p(x) \prod_{k=1}^{\ell}\left(x-\xi_{k}\right)}{\Theta(x) F_{j}(x)} d x$$
It will be useful to consider, in an affine-invariant way, the $(\ell \times \ell)$-matrix valued functions $\mathbf{G}=\left(\operatorname{Hess}\left(U^{0}\right)_{i j}\right)$ and $\mathbf{H}=\left(\left(\operatorname{Hess}\left(U^{0}\right)\right)_{i j}^{-1}\right)=\left(g_{\mathbf{a}}\left(K_{i}, K_{j}\right)\right)$ as the Gram matrices associated to two mutually inverse functions on $\stackrel{\circ}{\Sigma}_{\ell}$ with values respectively in $S^{2} \mathfrak{t}_{\ell}$ and $S^{2} \mathfrak{t}_{\ell}^{*}$, where $\mathfrak{t}_{\ell}=\operatorname{span}\left(K_{1}, \ldots, K_{\ell}\right)$ is the Lie algebra associated to the $T^{\ell}$ fibre of $P_{\mathbf{a}}$. It is shown in the proof of [6, Prop. 9], that $\mathbf{H}\left(\sigma_{1}, \ldots, \sigma_{\ell}\right)$ extends smoothly on the closure $\Sigma_{\ell}$ of $\stackrel{\circ}{\Sigma}_{\ell}$ and satisfies, at any face $F$ of $\stackrel{\circ}{\Sigma}_{\ell}$, the positivity and the boundary conditions of [6, Prop. 1] with respect to the weighted inward normals
$$\begin{aligned}
& u_{j}=c_{j}\left(\alpha_{j}^{\ell-1}, \ldots,(-1)^{r-1} \alpha_{j}^{\ell-r}, \ldots,(-1)^{\ell-1}\right), j=1, \ldots m \\
& u_{0}=c_{0}\left(0, \ldots, 0,(-1)^{\ell-1}\right)
\end{aligned}$$
where
$$\begin{aligned}
& c_{j}=\frac{2}{\Theta^{\prime}\left(\alpha_{j}\right)}=\frac{1}{\prod_{k \neq j}\left(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}\right)}=(-1)^{\ell-j} r_{j}, \\
& c_{0}=\frac{2}{\left(\frac{\Theta F_{\ell}}{p}\right)^{\prime}(0)}=\frac{(-1)^{m-\ell} \prod_{j=1}^{\ell} \alpha_{j}^{n_{j}}}{b_{0}},
\end{aligned}$$
where the product at the first line runs over $j, k \in\{1, \ldots, \ell\}$. (Note that, (3.15) explains the definition of $r_{j}$ in (3.1) and (3.4).)

Specifically, for this choice of inward normals, we have
$$\mathbf{H}_{\sigma}\left(u_{r}, \cdot\right)=0 ; \quad d \mathbf{H}_{\sigma}\left(u_{r}, u_{r}\right)=2 u_{r},$$
for the normal $u_{r}$ of a facet $F_{r} \subset \Sigma_{\ell} \subset \mathfrak{t}_{\ell}^{*}$ and $\sigma \in F_{r}$, where the differential $d \mathbf{H}$ is viewed as a smooth $S^{2} \mathfrak{t}_{\ell}^{*} \otimes \mathfrak{t}_{\ell}$-valued function on $\Sigma_{\ell}$, by identifying the cotangent space of $\mathfrak{t}_{\ell}^{*}$ at a point $\sigma \in \Sigma_{\ell} \subset \mathfrak{t}_{\ell}^{*}$ with $\mathfrak{t}_{\ell}$.

Consider the affine map
$$x_{j}=\frac{(-1)^{\ell-j} r_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\left(\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{r} \sigma_{r} \alpha_{j}^{\ell-r}\right), j=1, \ldots, \ell$$

Using (3.4) and the Vandermonde identities (see e.g. [5, (94)])
$$\begin{aligned}
& \sum_{j=1}^{\ell} \frac{\alpha_{j}^{\ell-s}}{\prod_{k \neq j}\left(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}\right)}=\delta_{s, 1}, s=1, \ldots, \ell, \\
& \sum_{j=1}^{\ell} \frac{\alpha_{j}^{-1}}{\prod_{k \neq j}\left(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}\right)}=\frac{(-1)^{\ell+1}}{\alpha_{1} \cdots \alpha_{\ell}},
\end{aligned}$$
(the second identity in (3.18) can be derived from the first applied to $\alpha_{0}, \ldots, \alpha_{\ell}$ with $\alpha_{0}:=0$ ) we compute
$$\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} x_{j} & =\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}\left((-1)^{r} \sigma_{r}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} \frac{\alpha_{j}^{\ell-r-1}}{\prod_{k \neq j}\left(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}\right)}\right)\right) \\
& =1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{1} \cdots \alpha_{\ell}} \sigma_{\ell},
\end{aligned}$$
showing that the polytope $\stackrel{\circ}{\Sigma}_{\ell}$ can be identified, via the map (3.17), with the standard unbounded simplex
$$\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell}=\left\{\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{\ell}\right): x_{i}>0,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} x_{j}-1\right)>0\right\} \subset \mathbb{R}^{\ell}$$
Recall that the $\sigma_{r}$ 's are the elementary symmetric functions of the variables $\left(\xi_{1}, \ldots, \xi_{\ell}\right)$, so that (3.17) identifies
$$M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}=\stackrel{\circ}{\Xi}_{\ell} \times P_{\mathbf{a}} \cong \stackrel{\circ}{P}_{\ell} \times P_{\mathbf{a}},$$
(see (3.5)), an identification which we tacitly use throughout the paper.
Finally, using (3.3), the inward normals $u_{j}$ to $\Sigma_{\ell}^{\circ}$ defined by (3.14) transform under (3.17) to the inward normals
$$v_{j}=\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{j}}\right) e_{j}, j=1, \ldots, \ell$$
to $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell}$, where $e_{j}$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^{\ell}$, and we can further put
$$c_{0}:=\frac{1}{a_{0}^{\ell}\left(a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}\right)^{\ell-2}}$$
so that $u_{0}$ transforms to
$$v_{0}=\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{0}}\right)\left(e_{1}+\cdots+e_{\ell}\right) .$$
With this choice, the free constant $b_{0}$ is determined via (3.15). Using (3.3) and (3.6), the Ricci-flat condition (3.11) then becomes
$$a_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(n_{j}+1\right) a_{j} .$$

By (3.17), the functions $\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{\ell}\right)$ are momenta for the Killing vector fields
$$X_{j}:=\frac{1}{\alpha_{j} \prod_{k \neq j}\left(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}\right)}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{r} \alpha_{j}^{\ell-r} K_{r}\right) .$$

Using standard Vandermonde identities again (see e.g. [5, App. B]) one can invert (3.8)
$$\tilde{\theta}_{j}=-\sum_{r=1}^{\ell} \sigma_{r-1}^{\alpha}\left(\hat{\alpha}_{j}\right) \alpha_{j} \theta_{r},$$
where $\sigma_{r-1}^{\alpha}\left(\hat{\alpha}_{j}\right)$ denotes the $(r-1)$-th elementary symmetric function of the $(\ell-1)$ elements $\left\{\alpha_{1}, \ldots, \alpha_{\ell}\right\} \backslash\left\{\alpha_{j}\right\}$. A direct inspection using (3.25) then shows that $\tilde{\theta}_{i}\left(X_{j}\right)=\delta_{i}^{j}$, i.e. $X_{j}$ are the standard $S^{1}$ generators for the $T^{\ell}$ action on $P_{\mathbf{a}}$.

We summarize the discussion in the following
Proposition 6. Under the identification (3.20), the Kähler metric (3.7) can be written on $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell} \times P_{\mathbf{a}}$ (where $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell}$ is the interior of the standard unbounded simplex in $\mathbb{R}^{\ell}$ defined in (3.19)) as follows

where
$$\tilde{U}^{0}\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{\ell}\right)=U^{0}\left(\sigma_{1}, \ldots, \sigma_{\ell}\right)=-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} \int^{\xi_{j}} \frac{p(t) \prod_{k=1}^{\ell}\left(t-\xi_{k}\right)}{\Theta(t) F_{j}(t)} d t .$$
Furthermore, $\mathbf{H}(x)=\left(\operatorname{Hess}\left(\tilde{U}^{0}\right)\right)^{-1}$ extends smoothly over the boundary of $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell}$, and satisfies the positivity and boundary conditions of [6, Prop. 1] at each face with respect to the weighted inward normals (3.21) and (3.23) whereas the lattice of $\mathbb{R}^{\ell}$ corresponding of circle subgroups of $P_{\mathbf{a}}$ is $\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\left\{e_{1}, \ldots, e_{\ell}\right\}$.

Remark 7. In the case $\ell=1$, (3.7) reduces to (2.1) by considering $p(x)=x^{n+1}$, $a_{0}=r, a_{1}=1$, and introducing the variable $\xi:=\xi_{1}-\alpha_{1} \in\left(\alpha_{1}, \infty\right)$. In this case $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{1}=(1, \infty)$ and the boundary condition the metric $g_{\mathbf{a}}$ satisfies at 1 is with respect to the inward normal normal $v_{0}=e_{1}$, showing the metric closes smoothly. \(\square\)
3.2. Orbifold extension of the metric over boundary points. In this section we shall use the well-known description of toric orbifolds in terms of rational simple convex polytopes [25], and its coordinate-free version developed in [6] according to which the orbifold structure is encoded by a polytope $\mathrm{P} \subset \mathbb{R}^{m}$, a lattice $\Lambda \in \mathbb{R}^{m}$ and inward normals to P which belong to $\Lambda$. We start with following preliminary observation.

Lemma 8. Let $\mathbf{a}=\left(a_{0}, \ldots, a_{\ell}\right)$ be any weight vector and $V_{\mathbf{a}}=\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{\ell}}$ the non-compact weighted projective space defined in Definition $A$. Then, $V_{\mathbf{a}}$ admits a symplectic form $\omega^{0}$ and an $\omega^{0}$-hamiltonian action of an $\ell$-dimensional torus $T^{\ell}$, such that the momentum image of $V_{\mathbf{a}}$ is $\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{a}}$, the lattice in $\mathbb{R}^{\ell}$ generated by the circle
subgroups of $T^{\ell}$ is
$$\Lambda_{\mathbf{a}}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{0}}\right)\left(e_{1}+\cdots+e_{\ell}\right),\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{j}}\right) e_{j}, j=1, \ldots, \ell\right\},$$
where $\left\{e_{1}, \ldots, e_{\ell}\right\}$ stands for the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^{\ell}$ and the weighted inward normals to $\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{a}}$ determining the orbifold structure are
$$v_{0}=\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{0}}\right)\left(e_{1}+\cdots+e_{\ell}\right), v_{j}=\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{j}}\right) e_{j}, j=1, \ldots, \ell$$
Proof. This is similar with an argument from [2] dealing with the compact weighted projective spaces, but we use here a different lattice to avoid orbifold quotients (see [6, Remark 2]).

The (non-compact) polytope $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell} \subset \mathbb{R}^{\ell}$ is simple and convex, whereas the inward normals $v_{0}, v_{1}, \ldots, v_{\ell}$ span a lattice $\Lambda_{\mathbf{a}}$ (contained in but in general different from the standard lattice $\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}$ ). The Delzant construction [16, 25] then associates to $\left(\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell}, \Lambda_{\mathbf{a}}\right)$ a (non-compact) symplectic orbifold $\left(V_{\mathbf{a}}, \omega^{0}\right)$, obtained as the Kähler quotient $\left(V_{\mathbf{a}}, \omega, J^{0}\right)$ of $\mathbb{C}^{\ell+1}$ (endowed whit its standard flat Kähler structure $\left.\left(g^{0}, J^{0}, \omega^{0}\right)\right)$, with respect to the weighed $S^{1}$-action
$$\rho_{\mathbf{a}}(t)\left(z_{0}, \ldots, z_{\ell}\right)=\left(e^{-2 \pi a_{0} t} z_{0}, e^{2 \pi a_{1} t} z_{1}, \ldots, e^{2 \pi a_{\ell}} z_{\ell}\right)$$
at the regular value $c:=a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}$ of the corresponding momentum map
$$\mu_{\mathbf{a}}\left(z_{0}, \ldots, z_{\ell}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-a_{0}\left|z_{0}\right|^{2}+a_{1}\left|z_{1}\right|^{2}+\cdots a_{\ell}\left|z_{\ell}\right|^{2}\right) .$$
GIT then identifies $\left(V_{\mathbf{a}}, J^{0}\right)$ with the non-compact weighted projective space $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{\ell}}$ of weight-vector $\mathbf{a}=\left(a_{0}, \ldots, a_{\ell}\right)$, as constructed in Definition A. \(\square\)

A simple variation of the arguments in the proof of Lemma 8 allows us to construct a non-compact symplectic orbifold, starting with the (non-compact) polytope $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell} \subset \mathbb{R}^{\ell}$ endowed with the same inward normals $v_{0}, v_{1}, \ldots, v_{\ell}$, but considering the standard lattice $\Lambda_{0}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\left\{e_{1}, \ldots, e_{\ell}\right\} \subset \mathbb{R}^{\ell}$ instead of $\Lambda_{\mathbf{a}}$. As noticed in [6, Rem. 2], the toric orbifold resulting from the Delzant construction associated to these data, which we denote by $V_{[\mathbf{a}]}$, is a $T^{\ell}$-equivariant symplectic orbifold quotient of $V_{\mathbf{a}}$ by an abelian finite group $G_{\mathbf{a}}$ of order $\left(a_{0} \ldots a_{\ell}\right)^{\ell-1}$, isomorphic to $\Lambda_{0} / \Lambda_{\mathbf{a}}$ (see [2, \& 2.3] for an explicit definition of the quotient). We thus have
$$V_{[\mathbf{a}]}=V_{\mathbf{a}} / G_{\mathbf{a}} .$$
Definition 9. Let $\hat{M}_{\mathbf{a}}:=P_{\mathbf{a}} \times{ }_{T^{\ell}} V_{[\mathbf{a}]}$ be the (toric) orbifold fibre-bundle associated to the principal $T^{\ell}$-bundle $P_{\mathbf{a}}$ defined in Definition 4, whose fibres are isomorphic to the toric orbifold $V_{[\mathbf{a}]}$ defined in (3.29).

By a straightforward orbifold version of Theorem 2 in [6] (noting that we do not have to deal with blow-downs to construct a $T^{\ell}$-invariant Kähler metric out of generalized Calabi data on $\hat{M}_{\mathbf{a}}$ ), $M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}$ (as defined by (3.20)) can be realized as an open dense subset of $\hat{M}_{\mathbf{a}}$. The fact that the vertical part of $\left(g_{\mathbf{a}}, \omega_{\mathbf{a}}\right)$ (in the sense of Remark 5) satisfy the boundary and positivity conditions of [6, Prop. 1] with respect to the inward weighted normals $v_{j}, j=0,1, \ldots, \ell$ to $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell}$ (see Proposition 6) imply

Corollary 10. The tensor fields $\left(g_{\mathbf{a}}, \omega_{\mathbf{a}}\right)$ extend smoothly (in the orbifold sense) to $\hat{M}_{\mathbf{a}}$.

As noticed in Remark 7, the above Corollary yields an orbifold scalar-flat Kähler when $\ell=1$ and $a_{0}, a_{1}$ are arbitrary co-prime positive integers, but for each factor $\mathbb{C} P^{n_{r}}$ with $n_{r}>0$ for $r=1, \ldots, \ell-1$, the horizontal part of $g_{\mathbf{a}}$ degenerates on the pre-image in $\hat{M}_{\mathbf{a}}$ of a facet of $\stackrel{\circ}{P}_{\ell}$. We shall show next that, nevertheless, up to (isometric) orbifold coverings and quotients, the metric (3.7) is the pull-pack to $\hat{M}_{\mathbf{a}}$ of a genuine (non-degenerate) Kähler orbi-metric, still denoted by $\left(g_{\mathbf{a}}, \omega_{\mathbf{a}}\right)$, which is defined on a different orbifold, namely
$$M_{\mathbf{a}}:=\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{\ell}, \ldots, a_{\ell}}^{m}$$
where each $a_{r}$ is counted $\left(n_{r}+1\right)$-times. This phenomenon has been studied in [6] (in the non-singular case) where it has been refereed to as a blow-down.

Remark 11. Note that the metric (3.7), which we defined on $M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}=\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell} \times P_{\mathbf{a}}$, can be lifted to any cover of $M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0}$, in particular to
$$M^{0}=\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{\ell} \times P,$$
where $P$ is the principal $T^{\ell}$-bundle over $S$ with 'primitive' Chern class $c_{1}(P)=$ $\left(\gamma_{1}, \ldots, \gamma_{\ell}\right)$ (compare with Definition 4). In terms of the local theory developed in Sect. 3.1, this amounts to observe that generators of circle subgroups of $P$ will then be the vector fields $\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{j}}\right) X_{j}$, or equivalently, the lattice generated by circle subgroups of $P$ in Proposition 6 is
$$\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{j}}\right) e_{j}, j=1, \ldots, \ell\right\} .$$
On the other hand, there is a natural geometric way to realize $M_{\mathbf{a}}$ (see (3.30)) as a complex orbifold blow-down of the manifold (compare with Definition 9)
$$N_{\mathbf{a}}:=P \times_{T^{\ell}} V_{\mathbf{a}} .$$
To see this explicitly, consider the smooth complex $(m+1)$-dimensional manifold which is the total space of the vector bundle
$$E=\mathcal{O} \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{\ell}}} \rightarrow S=\mathbb{C} P^{n_{1}} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{C} P^{n_{\ell}},$$
where $m=\ell+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{\ell}, \mathcal{O}$ denotes the trivial holomorphic vector bundle over $S$ and $\mathcal{O}(-1)_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}$ is the pull back to $S$ of the tautological holomorphic line bundle over the factor $\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}$. We then consider the fibre-wise $\mathbb{C}^{*}$-action $\rho_{\mathbf{a}}$ on $E$, associated to the weight-vector $\mathbf{a}=\left(a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{\ell}\right)$ with $a_{1}<\cdots<a_{\ell}$ (see Definition A). The quotient of $E \backslash E_{0}$ (where $E_{0} \cong \mathcal{O}$ is the the trivial bundle over $S$, realized as a sub-bundle of $E$ corresponding to first summand in (3.31)) is an $m$-dimensional complex orbifold identified with $N_{\mathbf{a}}$. A natural holomorphic map $q: N_{\mathbf{a}} \rightarrow M_{\mathbf{a}}=\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{\ell}, \ldots, a_{\ell}}^{m}$ is defined as follows: Let $x=\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{\ell}\right) \in S$ be a point of $S$ with $x_{j} \in \mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}$, and write $x_{j}=\left[x_{0}^{j}: \ldots x_{n_{j}}^{j}\right]$ in homogenous coordinates. Denote by $\left(x_{0}^{j}, \ldots, x_{n_{j}}^{j}\right)$ the corresponding generator of the complex line $\left(\mathcal{O}(-1)_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}\right)_{x_{j}}$ which gives rise to a coordinate $\lambda_{j}$ on the fibre over $x_{j}$. Using this trivialization, we introduce "homogenous coordinates" $\left[\lambda_{0}: \ldots: \lambda_{\ell}\right]$ for any point of the fibre $\mathbb{C} P_{-a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{\ell}}$ of $\hat{M}_{\mathbf{a}}$ over $x$. Then, we let
$$\begin{array}{r}
q\left(\left[x_{0}^{1}: \cdots: x_{n_{1}}^{1}\right], \ldots,\left[x_{0}^{\ell}: \cdots: x_{n_{\ell}}^{\ell}\right] ;\left[\lambda_{0}: \cdots: \lambda_{\ell}\right]\right)= \\
{\left[\lambda_{0}: \lambda_{1} x_{0}^{1}: \cdots \lambda_{1} x_{n_{1}}^{1}: \cdots: \lambda_{\ell} x_{0}^{\ell}: \cdots \lambda_{\ell} x_{n_{\ell}}^{\ell}\right]}
\end{array}$$
which is a well-defined, holomorphic map from $N_{\mathbf{a}}$ to $M_{\mathbf{a}}$.
Dealing with orbifold covers and quotients as above is quite tedious in general, and we are not going to use the map $q$ in the sequel. We shall instead adopt a different (symplectic) point of view, reflecting the fact that both $\hat{M}_{\mathbf{a}}$ and $M_{\mathbf{a}}$ are toric orbifolds. In this setting, an equivariant manifestation of the map $q$ is given in (3.37) below. \(\square\)

The main idea to associate the construction (3.7) to the non-compact weighted projective space $M_{\mathbf{a}}$ (see(3.30)) is the following. Each $\left(\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}, g_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}, \omega_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}\right)$ is also a toric Kähler manifold, so that, after lifting the torus action on each factor to $M_{\mathbf{a}}^{0},\left(g_{\mathbf{a}}, \omega_{\mathbf{a}}\right)$ becomes a toric metric under the action of $T^{m}$. By rewriting (3.7) in the moment/angle formalism of [1, 20], we shall then show that the metric (3.7) is defined on the interior of the unbounded standard simplex $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{m} \subset \mathbb{R}^{m}$, and that the corresponding symplectic potential $\tilde{U}_{\mathbf{a}}$ satisfies the positivity and boundary conditions of [6, Prop. 1] (or equivalently of [2, Thm. 2]) with respect to the inward normals associated to $\stackrel{\circ}{P}_{m}$ via Lemma 8. This will be then enough to conclude, using again the general theory in $[1,2]$, that $\left(g_{\mathbf{a}}, \omega_{\mathbf{a}}\right)$ gives rise to an orbifold Kähler metric on $M_{\mathbf{a}}$. We shall thus not worry about the lattices along the way (i.e. we shall ignore toric orbifold coverings and quotients) as the only ingredient we need is the asymptotic behaviour of the symplectic potential (which we shall describe explicitly in terms of (3.7)).

Each $\left(\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}, g_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}, \omega_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}\right)$ is a toric Kähler manifold whose Delzant polytope (with respect to the chosen normalization for $\omega_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}}$ ) is the compact simplex
$$\left\{\left(y_{1}^{j}, \ldots, y_{n_{j}}^{j}\right): y_{k_{j}}^{j} \geq 0, \quad\left(r_{j}-\sum_{k_{j}=1}^{n_{j}} y_{k_{j}}^{j}\right) \geq 0, k_{j}=1, \ldots, n_{j}, j=1, \ldots, \ell\right\},$$
endowed with the standard inward normals $e_{k_{j}}^{j}, k_{j}=1, \ldots, n_{j}$ and $e_{0}^{j}:=-\sum_{k_{j}=1}^{n_{j}} e_{k_{j}}^{j}$, where $\left(e_{k_{j}}^{j}\right), k_{j}=1, \ldots, n_{j}$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}, j=1, \ldots, \ell$ (with the obvious assumption to discard $y_{k_{j}}^{j}$ and $e_{k_{j}}^{j}$ when $n_{j}=0$ ). It can be mapped to the standard simplex
$$\mathrm{S}_{n_{j}}=\left\{\left(x_{1}^{j}, \ldots, x_{n_{j}}^{j}\right): x_{k_{j}}^{j} \geq 0, \quad\left(1-\sum_{k_{j}=1}^{n_{j}} x_{k_{j}}^{j}\right) \geq 0\right\},$$
via the affine map
$$x_{k_{j}}^{j}=\frac{1}{r_{j}} y_{k_{j}}^{j},$$
thus transforming the inward normals to
$$v_{k_{j}}^{j}:=r_{j} e_{k_{j}}^{j}, k_{j}=0, \ldots, n_{j} .$$
In momentum-angular coordinates, the Fubini-Study metric (restricted on the preimage $\left(\mathbb{C} \stackrel{\circ}{P}^{n_{j}}\right)$ by the moment map $x^{j}$ of the interior $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{S}}_{n_{j}}$ of $\mathrm{S}_{n_{j}}$ ) takes the form [1,20]
$$\begin{aligned}
g_{\mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}} & =\left(\operatorname{Hess}\left(U^{j}\right)\right)_{r s} d x_{r}^{j} d x_{s}^{j}+\left(\operatorname{Hess}\left(U^{j}\right)\right)_{r s}^{-1} d t_{s}^{j} d t_{r}^{j} \\

\end{aligned}$$

The $\left(n_{j} \times n_{j}\right)$-matrix-valued function $\mathbf{H}^{j}=\left(\left(\operatorname{Hess}\left(U^{j}\right)\right)_{r s}^{-1}\right)$ extends smoothly over $\stackrel{\mathrm{S}}{\mathrm{S}}_{n_{j}}$ and satisfies the boundary conditions (3.16) with respect to the inward normals $v_{k}^{j}$ to $\mathrm{S}_{n_{j}}$.

$$\begin{aligned}

& =\frac{1}{r_{j}}\left(\frac{a_{0} \cdots a_{\ell}}{a_{j}}\right) x_{k}^{j} x_{j}

where we have used (3.17) to derive the last equality. ${ }^{2}$

Definition 12. Let $\stackrel{\circ}{M}_{\mathbf{a}}:=\stackrel{\circ}{\Xi}_{\ell} \times \stackrel{\circ}{P}_{\mathbf{a}} \cong \stackrel{\circ}{P}_{\ell} \times \stackrel{\circ}{P}_{\mathbf{a}}$, where $\stackrel{\circ}{P}_{\mathbf{a}}$ is the pre-image in $P_{\mathbf{a}}$ of the interior $\prod_{j=1}^{\ell} \stackrel{\circ}{\Sigma}_{n_{j}}$ of the product of simplexes $\prod_{j=1}^{\ell} \Sigma_{n_{j}}$, under the projection $\pi$ : $P_{\mathbf{a}} \rightarrow \prod_{j=1}^{\ell} \mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}}$ composed with the momentum map $\left(x^{1}, \ldots, x^{\ell}\right): \prod_{j=1}^{\ell} \mathbb{C} P^{n_{j}} \rightarrow$ $\prod_{j=1}^{\ell} \Sigma_{n_{j}}$.

& =\sum_{p, q=1}^{m}\left(\operatorname{Hess}\left(\tilde{U}_{\mathbf{a}}\right)_{p q} d \tilde{x}_{p} d \tilde{x}_{q}+\left(\operatorname{Hess}\left(\tilde{U}_{\mathbf{a}}\right)\right)_{p q}^{-1} d \tilde{t}_{p} d \tilde{t}_{q}\right) \\

where $\tilde{U}_{\mathbf{a}}\left(\tilde{x}_{1}, \ldots, \tilde{x}_{m}\right)=U_{\mathbf{a}}\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{m}\right)$ is a convex function defined on $\stackrel{\circ}{\mathrm{P}}_{m}$. We are going to express $\tilde{U}_{\mathbf{a}}$ in terms of the coordinates $x_{j}$ and $x_{k}^{j}$.
