\section*{5. Regular subalgebras}

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple Lie algebra and $\mathfrak{h}$ a (fixed) Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$, with corresponding root system $\Phi$. For $\alpha \in \Phi$ we denote $\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$ the corresponding root space. Let $T \subseteq \Phi$ be a closed subsystem. Let $\mathfrak{t}$ be a subspace of $\mathfrak{h}$ containing $\left[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}, \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\right]$ for $\alpha \in T$ such that $-\alpha \in T$. Then
$$\mathfrak{s}_{T, \mathfrak{t}}=\mathfrak{t} \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in T} \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$$
is a regular subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$. Moreover, all regular subalgebras of $\mathfrak{g}$ arise in this manner.
Let $G$ be the adjoint group of $\mathfrak{g}$. There are many ways to characterize $G$. It is the identity component of the automorphism group of $\mathfrak{g}$. It is the connected algebraic subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{g})$ with Lie algebra adg. Thirdly, it is the group generated by all $\exp (\operatorname{ad} x)$ for $x \in \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}, \alpha \in \Phi$.

The Weyl group of $\Phi$ naturally acts on the dual space $\mathfrak{h}^{*}$. Further, by identifying $\mathfrak{h}$ and $\mathfrak{h}^{*}$ via the Killing form, the Weyl group also acts on $\mathfrak{h}$. Letting $s_{\alpha}$ denote the reflection corresponding to $\alpha \in \Phi$, this means that $s_{\alpha}(h)=h-\alpha(h) h_{\alpha}$, where $h_{\alpha}$ is the unique element of $\left[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}, \mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\right]$ with $\alpha\left(h_{\alpha}\right)=2$.

Proposition 5.1. The regular subalgebras $\mathfrak{s}_{T_{1}, \mathrm{t}_{1}}$ and $\mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathrm{t}_{2}}$ are conjugate under $G$ if and only if there is a $w \in W(\Phi)$ with $w\left(T_{1}\right)=w\left(T_{2}\right)$ and $w\left(\mathfrak{t}_{1}\right)=w\left(\mathfrak{t}_{2}\right)$.

Proof. Let $g^{\prime} \in G$ be such that $g^{\prime}\left(\mathfrak{s}_{T_{1}, \mathfrak{t}_{1}}\right)=\mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathfrak{t}_{2}}$. Let $\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\left\{x \in \mathfrak{g}:\left[x, \mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathfrak{t}_{2}}\right] \subset \mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathfrak{t}_{2}}\right\}$ be the normalizer of $\mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathrm{t}_{2}}$. This is the Lie algebra of the algebraic group $N_{G}\left(\mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathrm{t}_{2}}\right)=$ $\left\{g \in G: g\left(\mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathrm{t}_{2}}\right)=\mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathrm{t}_{2}}\right\}$. Since $\mathfrak{h}$ and $g^{\prime}(\mathfrak{h})$ are Cartan subalgebras of $\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(\mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathrm{t}_{2}}\right)$, there is a $g^{\prime \prime} \in N_{G}\left(\mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathfrak{t}_{2}}\right)$ with $g^{\prime \prime} g^{\prime}(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}$. Set $g_{0}=g^{\prime \prime} g^{\prime}$. Note that $g_{0} \in N_{G}(\mathfrak{h})$. There is a canonical homomorphism $N_{G}(\mathfrak{h}) \rightarrow W$ with kernel $Z_{G}(\mathfrak{h})$ and let $w \in W(\Phi)$ be the image of $g_{0}$. A short calculation shows that $g_{0}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\right)=\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}$ where $\beta(h)=\alpha\left(g_{0}^{-1}(h)\right)$ for $h \in \mathfrak{h}$. But this implies that $\beta=w(\alpha)$. (This is easy to see if $w$ is a reflection, and it then follows in general.) So we see that $w\left(T_{1}\right)=T_{2}$. Also $w\left(\mathfrak{t}_{1}\right)=g_{0}\left(\mathfrak{s}_{T_{1}, \mathfrak{t}_{1}} \cap \mathfrak{h}\right)=\mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathfrak{t}_{2}} \cap \mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{t}_{2}$.

For the reverse implication, let $g_{0} \in N_{G}(\mathfrak{h})$ map to $w$. Then $g_{0}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\right)=\mathfrak{g}_{w(\alpha)}$ and $g_{0}\left(\mathfrak{t}_{1}\right)=\mathfrak{t}_{2}$. It follows that $g_{0}\left(\mathfrak{s}_{T_{1}, \mathrm{t}_{1}}\right)=\mathfrak{s}_{T_{2}, \mathfrak{t}_{2}}$. \(\square\)

Corollary 5.2. Let $T \subseteq \Phi$ be a closed set and $W(\Phi)_{T}=\{w \in W(\Phi): w(T)=T\}$ its stabilizer in $W(\Phi)$. The regular subalgebras $\mathfrak{s}_{T, \mathfrak{t}_{1}}$ and $\mathfrak{s}_{T, \mathfrak{t}_{2}}$ are conjugate under $G$ if and only if there is a $w \in W(\Phi)_{T}$ such that $w\left(\mathfrak{t}_{1}\right)=\mathfrak{t}_{2}$.
5.1. Regular subalgebras of the simple Lie algebra of type $A_{3}$. The (complex) Lie algebra of type $A_{3}$ is the special linear algebra $\mathfrak{s l}(4, \mathbb{C})$ of traceless $4 \times 4$ complex matrices. The Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ that we use consists of the diagonal elements of $\mathfrak{s} \mathfrak{l}(4, \mathbb{C})$. The Weyl group $W$ is isomorphic to the symmetric group $S_{4}$, acting on $\mathfrak{h}$ by permuting the diagonal entries. More precisely, for $\pi \in S_{4}$ we have
$$\pi \cdot \operatorname{diag}\left(a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}, a_{4}\right)=\operatorname{diag}\left(a_{\pi(1)}, a_{\pi(2)}, a_{\pi(3)}, a_{\pi(4)}\right) .$$
We have that $W$ is generated by the simple reflections $s_{1}, s_{2}, s_{3}$, and the isomorphism $W \rightarrow S_{4}$ maps $s_{1} \mapsto(1,2), s_{2} \mapsto(2,3), s_{3} \mapsto(3,4)$. A $W$-invariant scalar product on $\mathfrak{h}$ is defined by
$$h \cdot h^{\prime}=\operatorname{trace}\left(h h^{\prime}\right), h, h^{\prime} \in \mathfrak{h} .$$

The simple roots of the root system of $\mathfrak{s l}(4, \mathbb{C})$ are $\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}, \alpha_{3}$. The other positive roots are $\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}, \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, \alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}$.

We now wish to classify the regular subalgebras of $\mathfrak{s l}(4, \mathbb{C})$. To do so, for each closed subset $T$ of the root system of type $A_{3}$, we will determine the possible $\mathfrak{t} \subset \mathfrak{h}$ such that $\mathfrak{s}_{T, \mathfrak{t}}$ is a regular subalgebra. The cases in which $\mathfrak{t}$ is 0 - or 3-dimensional are trivial, so we only consider the 1- and 2-dimensional cases.

In the following example, we explicitly describe the process of determining the regular subalgebras obtained from a given closed subset of $A_{3}$. Determining the regular subalgebras for all other closed subsets of $A_{3}$ is obtained in a similar manner, and the complete results are summarized in Table 11 through Table 13.

Example 5.3. Consider the special closed subset of $A_{3}$
$$T=\left\{\alpha_{2}, \alpha_{3}, \alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}, \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, \alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}\right\},$$
which is located in row 2 of Table 2. A calculation shows that the stabilizer of $T$, denoted $W_{T}$, is generated by the single element $s_{1}$.

Since $T$ consists of positive root vectors, we may construct a regular subalgebra
$$\mathfrak{s}_{T, \mathfrak{t}}=\mathfrak{t} \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in T} \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}$$
by letting $\mathfrak{t}$ be any subspace of the Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$. Then, the possible 1-dimensional $\mathfrak{t}$ are spanned by the non-trivial traceless elements $h_{a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}}=\operatorname{diag}\left(a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3},-a_{1}-a_{2}-a_{3}\right)$ for $a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3} \in \mathbb{C}$.

Two such regular subalgebras determined by $h_{a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}}$ and $h_{b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3}}$, respectively, are $W$-conjugate if and only if $s_{1} \cdot h_{a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}}$ is a scalar multiple of $h_{b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3}}$ by Corollary 5.2. Considering that $s_{1}$ acts as the permutation $(1,2)$ on the entries of $h_{a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}}$, as described in Eq. (3), these regular subalgebras are $W$-conjugate if and only if $\left(a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}\right)$ is a scalar multiple of $\left(b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3}\right)$ or $\left(b_{2}, b_{1}, b_{3}\right)$.

To determine the regular subalgebras constructed from 2-dimensional $\mathfrak{t}$, we use the fact that a 2-dimensional subspace is uniquely determined by a nonzero element of $\mathfrak{h}$ perpendicular to it. Since the inner product is $W$-invariant, two subspaces are $W$-conjugate if and only if their perpendicular vectors are. Thus, the 2-dimensional $\mathfrak{t}$ are the spaces orthogonal to $h_{a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}}$, for any $a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3} \in \mathbb{C}$, all of which are not zero.

Using the same reasoning as in the 1-dimensional case, regular subalgebras with $\mathfrak{t}$ given by the space orthogonal to $h_{a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}}$, is conjugate to the space orthogonal to $h_{b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3}}$ if and only if $\left(a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}\right)$ is a scalar multiple of $\left(b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3}\right)$ or $\left(b_{2}, b_{1}, b_{3}\right)$.

5.2. Regular subalgebras of the simple Lie algebra of type $B_{3}$. The (complex) Lie algebra of type $B_{3}$ is the special orthogonal algebra $\mathfrak{s o}(7, \mathbb{C})$. It is the Lie algebra of $7 \times 7$ complex matrices satisfying
$$X^{t} M=-M X,$$
where
$$M=\left(\begin{array}{ccc}
2 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & I_{3} \\
0 & I_{3} & 0
\end{array}\right) .$$

A Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ is given by elements of the form
$$h=\operatorname{diag}\left(0, \lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3}\right),$$
for $\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3} \in \mathbb{C}$. The positive roots are
$$\Phi^{+}=\left\{\begin{array}{lll}
\alpha_{1}, & \alpha_{2}, & \alpha_{3}, \\
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}, & \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, & \alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, \\
\alpha_{2}+2 \alpha_{3}, & \alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+2 \alpha_{3}, & \alpha_{1}+2 \alpha_{2}+2 \alpha_{3}
\end{array}\right\},$$
where
$$\begin{aligned}
& \alpha_{1}(h)=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}, \\
& \alpha_{2}(h)=\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}, \\
& \alpha_{3}(h)=\lambda_{3} .
\end{aligned}$$
Define a change of basis
$$\beta_{1}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, \quad \beta_{2}=\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, \quad \beta_{3}=\alpha_{3} .$$
The fundamental reflections are
$$\begin{array}{rlrl}
s_{i}\left(\beta_{i}\right) & =\beta_{i+1}, & s_{i}\left(\beta_{i+1}\right) & =\beta_{i}, \\
s_{i}\left(\beta_{j}\right) & =\beta_{j}, & & j=1,2, \\
s_{3}\left(\beta_{3}\right) & =-\beta_{3}, & s_{3}\left(\beta_{i}\right) & =\beta_{i}, \\
& i \neq 3 .
\end{array}$$
The Weyl group generated by the fundamental reflections is then seen to be isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3} \rtimes S_{3}$ : The isomorphism is given by $s_{1} \mapsto(1,2) \in S_{3}, s_{2} \mapsto(2,3) \in S_{3}$, and $s_{3} \mapsto(0,0,1) \in \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}$. The actions of $s_{1}, s_{2}$, and $s_{3}$ on the simple roots $\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}$, and $\alpha_{3}$ are
$$\begin{array}{ll}
s_{1}\left(\alpha_{1}\right)=-\alpha_{1}, & s_{2}\left(\alpha_{1}\right)=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}, \\
s_{1}\left(\alpha_{2}\right)=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}, & s_{2}\left(\alpha_{2}\right)=-\alpha_{2}, \\
s_{1}\left(\alpha_{3}\right)=\alpha_{3} & s_{2}\left(\alpha_{2}\right)=\alpha_{2}+2 \alpha_{3},
\end{array}$$

The correspondence, due to the Killing form, between $\mathfrak{h}^{*}$ and $\mathfrak{h}$ determines an action of the Weyl group on $\mathfrak{h}$. Specifically, a computation shows that we have the correspondence
$$\operatorname{diag}\left(0, \lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3}\right) \longleftrightarrow 2\left(\lambda_{1} \beta_{1}+\lambda_{2} \beta_{2}+\lambda_{3} \beta_{3}\right) .$$

The actions of $s_{1}, s_{2}$, and $s_{3}$ on $\mathfrak{h}$ are given by:
$$\begin{aligned}
& s_{1} \cdot \operatorname{diag}\left(0, \lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3}\right)=\operatorname{diag}\left(0, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{1}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{3}\right), \\
& s_{2} \cdot \operatorname{diag}\left(0, \lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3}\right)=\operatorname{diag}\left(0, \lambda_{1}, \lambda_{3}, \lambda_{2},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{3},-\lambda_{2}\right), \\
& s_{3} \cdot \operatorname{diag}\left(0, \lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3}\right)=\operatorname{diag}\left(0, \lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3}\right) .
\end{aligned}$$

We now describe a set of generators for $\mathfrak{s o}(7, \mathbb{C})$. Number matrix rows and columns $0,1,2,3,-1,-2,-3$. Then, positive root vectors $e_{\alpha}$ and negative root vectors $f_{\alpha}$ corresponding to the simple roots are
$$\begin{aligned}
& e_{\alpha_{1}}=E_{12}-E_{-2,-1}, \quad f_{\alpha_{1}}=E_{21}-E_{-1,-2}, \\
& e_{\alpha_{2}}=E_{23}-E_{-3,-2}, \quad f_{\alpha_{2}}=E_{32}-E_{-2,-3}, \\
& e_{\alpha_{3}}=2 E_{30}-E_{0,-3}, \quad f_{\alpha_{3}}=E_{03}-2 E_{-3,0} .
\end{aligned}$$
These root vectors determine the following basis of the Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ :
$$\begin{aligned}
h_{\alpha_{1}} & =\operatorname{diag}(0,1,-1,0,-1,1,0), \\
h_{\alpha_{2}} & =\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1,-1,0,-1,1), \\
h_{\alpha_{3}} & =\operatorname{diag}(0,0,0,2,0,0,-2) .
\end{aligned}$$

A $W$-invariant inner product on $\mathfrak{h}$ is defined by
$$h \cdot h^{\prime}=\operatorname{trace}\left(h h^{\prime}\right) .$$

We now wish to classify the regular subalgebras of $\mathfrak{s o}(7, \mathbb{C})$. To do so, for each closed subset $T$ of the root system of type $B_{3}$, we will determine the possible $\mathfrak{t} \subset \mathfrak{h}$ such that $\mathfrak{s}_{T, \mathfrak{t}}$ is a regular subalgebra. The cases in which $\mathfrak{t}$ is 0 - or 3-dimensional are trivial, so we only consider the 1- and 2-dimensional cases.

In the following example, we explicitly describe the process of determining the regular subalgebras obtained from a given closed subset of $B_{3}$. Determining the regular subalgebras for all other closed subsets of $B_{3}$ is obtained in a similar manner, and the complete results are summarized in Tables 14 through 20.

$$T=\left\{\begin{array}{l}

2 \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, \alpha_{1}+2 \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, 2 \alpha_{1}+2 \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3},-\alpha_{3}
\end{array}\right\},$$

5.3. Regular subalgebras of the simple Lie algebra of type $C_{3}$. The (complex) Lie algebra of type $C_{3}$ is the symplectic algebra $\mathfrak{s p}(6, \mathbb{C})$. It is the Lie algebra of $6 \times 6$ complex matrices satisfying
$$X^{t} M=-M X,$$
where
$$M=\left(\begin{array}{cc}
0 & I_{3} \\
-I_{3} & 0
\end{array}\right) .$$

A Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ is given by elements of the form
$$h=\operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3}\right),$$
for $\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3} \in \mathbb{C}$. The positive roots are
$$\Phi^{+}=\left\{\begin{array}{lll}
\alpha_{1}, & \alpha_{2}, & \alpha_{3}, \\
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}, & \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, & \alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, \\
2 \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, & \alpha_{1}+2 \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, & 2 \alpha_{1}+2 \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}
\end{array}\right\},$$
where
$$\begin{aligned}
& \alpha_{1}(h)=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}, \\
& \alpha_{2}(h)=\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}, \\
& \alpha_{3}(h)=2 \lambda_{3} .
\end{aligned}$$
Define a change of basis
$$\beta_{1}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\frac{1}{2} \alpha_{3}, \quad \beta_{2}=\alpha_{2}+\frac{1}{2} \alpha_{3}, \quad \beta_{3}=\frac{1}{2} \alpha_{3} .$$
The fundamental reflections are the same as those for $B_{3}$ :
$$\begin{array}{rlrl}
s_{i}\left(\beta_{i}\right) & =\beta_{i+1}, & s_{i}\left(\beta_{i+1}\right) & =\beta_{i}, \\
s_{i}\left(\beta_{j}\right) & =\beta_{j}, & & j=1,2, \\
s_{3}\left(\beta_{3}\right) & =-\beta_{3}, & s_{3}\left(\beta_{i}\right) & =\beta_{i}, \\
& i \neq 3 .
\end{array}$$
The Weyl group generated by the fundamental reflections is then seen to be isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3} \rtimes S_{3}$ : The isomorphism is given by $s_{1} \mapsto(1,2) \in S_{3}, s_{2} \mapsto(2,3) \in S_{3}$, and $s_{3} \mapsto(0,0,1) \in \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}$. The actions of $s_{1}, s_{2}$, and $s_{3}$ on the simple roots $\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}$, and $\alpha_{3}$ are
$$\begin{array}{lll}
s_{1}\left(\alpha_{1}\right)=-\alpha_{1}, & s_{2}\left(\alpha_{1}\right)=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}, & s_{3}\left(\alpha_{1}\right)=\alpha_{1}, \\
s_{1}\left(\alpha_{2}\right)=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}, & s_{2}\left(\alpha_{2}\right)=-\alpha_{2}, & s_{3}\left(\alpha_{2}\right)=\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, \\
s_{1}\left(\alpha_{3}\right)=\alpha_{3} & s_{2}\left(\alpha_{3}\right)=2 \alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}, & s_{3}\left(\alpha_{3}\right)=-\alpha_{3} .
\end{array}$$

The correspondence, due to the Killing form, between $\mathfrak{h}^{*}$ and $\mathfrak{h}$ determines an action of the Weyl group on $\mathfrak{h}$. Specifically, a computation shows that we have the correspondence
$$\operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3}\right) \longleftrightarrow 2\left(\lambda_{1} \beta_{1}+\lambda_{2} \beta_{2}+\lambda_{3} \beta_{3}\right) .$$

The actions of $s_{1}, s_{2}$, and $s_{3}$ on $\mathfrak{h}$ are given by:
$$\begin{aligned}
& s_{1} \cdot \operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3}\right)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{2}, \lambda_{1}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{3}\right), \\
& s_{2} \cdot \operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3}\right)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{3}, \lambda_{2},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{3},-\lambda_{2}\right), \\
& s_{3} \cdot \operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3}\right)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3},-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2}, \lambda_{3}\right) .
\end{aligned}$$

We now describe a set of generators for $\mathfrak{s p}(6, \mathbb{C})$. Number matrix rows and columns $1,2,3,-1,-2,-3$. Then, positive root vectors $e_{\alpha}$ and negative root vectors $f_{\alpha}$ corresponding to the simple roots are
$$\begin{array}{ll}
e_{\alpha_{1}}=E_{12}-E_{-2,-1}, & f_{\alpha_{1}}=E_{21}-E_{-1,-2}, \\
e_{\alpha_{2}}=E_{23}-E_{-3,-2}, & f_{\alpha_{2}}=E_{32}-E_{-2,-3}, \\
e_{\alpha_{3}}=E_{3,-3}, & f_{\alpha_{3}}=E_{-3,3} .
\end{array}$$
These root vectors determine the following basis of the Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ :
$$\begin{aligned}
h_{\alpha_{1}} & =\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0,-1,1,0), \\
h_{\alpha_{2}} & =\operatorname{diag}(0,1,-1,0,-1,1), \\
h_{\alpha_{3}} & =\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1,0,0,-1) .
\end{aligned}$$

A $W$-invariant inner product on $\mathfrak{h}$ is defined by
$$h \cdot h^{\prime}=\operatorname{trace}\left(h h^{\prime}\right) .$$

We now wish to classify the regular subalgebras of $\mathfrak{s p}(6, \mathbb{C})$. To do so, for each closed subset $T$ of the root system of type $C_{3}$, we will determine the possible $\mathfrak{t} \subset \mathfrak{h}$ such that $\mathfrak{s}_{T, \mathfrak{t}}$ is a regular subalgebra. The cases in which $\mathfrak{t}$ is 0 - or 3 -dimensional are trivial, so we only consider the 1- and 2-dimensional cases.

In the following example, we explicitly describe the process of determining the regular subalgebras obtained from a given closed subset of $C_{3}$. Determining the regular subalgebras for all other closed subsets of $C_{3}$ is obtained in a similar manner, and the complete results are summarized in Tables 22 through 29.

Example 5.5. Consider the symmetric closed subset $T=\left\{\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{1}\right\}$ of $C_{3}$, which is located in row 79 of Table 9. A calculation shows that the stabilizer of $T$, denoted $W_{T}$, is generated by the elements $s_{1}, s_{3}$, and $s_{2} s_{1} s_{3} s_{2} s_{1} s_{3} s_{2}$.

Clearly there is only one 1-dimensional $\mathfrak{t}$, which is given by $\left\langle h_{\alpha_{1}}\right\rangle$. We now determine the 2-dimensional $\mathfrak{t}$, each of which must contain $h_{\alpha_{1}}$. Each such subspace is determined by a vector orthogonal to it, and since $\mathfrak{t}$ contains $h_{\alpha_{1}}$, the orthogonal vector must be of the form $\operatorname{diag}(a, a, b,-a,-a,-b)$.

The actions of the generators of $W_{T}$ on $\operatorname{diag}(a, a, b,-a,-a,-b)$ are
$$\begin{aligned}
s_{1} \cdot \operatorname{diag}(a, a, b,-a,-a,-b) & =\operatorname{diag}(a, a, b,-a,-a,-b), \\
s_{3} \cdot \operatorname{diag}(a, a, b,-a,-a,-b) & =\operatorname{diag}(a, a,-b,-a,-a, b), \\
s_{2} s_{1} s_{3} s_{2} s_{1} s_{3} s_{2} \cdot \operatorname{diag}(a, a, b,-a,-a,-b) & =\operatorname{diag}(-a,-a, b, a, a,-b) .
\end{aligned}$$
Hence, by Corollary 5.2, the space orthogonal to $\operatorname{diag}(0, a, a, b,-a,-a,-b)$ is conjugate to that orthogonal to $\operatorname{diag}\left(a^{\prime}, a^{\prime}, b^{\prime},-a^{\prime},-a^{\prime},-b^{\prime}\right)$ if and only if $\left(a^{\prime}, a^{\prime}, b^{\prime}\right)$ is a scalar multiple of $(a, a, \pm b)$.
