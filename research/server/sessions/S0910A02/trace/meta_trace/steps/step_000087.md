## Deep-Reasoning

This Agent4 Execute step changed the negative-branch landscape by separating three objects that had been conflated: gauge-theoretic no-Einstein certificates, the special A1-resolution construction class, and homotopy/entropy no-Einstein certificates. The BF/monopole audit from Step 86 survives but only with the intended scope. Iida--Mukherjee--Taniguchi prove that a compact oriented Spin^c 4-manifold with contact boundary and nonzero relative Bauer--Furuta-type invariant cannot contain a non-torsion embedded 2-sphere of nonnegative self-intersection; their later relative BF theorem gives the same obstruction for rational-homology-sphere homotopy-L-space boundary, and their gluing formulas explicitly record BF(D^4)=Id, BF(X^o)=BF(X) for the punctured closed manifold, and connected-sum composition. Thus the reversed A1 rows, whose reversed node inserts a non-torsion +2 sphere, cannot carry the standard relative BF/nonzero closed BF data used by Ishida--LeBrun/Kotschick-type no-Einstein arguments, provided the ball is chosen away from the sphere and the boundary/contact/Spin^c hypotheses are matched. Compatible A1 rows remain in the Kähler smoothing/ample-canonical regime where the known construction gives negative KE metrics rather than no-Einstein examples. This is a disjointness theorem for the known BF/monopole certificates within the A1 construction machine; it is not a theorem that every conceivable gauge invariant vanishes, and it is not a class-level negative-pinching obstruction. On the positive side there is a true class-level disjointness: tight positive Ricci pinching gives positive scalar curvature and the Weitzenböck argument kills monopole classes. On the negative side there is no such class-level exclusion; LeBrun-type scalar estimates are compatible with negative Ricci pinching by integral accounting, and another mechanism could still put tight negative pinching on a LeBrun- or entropy-obstructed manifold.

The major correction is that the field already has a non-monopole all-sign no-Einstein reservoir inside the pinching-compatible topological window: Sambusetti's Besson--Courtois--Gallot entropy obstruction. The primary Sambusetti paper was not acquired in full during this step despite focused knowledge requests, but the source network is strong enough to identify the theorem and its constants. LeBrun's 1996 footnote states that Sambusetti used BCG entropy inequalities to show, for example, that the connected sum of two copies of any closed real-hyperbolic 4-manifold never admits an Einstein metric despite satisfying strict Hitchin--Thorpe. Kotschick's full-text `Entropies, Volumes, and Einstein Metrics` gives the exact modern formulation: any closed oriented Einstein 4-manifold X satisfies \(\chi(X)\ge \frac32|\sigma(X)|+\lambda(X)^4/(108\pi^2)\), with equality only in the flat, locally Calabi--Yau, or constant negative curvature cases; combining this with BCG gives Sambusetti's corollary that if X admits a nonzero-degree map of degree d to a closed real-hyperbolic 4-manifold Y, then \(\chi(X)\ge \frac32|\sigma(X)|+|d|\chi(Y)\), equality only if X is real hyperbolic. For \(M=N\# N\), where N is closed real-hyperbolic with sectional curvature -1, there is a degree-2 map \(M\to N\) obtained by collapsing the neck and mapping both summands by the identity; \(\chi(M)=2\chi(N)-2\), \(\tau(M)=0\), and simplicial volume is positive and additive. Sambusetti's inequality would require \(2\chi(N)-2\ge2\chi(N)\), impossible. This settles all Einstein signs for this family, not by monopoles, Kähler geometry, symplectic structure, or simple connectivity, but by entropy and homotopy degree. It also shows Step 85's 'only known reservoir' framing was false.

For Yau--26 the decisive issue is no longer merely whether \(N\#N\) is Einstein-free; it is whether the entropy obstruction is quantitatively stable for near-negative Ricci pinching and whether matching metrics can be constructed at the critical rate. The Bessières--Besson--Courtois--Gallot full-text source states a Ricci-lower-bound form of BCG: if f:Y->X has nonzero degree and X is closed hyperbolic, then every metric g on Y with \(\operatorname{Ric}_g\ge-(n-1)g\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Vol}_g(Y)\ge |\deg f|\operatorname{Vol}(X)\), with equality only for a Riemannian covering; their Corollary 1.7 further gives, for fixed X, an \(\varepsilon>0\) such that any \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge-(n-1)g\) metric on \(X\#X\) has volume at least \(2(1+\varepsilon)\operatorname{Vol}(X)\). Thus the BCG lower-volume side does extend beyond Einstein metrics under Ricci lower bounds. The upper-volume side for a metric satisfying \(\|\operatorname{Ric}+3g\|_{op}\le\delta\) should come from four-dimensional Chern--Gauss--Bonnet: eigenvalues \(-3+\eta_i\), \(|\eta_i|\le\delta\), give \(s=-12+O(\delta)\) and \(|E|=O(\delta)\), hence \(8\pi^2\chi\ge (6-O(\delta))\operatorname{Vol}\). For \(N\#N\), this is approximately \(\operatorname{Vol}\le2\operatorname{Vol}(N)(1-1/\chi(N))(1+O(\delta))\), while the Ricci-lower-bound/BCG estimate after rescaling gives at least \(2\operatorname{Vol}(N)(1-O(\delta))\), possibly with an additional fixed-X gap. Therefore the entropy obstruction should rule out defects below a constant multiple of \(1/\chi(N)\), not all defects tending to zero. This converts A03's hyperbolic connector problem into a sharp negative-branch target: constructing metrics on \(N\#N\) with defect \(O(1/\chi(N))\) would refute \(P_4^-\), while proving a stronger lower bound on the neck turnaround defect would support the negative branch. The geometric tension is clear: the hyperbolic pieces have \(\operatorname{Ric}=-3g\) exactly and can be made large, but the neck or waist where the torus/sphere scale turns around tends to pay order-one Ricci anisotropy unless its cost can be spread over a scale comparable to the hyperbolic volume. This mechanism does not touch \(P_4^+\), since nonnegative Ricci forces zero simplicial volume/entropy in the relevant exact Einstein cases, so the positive branch remains with the mixed-row or proof-side program.
