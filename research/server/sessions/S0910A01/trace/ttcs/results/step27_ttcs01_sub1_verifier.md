## 1. Short-time existence

### Effect of \(-2\chi^2g\)

For fixed \(a=\chi^2\), the linearization of the added term is algebraic:

\[
D_g(-2ag)[h]=-2ah.
\]

After DeTurck gauge fixing, the principal metric operator remains schematically

\[
\partial_t h-a\,g^{ij}\partial_i\partial_jh,
\]

with \(-2ah\) entering only at zeroth order. In Yang’s transported-coefficient formulation, \(a\circ\varphi^{-1}\) appears in front of both \(\operatorname{Ric}(\hat g)\) and \(\hat g\); pulling back recovers the original fixed \(a\). Thus the normalization does not alter the principal symbol or the tame derivative count in a Nash–Moser construction.

### Problem with the \(\varepsilon\)-argument

For

\[
\partial_tg_\varepsilon=-2(\varepsilon^2+a)(\operatorname{Ric}_{g_\varepsilon}+g_\varepsilon),
\]

strict parabolicity holds for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), but the ellipticity constant is \(\varepsilon^2\). Ordinary Schauder or Sobolev constants therefore need not remain uniform as \(\varepsilon\to0\).

The implication in §1 of the continuation note,

\[
\int |\operatorname{Rm}_{g_\varepsilon}|^{2}\le C
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_\varepsilon\ge T_0,\qquad
\|g_\varepsilon\|_{C^k}\le C_k,
\]

does not follow. In four dimensions an \(L^2\) curvature bound does not control pointwise curvature or metric \(C^k\) norms. Arzelà–Ascoli therefore cannot be invoked from (1.3) alone.

At the energy level, the normalization terms can plausibly be treated by Gronwall. For \(b=\varepsilon^2+a\), the variable conformal part contributes terms of the form

\[
\int_M \langle\operatorname{Rm},g\owedge\nabla^2b\rangle\,d\mu,
\]

which, on a provisional interval with controlled cutoff derivatives, satisfy

\[
\left|\int \langle\operatorname{Rm},g\owedge\nabla^2b\rangle\right|
\le C\|\operatorname{Rm}\|_2\|\nabla^2b\|_2
\le C\int|\operatorname{Rm}|^2+C\operatorname{Vol}(M).
\]

Hence an inequality

\[
\mathcal E_\varepsilon'(t)
\le C\mathcal E_\varepsilon(t)+C\operatorname{Vol}_{g_\varepsilon(t)}(M)
\]

does give a uniform energy estimate once volume and cutoff derivatives are already controlled. It does not provide the uniform high regularity needed to pass to the degenerate flow.

Accordingly, Theorem 1.1 is best grounded directly in Yang’s degenerate local-flow theorem or its Nash–Moser proof, with the extra tame zeroth-order map \(g\mapsto-2ag\). The regularization proof currently written is insufficient. The same applies to the sentence invoking “standard parabolic uniqueness”: uniqueness for the degenerate equation needs Yang’s argument or a corresponding degenerate DeTurck estimate.

## 2. Extension under \(\lvert a\operatorname{Rm}\rvert\le K\)

### Covariant derivatives of \(ag\)

With the evolving Levi-Civita connection,

\[
\nabla g=0
\]

identically at every time. Therefore

\[
\nabla^m(ag)=(\nabla^m a)*g.
\]

There are no terms involving \(\nabla^jg\), nor derivatives of Christoffel symbols obtained by differentiating \(g\) as though it were an ordinary coordinate tensor. Thus the concern that \(\nabla^m(ag)\) directly requires \(m\)-th derivatives of the connection is resolved by metric compatibility.

However, the assertion in Theorem 2.1 that “\(\nabla^i\chi\) is time-independent” is incorrect for \(i\ge2\). The scalar function is fixed, but its covariant Hessian and higher covariant derivatives depend on \(g(t)\). Schematically,

\[
\partial_t\Gamma=g^{-1}*\nabla(aE)
=g^{-1}*\bigl(\nabla(a\operatorname{Ric})+(\nabla a)*g\bigr),
\]

and for \(m\ge2\),

\[
\partial_t(\nabla^m a)
=
\sum_{r+s=m-2}
\nabla^r(\partial_t\Gamma)*\nabla^{s+1}a.
\]

The highest curvature derivative here is of order \(m-1\) in \(a\operatorname{Ric}\), so Wang’s coupled induction can remain triangular if it simultaneously estimates \(\nabla^m a\) and weighted curvature derivatives. Metric equivalence by itself controls \(|da|\), but not \(\nabla^2a,\nabla^3a,\ldots\).

### Terms omitted from the displayed higher-derivative equation

The curvature variation produced by \(-2ag\) contains

\[
a*\operatorname{Rm}+(\nabla^2a)*g.
\]

After applying \(\nabla^m\), this generates

\[
\sum_{i+j=m}\nabla^ia*\nabla^j\operatorname{Rm}
\quad\text{and}\quad
(\nabla^{m+2}a)*g,
\]

plus commutator terms. The continuation note lists only derivatives of \(a\) up to order \(m\), so it misses the \(\nabla^{m+2}a\) contribution. It also describes the pointwise curvature source using only \(a\operatorname{Rm}\) and \((\nabla\chi)^2g\), whereas

Wang’s original local-flow induction already has to manage high cutoff derivatives because the coefficient \(a\operatorname{Ric}\) itself produces \(\nabla^2a*\operatorname{Ric}\). The normalized term is therefore structurally compatible with a sufficiently detailed coupled hierarchy, but that hierarchy is not supplied in Theorem 2.1. In particular, the sentence invoking metric equivalence and “time-independent” cutoff derivatives does not establish smooth extension.

The estimate

\[
0<t_0<\min\{T_*,T_1\}.

\[
K_0=\sup_{0\le t\le t_0}\|a\operatorname{Rm}(t)\|_\infty<\infty.

\[
\sup_{t_0\le t<T_*}\|a\operatorname{Rm}(t)\|_\infty

Consequently,

\[
\sup_{0\le t<T_*}\|a\operatorname{Rm}(t)\|_\infty

The extension theorem can then be applied at the terminal time \(T_*\). ## 4. Three-factor Moser iteration

### Algebra

The central identity is exact:

\[
a^{p-1}Ff^p
=(aF^3)^{1/3}
(a^{p-2}f^p)^{1/3}
\left(a^{(p-1)/2}f^{p/2}\right)^{4/3}.
\]

The powers are

\[
\frac13+\frac{p-2}{3}+\frac{2(p-1)}3=p-1,
\qquad
\frac p3+\frac{2p}3=p.
\]

Because \(\eta=1\) wherever \(a>0\), omission of \(\eta\) in this factorization is legitimate. Hölder gives

\[
R_p\le Y^{1/3}H_p^{1/3}\|z_p\|_4^{4/3}.
\]

Combining

\[
\|z_p\|_4^2\le C_pS(D_p+r^{-2}H_p)
\]

with Young’s inequality yields, with a polynomial power \(p^N\),

\[
C_pR_p
\le \varepsilon D_p
+Cp^N\bigl(r^{-2}+S^2Y\bigr)H_p.
\]

The precise exponent \(N\) is immaterial for the infinite product, provided it is fixed and finite.

The iteration should use the pointwise estimate

\le \frac{2C\beta^3}{t},

Then every Moser step has an algebraic factor

\[
\left[Cp_k^N4^kS(t^{-1}+r^{-2})\right]^{1/p_k},

## 5. Terminal calibration and the Derdziński germ

Metric comparison alone cannot transport Weyl data from \(g(0)\) to \(g(T)\). Curvature depends on second derivatives of the metric, while F1 provides only bilipschitz comparison. The scientifically valid mechanism is to apply the topological and Seiberg–Witten inequalities anew to the terminal metric.

For each pointwise density \(\mathcal D_j(g)\ge0\), the needed terminal statement has the form

\[
0\le
\frac1A\int_M\mathcal D_j(g(T))\,d\mu_{g(T)}
\le
C\frac{Q_{\mathrm{global}}(T)}A+o(1).
\]

Since the topology is unchanged and

\[
Q_{\mathrm{global}}(T)
\le e^{C\theta}Q_{\mathrm{global}}(0),
\]

this would yield vanishing terminal deficits. No explicit Step 5–8 formulas establishing these inequalities for an arbitrary terminal metric are included among the supplied artifacts, so this part cannot be independently verified here.

Two further points matter:

1. On a plateau at \(T=\theta r^2\),

   \[
   \|E(T)\|_\infty
   \le C\delta\theta^{-1/3}\to0.
   \]

   Hence any smooth local limit there is Einstein. This is stronger locally than the global \(L^2\) estimate.

2. The selected balls may collapse. Bounded curvature does not provide a uniform ordinary harmonic radius without a volume lower bound; the Maheux–Saloff-Coste constant itself scales as \(V^{-1/2}\). Local exponential pullbacks below the conjugate radius, or suitable local covers, can provide smooth germs under the plateau curvature and Shi derivative estimates. To obtain the calibrated spectrum on an open germ, the global terminal deficit integral must first be localized to a plateau ball with small relative mean and then upgraded to uniform smallness on a smaller ball using derivative bounds and relative volume comparison.

Finally, Derdziński’s theorem requires an Einstein limit, hence harmonic \(W^+\), together with the exact two-eigenvalue structure and \(W^+\neq0\) on an open set. The supplied chain lists “calibrated Weyl spectrum” and “forbidden germ” but does not provide the algebraic spectrum, its open-set localization, or the precise incompatibility with the resulting local Kähler structure.
