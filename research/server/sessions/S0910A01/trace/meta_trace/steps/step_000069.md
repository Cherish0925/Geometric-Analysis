This step strengthened the restricted Ishida--LeBrun negative-gap line by correcting two load-bearing regressions introduced in the Step 68 assembly, while preserving the larger Yau--26 quantifier structure. The important mathematical point is that the IL smoothing argument is not a local bounded-sectional-curvature Ricci-flow argument. It is a critical four-dimensional mean-curvature mechanism: the global Chern--Weil/topological budget gives \(\int F_T^2\le C A_i\), maximal-function selection transfers this to the whole auxiliary buffer only if the maximal radius satisfies \(R_0\ge L_\eta r\), and collapse is handled through the product \(\mathcal S_\alpha H_{2,\alpha}^{1/2}\), not through either factor separately. In collapse, \(\mathcal S_\alpha\) may diverge like \(r^2\operatorname{Vol}(U_\alpha)^{-1/2}\), while \(H_{2,\alpha}^{1/2}\) scales like \(\operatorname{Vol}(U_\alpha)^{1/2}\), so the small object is \(\gamma_\alpha(0)\le C(r^2+\widetilde\gamma)\). This preserves the scientific reason the cutoff flow was introduced: it converts critical averaged curvature on selected buffered supports into positive-time pointwise plateau curvature; it must not assume at time zero the very curvature regularity it is designed to produce. The second correction concerns time structure in the defect Moser argument. The coefficient \(\mathcal S^2Y\lesssim\gamma^3/t\) is a positive-time consequence of the curvature branch and cannot be integrated from zero. The time-zero input is the independent integrated \(q=2\) curvature-energy estimate \(K_2^\eta(t)+\int_0^tD_2^\eta\le CK_2^\eta(0)\), which gives \(\int_0^t \mathcal S^2Y\le C\gamma^3\). Only after this does one select a good slice, propagate the \(q=3\) moment, and use \(\mathcal S^2Y\lesssim\gamma^3/t\) on nested positive-time slabs. This separation removes a false logarithmic singularity and keeps the defect endpoint tied to the curvature branch without circular dependence. The repaired assembly is therefore more faithful to the real analytic mechanism: support selection supplies collapse-cancelling smallness; the curvature branch supplies both integrated and positive-time control; the defect branch improves \(V=\int\|aE\|_\infty\); and the terminal IL calibration plus Liouville pullback converts \(Q(T)=o(A_i)\) into the forbidden Derdzinski germ. It remains a restricted four-dimensional negative-sign theorem assembly, not the sign-unrestricted all-dimensional Yau--26 resolution. The broader research judgment also sharpened: even a surface-bundle family with \(\delta_-\to0\) and no Einstein metric of any sign would only defeat intervals whose normalized negative branch is relevant; the positive branch still must be defeated, possibly by different manifolds, before the displayed proposition is refuted. Conversely, a positive proof of Yau--26 need not imitate the IL budget/forbidden-germ route; any dimension-only mechanism forcing some Einstein metric on the underlying smooth manifold would suffice. A02's flux/kernel work provides stable local algebra and a useful Lean asset, but it reinforces rather than weakens this distinction: local A1 annulus flux identities, scalar/gauge kernel separation, and formalized rational constants are valuable for desingularization and bubbling analysis, yet they do not exclude far-away Einstein metrics on the same smooth manifold.
