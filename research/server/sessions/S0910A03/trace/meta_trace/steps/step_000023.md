The full Yau 26 proposition remains far beyond the present four-dimensional negative laboratory, but this step materially reorganized its central smoothing problem. Re-reading Yang and Wang showed an additional simplification: their critical local-Ricci-flow mechanism is naturally formulated on one controlled support with one flow cutoff, whereas the recent global micro-component graph was an artifact of trying to cover controlled and uncontrolled regions simultaneously.

The existing maximal-function/Vitali construction already produces good macro-supports whose inner balls retain a fixed positive volume proportion after bounded-loss thinning. On each such support, an auxiliary cutoff can equal one near the entire support of the flow cutoff. Then the unweighted factor in Hölder is controlled by the same component energy, while the Sobolev variables retain the flow cutoff: v₂=χF and v₃=χ²F^{3/2}. Their gradients contain precisely the component dissipation plus r^{-2} zero-order terms. This removes both the unsupported outer-to-inner dissipation absorption in Step 22 and the need for a maximal net through the bad region. It also aligns the argument with Yang's one-support Theorem 9.2 and Wang's fixed q=2,3 calculations.

At the integrated tensor level, the decisive structure is preserved by keeping the velocity as -2aE, with E=Ric+g. The curvature variation has the form aΔRm+a(Rm*Rm+Rm)+da*∇E+(∇²a)*E. Since E and ∇E are contractions of Rm+g and ∇Rm, one integration by parts converts every Hessian-a term into the existing tensor-gradient dissipation and the regular factor |da|²/a=4|dχ|². Inverse-metric and measure variations contribute only the weighted curvature reaction. Thus, on a provisional smooth bilipschitz/Sobolev interval, the q=2 and q=3 energies satisfy matched weak inequalities with no evolving cutoff-Hessian bound.

The temporal issue is now explicit. The q=2 estimate gives only an integral of H₃. A good slice in an earlier part of the interval is propagated to the endpoint using the separate fixed-q=3 inequality H₃'≤Cr^{-2}H₃. This rules out narrow terminal spikes and yields the correctly normalized estimate S_j²H₃,j(t)≤Cγ_j³/t, where S_j is the relative Sobolev coefficient and γ_j=S_j||F||₂. The same support geometry also simplifies the homogeneous defect side: global Q obeys Q'≤Cr^{-2}Q after componentwise curvature absorption, and fixed J₆ persists from time zero with its δ⁶ factor, without integrating a 1/t potential from zero.

The symbolic and scaling computations confirm the exact cutoff exponents, collapse-volume cancellation γ=C r²√K₀, cancellation in the defect smoothing prefactor, integrability of metric drift for p>2, and normalization signs on homothetic constant-curvature solutions. They also correct the stationary-model test: when E=0 the total flow and all time derivatives vanish, but H₃ is generally positive and F can vary spatially. What remains is substantial: the universal contraction estimates need a fully written tensor proof with fixed constants; the enlarged support radii and positive-volume thinning must be verified in the actual geometry; positive-time subcritical curvature and defect iterations must be completed; their integrable velocity must close metric and Sobolev comparability; and Yang-Wang continuation must be adapted to the normalized lower-order term. Only then can the bounded-curvature plateaux feed the exponential-chart and calibrated-projector contradiction.
