## Summary

The central Step 20 observation is mathematically valid: with all tensors and covariant derivatives taken relative to the current metric \(g(t)\),
\[
E=\operatorname{Ric}+g=\operatorname{tr}_{13}\operatorname{Rm}+g,
\qquad
\nabla E=\operatorname{tr}_{13}(\nabla\operatorname{Rm}),
\]
so the first derivatives of \(E\) generated by integrating \(\nabla^2a\) by parts can be controlled by the full \(\nabla\operatorname{Rm}\) dissipation. A separate high-power \(\nabla E\) energy is unnecessary for this curvature-power calculation.

The triangular mechanism for the unweighted \(L^2\) defect is also structurally correct: because the velocity is \(-2aE\), the evolution of \(E\) is homogeneous in \(E\) and its derivatives. Thus curvature enters the defect energy as a potential multiplying \(|E|^2\), rather than through an additive curvature-only term.

Several formulas still need correction or completion:

1. The term \((\nabla a)*\operatorname{Rm}*E\) in (7.1) is not produced by the Ricci variation formula and is inconsistent with (7.2). It should be removed; algebraic curvature terms are \(a\,\operatorname{Rm}*E\), usually already included in \(a\Delta_LE\).
2. The normalized \(a\operatorname{Rm}\) term does not arise from \(a\nabla^2E\); it arises from the algebraic \(h*\operatorname{Rm}\) part of curvature variation.
3. The exponent table in `step20_tensor_closed_curvature_energy.py` is illustrative only. The displayed inequality (5.1) by itself gives worse \(q\)-dependence than the table unless the enhanced radial diffusion term is retained.
4. Reaching a fixed \(q_0>2\) from only initial \(L^2\) curvature has not been proved. Initial \(L^{q_0}\) can be arbitrarily large even when the critical \(L^2\) quantity is small.
5. The countable Whitney summation and time-uniform small Sobolev product still require a rigorous local energy-persistence construction.

## 1. Contraction identities and Hessian-\(a\) terms

For the current Levi-Civita connection,
\[
E_{ij}=g^{k\ell}R_{kilj}+g_{ij},
\qquad
\nabla_pE_{ij}=g^{k\ell}\nabla_pR_{kilj},
\]
because \(\nabla g=0\). Consequently,
\[
|\nabla E|\le C_n|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|,
\qquad
|E|\le C_n(1+|\operatorname{Rm}|^2)^{1/2}=C_nF.
\]
This would not be exact if \(\nabla\) denoted the fixed initial connection; the current-connection convention is essential.

For
\[
I_q=q\int\eta^2F^{q-2}\operatorname{Rm}*(\nabla^2a)*E,
\]
one integration by parts gives terms containing
\[
\nabla a*\nabla\operatorname{Rm}*E,\qquad
\nabla a*\operatorname{Rm}*\nabla E,\qquad
(q-2)\nabla a*\nabla F*\operatorname{Rm}*E,
\]
plus localization terms. The contraction identities reduce every derivative term to
\[
Cq^2\int\eta^2|\nabla a|F^{q-1}|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|.
\]
Using
\[
\frac{|\nabla a|^2}{a}=4|\nabla\chi|^2
\]
on \(\{\chi>0\}\), Young’s inequality produces weighted curvature dissipation and a regular zeroth-order term. Therefore explicit control of \(\nabla^2a\) is not needed in this weak energy calculation, and no inverse power of \(\chi\) appears.

The coarse estimate
\[
\varepsilon q\int\eta^2aF^{q-2}|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|^2
 +C_\varepsilon q^3\int\eta^2\frac{|\nabla a|^2}{a}F^q
\]
is valid. A sharper calculation can use the additional diffusion
\[
q(q-2)\int \eta^2aF^{q-2}|\nabla F|^2
\]
to reduce the worst cutoff coefficient from \(q^3\) to approximately \(q^2\).

The script’s choice \(C_E=\sqrt{n+1}\) is not established as a valid tensor constant. A simple universally valid estimate is
\[
|E|^2\le 2n\bigl(1+|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\bigr),
\]
so \(C_E=\sqrt{2n}\) suffices. This numerical constant has no effect on the structural argument.

## 2. Curvature variation and stationarity at \(E=0\)

For a fixed scalar cutoff \(a=\chi^2\) and \(h=-2aE\), the curvature variation has the schematic form
\[
\partial_t\operatorname{Rm}
 =a\Delta\operatorname{Rm}
  +a\,\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm}
  +a\,\operatorname{Rm}
  +(\nabla a)*\nabla E
  +(\nabla^2a)*E
  +aE*\operatorname{Rm},
\]
with convention-dependent redistribution among the algebraic terms.

Every derivative-of-\(a\) contribution is multiplied by \(E\) or \(\nabla E\). Thus an exact metric satisfying \(E=0\) is stationary even where \(a\) varies. This is an exact consequence of inserting the full velocity \(h=-2aE\) into the linear curvature variation.

Equation (2.2) assigns the normalized \(a\operatorname{Rm}\) term to \(a\nabla^2E\). Since \(\nabla^2g=0\), that attribution is inaccurate. The linear normalized term comes from the algebraic \(h*\operatorname{Rm}\) part associated with the \(-2ag\) component. The final energy ledger remains unchanged because it is bounded by \(CaF^{q+1}\) or \(CaF^q\).

The evolving norm and volume form add
\[
\partial_t|\operatorname{Rm}|^2
 =2\langle\partial_t\operatorname{Rm},\operatorname{Rm}\rangle
   +aE*\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm},
\qquad
\partial_t d\mu=-a\,\operatorname{tr}E\,d\mu.
\]
Using \(|E|\le C_nF\), these are precisely of reaction type \(CaF^{q+1}\). Thus they do not disrupt closure in curvature.

## 3. The \(q\)-dependent inequality

The schematic inequality
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac d{dt}\int\eta^2F^q
 +cq\int\eta^2aF^{q-2}|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|^2

&+Cq^3\int\eta^2\left(\frac{|\nabla a|^2}{a}+a\right)F^q\\
&+Cq\int a|\nabla\eta|^2F^q
\end{aligned}
\]
is scientifically plausible and has the correct structural terms. It still needs an indexed derivation fixing the tensor type of \(\operatorname{Rm}\), Laplacian sign, and all constants.

On a Whitney ball, the Sobolev estimate gives
\[
\int\eta^2aF^{q+1}
 \le \beta_\ell\left[
 \int a|\nabla(\eta F^{q/2})|^2
 +\ell^{-2}\int a\eta^2F^q\right].
\]
Expanding the gradient introduces a factor \(q^2\). From the coarse dissipation displayed in (5.1), absorption requires roughly
\[
Cq^2\beta_\ell<c.
\]
If the enhanced radial diffusion is retained, this can improve to approximately
\[
Cq\beta_\ell<c.

Either form worsens as \(q\to\infty\). Therefore the warning that critical \(L^2\) smallness does not automatically support direct absorption at every exponent is correct.

Thus (6.2) needs an additional \(Cr^2\) term, or \(K_0\) must be replaced by \(1+K_0\).

## 4. Triangular unweighted defect energy

The correct schematic evolution is
\[
\partial_tE
 =a\Delta_LE-2aE
   +\mathcal A(\nabla a,\nabla E)
   +\mathcal B(\nabla^2a,E),
\]
where \(\Delta_L\) contains algebraic curvature action. Depending on convention, one may also display \(a\,\operatorname{Rm}*E\) separately.

The printed term
\[
(\nabla a)*\operatorname{Rm}*E
\]
in (7.1) is not generated by \(D\operatorname{Ric}(aE)\), whose terms are of the forms
\[
a\nabla^2E,\quad \nabla a*\nabla E,\quad
\nabla^2a*E,\quad a\,\operatorname{Rm}*E.
\]
Moreover, the printed term would yield a potential
\(|\nabla a||\operatorname{Rm}||E|^2\), which is not controlled by the right side of (7.2). It should therefore be removed or replaced by \(a\,\operatorname{Rm}*E\).

After that correction, testing by \(\eta^2E\), including norm and volume variation, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac d{dt}\int\eta^2|E|^2
 +c\int\eta^2a|\nabla E|^2
\le C\int\eta^2
 \left(\frac{|\nabla a|^2}{a}+a+a|\operatorname{Rm}|\right)|E|^2
 +C\int a|\nabla\eta|^2|E|^2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
The cubic terms from evolving norms and volume satisfy
\[
a|E|^3\le C a(1+|\operatorname{Rm}|)|E|^2.
\]

On a Whitney ball,
\[
\int a|\operatorname{Rm}|\,|\eta E|^2
 \le \|\operatorname{Rm}\|_2
       \|\chi\eta E\|_4^2.
\]
Tensor Kato and the local Sobolev inequality give
\[
\le C\beta_\ell\int\eta^2a|\nabla E|^2
 +C\beta_\ell(c^{-2}+L^2)r^{-2}
       \int_{\widetilde W}|E|^2.
\]
Thus sufficiently small, time-uniform \(\beta_\ell\) absorbs the curvature potential without introducing any additive curvature-dissipation quantity. This directly repairs the invalid inference involving \(\varepsilon X\).

For a countable Whitney cover, the required global estimate follows if:

- expanded Whitney balls remain in \(\{\chi>0\}\);
- enlarged intersecting balls have comparable radii;
- overlap multiplicity and graph degree are uniformly bounded;
- local energies are unnormalized integrals;
- the local curvature budget holds on each required enlargement;
- infinite sums are obtained from finite truncations and monotone convergence.

Under these assumptions,
\[
Q_{\rm active}(T)
 \le \exp\!\left(C(c,L,N)T/r^2\right)Q_{\rm active}(0).
\]
The metric is stationary on the complement of the active support, so
\[
Q(T)\le C\,Q(0).
\]
For \(T=\theta r^2\), the factor is uniform. Hence if a topology parameter \(\mathcal A_k\) satisfies
\[
Q_k(0)/\mathcal A_k\to0,
\]
then the same holds at time \(T\), provided \(c,L,N,\theta\), metric comparability, and the small \(\beta_\ell\) are uniform in \(k\).
