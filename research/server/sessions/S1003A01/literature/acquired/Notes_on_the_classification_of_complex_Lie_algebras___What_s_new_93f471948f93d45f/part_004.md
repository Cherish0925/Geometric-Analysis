_Proof:_ If ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) can be covered by two orthogonal subspaces ![{V \cup W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV+%5Ccup+W%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then if we consider the subspace of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

![\displaystyle V \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi \cap V} {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+V+%5Coplus+%5Cbigoplus_%7B%5Calpha+%5Cin+%5CPhi+%5Ccap+V%7D+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

where we use the inner product ![{\langle,\rangle}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle%2C%5Crangle%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to identify ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and thus ![{V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with a subspace of ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (thus for instance this identifies ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{t_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bt_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)), then one can check using [(19)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#add) and [(13)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#k-bracket) that this is a proper ideal of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), contradicting simplicity. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

It is easy to see that every root system is expressible as the union of irreducible root systems (on orthogonal subspaces of ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)). As it turns out, the irreducible root systems are completely classified, with the complete list of root systems (up to isomorphism) being described in terms of the [Dynkin diagrams](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynkin_diagram)![{A_n, B_n, C_n, D_n, E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA_n%2C+B_n%2C+C_n%2C+D_n%2C+E_6%2C+E_7%2C+E_8%2C+F_4%2C+G_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) briefly mentioned in Theorem [2](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#class). We will now turn to this classification in the next section, and then use root systems to recover the Lie algebra.

**— 7\. Classification of root systems —**

In this section we classify all the irreducible root systems ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) on a finite dimensional complex Hilbert space ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), up to Hilbert space isometry. Of course, we may take ![{{\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to be a standard complex Hilbert space ![{{\bf C}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) without loss of generality. The arguments here are purely elementary, proceeding purely from the root system axioms rather than from any Lie algebra theory.

Actually, we can quickly pass from the complex setting to the real setting. By axiom (v), ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) contains a basis ![{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha_1%2C%5Cldots%2C%5Calpha_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\bf C}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); by axiom (iv), the inner products between these basis vectors are real, as are the inner products between any other root and a basis root. From this we see that ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) lies in the _real_ vector space spanned by the basis roots, so by a change of basis we may assume without loss of generality that ![{\Phi \subset {\bf R}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Henceforth ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is assumed to lie in ![{{\bf R}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). From two applications of (iv) we see that for any two roots ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), the expression

![\displaystyle \frac{\langle \alpha,\beta \rangle}{\langle \alpha,\alpha \rangle} \frac{\langle \alpha,\beta \rangle}{\langle \beta,\beta \rangle} ](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Cfrac%7B%5Clangle+%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta+%5Crangle%7D%7B%5Clangle+%5Calpha%2C%5Calpha+%5Crangle%7D+%5Cfrac%7B%5Clangle+%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta+%5Crangle%7D%7B%5Clangle+%5Cbeta%2C%5Cbeta+%5Crangle%7D+&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

lies in ![{\frac{1}{4} {\bf Z}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B4%7D+%7B%5Cbf+Z%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); but it is also equal to ![{\cos^2 \angle(\alpha,\beta)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ccos%5E2+%5Cangle%28%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and hence

![\displaystyle \cos^2 \angle(\alpha,\beta) \in \{ 0, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{3}{4}, 1 \}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Ccos%5E2+%5Cangle%28%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%29+%5Cin+%5C%7B+0%2C+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B4%7D%2C+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B2%7D%2C+%5Cfrac%7B3%7D%7B4%7D%2C+1+%5C%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for all roots ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Analysing these cases further using (iv) again, we conclude that there are only a restricted range of options for a pair of roots ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002):

> **Lemma 26** Let ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be roots. Then one of the following occurs:
>
> - (0) ![{\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are orthogonal.
> - (1/4) ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) have the same length and subtend an angle of ![{\pi/3}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cpi%2F3%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) or ![{2\pi/3}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B2%5Cpi%2F3%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).
> - (1/2) ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has ![{\sqrt{2}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csqrt%7B2%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) times the length of ![{\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) or vice versa, and ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) subtend an angle of ![{\pi/4}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cpi%2F4%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) or ![{3\pi/4}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B3%5Cpi%2F4%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).
> - (3/4) ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) has ![{\sqrt{3}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csqrt%7B3%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) times the length of ![{\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) or vice versa, and ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) subtend an angle of ![{\pi/6}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cpi%2F6%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) or ![{5\pi/6}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B5%5Cpi%2F6%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).
> - (1) ![{\beta = \pm \alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta+%3D+%5Cpm+%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

We next record a useful corollary of Lemma [26](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#ang) (and axiom (ii)):

> **Corollary 27** Let ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be roots. If ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) subtend an acute angle, then ![{\alpha-\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha-%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\beta-\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta-%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are also roots. Equivalently, if ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) subtend an obtuse angle, then ![{\alpha-\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha-%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\beta-\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta-%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are not roots.

This follows from a routine case analysis and is omitted.

We can leverage Corollary [27](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#obtuse) as follows. Call an element ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\bf R}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)_regular_ if it is not orthogonal to any root, thus generic elements of ![{{\bf R}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are regular. Given a regular element ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), let ![{\Phi_h^+ := \{ \alpha \in \Phi: \langle \alpha,h\rangle > 0 \}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi_h%5E%2B+%3A%3D+%5C%7B+%5Calpha+%5Cin+%5CPhi%3A+%5Clangle+%5Calpha%2Ch%5Crangle+%3E+0+%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) denote the roots ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) which are _![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-positive_ in the sense that their inner product with ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is positive; thus ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is partitioned into ![{\Phi_h^+}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi_h%5E%2B%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{-\Phi_h^+}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B-%5CPhi_h%5E%2B%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). We will abbreviate ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-positive as _positive_ if ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is understood from context. Call a positive root ![{\alpha \in \Phi_h^+}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha+%5Cin+%5CPhi_h%5E%2B%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) a _![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple root_ (or _simple root_ for short) if it cannot be written as the sum of two positive roots. Clearly every positive root is then a linear combination of simple roots with natural number coefficients. By Corollary [27](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#obtuse), two simple roots cannot subtend an acute angle, and so any two distinct simple roots subtend a right or obtuse angle.

> **Example 28** Using the root system ![{\{ e_i -e_j: 1 \leq i,j \leq n; i \neq j \}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5C%7B+e_i+-e_j%3A+1+%5Cleq+i%2Cj+%5Cleq+n%3B+i+%5Cneq+j+%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{\mathfrak{sl}_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cmathfrak%7Bsl%7D_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) discussed previously, if one takes ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to be any vector in ![{{\bf C}^n_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+C%7D%5En_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with decreasing coefficients, then the positive roots are those roots ![{e_i - e_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Be_i+-+e_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{i<j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bi%3Cj%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and the simple roots are the roots ![{e_i - e_{i+1}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Be_i+-+e_%7Bi%2B1%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for ![{1 \leq i < n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B1+%5Cleq+i+%3C+n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Define an _admissible configuration_ to be a collection of unit vectors in ![{{\bf R}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in a open half-space ![{\{ v: \langle v,h \rangle>0\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5C%7B+v%3A+%5Clangle+v%2Ch+%5Crangle%3E0%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with the property that any two vectors in this collection form an angle of ![{\pi/2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cpi%2F2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{2\pi/3}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B2%5Cpi%2F3%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{3\pi/4}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B3%5Cpi%2F4%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), or ![{5\pi/6}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B5%5Cpi%2F6%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and call the configuration _irreducible_ if it cannot be decomposed into two non-empty orthogonal subsets. From Lemma [26](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#ang) and the above discussion we see that the unit vectors ![{\alpha/\|\alpha\|}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2F%5C%7C%5Calpha%5C%7C%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) associated to the simple roots are an admissible configuration. They are also irreducible, for if the simple roots partition into two orthogonal sets then it is not hard to show (using Corollary [27](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#obtuse)) that all positive roots lie in the span of one of these two sets, contradicting irreducibility of the root system.

We can say quite a bit about admissible configurations; the fact that the vectors in the system always subtend right or obtuse angles, combined with the half-space restriction, is quite limiting (basically because this information can be in violation of inequalities such as the [Bessel inequality](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_inequality), or the positive (semi-)definiteness ![{\| \sum_i c_i v_i \|^2 \geq 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5C%7C+%5Csum_i+c_i+v_i+%5C%7C%5E2+%5Cgeq+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of the [Gram matrix](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_matrix)). We begin with an assertion of linear independence:

> **Lemma 29** If ![{v_1,\ldots,v_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_1%2C%5Cldots%2Cv_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is an admissible configuration, then it is linearly independent.

Among other things, this shows that the number of simple roots of a semisimple Lie algebra is equal to the rank of that algebra.

_Proof:_ Suppose this is not the case, then one has a non-trivial linear constraint

![\displaystyle \sum_{i \in A} c_i v_i = \sum_{j \in B} c_j v_j](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Csum_%7Bi+%5Cin+A%7D+c_i+v_i+%3D+%5Csum_%7Bj+%5Cin+B%7D+c_j+v_j&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for some positive ![{c_i,c_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bc_i%2Cc_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and disjoint ![{A,B \subset \{1,\ldots,n\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA%2CB+%5Csubset+%5C%7B1%2C%5Cldots%2Cn%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). But as any two vectors in an admissible configuration subtend a right or obtuse angle, ![{\langle \sum_{i \in A} c_i v_i, \sum_{j \in B} c_j v_j\rangle \leq 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle+%5Csum_%7Bi+%5Cin+A%7D+c_i+v_i%2C+%5Csum_%7Bj+%5Cin+B%7D+c_j+v_j%5Crangle+%5Cleq+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and thus ![{\sum_{i \in A} c_i v_i = \sum_{j \in B} c_j v_j=0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Csum_%7Bi+%5Cin+A%7D+c_i+v_i+%3D+%5Csum_%7Bj+%5Cin+B%7D+c_j+v_j%3D0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). But this is not possible as all the ![{v_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) lie in an open half-space. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

Define the [Coxeter diagram](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) of an admissible configuration ![{v_1,\ldots,v_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_1%2C%5Cldots%2Cv_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to be the graph with vertices ![{v_1,\ldots,v_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_1%2C%5Cldots%2Cv_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and with any two vertices ![{v_i,v_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%2Cv_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) connected by an edge of multiplicity ![{4 \cos^2 \angle v_i,v_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B4+%5Ccos%5E2+%5Cangle+v_i%2Cv_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), thus two vertices are unconnected if they are orthogonal, connected with a single edge if they subtend an angle of ![{2\pi/3}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B2%5Cpi%2F3%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), a double edge if they subtend an angle of ![{3\pi/4}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B3%5Cpi%2F4%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and a triple edge if they subtend an angle of ![{5\pi/6}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B5%5Cpi%2F6%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The irreducibility of a configuration is equivalent to the connectedness of a Coxeter diagram. Note that the Coxeter diagram describes all the inner products between the ![{v_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and thus describes the ![{v_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) up to an orthogonal transformation (as can be seen for instance by applying the Gram-Schmidt process).

> **Lemma 30** The Coxeter diagram of an admissible configuration is [acyclic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_(graph_theory)) (ignoring multiplicity of edges). In particular, the Coxeter diagram of an irreducible admissible configuration is a [tree](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_(graph_theory)).

_Proof:_ Suppose for contradiction that the Coxeter diagram contains a cycle ![{v_1,\ldots,v_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_1%2C%5Cldots%2Cv_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we see that ![{\langle v_i,v_{i+1}\rangle \leq - \frac{1}{2}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle+v_i%2Cv_%7Bi%2B1%7D%5Crangle+%5Cleq+-+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B2%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for ![{i=1,\ldots,n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bi%3D1%2C%5Cldots%2Cn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (with the convention ![{v_{n+1}=v_1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_%7Bn%2B1%7D%3Dv_1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)) and ![{\langle v_i,v_j \rangle \leq 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle+v_i%2Cv_j+%5Crangle+%5Cleq+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all other ![{i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). This implies that ![{\|\sum_{i=1}^n v_i\|^2 \leq 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5C%7C%5Csum_%7Bi%3D1%7D%5En+v_i%5C%7C%5E2+%5Cleq+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), which contradicts the linear independence of the ![{v_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

> **Lemma 31** Any vertex in the Coxeter diagram has degree at most three (counting multiplicity).

_Proof:_ Let ![{v_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a vertex which is adjacent to some other vertices ![{v_1,\ldots,v_d}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_1%2C%5Cldots%2Cv_d%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), which are then an orthonormal system. By Bessel’s inequality (and linear independence) one has

![\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^d \langle v_0,v_i\rangle^2 < 1.](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Csum_%7Bi%3D1%7D%5Ed+%5Clangle+v_0%2Cv_i%5Crangle%5E2+%3C+1.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

But from construction of the Coxeter diagram we have ![{\langle v_0,v_i\rangle^2 = \frac{m_i}{4}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle+v_0%2Cv_i%5Crangle%5E2+%3D+%5Cfrac%7Bm_i%7D%7B4%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for each ![{i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), where ![{m_i \in \{1,2,3\}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bm_i+%5Cin+%5C%7B1%2C2%2C3%5C%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the multiplicity of the edge connecting ![{v_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{v_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). The claim follows. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

We can also contract simple edges:

> **Lemma 32** If ![{v_1,\ldots,v_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_1%2C%5Cldots%2Cv_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is an admissible configuration with ![{v_i,v_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%2Cv_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) joined by a single edge, then the configuration formed from ![{v_1,\ldots,v_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_1%2C%5Cldots%2Cv_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) by replacing ![{v_i,v_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%2Cv_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with the single vertex ![{v_i+v_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%2Bv_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is again an admissible configuration, with the resulting Coxeter diagram formed from the original Coxeter diagram by deleting the edge between ![{v_i}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{v_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and then identifying ![{v_i,v_j}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bv_i%2Cv_j%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) together.

This follows easily from acyclicity and direct computation.

By Lemma [31](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#loo) and Lemma [32](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#con), the Coxeter diagram can never form a vertex of degree three no matter how many simple edges are contracted. From this we can easily show that connected Coxeter diagrams must have one of the following shapes:

- ![{A_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002): ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) vertices joined in a chain of simple edges;
- ![{BCF_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BBCF_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002): ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) vertices joined in a chain of edges, one of which is a double edge and all others are simple edges;
- ![{DE_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BDE_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002): three chains of simple edges emenating from a common vertex (forming a “Y” shape), connecting ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) vertices in all;
- ![{G_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BG_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002): Two vertices joined by a triple edge.

We can cut down the ![{BCF_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BBCF_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{DE_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BDE_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) cases further:

> **Lemma 33** The Coxeter diagram of an admissible configuration cannot contain as a subgraph
>
> - (a) A chain of four edges, with one of the interior edges a double edge;
> - (b) Three chains of two simple edges each, emenating from a common vertex;
> - (c) Three chains of simple edges of length ![{1, 2, 5}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B1%2C+2%2C+5%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) respectively, emenating from a common vertex.
> - (d) Three chains of simple edges of length ![{1, 3, 3}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B1%2C+3%2C+3%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) respectively, emenating from a common vertex.

_Proof:_ To exclude (a), suppose for contradiction that we have two chains ![{(u_1,u_2)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28u_1%2Cu_2%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{(v_1,v_2,v_3)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28v_1%2Cv_2%2Cv_3%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of simple edges, with ![{u_2,v_3}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bu_2%2Cv_3%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) joined by a double edge. Writing ![{U := \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} (u_1+2u_2)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B%5Csqrt%7B3%7D%7D+%28u_1%2B2u_2%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{V := \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(v_1+2v_2+3v_3)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B%5Csqrt%7B6%7D%7D%28v_1%2B2v_2%2B3v_3%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), one computes that ![{U,V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU%2CV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are unit vectors with inner product ![{\langle U,V \rangle = -1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle+U%2CV+%5Crangle+%3D+-1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), implying that ![{U,V}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU%2CV%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are parallel, contradicting linear independence.

To exclude (b), suppose that we have three chains ![{(u_1,u_2,x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28u_1%2Cu_2%2Cx%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{(v_1,v_2,x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28v_1%2Cv_2%2Cx%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{(w_1,w_2,x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28w_1%2Cw_2%2Cx%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of simple edges joined at ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then the vectors ![{U := \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(u_1+2u_2), V := \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(v_1+2v_2), W := \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(w_1+2w_2)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B%5Csqrt%7B3%7D%7D%28u_1%2B2u_2%29%2C+V+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B%5Csqrt%7B3%7D%7D%28v_1%2B2v_2%29%2C+W+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B%5Csqrt%7B3%7D%7D%28w_1%2B2w_2%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are an orthonormal system that each have an inner product of ![{-1/\sqrt{3}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B-1%2F%5Csqrt%7B3%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) each with ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Comparing this with Bessel’s inequality we conclude that ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) lies in the span of ![{U,V,W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU%2CV%2CW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), contradicting linear independence.

To exclude (c), suppose we have three chains ![{(u_1,x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28u_1%2Cx%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{(v_1,v_2,x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28v_1%2Cv_2%2Cx%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{(w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4,w_5,x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28w_1%2Cw_2%2Cw_3%2Cw_4%2Cw_5%2Cx%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of simple edges joined at ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Writing ![{U := u_1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU+%3A%3D+u_1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{V := \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(v_1+2v_2)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B%5Csqrt%7B3%7D%7D%28v_1%2B2v_2%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{W := \frac{1}{\sqrt{15}}(w_1+2w_2+3w_3+4w_4+5w_5)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B%5Csqrt%7B15%7D%7D%28w_1%2B2w_2%2B3w_3%2B4w_4%2B5w_5%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we compute that ![{U,V,W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU%2CV%2CW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are an orthonormal system that have inner products of ![{-1/2, -1/\sqrt{3}, -\frac{5}{\sqrt{60}}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B-1%2F2%2C+-1%2F%5Csqrt%7B3%7D%2C+-%5Cfrac%7B5%7D%7B%5Csqrt%7B60%7D%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) respectively with ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). As ![{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{25}{60} = 1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B4%7D%2B%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B3%7D%2B%5Cfrac%7B25%7D%7B60%7D+%3D+1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), this forces ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to lie in the span of ![{U,V,W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU%2CV%2CW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), again contradicting linear independence.

The same argument works to exclude (d): from three chains ![{(u_1,x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28u_1%2Cx%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{(v_1,v_2,v_3,x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28v_1%2Cv_2%2Cv_3%2Cx%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{(w_1,w_2,w_3,x)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%28w_1%2Cw_2%2Cw_3%2Cx%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), repeat the previous argument with ![{U := u_1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU+%3A%3D+u_1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{V := \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(v_1+2v_2+3v_3)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BV+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B%5Csqrt%7B6%7D%7D%28v_1%2B2v_2%2B3v_3%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{W := \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(w_1+2w_2+3w_3)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B%5Csqrt%7B6%7D%7D%28w_1%2B2w_2%2B3w_3%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); ![{U,V,W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BU%2CV%2CW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are now an orthonormal system with inner products of ![{-\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{3}{2\sqrt{6}}, -\frac{3}{2\sqrt{6}}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B-%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B2%7D%2C+-%5Cfrac%7B3%7D%7B2%5Csqrt%7B6%7D%7D%2C+-%5Cfrac%7B3%7D%7B2%5Csqrt%7B6%7D%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and as ![{\frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = 1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B4%7D+%2B+%5Cfrac%7B3%7D%7B8%7D+%2B+%5Cfrac%7B3%7D%7B8%7D+%3D+1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) one can contradict linear independence as before. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

We remark that one could also obtain the required contradictions in the above proof by verifying in all four cases that the [Gram matrix](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramian_matrix) of the subconfiguration has determinant zero.

> **Corollary 34** The Coxeter diagram of an irreducible admissible configuration must take one of the following forms:
>
> - ![{A_n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA_n%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002): ![{n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) vertices joined in a chain of simple edges for some ![{n \geq 1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bn+%5Cgeq+1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002);

Now we return to root systems. Fixing a regular ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we define the [Dynkin diagram](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynkin_diagram) to be the Coxeter diagram associated to the (unit vectors of the) simple roots, except that we orient the double or triple edges to point from the longer root to the shorter root. (Note from Lemma [26](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#ang) that we know exactly what the ratio between lengths is in these cases; in particular, the Dynkin diagram describes the root system up to a unitary transformation and dilation.) > **Remark 36** A slightly different way to reach the classification is to replace the Dynkin diagram by the _extended Dynkin diagram_ in which one also adds the maximal negative root in addition to the simple roots; this breaks the linear independence, but one can then label each vertex by the coefficient in the linear combination needed to make the roots sum to zero, and one can then analyse these multiplicities to classify the possible diagrams and thence the root systems.

Now we show how the simple roots can be used to recover the entire root system. Define the [Weyl group](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_group)![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to be the group generated by all the reflections ![{s_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) coming from all the roots ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); as the roots span ![{{\bf R}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and obey axiom (ii), the Weyl group acts faithfully on the finite set ![{\Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and is thus itself finite.

> **Lemma 37** Let ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be regular, and let ![{h'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be any element of ![{{\bf R}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then there exists ![{w \in W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw+%5Cin+W%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) such that ![{\langle w(h'), \alpha \rangle \geq 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle+w%28h%27%29%2C+%5Calpha+%5Crangle+%5Cgeq+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for all ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple roots ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (or equivalently, for all ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-positive roots ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)). In particular, if ![{h'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is regular, then ![{\Phi_{w(h')}^+ = \Phi_h^+}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi_%7Bw%28h%27%29%7D%5E%2B+%3D+%5CPhi_h%5E%2B%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so that all ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple roots are ![{w(h')}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw%28h%27%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple and vice versa.
>
> Furthermore, every root can be mapped by an element of ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to an ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple root.
>
> Finally, ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is generated by the reflections ![{s_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) coming from the ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple roots ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

_Proof:_ Let ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be a simple root. The action of the reflection ![{s_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) maps ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to ![{-\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B-%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and maps all other simple roots ![{\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to ![{\beta+m\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta%2Bm%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for some non-negative ![{m}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bm%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (since ![{\alpha,\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) subtend a right or obtuse angle). In particular, we see that ![{s_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) maps all positive roots other than ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to positive roots, and hence (as ![{s_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is an involution)

![\displaystyle s_\alpha(\Phi_h^+) = \Phi_h^+ \cup \{-\alpha\} \backslash \{\alpha\}.](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+s_%5Calpha%28%5CPhi_h%5E%2B%29+%3D+%5CPhi_h%5E%2B+%5Ccup+%5C%7B-%5Calpha%5C%7D+%5Cbackslash+%5C%7B%5Calpha%5C%7D.&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

In particular, if we define ![{\rho := \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\beta \in \Phi_h^+} \beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Crho+%3A%3D+%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B2%7D+%5Csum_%7B%5Cbeta+%5Cin+%5CPhi_h%5E%2B%7D+%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), then

![\displaystyle s_\alpha(\rho) = \rho-\alpha \ \ \ \ \ (17)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+s_%5Calpha%28%5Crho%29+%3D+%5Crho-%5Calpha+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2817%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for all simple roots ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Let ![{W_h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW_h%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the subgroup of ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) generated by the ![{s_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for the simple roots ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and choose ![{w \in W_h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bw+%5Cin+W_h%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to maximise ![{\langle w(h'), \rho \rangle}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle+w%28h%27%29%2C+%5Crho+%5Crangle%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then from [(17)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sar) we have ![{\langle w(h'),\alpha \rangle \geq 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Clangle+w%28h%27%29%2C%5Calpha+%5Crangle+%5Cgeq+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), giving the first claim. Since every root ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is ![{h'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple for some regular ![{h'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) (by selecting ![{h'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to very nearly be orthogonal to ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)), we conclude that every root can be mapped by an element of ![{W_h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW_h%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to a ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple root in ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), giving the second claim. Thus for any root ![{\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{s_\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is conjugate in ![{W_h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW_h%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to a reflection ![{s_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for a ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple root ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so ![{s_\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) lies in ![{W_h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW_h%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and so ![{W=W_h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%3DW_h%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), giving the final claim. ![\Box](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CBox&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

> **Remark 38** The set of all ![{h'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) for which ![{\Phi_{h'}^+ = \Phi_h^+}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi_%7Bh%27%7D%5E%2B+%3D+%5CPhi_h%5E%2B%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is known as the [Weyl chamber](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_group) associated to ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); this is an open polyhedral cone in ![{{\bf R}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and the above lemma shows that it is the interior of a fundamental domain of the action of the Weyl group. In the case of the special linear group, the standard Weyl chamber (in ![{{\bf R}^n_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) now instead of ![{{\bf R}^n}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)) would be the set of vectors ![{h' \in {\bf R}^n_0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%27+%5Cin+%7B%5Cbf+R%7D%5En_0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with decreasing coefficients.

From the above lemma we can reconstruct the root system from the simple roots by using the reflections ![{s_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bs_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) associated to the simple roots to generate the Weyl group ![{W}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BW%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and then applying the Weyl group to the simple roots to recover all the roots. Note that the lemma also shows that the set of ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple roots and ![{h'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)-simple roots are isomorphic for any regular ![{h,h'}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%2Ch%27%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so that the Dynkin diagram is indeed independent (up to isomorphism) of the choice of regular element ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as claimed earlier. We have thus in principle described the irreducible root systems (up to isomorphism) as coming from the Dynkin diagrams ![{A_n,B_n,C_n,D_n,E_6,E_7,E_8,F_4,G_2}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BA_n%2CB_n%2CC_n%2CD_n%2CE_6%2CE_7%2CE_8%2CF_4%2CG_2%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); see for instance the [Wikipedia page on root systems](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_system) for explicit descriptions of all of these. With these explicit descriptions one can verify that all of these systems are indeed irreducible root systems.

**— 8\. Chevalley bases —**

Now that we have described root systems, we use them to reconstruct Lie algebras. We first begin with an abstract uniqueness result that shows that a simple Lie algebra is determined up to isomorphism by its root system.

> **Theorem 39 (Root system uniquely determines a simple Lie algebra)** Let ![{{\mathfrak g}, \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%2C+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be simple Lie algebras with Cartan subalgebras ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{\tilde {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and root systems ![{\Phi \subset {\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi+%5Csubset+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), ![{\tilde \Phi \subset \tilde {\mathfrak h}^*}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%5CPhi+%5Csubset+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%5E%2A%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Suppose that one can identify ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) with ![{\tilde {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as vector spaces in such a way that the root systems agree: ![{\Phi = \tilde \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi+%3D+%5Ctilde+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then the identification between ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\tilde {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) can be extended to an identification of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as Lie algebras.

_Proof:_ First we note from [(11)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#gha) and the identification ![{\Phi=\tilde \Phi}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5CPhi%3D%5Ctilde+%5CPhi%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) that the Killing forms on ![{{\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\tilde {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) agree, so we will identify ![{{\mathfrak h}, \tilde {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%2C+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as Hilbert spaces, not just as vector spaces.

The strategy will be exploit a Lie algebra version of the [Goursat lemma](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goursat_lemma) (or the [Schur lemma](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur_lemma)), finding a sufficiently “non-degenerate” subalgebra ![{{\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and using the simple nature of ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to show that this subalgebra is the graph of an isomorphism from ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) to ![{\tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). This strategy will follow the same general strategy used in Theorem [20](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#sl2), namely to start with a “highest weight” space and apply lowering operators to discover the required graph.

We turn to the details. Pick a regular element ![{h}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bh%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) of ![{{\mathfrak h} =\tilde {\mathfrak h}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D+%3D%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), so that one has a notion of a positive root. For every simple root ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), we select non-zero elements ![{X_\alpha, Y_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX_%5Calpha%2C+Y_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), of ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha, {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{-\alpha}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%2C+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B-%5Calpha%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) respectively such that

![\displaystyle [X_\alpha,Y_\alpha] = H_\alpha \ \ \ \ \ (18)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5BX_%5Calpha%2CY_%5Calpha%5D+%3D+H_%5Calpha+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2818%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

where ![{H_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BH_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is the co-root of ![{\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); similarly select ![{\tilde X_\alpha, \tilde Y_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+X_%5Calpha%2C+%5Ctilde+Y_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{\tilde {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\alpha, \tilde {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_{-\alpha}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Calpha%2C+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%7B-%5Calpha%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and set ![{X'_\alpha := X_\alpha \oplus \tilde X_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX%27_%5Calpha+%3A%3D+X_%5Calpha+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+X_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{Y'_\alpha := Y_\alpha \oplus \tilde Y_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BY%27_%5Calpha+%3A%3D+Y_%5Calpha+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+Y_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Let ![{{\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the subalgebra of ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus {\mathfrak g}' }](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%27+%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) generated by the ![{X'_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX%27_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{Y'_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BY%27_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). It is not hard to see that the ![{X_\alpha, Y_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX_%5Calpha%2C+Y_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) generate ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) as a Lie algebra, so ![{{\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) surjects onto ![{{\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); similarly ![{{\mathfrak k}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+k%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) surjects onto ![{{\mathfrak g}' }](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%27+%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

(It is in fact unique, though we will not need this fact here.) Then we have one-dimensional spaces ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{\tilde {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), and thus a two-dimensional subspace ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\beta \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Cbeta+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) in ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Inside this subspace, we select a one-dimensional subspace ![{L}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BL%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) which is not equal to ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\beta \oplus 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Cbeta+%5Coplus+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) or ![{0 \times \tilde {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B0+%5Ctimes+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); in particular, ![{L}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BL%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is not contained in ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus 0}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+0%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) or ![{0 \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B0+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Let ![{{\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) be the subspace of ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus {\mathfrak g}' }](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%27+%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) generated by ![{L}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BL%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and the adjoint action of the lowering operators ![{Y'_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BY%27_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002), thus it is spanned by elements of the form

![\displaystyle \hbox{ad} Y'_{\alpha_1} \ldots \hbox{ad} Y'_{\alpha_k} x \ \ \ \ \ (19)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cdisplaystyle+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+Y%27_%7B%5Calpha_1%7D+%5Cldots+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+Y%27_%7B%5Calpha_k%7D+x+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%5C+%2819%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002)

for simple roots ![{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha_1%2C%5Cldots%2C%5Calpha_k%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and ![{x \in L}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx+%5Cin+L%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). Then ![{{\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) contains ![{L}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BL%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) and is thus not contained in ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus 0, 0 \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+0%2C+0+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002); because [(19)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#add) only involves lowering operators, we also see that ![{L}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BL%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) does not contain any other element of ![{{\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\beta \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}^{\mathfrak h}_\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Cbeta+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%5E%7B%5Cmathfrak+h%7D_%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) other than ![{{\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). In particular, ![{L}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BL%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is not all of ![{{\mathfrak g} \oplus \tilde {\mathfrak g}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D+%5Coplus+%5Ctilde+%7B%5Cmathfrak+g%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002).

Clearly ![{{\mathfrak l}}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%7B%5Cmathfrak+l%7D%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) is closed under the adjoint action of the lowering operators ![{Y'_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BY%27_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). We claim that it is also closed under the adjoint action of the raising operators ![{X'_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX%27_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). To see this, first observe that ![{X'_\alpha, Y'_\gamma}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7BX%27_%5Calpha%2C+Y%27_%5Cgamma%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) commute when ![{\alpha,\gamma}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cgamma%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) are distinct simple roots, because ![{\alpha-\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha-%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) cannot be a root (since this would make one of ![{\alpha,\gamma}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Calpha%2C%5Cgamma%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) non-simple). Next, from [(18)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#xya) we see that ![{\hbox{ad} X'_\alpha \hbox{ad} Y'_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+X%27_%5Calpha+%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+Y%27_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) acts as a scalar on any element of the form [(19)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2013/04/27/notes-on-the-classification-of-complex-lie-algebras/#add), while from the maximality of ![{\beta}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Cbeta%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) we see that ![{\hbox{ad} X'_\alpha}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7B%5Chbox%7Bad%7D+X%27_%5Calpha%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002) annihilates ![{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000000&s=0&c=20201002). From this the claim easily follows.
