\section{Endpoint and extremal questions}
\label{sec:open-problems}
The preceding conclusions leave two specific questions in the obstruction
theory. They do not enter the proofs of the finite-parameter separation or
linear coercivity theorems. They are tests of endpoint completeness and
optimal cost, not a claim that all further field interfaces or geometric
realization problems reduce to these two questions.

\begin{problem}[The density-independent weak zero-Weyl endpoint]
Assume the original actual conformal monopole supply, $p\ge A>0$,
$a^2\ge T_+$. Does there exist a universal $c>0$ such that, for every
admissible metric and every $\beta\ge2$,
\[
 \kappa_\beta:=\fint_M|\Ric+g|^2+\frac{D_\beta}{V}
 \ge c\frac{(\beta-2)^2}{\beta^2}?
\]
Here no upper bound for $\chi(M)/A$ is prescribed.
\end{problem}
Every fixed finite-density version is proved in
Section~\ref{sec:finite-density-endpoint}; a constant
$c_0(1+B)^{-N}$ is supplied in Section~\ref{sec:web-primitive-rate}.
Thus a sequence violating the remaining target must leave every fixed
density class. The bounded-density theorem is no longer an open
intermediate step.
In particular, Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-density-original-family}
already settles the endpoint uniformly on the original balanced
surgery family. The density-independent question enlarges that
topological range; the first-order question below concerns the
optimal cost within the original family.
The $D_\beta/V$ normalization here differs from the finite-parameter
quantity $D_\beta/(\beta V)$. The unknown case is a sequence with
$\beta\to\infty$ and $\kappa_\beta\to0$ in which the exterior geometry
degenerates together with the core. A counterexample must be a sequence
of closed metrics with the full stated actual supply and budgets.
Local actual cores or formal curvature tensors alone do not supply it.
For the bounded self-dual forms associated with this limit, the Hodge
identity forces
\[
 \fint_M|\nabla\omega|^2\longrightarrow\frac83,
\]
not parallelism. Sections~\ref{sec:web-double-weighted} and
\ref{sec:web-mass-transfer} remove several mixed error terms and
transfer the radial defect back to the actual torsion. The inverse
amplitude tests in Section~\ref{sec:web-inverse-amplitude} exclude
gradient energy deep in arbitrarily low amplitude wells. They do not
exclude transition layers in a fixed intermediate amplitude band.
The full radial one-form in Section~\ref{sec:web-radial-matrix}
has an indefinite zeroth-order term, so it does not inherit the
fifth-line positive-potential contradiction without further information.

After the scalar-minus-four Yamabe reduction, one sufficient, narrower
route is to find fixed cutoffs defining fifth-line
sections $c_j$, and a fixed radial cap $T_M$, for which
\[
 \liminf_j\operatorname{Re}\fint\langle c_j,T_M(c_j)\rangle>0,
 \qquad (\Delta_{5A_j}+10/3)w_j=T_M(c_j),
\]
while the complete actual weak equation gives
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\fint
 \langle c_j,(\Delta_{5A_j}+10/3)w_j\rangle\longrightarrow0.
\]
These two conclusions would contradict each other. Both the retained
mass and the full weak equation must be supplied at unbounded density;
vanishing of one radial derivative or one selected mixed error is not
by itself the required pair of conclusions. A different direct signed
identity, or an actual closed escaping construction, remains possible.

\begin{problem}[The balanced first-order coefficient]
For the balanced family in Section~\ref{sec:variational-geometry}, let
$Q_j=A_jh_j$ and $\alpha_j=\inf_g\E(g)$. Determine
$\liminf\alpha_j/h_j$ and $\limsup\alpha_j/h_j$, with matching recovery
sequences. Equivalently, determine the first-order asymptotics of
$\inf_gP(g)$.
\end{problem}
The exact variational reduction gives
\[
 \alpha_j=4\frac{\inf_gP(g)}{8\pi^2A_j}+O(h_j^2).
\]
Consequently a coefficient of $4$ is equivalent to
$\inf_gP(g)=8\pi^2Q_j+o(Q_j)$. A lower-cost construction on the same
family is an equally legitimate alternative. The required precision is
$o(Q_j)$, not merely $o(V_j)$. A near-minimizer cannot retain a positive
density of removable over-costly cells; that fact by itself does not
exclude cheaper cells or diffuse competing geometry. The signed
characteristic formulas make this distinction explicit. In the notation
of \eqref{proof:signed-selection:e5.1},
\[
 P=8\pi^2N+Z.
\]
The sufficient compensated estimate on genuine near-minimizers is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:open-compensated}
 Z\ge8\pi^2(Q-N)-o(Q),
\end{equation}
as in \eqref{proof:signed-selection:e5.3}. Separate lower bounds for
$N$ and $Z$ are not necessary. Competitive metrics that violate an
uncompensated selection rule need not be near-minimizers, and do not by
themselves decide the optimal coefficient.

Section~\ref{sec:variational-selection-global-cost} supplies
near-minimizing sequences with $o(Q)$ first- and second-variation
control against the critical integral test class. Their admissible
tests need not have fixed pointwise derivative bounds. This does not
make an arbitrary curvature tensor an admissible test or turn weak
near-stationarity into a strong critical-metric equation. The
simultaneous actual-field decomposition gives an alternative sufficient
target: control its kinetic term by its nonnegative remainder up to
$o(Q)$. The ideal-minimizer route must instead recover enough of the
original marked topology and all end corrections. Neither the
AE cost for a complete ordinary filling nor a compact orbifold
decomposition supplies that conclusion in every ALE or collapsing case.

The associated existence questions are separate. At the Chern boundary
$T_+=3T_->0$, it remains to determine whether the diameter hypothesis in
the complex-hyperbolic realization theorem can be removed. On the fixed
single-Dehn-twist surface bundle $E_D$, the constructed metrics satisfy
\[
 \delta_-(G_\ell)\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \Vol(G_\ell)=16\pi^2(p-1)(q-1)
\]
for fiber and base genera $p,q\ge2$. The value $16\pi^2$ is the
genus-two case in both factors. Whether the same smooth manifold admits
any negative Einstein metric is not decided by failure of a local repair
procedure or by nonattainment of one variational infimum.

Finally, the weaker single-solution hypothesis raises an application
question: identify a natural family not covered by the previous actual
suppliers but covered by the new hypothesis. For the stable-cohomotopy
examples, a comparison with every possible monopole class requires an
upper bound for the complete classical budget, not only the supplied
sign family. These are precise extensions of the theorems, rather than
additional assumptions in their proofs.
